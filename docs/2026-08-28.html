1. Trump renames Lake Ontario 'Lake America'

US President Donald Trump has signed an executive order seeking to change the name of Lake Ontario to Lake America, saying the renaming was effective immediately. The executive order gives the US Department of the Interior 30 days to update the name change with the Geographic Names Information Service. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney responded that "things must be called by their name, and this lake is called Lake Ontario - today and forever", noting the name can be traced back more than 400 years. Canadian officials from Ontario Premier Doug Ford to Democratic US lawmakers including Debbie Dingell of Michigan pushed back, with Dingell calling the order "idiotic" and vowing to introduce a bill in Congress to nullify it.

Why it matters: The move deepens an already serious rift in US-Canada relations following collapsed trade talks and new 50% tariffs, though there was a hint of possible return to negotiations after Canada's trade minister said the US had withdrawn some demands around French labelling requirements.

Source: BBC

2. Nepal flood death toll passes 460

The confirmed death toll from flash floods that hit the Nepal-Tibet border has surpassed 460 people, and more than 1,500 people are reported missing, with around 90 Americans among the unaccounted for according to the US State Department. Rasuwa, one of the hardest-hit regions, remains accessible only by helicopter, while search and rescue efforts continue as receding waters leave behind mud and debris. Authorities warn of potential secondary floods due to an overflowing lake in the area where the first glacier collapsed, and experts are concerned that the lake may burst. The State Department said it would provide the region with $500,000 in aid for the rescue efforts.

Why it matters: Nepali authorities have rescued more than 1,500 people, but it remains unclear if they are accounted for on the lists of missing persons, and the threat of secondary flooding continues to complicate rescue operations.

Source: NPR

3. 33 missing Britons spark Nepal family rush

Families are rushing to Nepal to search for the 33 British nationals who are still missing after catastrophic flash floods killed at least 538 people and left more than 1,500 missing. Relatives of missing Britons have shared their devastation as they try to reach loved ones last seen in the Rasuwa district, where power and communication lines are down, with many scrambling to book flights to Kathmandu. On Friday, authorities in Nepal and China issued warnings about the risk of a second wave of flooding, with Chinese authorities saying an artificial lake formed after a mudslide risked "sending flood waters downstream". Rescuers were forced to suspend their search for survivors, while residents who had returned to Rasuwa were told to evacuate their homes.

Source: The Guardian

4. Iran navy blockade cuts oil exports 80%

Iranian crude oil exports have plunged in August, with Tehran loading about 260,000 barrels per day for export so far this month, a decline of more than 80% compared with 1.7 million bpd in August 2025, according to trade intelligence firm Kpler. President Trump reimposed the blockade on July 14 in retaliation for Iran attacking oil tankers transiting the Hormuz Strait, and the US military has redirected 75 commercial ships, disabled three vessels and boarded two to enforce compliance. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced "Operation Economic Outcast" on Monday, describing it as a plan to sever Iran's financial connections around the world. Kpler tracks between 5 million bpd and 6 million bpd of crude oil transiting the strait, around a third of the 15 million bpd that exited before the war started on February 28.

Why it matters: The Trump administration believes Iran will eventually run out of money and have to capitulate, though Iran has remained defiant, rejecting US demands and calling on Washington to accept its conditions for Hormuz to reopen.

Source: CNBC

Iran's Foreign Ministry called on "all countries" not to implement US sanctions against Tehran on Friday, warning that doing so would represent "complicity" in imposing the "illegal will" of the United States. We're not looking to meet or anything."

Why it matters: Iran is allowing a "temporary and limited" corridor for ships to pass through the Hormuz Strait, with the future of maritime traffic depending on developments in understandings with Washington, though the Wall Street Journal reported the Trump administration has no interest in returning to the terms of the June memorandum of understanding.

Source: CNBC

Closer to Home

Celtic and Rangers ordered to play cup ties behind closed doors

Celtic and Rangers have both been ordered to play their next home Scottish Cup matches behind closed doors after last season's quarter-final was marred by fan disorder. Both clubs will also have to pay 100,000 pounds to the team they are drawn against in that fixture, and could face a second home match without fans if there is a further incident of unacceptable conduct in a Scottish Cup fixture. The sanctions relate to scenes in March after Celtic's penalty shootout victory at Ibrox, when supporters threw fireworks and other missiles, police officers were injured and a number of arrests were made. An independent review published last month concluded the incident came within a "very small margin" of becoming a violent mass confrontation.

Source: BBC
