1. Meta fined $567m in child safety ruling

A US judge in New Mexico ordered Meta to pay $567m (£421m) for its failure to warn the public about dangers its platforms posed to children, marking the largest ruling against the company over child safety. Judge Bryan Biedscheid said Meta is a "public nuisance" akin to air pollution and that it must put the money in a fund aimed at reducing future harms. The ruling is in addition to $375m in fines Meta was already ordered to pay in the case, for a total of $942m. The judge also ordered Meta to implement a mandatory usage limit for users under 18 of 90 cumulative hours per month across Instagram and Facebook, ban push notifications between 10:00 PM and 7:00 AM, and ensure no adult can message an underage user.

Why it matters: The ruling appears to be the first time a social media company has been deemed a public nuisance, and next week another major trial begins in California where nearly three dozen US states' attorneys general are suing Meta for violating child privacy laws.

Source: BBC

2. EasyJet agrees £5.7bn Apollo takeover

No-frills airline EasyJet has agreed to be taken over in a £5.7bn deal by US firm Apollo after rival bidder Castlelake withdrew from the bid battle. Under the offer, EasyJet shareholders will receive £7.15 per share. Apollo said it does not intend to cut any jobs in the first 12 months after the takeover is completed. EasyJet employs more than 19,000 people and flies around 1,200 routes across 35 European countries.

Why it matters: The deal will still need regulatory approval, including in the EU where rules mean EasyJet's owners will have to be majority EU-based; if EasyJet delists from the stock exchange, some jobs related to maintaining its public-listed operations could go.

Source: BBC

3. DRC Ebola cases top 4,000; virus may be mutating

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo has passed 4,000 confirmed cases with 1,801 deaths recorded as of 4 August, making it the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record. Africa CDC director general Dr Jean Kaseya said officials planned studies "to check if there is no additional issue, or maybe if the virus is not mutating," warning that "the level of severity of this Bundibugyo outbreak is unprecedented." More than two-thirds of Ebola deaths are happening in the community rather than in treatment centres, and contact tracing is severely insufficient, with only 10 contacts identified per patient when 40 would be expected. Officials announced a shift to active case search with community health workers going "door to door" and plans to begin using antiviral remdesivir on a compassionate use basis.

Why it matters: The outbreak is centred on the conflict-affected mining province of Ituri but has spread to four other provinces, and DRC authorities are investigating a suspected Ebola death on a boat heading to the capital Kinshasa.

Source: The Guardian

4. Trump imposes 15% tariff on polysilicon

US President Donald Trump signed an executive order imposing a 15% tariff on imported products made from polysilicon, a crucial material used in semiconductors and solar panels, along with minimum import prices on polysilicon and related products. The move follows a national security investigation and is intended to help protect US manufacturers from Chinese competition. The US share of global polysilicon production has fallen from 50% in 2005 to less than 2% in 2024, Trump said, noting that China holds a near monopoly. The measures are due to take effect in December.

Why it matters: China's embassy said the move "seriously disrupts" trade and warned Beijing will act to protect its companies, while the tariff follows other US restrictions on Chinese drones, humanoid robots and other tech products.

Source: BBC

5. AI used to create novel viruses for first time

Researchers at Stanford University and the Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard used a naturally occurring phage as a template to generate thousands of genomes with AI, chemically synthesising nearly 300 of them and testing them in laboratory conditions, resulting in 16 viable viruses. Closer to Home

Boxer charged over Scottish aid worker's Athens murder

Afghan boxer Sharif Ahmadzai, 26, has been charged with homicide with intent - equivalent to a murder charge in Scots law - alongside robbery and weapons offences in connection with the death of Scottish aid worker Elisabeth Ross, 38, whose body was discovered in a suitcase in the Kypseli area of Athens on 18 July. His wife, Alaina Hall, gave police "key" and "decisive" details including location data from a tracking app that placed both Ahmadzai and Ross at the same apartment on 15 July - the day Ross is thought to have been killed. Ahmadzai is also alleged to have stolen more than 10,000 euros (£8,500) from the victim's bank accounts using her bank cards. He was remanded in custody until his trial, which could take up to 18 months.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Business rates cut 20% for pubs, clubs, live music venues

From April 2027, pubs, social clubs and live music venues across England will receive a 20% cut to their business rates bills, saving the typical pub an estimated £1,100 in the next financial year. The package is worth around £100 million a year and will benefit nearly 32,000 pubs, clubs and live music venues. The cut comes on top of a 15% relief off 2026/27 bills announced in January, with bills frozen in real terms for a further two years.

Source: HM Treasury

Bank Rate held at 3.75% in July 2026

At its meeting ending 29 July 2026, the Monetary Policy Committee voted 6-3 to maintain Bank Rate at 3.75%, with three members voting to increase Bank Rate by 0.25 percentage points to 4%. CPI inflation has fallen to 2.6% but is expected to rise later this year as higher energy prices continue to pass through. The next rate decision is due 17 September 2026.

Source: Bank of England
