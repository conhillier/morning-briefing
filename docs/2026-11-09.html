1. Krakow gunman dies after mass shooting

The 24-year-old Polish man suspected of killing six people in central Krakow has died in hospital early Monday morning, Polish police confirmed. The attack began shortly before 15:00 local time in Krakow's busy Main Market Square, where armed officers engaged the suspect in a gunfight and shot him within three minutes of the attack beginning. Four victims are Polish, while a British national and an Austrian woman were also killed.

Why it matters: Poland declared two days of national mourning. Prime Minister Donald Tusk said: "Until now in Poland we haven't had such drastic cases of terrorism...that's what we have to call this cruel attack on people's lives here in Krakow."

Source: BBC

2. Nvidia investigates CEO after spy affair admission

Nvidia said on Sunday it "is initiating an independent investigation" after reports that founder and CEO Jensen Huang had a close relationship with a Chinese businesswoman who passed information to Beijing. The company's board said it has no reason to believe that either US national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, but the inquiry will examine all available information.

Why it matters: The investigation follows a rough period for Nvidia on Wall Street, with a sell-off of tech stocks leading to a total decline of nearly $1 trillion in value off the Nasdaq in one week and erasing about $2 trillion in a month.

Source: The Guardian

3. Europe joins Ukraine peace talks in Abu Dhabi

European officials will attend US-mediated peace talks between Russia and Ukraine in the United Arab Emirates this week, France's foreign minister confirmed on Sunday, defying earlier Kremlin objections. The meetings are set to take place in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday and Wednesday and had previously been presented as a three-way session between Washington, Moscow and Kyiv.

Why it matters: The gaps between the two countries remain substantial, with Russia demanding Ukraine give up large parts of its territory and renounce NATO membership, while Ukrainian President Zelenskyy has warned that any Trump-referenced agreement could be "dangerous."

Source: NPR

5. Egypt train crash kills seven, injures 125

Seven people were killed and at least 125 injured when two trains collided on Sunday south of Cairo, officials said. The crash occurred in al-Ayyat district in Giza province, about 50km south of Cairo, when a train travelling to Fayoum province crashed into the rear of a stationary train heading to the southern province of Aswan. The health ministry said more than 70 of the injured were discharged after receiving treatment. Egypt's president Abdel Fatah al-Sisi ordered a prompt investigation via social media.

Why it matters: Train derailments and crashes are common in Egypt, where the railway system has a history of mismanagement and poor maintenance of equipment, and the government has announced modernisation initiatives in recent years.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Ukrainian refugee faces PS9,000 Scots property tax

Kateryna Stepanovych, 38, who fled Ukraine in 2023, has been told she could face a PS9,000 Additional Dwelling Supplement (ADS) charge because she still owns a flat in Kyiv, bought in 2018 before the Russian invasion. The charge arose when she tried to buy a house in North Ayrshire with her Scottish partner Mattias Lindgren; unable to afford the additional cost, they had to withdraw their offer. Deputy First Minister Jenny Gilruth responded to the case in a letter stating there are "no provisions within the legislation that allow either the Scottish government or Revenue Scotland to disapply ADS on a discretionary or case-by-case basis." First Minister John Swinney said he would ask Revenue Scotland to investigate the case after it was raised in the Scottish Parliament by Lib Dem MSP Liam McArthur.

Source: BBC
