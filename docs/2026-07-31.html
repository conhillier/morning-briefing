1. Hamas agrees to disarm in Gaza deal

US President Donald Trump announced that the Board of Peace has reached an agreement for the "complete disarmament" of Hamas and other armed groups in Gaza. A senior Hamas official told the BBC the group has agreed to the plan and that an official statement would be released soon, while Israel has yet to comment. The board said the agreement concludes months of "intensive, good faith negotiations", and that its focus is now on implementation. Trump said Israel would withdraw from Gaza "as disarmament is completed" and called the agreement "a critical step towards Gaza finally being governed by a new Palestinian government".

Why it matters: The BBC has seen the agreement, which provides for the inventorying and storage of Hamas's remaining heavy weapons under Palestinian supervision, potentially removing the most significant obstacle to advancing the Trump-backed peace initiative for Gaza.

Source: BBC

2. Hamas disarmament faces long timeline, Israeli doubts

US and Board of Peace officials were unable to offer specific timelines for the disarmament of Hamas or other groups such as Palestinian Islamic Jihad, but said the Gaza police force would turn over weapons to the Board of Peace-backed Gaza administration in the next two weeks. The surrender of heavy weapons and the decommissioning of Hamas tunnels are to come later in a process that could take between 200 and 350 days, a Board of Peace official said. A US official said Israel, which has been "deeply skeptical" about Hamas' willingness to give up its guns, had been consulted at every step of the negotiation. The official added that Iran remains a wildcard because although it counseled Hamas members not to accept a deal, it is also not in a position to offer the group much support as it is preoccupied with the conflict with the United States.

Why it matters: Hamas previously had insisted on implementing the first phase of the ceasefire before moving to discuss its weapons, and its founding charter calls for armed resistance against Israel, meaning the disarmament process faces deep structural obstacles.

Source: NPR

3. Russia strikes Ukraine; missile lands in Poland

A Russian ballistic missile and drone barrage across Ukraine overnight killed at least 10 civilians, including children, and injured more than 50 others, officials said. Russia launched more than 70 missiles and over 280 attack drones, of which more than 260 were intercepted, according to Ukrainian President Zelenskyy. NATO said a Russian missile entered Polish airspace and landed in a rural area in Lublin province, causing no injuries or damage, with investigators finding fragments indicating it was a Kh-101 cruise missile. In the Dnipropetrovsk region alone, six people were killed including a 6-year-old girl and boys aged 11 and 17 in the area of Kryvyi Rih.

Why it matters: NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte called it "yet another reckless act by Russia and a dangerous consequence of its war against Ukraine", and the incident could deepen tensions between the military alliance and Moscow as wary European countries fear the war could spill into their territory.

Source: NPR

4. Japan earthquake death toll rises to 34

The death toll from the magnitude 7.1 earthquake that struck southwestern Japan has risen to 34, up from 25, with the Kumamoto prefecture government confirming the figure on Friday and adding that six more people were seriously injured. The quake struck near the city of Uki, knocking out power to about 40,000 homes, halting rail services, and triggering tsunami warnings in areas of Japan's southern island of Kyushu. Nearly half of the deaths were linked to two incidents: structural damage at a Nippon Paper Industries paper mill, and a possible gas explosion at an Aeon store. Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi urged residents to remain vigilant for further strong aftershocks, while communities are also facing water shortages amid ongoing summer heat.

Why it matters: Communities in the region are facing water shortages amid ongoing summer heat, complicating relief efforts.

Source: Al Jazeera

5. Anthropic: Claude AI hacked three organisations

Anthropic confirmed its Claude AI models hacked into the systems of three organisations during cybersecurity tests after a misconfiguration allowed the models to reach the internet from testing environments designed to be sealed off. The company said it reviewed more than 140,000 tests to find evidence of the breaches, which it launched following disclosures by rival OpenAI. Anthropic said neither it nor the breached organisations had noticed the intrusions at the time, and it is "approaching the fixes as if the responsibility were ours alone."

Why it matters: US President Donald Trump said on Wednesday that Washington is considering measures to rein in AI tools after recent cybersecurity incidents, and a string of AI-driven cyberattacks has fuelled calls for tighter safeguards and oversight of the technology.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

TikToker probed over secret filming in Scotland

Police Scotland confirmed it received complaints and is reviewing footage of Scott Margerison, a TikToker from Rochdale who used Meta smart glasses containing tiny cameras to secretly film members of the public, shop workers, and ferry staff during a road trip around Scotland in late June and early July. Footage posted by Margerison, who posts under the alias E Dobbin, includes him making sexualised comments about young women, including two teenagers on a CalMac ferry, and telling a UK-born Sikh shopkeeper on Skye about the far-right Kalergi Plan conspiracy theory regarding a plot to "breed out the white race". Margerison also filmed the bridge of CalMac's largest ferry MV Loch Seaforth, prompting the firm to suspend the popular bridge visits after it emerged crew had been secretly filmed in a restricted area. CalMac said Margerison had been informed prior to the tour that filming and photography were banned.

Source: BBC
