1. Tanker attacked in Strait of Hormuz, two missing

Two sailors are missing and 23 others have been rescued following an attack on an oil tanker in the Strait of Hormuz, according to Oman. Oman's Maritime Security Centre said the Panama-flagged El Gaia was being towed to an Omani port after a fire broke out in its engine room, with the vessel targeted 1.3 nautical miles (2.4km) north of the Musandam peninsula. Iran's Revolutionary Guards claimed the tanker hit naval mines, but the US military insisted the Iranian report was false, saying the tanker was struck by an Iranian missile last month and again by a drone over the weekend. The International Maritime Organization says at least 22 seafarers have been killed in 79 confirmed incidents in the Strait of Hormuz and the rest of the Middle East since the start of the US and Israel's war with Iran at the end of February.

Why it matters: The Strait of Hormuz, through which a fifth of global oil and gas shipments usually pass, has effectively been closed due to Iranian missile and drone attacks on commercial vessels and a US naval blockade of Iranian ports.

Source: BBC

2. Houthis accused of targeting Mecca with drone

Saudi Arabia said Wednesday that its air defenses intercepted and destroyed a Houthi drone before it could enter the airspace over Mecca, marking the first time fighting has reached the holy city in the ongoing conflict.

Why it matters: The incident marks the latest escalation in a conflict testing a month-old defense pact among Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan, and adds a new flashpoint to a Middle East already destabilised by the US-Iran conflict.

Source: CNBC

3. UK inflation jumps to 3.1% on fuel surge

UK inflation rose from 2.9% in July to 3.1% in August, driven by motor fuel prices rising by almost a quarter, according to figures from the Office for National Statistics. The average petrol price rose by 9.1p between July and August to 161.3p a litre, the highest level since November 2022, while diesel prices rose by 14.2p to 181.8p a litre. Air fares also rose sharply, with an increase of 6.2% between July and August, particularly for long-haul routes. The oil price has soared to more than $108 a barrel as fighting across the Middle East intensifies, and the Bank of England faces a crunch decision on interest rates on Thursday.

Why it matters: The Bank of England previously warned that a worst-case scenario in the Middle East could drive UK inflation to a peak of 4.5% by the middle of 2027, and the chancellor faces a tough budget next month as rising inflation and higher borrowing costs complicate funding priorities.

Source: The Guardian

4. DOJ charges five in Russian murder-for-hire plots

The US Department of Justice has unsealed charges against five people over alleged Russian intelligence-linked plots involving surveillance, recruitment and planned killings internationally, including within the US. The defendants were identified as Russian national Yuri Khrameev, 63, his son Kirill Khrameev, 27, Cubans Oemis Romagoza Durruthy and Yaidel Delgado Suarez, both 35, and 22-year-old Venezuelan Angel Eduardo Castro, all of whom remain at large. All five were charged with conspiring to finance terrorism, while Khrameev, Suarez and Castro face additional charges of conspiring to commit murder for hire. In one instance, a recruit was reportedly promised $40,000 to make a US-based target "disappear", and a separate recruit was allegedly offered $25,000 the previous year to kill someone in Lithuania.

Why it matters: Attorney General Todd Blanche said the plots included attempts to kill a Russian dissident believed to be living in the Washington, DC area, with the network recruiting several people in the US to carry out surveillance on Russian dissidents both domestically and in Lithuania.

Source: Al Jazeera

5. DRC Ebola outbreak said to have peaked

Congolese health minister Samuel Roger Kamba announced that the Bundibugyo variant Ebola outbreak, declared on 15 May, has reached its peak, with the number of daily new cases declining from about 120 per day at the worst to around 80 per day. The latest data from the Congolese health ministry showed more than 7,200 confirmed cases, including more than 3,475 deaths, as of 14 September. Kamba said six health zones could be definitively removed from the active list after going more than 42 days without new cases, and four more after going more than 21 days without a new case. However, an MP for Bunia warned against "triumphalism" and called for objective indicators to be made public to strengthen public confidence.

Why it matters: No effective treatment for the Bundibugyo variant has been found so far, though the DRC received more than 70,000 doses of the Ervebo vaccine last month, which is the only approved treatment for the more common Zaire Ebola virus.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

MSPs urged to pause Scotland data centre rush

A debate at Holyrood, led by the Scottish Greens, will call for planning applications for hyperscale data centres to be put on hold until new national guidelines are in force, with an emphasis on energy demand and the environment. BBC Scotland News research identified, as of 24 June, at least 23 projects in the planning process, though most experts believe many are speculative and will never be built. Dr Callum MacIver warned MSPs this week that the additional demand could make climate change targets difficult to achieve because of the additional reliance on gas. Last year, Irish data centres consumed 23% of the country's electricity, and concerns exist that a similar outcome could push up bills for Scottish consumers.

Source: BBC
