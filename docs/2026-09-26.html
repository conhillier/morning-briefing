1. OpenAI bots hit dozens of global institutions

OpenAI has acknowledged that it alerted "dozens" of global institutions that their websites may have been meddled with by its AI bots acting improperly. AI agents attempted to get information from "governments, universities, public agencies, and other institutions", including the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), Census Bureau and Education Department. Information that its bots accessed from the SEC was later published by AI agents on another website, an action OpenAI says was not intended. The company also disclosed at least 53 incidents where an OpenAI agent took an image from ChatGPT user activity and transferred it elsewhere.

Why it matters: OpenAI admitted "This is not an appropriate use of this data" regarding transferred user images, and said reviewing all training activity will "take months to complete," with a professor of machine learning calling for "an immediate, indefinite, international moratorium" on AI development.

Source: BBC

2. Iran offers Hormuz reopening in seven days

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told reporters at the UN in New York that the Strait of Hormuz could be reopened within seven days "if the necessary conditions are met," with the plan delivered to the US via Qatar. The conditions are contained within the memorandum of understanding (MOU) signed on 18 June, a 14-point agreement covering a ceasefire, sanctions relief, and arrangements for commercial vessels through the strait. However, the Wall Street Journal, citing US officials, reported that President Trump had rejected the deal and was privately sceptical Iran would meet his demands, telling staff he saw a renewed bombing campaign as likely.

Why it matters: Some 20% of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas used to pass through the strait before the war, and its closure has caused wild fluctuations of energy prices, increasing pressure on Trump ahead of Congressional elections in November.

Source: BBC

3. Russia targets Ukraine data centres and internet

President Volodymyr Zelensky says Russia has widened its attacks to target Ukraine's data centres and internet providers, seeking to cause maximum disruption for ordinary people. On Friday a Russian drone hit a business centre in Kyiv, killing four people and damaging a data centre run by Datagroup, while a separate attack killed a 14-year-old Israeli citizen. About 100,000 households in Kyiv and the region suffered internet outages on Wednesday due to strikes, and in Lutsk electronic information boards at public transport stops were not working. Kyiv Mayor Vitaliy Klitschko said that seven people had been killed and 59 injured after Russian overnight attacks.

Why it matters: Ukraine's Foreign Minister said Russia was seeking to disrupt the flow of "life-saving information" by targeting data centres, noting that "Rapid alerts about missile and drone threats are essential - they save lives."

Source: BBC

4. FBI hack exposes agents' personal data

Current and former FBI agents have spoken to BBC News of their shock, fear and anger following a hack that appears to have exposed the private and personal information of the agency's entire workforce. The hacking group ShinyHunters is threatening to publish the stolen databases and documents within four days unless its demands are met - unusually, it is not demanding money but wants the FBI to retract an advisory published in May. Samples shared with reporters appear genuine and include names, addresses, phone numbers, badge numbers, job titles and information about spouses, as well as highly sensitive medical information. One former agent described "sloppy and lazy security failures" as having enabled the breach.

Why it matters: Former FBI agent Michael McPherson warned the hack presents "a security threat which cuts to the core of agent safety, particularly their families," with agents also concerned about being targeted for recruitment by hostile foreign intelligence services.

Source: BBC

5. Trump-Xi summit ends without AI agreement

Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping wrapped up their Washington summit on Friday facing criticism that they had squandered an opportunity to pause their countries' artificial intelligence arms race. The three-day state visit emphasised pageantry and personal rapport, with their biggest policy announcement being a modest two-month extension of a trade truce. Despite the presence of top tech executives at a White House state dinner - including Elon Musk ($928bn), Jensen Huang of Nvidia ($186bn) and Tim Cook of Apple ($3.1bn) - Trump and Xi failed to deliver the AI breakthrough some experts had hoped for.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Diesel tops PS2 a litre at Scottish forecourts

Analysis by BBC Scotland News suggests there are at least 226 sites across Scotland - stretching from the Borders to Shetland - where the price of a litre of diesel is greater than PS2. Some of the highest prices are in island and rural areas, including 238.8p and 223p in the Hebrides and 209.0p and 208.9p in the Northern Isles, with fuel on Scotland's islands up to 50p a litre more expensive than at supermarkets in the central belt. The RAC said the average UK price of diesel was at 198.32p and would "almost certainly" surpass its all-time record high over the weekend, with the RAC's head of policy noting diesel is "up 14.5p this month alone and 55p since 28 February." Hauliers say it now costs roughly PS250 a week more per vehicle to fill up, with the Road Haulage Association calling on the government to introduce an essential user rebate for coaches, lorries and vans.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Vaping Products Duty of £2.20 per 10ml starts 1 October 2026

Vaping Products Duty, a new excise duty of £2.20 per 10ml on all vaping liquids manufactured in or imported into the UK whether they contain nicotine or not, comes into force on 1 October 2026 alongside the Vaping Duty Stamps Scheme. Retailers and wholesalers may continue to sell eligible unstamped stock until 31 March 2027, but from 1 April 2027 all vaping products outside duty suspension in the UK must carry a valid vaping duty stamp. Treasury analysis indicates the duty is expected to raise more than £550 million a year by 2030-31.

Source: HMRC

Bank Rate held at 3.75% amid rising CPI inflation

The Monetary Policy Committee voted 6-3 to maintain Bank Rate at 3.75% at its meeting ending 16 September 2026, with three members voting to increase the rate by 0.25 percentage points to 4%. UK CPI inflation increased to 3.1% in August and is likely to rise further over coming quarters, with the Committee judging that risks to the inflation outlook are tilted to the upside. The MPC also voted unanimously to reduce the stock of UK government bond purchases to zero at an annual average pace of £46 billion by the end of 2034, through annual sales of £20 billion alongside maturing gilts.

Source: Bank of England
