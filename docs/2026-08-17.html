1. Ukraine launches largest drone attack of 2026

Five people died in Russia's south-western region of Rostov following overnight strikes, while an 83-year-old man died in a drone attack in the Moscow region. A Wildberries warehouse in Koledino, 25km from the centre of Moscow, was hit, injuring three, and Ukraine's defence ministry said seven out of ten of the largest Wildberries logistics centres had been "disabled".

Why it matters: Russia has intensified missile attacks across Ukraine in recent weeks, with Kyiv also stepping up drone strikes deep inside Russia, marking a significant escalation in the aerial dimension of the war.

Source: BBC

2. DRC Ebola now deadliest in country's history

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has become the deadliest in the country's history, with at least 2,325 people killed according to government data, surpassing the toll from the 2018-20 outbreak. Confirmed cases have risen to 4,945, including 101 new cases detected in the previous 24 hours, making this the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak in the known history of the virus. The UN warned on Friday that "Ebola is winning in the Democratic Republic of the Congo" and said the epidemic was now killing one person every 30 minutes. There are no approved vaccines or treatments for the rare Bundibugyo strain responsible for the current surge.

Why it matters: A public health specialist with Medecins Sans Frontieres warned that many cases are being detected very late, when treatment is less likely to succeed, with many identified only after patients die in the community.

Source: The Guardian

3. Kushner meets Hamas to push Gaza peace plan

US President Donald Trump's Middle East envoy Jared Kushner held rare talks with Hamas on Sunday, meeting a delegation led by Hamas's new leader Khalil al-Hayya in Egypt. Board of Peace envoys Nickolay Mladenov and UK ex-Prime Minister Tony Blair were also part of the meeting, alongside representatives of Egypt, Qatar and Turkey. Eight Muslim countries - Egypt, UAE, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Jordan, Turkey, Pakistan and Indonesia - issued a joint statement saying Israel's rejection of the US plan threatened Trump's efforts for peace.

Why it matters: According to Israeli media reports, Kushner, Mladenov and Blair will meet Netanyahu on Monday with the aim of advancing Trump's plan, but Netanyahu has said he wants full Hamas disarmament before implementing any deal.

Source: BBC

4. Indonesia quake death toll rises to 53

Preliminary reports indicate more than 900 homes and hundreds of other buildings have been severely damaged by the tremor and 341 subsequent aftershocks, the National Disaster Management Agency said. The initial earthquake, which occurred just 15km below the surface, was the south-east Asian nation's deadliest since 2022. Ruteng Regional Hospital in Manggarai was among the damaged facilities, with images showing operating theatre and clinic ceilings caved in, requiring patients to be treated in tents.

Why it matters: The full scale of the disaster is still being uncovered, with access to some villages blocked by landslides and the affected area described as remote and mountainous, making assessment difficult.

Source: BBC

5. Stripe to acquire OpenRouter for over $7bn

Stripe has finalised a deal to acquire AI gateway startup OpenRouter at a price of more than $7 billion, according to a Bloomberg report. OpenRouter helps customers select different AI models to perform different tasks depending on their specific needs and budget, and previously raised a $113 million Series B at a reported $1.3 billion valuation in May. The startup claimed to have 8 million global users and to provide access to more than 400 models, with investors including Sequoia, Andreessen Horowitz, Menlo Ventures and Alphabet's Capital G. A Stripe spokesperson told TechCrunch that the company does not comment on rumours or speculation.

Source: TechCrunch

Closer to Home

Pocognoli confirmed as Scotland football boss

The Scottish FA has appointed Sebastien Pocognoli, a 39-year-old Belgian, as Scotland men's new head coach on a two-year deal, replacing Steve Clarke. Pocognoli left Monaco at the end of last season and previously led Union Saint-Gilloise to a first league title in 90 years in 2024-25, having not won the title since 1935. He becomes just the second foreign boss of the Scotland men's team, after German Berti Vogts, and earned 13 caps for Belgium during his playing career, also spending time at West Bromwich Albion and Brighton and Hove Albion in England. He is said to favour an attacking 3-4-1-2 system, which he has described as "a dominant one, with and without the ball. High pressing, counter-pressing, but also real mastery in possession".

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Business rates cut 20% for pubs, clubs and live music venues

From April 2027 (2027/28 financial year), pubs, social clubs and live music venues across England will receive a 20% cut to their business rates bills, saving the typical pub an estimated £1,100 in the next financial year. The package is worth around £100 million a year and will benefit nearly 32,000 pubs, clubs and live music venues. The 20% cut comes on top of a 15% relief already announced for 2026/27 bills, with bills frozen in real terms for a further two years.

Source: HM Treasury

Bank Rate held at 3.75% in July 2026 MPC vote

At its meeting ending on 29 July 2026, the Monetary Policy Committee voted 6-3 to maintain Bank Rate at 3.75%, with three members voting to increase it by 0.25 percentage points to 4%. CPI inflation has fallen to 2.6% since the previous meeting, although it is expected to rise later in the year as higher energy prices continue to pass through. The next MPC decision is due on 17 September 2026.

Source: Bank of England
