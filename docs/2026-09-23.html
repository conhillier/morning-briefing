1. US and Iran hold UNGA ceasefire talks

A senior Iranian official said Iran could reopen the Strait of Hormuz within a week if the US eased military pressure and lifted its blockade of Iranian ports.

Why it matters: The Strait of Hormuz carried about a fifth of global oil and gas shipments before the war, and confirmed transits averaged just 6.98 million barrels a day in the seven days to 20 September - about 38% of the 18.3 million barrel pre-war baseline.

Source: CNBC

2. US to build two military bases in Greenland

The US, Denmark and Greenland have signed a defence deal allowing Washington to build two new military bases in the Arctic territory at Narsarsuaq and Mestersvig, while reaffirming Copenhagen's sovereignty. The new pact amends and updates a 1951 defence agreement, and also allows the US to "modernise and expand its activities" at its existing Pituffik Space Base. No territory is ceded to the US, and the deal recognises both Denmark's "sovereignty and territorial integrity" and Greenland's right to self-determination. The agreement still requires approval by both Denmark's and Greenland's parliaments.

Why it matters: The deal also stipulates that no non-NATO state shall be allowed to establish military installations in Greenland, and confirms Greenland would remain in NATO if it were to gain independence from Denmark.

Source: BBC

3. Trump rallies Americas coalition against cartels

President Trump pledged to use "unmatched military might" against drug cartels during a meeting of the Shield of the Americas coalition on the sidelines of UNGA in New York. Brazil and Mexico have not joined the coalition.

Why it matters: Since returning to office, Trump has ordered dozens of strikes on boats in the Caribbean and eastern Pacific said to be carrying narcotics, and UN experts say those strikes have killed more than 223 people and could amount to crimes against humanity.

Source: Al Jazeera

4. China builds AI data centres at 'China speed'

In the grasslands of Ulanqab, Inner Mongolia, China is rapidly constructing data centres for companies including Huawei, ByteDance and DeepSeek, as President Xi Jinping heads to Washington for a summit with Trump that includes AI safety on the agenda. China's Communist Party aims to embed AI across 90% of industries and society by 2030, and has built up vast renewable energy capacity in sparsely-populated northern and south-western provinces to power the centres. Researchers say fewer students are heading to the US and more are returning from America to China, partly because Trump's restrictions on immigration have "soured the American dream." Xi is also touting an alternative open-source AI model approach that has helped Chinese firms innovate quickly despite restricted access to advanced chips.

Source: BBC

5. Eleven killed in South Africa house shooting

At least 11 people have been killed in a mass shooting at a residential building in the KwaMakhutha township near Durban, South Africa. Gunmen stormed a house late on Tuesday and opened fire on 14 people; ten were killed at the scene and four were taken to hospital, where one later died. Police said "information available at this stage suggests that 13 men and one woman, who is believed to be pregnant, were reportedly having a party inside a house when three men arrived and opened fire on them" - the pregnant woman is among those who died. Police said they could not rule out a "feud between rival groupings" as a motive.

Why it matters: South Africa has one of the world's highest murder rates, with police data showing a murder rate equivalent to more than 60 deaths a day.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Scotland councils resist local government shake-up

Council leaders across Scotland have voiced concerns about Scottish government proposals for the biggest shake-up of local government in 30 years, which would potentially reduce the number of authorities from 32 to six regional bodies, alongside between 120 and 160 community authorities. Fife Council leader David Ross said it would be "much simpler and more effective" to maintain the status quo and allow local authorities to "merge if they wished," arguing there would be no financial savings from restructuring.

Source: BBC
