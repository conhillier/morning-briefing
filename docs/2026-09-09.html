1. US strikes Iranian tankers, oil nears $100

US forces struck five Iranian oil tankers on Tuesday, sinking one, after Iran twice targeted a US Navy warship with ballistic missiles. The tankers destroyed in the Gulf of Oman were the Kivik, Charminar, Horizon 1 and the Riesco, while the vessel Derya was destroyed near Kharg Island. Iran responded by launching missiles at a US base in Jordan, with Jordan's military intercepting 18 of the ballistic missiles and two others falling in unpopulated areas.

Why it matters: Iran's Revolutionary Guards warned crews of oil tankers near Kuwait and Bahrain to immediately leave their vessels, saying the ships could be targeted in retaliation, raising the risk of further disruption to global energy supplies.

Source: NPR

2. Iran fires on Jordan; Houthis hit Saudi Arabia

After the US strikes on its tankers, Iran launched missiles at a US base in Jordan, and Tehran said it also attacked two US vessels and eight oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. Jordan said it had shot down 18 of the 20 Iranian missiles, while two fell in unpopulated areas. Also on Tuesday, Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi movement attacked energy facilities and civilian infrastructure in Saudi Arabia, injuring 73 people, according to Saudi authorities. The drone and missile attack caused fires at oil facilities that led to a temporary halt in operations, Saudi Arabia's military and energy ministry said.

Why it matters: Secretary of State Marco Rubio warned that every time Iran targets US naval ships, Iran will lose tankers, signalling the tit-for-tat cycle is set to continue.

Source: BBC

3. Trump bans Canadian alcohol, motorbikes and dairy

President Trump signed a series of executive orders on Tuesday banning a range of Canadian products, with the import bans set to begin on 29 September. Banned goods include alcoholic spirits, dairy products such as whey, non-alcoholic beer, and motorbikes including mopeds, while items such as cheeses and motorboats face higher import taxes. The measures come as Canada's retaliatory counter-duties on US goods - including steel, clothing and furniture - came into effect after midnight on Tuesday. Trade expert Deborah Elms said the ban could have a "strong" impact on Canadian businesses with US buyers, though early estimates show a "modest impact" affecting around $1bn worth of goods.

Why it matters: The White House said Canada is "discriminating" against US businesses, and economics lecturer Scott French warned the trade war will be "costly for both sides due to the historical level of integration of the North American economy," with consumers on both sides ending up as "the biggest losers".

Source: BBC

4. OpenAI claims 90-year maths breakthrough in 88 hours

OpenAI says it solved a major aspect of the Navier-Stokes equations - a 90-year-old fluid dynamics problem - in just 88 hours, using roughly 10,000 AI agents running on a new internal model. The bots exchanged nearly 3 million messages and used up 130 billion output tokens, an effort that would have cost roughly $10m based on OpenAI's own pricing. OpenAI said the solution resolved two out of four statements in the proof demanded by the Millennium Prize, which is worth $1m to a winner, but stated it does not intend to claim the prize.

Source: BBC

5. Somali piracy surges amid regional naval diversion

Somali piracy has returned to levels not seen in a decade, with eight hijackings since late April, including two within a fortnight in August: one targeting an oil tanker with ties to Iran and another a Cameroon-flagged vessel, the Lutuf, carrying military equipment for Turkey. The Lutuf's release two weeks after its capture involved a joint Turkish-Somali operation that destroyed four pirate vessels, though local officials said a $2.5m ransom was also paid. About 70% of maritime traffic that previously passed through the Red Sea is now being rerouted around the Cape of Good Hope.

Why it matters: Analysts note that rising oil prices driven by the war have made oil cargoes a more valuable target, and local officials warn that ransom payments risk encouraging similar incidents.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Three arrested after fatal A8 crash near Edinburgh

Three people have been arrested after a multi-vehicle crash on the A8 near Edinburgh Airport on Monday in which a 58-year-old woman driving a Hyundai i30 was pronounced dead at the scene. Police said two women aged 23 and 19, and a 32-year-old man were arrested in connection with the crash, which happened at around 09:25 near Newbridge. Four other people were taken to hospital but later released, and four vehicles in total were involved including a grey Skoda Karoq and a Renault Captur which was seen flipped onto its roof. The road was closed for a number of hours but reopened at 19:05.

Source: BBC
