1. ICJ finds Myanmar guilty of Rohingya genocide

The International Court of Justice has found Myanmar guilty of committing genocide against its Rohingya minority, marking the first time a state has ever been found to have committed genocide by an international court. ICJ President Yuji Iwasawa said Myanmar had the "intention to destroy" a substantial part of the Rohingya population and engaged in "extensive destruction of evidence" to hide its crimes. The court determined that around 10,000 Rohingya were "violently killed" during clearance operations in 2017, while more than 700,000 fled to Bangladesh. The verdict was reached by a majority of nine votes to six among a panel of judges, and the ICJ also found Myanmar had violated several other parts of the 1948 Genocide Convention.

Why it matters: Myanmar now carries the stain of being the first state found to have violated the 1948 Genocide Convention. The court said Myanmar has an obligation to pay proportionate reparations, with an exact figure to be determined at a later date.

Source: BBC

2. Russia strikes three Kyiv bridges, kills six

Russia launched a major missile and drone attack on several Ukrainian cities overnight, targeting three bridges over the Dnipro River in Kyiv and killing six people. Ukraine's air force says almost 100 missiles were launched and the attack also involved around 400 drones, making it one of the largest attacks since the invasion began in 2022. The three Kyiv bridges targeted - the Podilsko-Voskresensky Bridge, the Darnytskyi Bridge and the Southern Bridge - have all been closed to traffic. Two people were also killed in Russian strikes on the Sknyliv railway station in the western city of Lviv.

Source: BBC

3. Iran escalates Strait of Hormuz ship attacks

Iran's attacks on ships in the Strait of Hormuz have hit a record high, with two vessels struck in a single 24-hour period this week - an oil tanker and a vessel carrying liquefied gas that "exploded into a ball of fire," forcing Indian crewmembers to jump into the water without time to board lifeboats. Oman said its forces rescued 24 men from that vessel, while 11 crewmembers on a tanker belonging to Abu Dhabi's oil company were hospitalised and one remains missing. October saw more attacks on ships in the Strait of Hormuz than any month since the start of the war, and November is on pace to exceed that figure. Iran has also launched drones at US radars in Bahrain and expanded attacks to target air defence systems and missile launch sites belonging to the US in Kuwait.

Why it matters: The escalation is not only keeping oil prices high but is also killing seafarers, and some tankers have sunk or spilled oil - with stretches of the UAE's coastline still closed to the public following an oil spill from a tanker struck on 9 October.

Source: NPR

4. Anthropic closes debut day at $1.3tn valuation

Anthropic closed its first day of trading on the US stock market about 17% higher than its IPO price of $83, with shares ending at just over $97 and a market capitalisation of nearly $1.3tn, or $1.75tn fully diluted. The shares had initially soared 34% to reach $111.50, briefly valuing the company at more than $2tn, before retreating by the close. The AI startup, founded in 2021 and best known for its Claude chatbot, raised $81.3bn by selling 980 million shares to underwriters - surpassing the previous IPO record set by SpaceX's initial $75bn fundraise in June. The company reported revenues of $16.2bn in the first six months of 2026, up from $4.6bn for all of 2025, though it also reported losses of $104bn in the first nine months of 2026.

Why it matters: Anthropic is the first firm focused entirely on AI to be publicly traded, making its debut a bellwether for the multitrillion-dollar AI boom.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

UK-EU youth mobility deal nears 100,000-per-year cap

Up to 100,000 young people from the EU would be allowed to travel, work and study in the UK per year under a new youth mobility scheme expected to be agreed at the UK-EU summit in Brussels on Friday, with the same number of young Britons allowed into Europe. Downing Street said talks were "down to the wire" on Thursday, with sticking points including the UK's request to limit numbers and the EU's request that EU students pay the same tuition fees at UK universities as domestic students. Prime Minister Andy Burnham said the deal could mean "lower food prices" and "cutting red tape," and that the government would introduce legislation to Parliament next week if a deal were secured.

Source: BBC
