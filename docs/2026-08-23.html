1. Canada-US trade talks collapse, tariffs imposed

The US imposed 50% tariffs on roughly $20 billion worth of Canadian goods after trade talks fell apart late on Friday, covering products including wine, dairy, cement, clothing, and hockey equipment. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said the US "asked too much and offered too little," accusing Washington of adding last-minute terms that were "unacceptable," including curbing Canada's ability to strike new trade deals with other countries. Carney announced Canada would retaliate "dollar for dollar" with its own tariffs targeting US steel, dairy, agricultural equipment, and electronics, taking effect on 8 September.

Why it matters: Trade experts said the tariffs could result in some job losses and store closings, with the largest impact expected to be political, driving a further wedge between the traditional allies.

Source: BBC

2. US plans 'unprecedented' economic war on Iran

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC that the Trump administration plans to create "the greatest coordinated economic isolation in the history of the world" against Iran, with an announcement on details due Monday. Bessent said the measures would likely negate the need for major US military operations against Iran, and that the US would tell all allies "You are either with us or against us." Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei condemned the planned sanctions as "an assertion of extraterritorial sovereignty over every independent Member State of the United Nations." Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz remains a fraction of pre-war levels, with only 10 crossings on Monday and two transits on Sunday, according to trade intelligence firm Kpler.

Why it matters: Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei warned that any state joining US sanctions against Iran will be considered an enemy, and Tehran will target their interests in retaliation.

Source: CNBC

3. Ukraine mall rescue continues after 16 killed

Rescuers in Ukraine are continuing to search for survivors in the wreckage of a shopping centre in Kryvyi Rih hit by a Russian double-tap drone strike on Friday, with 16 people killed, 130 injured, and four people still missing. The search and rescue operation, drawing teams from seven other Ukrainian regions, could last another two days due to the scale of devastation and constant threats of further Russian attacks. President Volodymyr Zelensky condemned the "cynical and despicable" attack, in which the second drone targeted emergency workers. A day of mourning is being observed across the Dnipropetrovsk region, with 52 injured people, including 14 children, still in city hospitals.

Why it matters: The attack came a day after 17 people were killed and more than 40 injured in a major Russian missile and drone attack on Ukraine's capital Kyiv.

Source: BBC

4. TikTok pays $400m in child privacy deal

TikTok has agreed to pay $400m (£293m) to the US to settle a lawsuit alleging its platform violated children's privacy under the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act, marking one of the largest ever settlements over the issue. The deal stems from a 2024 suit by the Department of Justice under former President Joe Biden, alleging TikTok and parent company ByteDance collected "vast amounts of data" on millions of users under the age of 13. Under the terms of the deal, TikTok and ByteDance will immediately pay the DOJ $300m, with another $100m due when the government vacates a 2019 consent decree with the Federal Trade Commission. The justice department noted that since it sued the platform, TikTok has "undergone significant changes," including to its ownership, privacy practices and platform controls for young users.

Why it matters: Meta is now facing penalties that could exceed hundreds of billions of dollars stemming from COPPA violations alleged by attorneys general of 29 US states, with a jury trial having started this week.

Source: BBC

5. DeepMind alumni AI startup beats Anthropic, OpenAI

London-based AI startup Inherent, founded by Google DeepMind alumni, says its AI agent Faraday has outperformed larger models from Anthropic and OpenAI at independently reproducing the findings of published scientific papers without being told the answer in advance. Faraday runs on a model called Qwen 3.6 with just 27 billion parameters, compared against Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 and OpenAI's GPT-5.5, both described as much larger, frontier-scale systems. The startup emerged from stealth just weeks ago with a $50 million seed round and operates with a dozen employees out of an office in King's Cross, London. Inherent plans to grow its headcount to "about 20 to 25" by the end of the year.

Source: TechCrunch

Closer to Home

Seven killed in A66 crash; two officers named

Police constables Matthew Blades and Tom Clough were among seven people killed after their marked police car collided with a Volkswagen Passat travelling the wrong way along the A66 in North Yorkshire at 3.39am on Saturday. The police watchdog, the Independent Office for Police Conduct (IOPC), said it understood the officers had been pursuing a suspicious vehicle when the accident occurred, and has launched a mandatory investigation.

Source: The Guardian

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Business rates cut 20% for pubs, clubs, live music venues

From April 2027, pubs, social clubs and live music venues across England will receive a 20% cut to their business rates bills, saving the typical pub an estimated £1,100 in the next financial year. The package is worth around £100 million a year and will benefit nearly 32,000 premises. The additional 20% cut comes on top of a 15% relief already announced in January for pubs and live music venues off their 2026/27 bill.

Source: HM Treasury

Bank Rate held at 3.75% with three-way split vote

At its meeting ending 29 July 2026, the MPC voted 6-3 to maintain Bank Rate at 3.75%; three members voted to increase Bank Rate by 0.25 percentage points to 4%. CPI inflation has fallen to 2.6% but is expected to rise later this year as the effects of higher energy prices continue to pass through. The next MPC decision is due 17 September 2026.

Source: Bank of England
