1. Missiles hit Kyiv amid peace talk hopes

Kyiv came under a new missile attack on Wednesday night, with several houses hit and six people injured, according to Mayor Vitaliy Klitschko. The attack followed Russia saying it was "ready in principle" to resume peace talks, and Trump saying the world was "the closest we've been" to ending the war. US envoy Steve Witkoff met Putin in St Petersburg for talks lasting about three hours, while Jared Kushner participated via video link. Putin's aide Yuri Ushakov suggested a new round of talks could be held around 10 November in Abu Dhabi.

Why it matters: Russia's continued missile strikes on Kyiv, even as it signals openness to talks, underscores the fragility of the diplomatic push and the gap between rhetoric and action on the ground.

3. Meta hit with record $25bn privacy fine

A judge in New Mexico ordered Meta to pay $25bn (£18.6bn) following a two-week trial that found the company had spent years lying to the public about how it handled Facebook user data. Judge Francis Mathew said Meta's violations were "willful" and spanned "more than 11 years", stretching from 2010 to 2021, with communications from senior executives including Mark Zuckerberg "designed to mislead the public". A jury found Meta had violated state laws more than 40 million times, affecting roughly two million people - the entire population of New Mexico. Meta intends to appeal, with its head lawyer CJ Mahoney saying the ruling made it "all but impossible to do business in the state of New Mexico".

Why it matters: The fine is the largest ever against Meta, topping an $18bn settlement with California over child safety earlier this year, and comes as the company is already reporting negative cash flow of more than $2bn due to heavy AI spending.

Source: BBC

4. US pulls Saudi diplomats, Iran strike plans drawn up

The US ordered non-emergency government employees and their families to leave Saudi Arabia as Houthi attacks on the kingdom escalate. The US and Israeli militaries have drawn up specific plans for renewed strikes on Iran after next month's midterm elections on 3 November, according to The New York Times, citing unnamed US officials, with planning for a "short but intensive" operation progressing. A strike on King Fahd International Airport in Dammam killed at least one person and injured eight, while two people died in a drone attack on Saudi Aramco's Shaybah oil field. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth reportedly met Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu during an unannounced visit to Israel to discuss potential new strikes.

Why it matters: Brent crude futures topped $110 a barrel on the escalation news, while the developments point to a possible widening of the nearly eight-month war even as US and Iranian negotiators continue talks on Tehran's nuclear program and the Strait of Hormuz.

Source: CNBC

5. Russia frees American prisoner Hubbard

Russian President Vladimir Putin freed 74-year-old Michigan native Stephen Hubbard from a Russian prison at the request of President Trump, with the release taking place during Witkoff's visit to St Petersburg. Hubbard, a former school teacher, had been detained by Russian forces in Eastern Ukraine in 2022 and sentenced in a closed trial to nearly seven years in prison for allegedly fighting on behalf of Ukraine. Trump said in a social media post that the US had not given "anything in return" for Hubbard's release. Putin confirmed the first shipments of Russian diesel - 60,000 tons - would be heading to the US in the next few days, and a Kremlin aide said Putin had agreed to resuscitate trilateral peace talks with the US and Ukraine.

Why it matters: Hubbard's release came against a backdrop of Trump making a series of gestures towards Russia, including agreeing to buy previously sanctioned Russian diesel fuel and placing a waiver on a congressional sanctions package he himself had signed into law.

Source: NPR

Closer to Home

NHS Lothian upholds complaint over dying girl's bedside care row

Six-year-old Flora Gentleman, who had lived with neuroblastoma for four years, was receiving palliative care at Edinburgh's Royal Hospital for Children and Young People in December 2024 when her mother Stephanie Kent witnessed medical teams arguing at her bedside while Flora was in visible pain. Stephanie said the conversation between the oncology and palliative care teams became so "insensitive" that the group of medics had to leave the room, with Flora unable to communicate her distress as she was autistic and non-verbal. NHS Lothian upheld the complaint on the grounds that the family were left out of decision making and experienced distress due to "visible" divisions between clinical teams. The health board identified eight "major learning outcomes" following a review of Flora's care, including the need to better coordinate working between teams and greater consideration of how Flora's neurodiversity affected her care.

Source: BBC
