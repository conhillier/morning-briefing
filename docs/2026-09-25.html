1. Trump and Xi toast friendship at state dinner

US President Donald Trump welcomed Chinese President Xi Jinping to the White House for a state dinner, with both leaders touting a new chapter in their relationship.

Why it matters: Experts have cautioned that Xi's visit is yet to yield obvious progress on pressing issues, despite the warm words from both leaders.

2. Trade truce extended only months, not a year

Despite Trump rolling out the red carpet for Xi, the Chinese leader did not get everything he wanted from the Washington summit. The trade truce that paused a tit-for-tat tariff war was extended by only a few months - from November to January - when Beijing had hoped for at least a year. This means key disputes on tariffs, China's dominance on rare earth supplies and access to advanced US chips are being kicked forward into yet another round of negotiations. On Taiwan, Xi reiterated his hope that the US would adhere to the "correct position" of opposing Taiwan independence, while Trump has yet to make any public response.

Why it matters: Xi's domestic economy is in trouble, with youth unemployment stubbornly high and property prices slumped, making access to overseas export markets more important than ever and leaving him exposed if the tariff truce collapses.

Source: BBC

3. Iran offers Hormuz open in six days

Iran told the US it is willing to open the Strait of Hormuz within six days, provided Washington lifts sanctions on Iranian oil exports, ends the war on all fronts including in Lebanon, and releases some of Iran's frozen assets. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said he was waiting in New York over the weekend to hear an American response, adding that a deal on the temporary route through the strait had already been agreed with Oman and was ready to be registered with the International Maritime Organisation. Araghchi said talks on Iran's nuclear programme would start the day after the strait reopened, accelerating a timeline that under the existing memorandum of understanding would not have begun for 60 days. Privately, Iranian officials believe a deal with the Americans is reachable but fear Israel will use its influence to pull the US president away from the proposal.

Why it matters: Araghchi said he would like the deal to go through before the US midterm elections, since opening the strait would reduce energy prices - something Trump badly needs for the Republican party to avoid a political mauling.

Source: The Guardian

4. Houthis strike Saudi Arabia, oil markets rattled

The Houthis fired dozens of missiles and drones at Saudi targets on Friday morning, with Saudi authorities saying they intercepted six ballistic missiles. Emergency alerts were issued in Mecca, Jeddah and Yanbu, and Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan planned an urgent meeting of their chiefs of staff to discuss ways to support the kingdom under the Mecca Joint Defense Agreement. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian sought to distance Tehran from the escalation, telling Fox News "the Houthis are responsible for their own actions" and that Iran is "not at war with Saudi Arabia." Vessel transits through the Strait of Hormuz fell to 9, far below peacetime levels, according to Reuters citing preliminary ship-tracking data.

Why it matters: China's special envoy for the Middle East said the consensus reached at the Trump-Xi summit "will be critical to cooling the temperature in the conflict," underscoring how the war has become entangled with great-power diplomacy.

Source: CNBC

5. Ethiopia edges toward civil war after rebel offensive

Growing fears of a return to civil war in Ethiopia have emerged after fighters from Tigray launched an offensive into the neighbouring states of Afar and Amhara and seven armed groups formed a new rebel alliance aimed at overthrowing the government of Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed. The Tigray People's Liberation Front seized control of three airports from federal police on Wednesday, and Ethiopian Airlines said it had suspended flights to the region. The TPLF's vice-president Amanuel Assefa told AFP: "We are in the situation of full-blown war now," while Ethiopia's army claimed it had foiled attacks and killed, wounded or captured more than 500 enemy fighters. A resident of Adigrat described panic-buying, saying: "Food shortages are worsening as market shelves sit empty... Essential medicines remain scarce and astronomically expensive."

Why it matters: The renewed fighting marks the most serious challenge yet to the 2022 Pretoria agreement and raises fears of a broader regional conflict that could draw in armed groups from across a region already strained by war in neighbouring Sudan and tensions along the Red Sea.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Glasgow Council to fire and rehire 23,000 staff

Glasgow City Council is to sack more than 23,000 members of non-teaching staff and rehire them on new contracts after pay talks with unions broke down on Wednesday when Unison refused to ballot its members on a proposed pay offer. Letters of dismissal will be sent next week to all non-teaching staff on the council's books - including chief executive Susanne Millar, nursery staff and refuse collectors - with new contracts taking effect from 1 January. The council said the new pay and grading system removes unequal pay and sees pay rise or stay the same for 90% of staff, but 11% will have their wages cut, with the highest drop expected to be around PS11,500. Unison Scotland's Mandy McDowall said staff "are not going to be bounced into a shoddy deal by bosses trying to bully them with arbitrary deadlines," while Unite warned the move "will be met with the full force of Unite."

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Vaping Products Duty of GBP2.20 per 10ml from 1 October 2026

A new excise duty of GBP2.20 per 10ml on all vaping liquids manufactured in, or imported into, the UK, whether they contain nicotine or not, comes into force on 1 October 2026 alongside the Vaping Duty Stamps Scheme. From 1 April 2027, the retail packaging of all vaping products sold or supplied in the UK must carry a valid vaping duty stamp, with a six-month transition period for existing unstamped stock running from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027. Businesses that do not comply with the new rules may face civil or criminal sanctions.

Source: HMRC
