1. Kenya records second Ebola death

Kenya has recorded its second death from Ebola as it handles its first ever outbreak of the disease. The victim, a woman aged 65, was a close family member of the first victim and was under quarantine when she developed symptoms on 13 October. Seven other Ebola patients were receiving treatment at two isolation facilities, with all cases within "one cluster, with no evidence of community spread", the health ministry said. The US has warned its citizens against travelling to Kenya, while neighbouring countries including Uganda, Ethiopia and Somalia are also on alert.

Why it matters: With more than 9,500 cases in DR Congo, Kenya faces a steady risk of new cases arriving across borders, according to the International Rescue Committee, which has been working alongside both governments to contain the spread.

Source: BBC

2. Nigeria Ebola source a mystery, spread feared

Health officials across Africa are racing to determine how Nigeria's first Ebola patient in more than a decade became infected, amid fears the outbreak could spread further after reaching a fourth African country. The Swiss foreign ministry said the patient, the Ugandan-born wife of a Swiss diplomat based in Abuja, had not travelled to the Democratic Republic of Congo and was believed to have contracted Ebola while travelling with her husband in Uganda. The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention is investigating whether the patient may have come into contact with an Ebola patient in Kenya, as both travelled from Uganda's Entebbe airport on 3 October. The current outbreak, caused by the rare Bundibugyo virus with no approved treatment or vaccine, has infected more than 9,500 people and killed nearly half of them.

Why it matters: The new information contradicts accounts from Uganda and will raise concerns about the ability of authorities to contain the outbreak, which is already described as the most geographically widespread in history.

Source: The Guardian

3. PHP cyber flaw: hacking attempts reach millions

Cybersecurity firm Wiz Research, using OpenAI technology, announced it had discovered security flaws in PHP, a programming language that helps run some 70% of the world's websites. Matthew Prince, head of cybersecurity firm Cloudflare, said his organisation saw tens of thousands of attempts to exploit the flaw in the first 45 minutes of its discovery, a figure now in the millions. Corporations and governments from the UK to Japan are rushing to prevent hackers from capitalising on the flaws, with the US National Security Agency warning organisations using PHP to "maintain heightened awareness" over the coming weeks. Wiz Research said the flaws had been present for decades but that demonstrating their exploitability had required "an exceptionally complex chain of steps, at the edge of what human researchers could practically achieve".

Why it matters: OpenAI called the bug "one of the most impactful vulnerabilities in recent memory," with Wiz warning that successful exploitation could allow attackers to take control of affected servers, steal sensitive data, and deface websites running on them.

Source: NPR

4. IT workers 'relentless' amid PHP emergency

Microsoft boss Satya Nadella said he has not seen anything like the PHP emergency "in a long, long time," telling a conference "it was a long day and a long night, I know, for all the IT and security folks". UK-based system administrator Adam Mudd said he had never had so many messages from people worried about their websites, describing his biggest challenge as "juggling clients who want their sites taken offline as a precaution, while trying to coordinate updates and fixes across countless different setups". In Mauritius, IT administrator Loganaden Velvindron said many versions of PHP running are "completely end-of-life", forcing his team to rebuild web apps and change code in some cases. Despite warnings from Cloudflare and AWS that "millions of hacking attempts" had been made every hour, there is no evidence of large-scale data breaches or cyber-attacks.

Source: BBC

5. UK commits £100m to AI biosecurity lab

The UK government has announced it will establish a national AI Biosecurity Lab (AIBL), committing £100m to the "world first" institute as part of Wednesday's Budget. The AI Minister Kanishka Narayan described it as a "major new global institution" that brings together "world-leading scientific expertise" to ensure the UK can safeguard its citizens as AI develops. OpenAI and Anthropic will support the lab's creation by providing £200m worth of AI model credits. The Chancellor also pledged an additional £120m for the UK's AI Security Institute, £60m for a "National Cyber Shield" and £70m towards a National Exposure and Real-Time Vulnerability Centre.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Storm Austen leaves 31,000 homes powerless

Storm Austen brought severe conditions to parts of south-west England, with a gust of 76mph recorded at St Mary's airport on the Isles of Scilly on Friday morning. By 3pm, more than 31,000 homes in the south-west were without power according to National Grid, though engineers had by then restored power to more than 52,000 properties. Dozens of schools across Cornwall were forced to delay opening or did not open at all, flights were disrupted at airports in the south-west and on the Channel Islands, and images showed part of Brighton's West Pier falling into the sea. National Rail reported severe disruption across the rail network with cancellations across England, Wales and Scotland.

Source: The Guardian
