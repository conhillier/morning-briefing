1. Binance frozen funds at $3bn after record hack

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, says it has frozen around $3bn of the $6.3bn (£4.7bn) stolen in what is believed to be a record heist. The firm's co-CEO Richard Teng said the attack was not carried out by one person and that hackers used "a self-destruct mechanism that destroyed tooling and traces as they went, making it more difficult for us to trace and investigate". Binance has set aside more than $6.34bn in company funds to cover the stolen assets and told users "your money is safe, and we're covering every loss".

Why it matters: Several experts have warned customers not to regard Binance's promise to cover losses as legally binding and said they may need to pursue litigation to recover them, raising fears of wider damage to the crypto industry.

Source: BBC

2. US approves $2.1bn Patriot missile sale to Ukraine

The United States has approved the sale of 500 Patriot PAC-3 interceptor missiles to Ukraine at a cost of $2.1bn (£1.6bn), with the purchase to be "funded entirely" by Kyiv's allies in Europe and Canada. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky warned it could take "several years" for the deliveries to arrive, as the order is for new builds rather than existing missiles. The State Department said Ukraine would pay through donations from Canada, Germany, and Norway via the JUMPSTART program and national funds financed by the European Union's Ukraine Support Loan, with Lockheed Martin named as the principal contractor. Zelensky noted that Russia had launched "more than 30 Russian strikes" on "critical parts" of Ukraine's power grid over the past two weeks, intensifying ahead of winter.

Why it matters: Patriots are described as the only effective weapon for downing Russia's ballistic missiles, but with only 600 missiles produced per year according to the US Department of Defence, a rapid delivery is considered unlikely.

Source: BBC

3. G20 family photo cancelled over Russia's presence

The traditional "family photo" at the G20 foreign ministers' meeting in Atlanta was abruptly cancelled after about one-third of countries - including Australia, the UK, and EU nations - refused to appear beside Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio pulled the plug after several European officials, the UK, and Australia made known they would boycott the snapshot. Participants said the disagreements would probably prevent the foreign ministers from producing a joint communique when meetings wrap up on Saturday.

Why it matters: The schism in Atlanta is described as further evidence of the rupture caused by the Trump administration's attempt to reintegrate Russia into the international community despite Putin's ongoing war against Ukraine.

Source: The Guardian

4. Tech stocks tumble as AI spending tops $800bn

Tech stocks fell for a second consecutive day on Friday, with Intel shares plummeting 28% and shedding more than $130bn in value, while Google owner Alphabet fell a further 1.4% after its 12% plunge on Thursday. The Nasdaq index ended October with its worst monthly slide since March 2025, closing 2.8% lower on the day. Meta, Alphabet, Oracle, Amazon, and Microsoft have committed to spending $800bn between them on AI in 2026, but analysts said there were questions about the payout from those huge investments. Reddit stock plunged 29% after the social media platform revealed weaker-than-expected daily user figures and warned that Google's growing AI-driven search was hurting referral traffic.

Source: BBC

5. Google loses $500bn as AI search hurts ad revenue

Google-owner Alphabet lost just over $500bn (£374bn) in value on Thursday after its own AI summaries were found to be hitting search advertising revenue, with shares plummeting over 12% to a six-month low. Chief business officer Philipp Schindler said Google's "AI mode", where it answers questions conversationally, was eating into the money it makes from ads appearing next to traditional search results. Total revenue grew 25% in the three months to the end of September, hitting $127.6bn, but the firm did not make as much money as analysts had predicted. By the end of Thursday, Alphabet's total stock market value had fallen to $3.46 trillion from $3.97 trillion the day before.

Why it matters: Tracy Woo, a principal analyst at Forrester, said Alphabet's disappointing Search results "highlights the challenge of funding massive AI investments while maintaining the strength of established businesses."

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Scottish councils reject Holyrood's local government overhaul

Following a meeting of all 32 council leaders, umbrella body Cosla said a change of structure alone is not the answer, and instead pushed for more powers devolved to a local level and a review of funding arrangements, including council tax. Cosla said plans should start with the existing 32 councils, building in better "regional collaboration and shared capacity", with voluntary consolidation only "subject to democratic agreement". The Scottish government's own medium-term financial strategy projects a £4.8bn shortfall between spending plans and expected income by 2029-30, adding urgency to the dispute.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Vaping Products Duty at GBP2.20 per 10ml from 1 October 2026

Vaping Products Duty comes into force from 1 October 2026, charged at GBP2.20 per 10ml of vaping liquid, payable by manufacturers, importers and warehousekeepers approved by HMRC. All vaping products sold in the UK must carry a valid vaping duty stamp from 1 April 2027, with a six-month grace period allowing wholesalers and retailers to sell existing unstamped, non-duty liable stock until 31 March 2027. Tobacco duty rates also increased on 1 October 2026 by a one-off pro-rata amount of GBP2.20 per 100 cigarettes or per 50 grams of tobacco, in addition to the standard tobacco duty escalator.

Source: HMRC

Companies House pauses P&L filing for small companies; software mandatory April 2028

The government has decided to pause implementing the requirement for small companies and micro-entities to file profit and loss accounts with Companies House from April 2028, and will continue to allow companies to file abridged accounts and retain the option to fillet profit and loss information. From April 2028, all UK registered companies will be required to file accounts in Inline eXtensible Business Reporting Language (iXBRL) format using commercial software, with web and paper-based filing systems closing for accounts filings from that date.

Source: Companies House
