1. Saudi, Turkey, Pakistan sign mutual defence pact

Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan have signed a joint defence agreement stating that an attack on one of them would be seen as an attack on all three. The pact was announced at a meeting between Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif in the Saudi city of Mecca. The agreement aims "to strengthen collective deterrence against any act of aggression, and stipulates that any armed attack against any one of the three States shall be regarded as an attack against them all". A Turkish official quoted by Reuters said the pact was "purely defensive in nature".

Why it matters: The pact comes after the US and Israel attacked Iran on 28 February, following which Tehran and its allies struck targets in Saudi Arabia and disrupted traffic through the Strait of Hormuz and the Red Sea, upending the region's security architecture.

Source: BBC

2. Spain hits Italy with border controls over Ceuta

Spain has imposed border controls on all air and sea travel from Italy, starting at midnight (22:00 GMT) and lasting for a month, as a row between the two EU countries intensifies. The measures come a week after Italy introduced similar restrictions following an influx of about 78,000 migrants from Morocco into the Spanish exclave of Ceuta. On Friday, Spain demanded Italy reverse its checks by Sunday, but Rome said they would stay until at least 15 August. Italy's government said it would not accept "ultimatums" and did not intend "under any circumstances to reconsider the decision to suspend the application of the Schengen Agreement for third-country nationals arriving from Spain".

Why it matters: The crisis has pitted Spain's socialist leader Pedro Sanchez against Italy's conservative leader Giorgia Meloni, with Italy's decision to suspend Schengen backed by Finland and Denmark, and the Czech Republic calling for Spain's membership to be temporarily suspended.

Source: BBC

3. US economy sheds 23,000 jobs in July

The US economy unexpectedly lost 23,000 jobs in July, with declines driven by cuts in local government education, which shed 50,000 jobs, and retail, which lost 19,000, while the private sector gained 30,000 jobs. Figures for May and June were also revised down by a combined 103,000 jobs. The unemployment rate held steady at 4.1%, while average hourly earnings rose by 3.2% in the year to July. Economists had projected an unchanged unemployment rate and 83,000 new jobs for the month.

Why it matters: The weaker jobs data may cool pressure on the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates at its next meeting, though officials have already indicated they expect at least one rate hike before the end of the year to combat an annualised inflation rate of 3.5%.

Source: The Guardian

4. New Mexico orders Meta to pay total $942m

A New Mexico court ordered Meta to pay an additional $567m fine on top of the $375m levied in March, bringing the total the company must pay to $942m in a case regarding social media harms and addiction. The court also ordered Meta to remove Like counts and only show such metrics to children under 18 with approval from a parent or guardian, and to pause push notifications to underage users between 10 p.m. and 7 a.m., limiting usage to 90 hours a month. The judge acknowledged Meta is not the only platform causing a mental health crisis among youth but maintained its platforms play a significant part, ruling the company has caused a significant "public nuisance" in New Mexico. Meta said it plans to appeal the judgment.

Source: TechCrunch

5. OpenAI pauses Astra model over cyberattack risk

OpenAI said Friday it has suspended work on some aspects of its upcoming model Astra after an internal review found it had reached its "critical cybersecurity threshold", meaning it could independently identify and carry out cyberattacks against traditionally well-protected real-world systems. Under the company's "Preparedness Framework", created in 2023, this triggered additional safeguards. OpenAI stated that "preliminary evaluations indicate strong enough performance that we cannot rule out Critical capability level at this time", and confirmed that "Astra is an upcoming model, and was not involved in exploiting Hugging Face". The lab said it is enacting stricter security controls and working with relevant government agencies and "select AI safety organizations" to test the model's capabilities.

Why it matters: The disclosure comes after a different unreleased OpenAI model breached Hugging Face's systems during internal testing - described as the first verifiable incident of an AI lab losing control of its model - increasing scrutiny on the entire sector.

Source: TechCrunch

Closer to Home

Ahmadzai charged and remanded over Lisa Ross murder

Sharif Ahmadzai, a 26-year-old born in Afghanistan who arrived in Greece as a refugee when he was 15, has been charged and remanded in custody over the murder of Scottish aid worker Lisa Ross, 38, whose body was found inside a suitcase in an abandoned former car showroom in Athens' Kypseli area. Ahmadzai was in court on Thursday to be charged and remanded until his trial, which could take up to 18 months. The police union told the BBC that officers arrested Ahmadzai after his wife provided "key" and "decisive" details placing her husband and Lisa at the same apartment on 15 July. Ahmadzai is said to deny homicide with intent - equivalent to a murder charge in Scotland.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

20% business rates cut for pubs, clubs, live music venues

From April 2027/28, pubs, social clubs and live music venues in England will receive a 20% cut to their business rates bills, saving the typical pub an estimated £1,100 in the next financial year. The package is worth around £100 million a year and will benefit nearly 32,000 premises; the very largest live music venues are excluded. The cut comes on top of a 15% relief already announced for 2026/27 bills, with bills frozen in real terms for a further two years.

Source: HM Treasury

Bank Rate held at 3.75%, MPC vote 6-3

At its meeting ending 29 July 2026, the MPC voted 6-3 to maintain Bank Rate at 3.75%; the three dissenting members voted to raise it by 0.25 percentage points to 4%. CPI inflation has fallen to 2.6% but is expected to rise later in the year as higher energy prices continue to pass through. The next rate decision is due 17 September 2026.

Source: Bank of England
