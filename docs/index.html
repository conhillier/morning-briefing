1. Brazil votes in tight presidential run-off today

Brazilians are going to the polls on Sunday to elect their new president in a closely fought run-off between 80-year-old incumbent Lula and right-wing senator Flavio Bolsonaro, 45. Bolsonaro is the son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, who is serving a 27-year prison sentence for plotting a coup after losing the last election in 2022. Bolsonaro came top in the first round of voting on 4 October, which US President Donald Trump called a "very big" victory. On Saturday, Lula told a huge crowd on Rio de Janeiro's Copacabana beach that this election was a matter of "sovereignty".

Why it matters: Never before has a candidate who trailed after the first round managed to overturn the deficit and win in the second round, making the outcome genuinely uncertain.

Source: BBC

2. Serbia votes as student movement challenges Vucic

Serbians are voting in parliamentary elections today amid a mass student protest movement seeking to oust longtime leader Aleksandar Vucic, who has held power for more than a decade. The snap elections, called by Vucic in September, follow almost two years of student-led demonstrations against perceived corruption and authoritarianism, triggered by the deaths of 16 people when a train station canopy collapsed in Novi Sad in 2024. An Ipsos poll published on Thursday gives Vucic's Serbian Progressive Party 47 percent of the vote to the students' 40 percent, though other polls have put the students ahead. The Student List movement is led by cardiologist Ilija Srdanovic and its roster includes 250 largely unfamiliar candidates.

Why it matters: A loss for Vucic would mark a profound political shift in the Balkan country of some 6 million people, reminiscent of the defeat of Hungary's Viktor Orban in April.

Source: Al Jazeera

3. Gunmen kill seven at church funeral in Jordan

Gunmen opened fire during a funeral at a Greek Orthodox church in Karak, Jordan on Friday, killing seven people and wounding 40 others. The shooting took place about 90 kilometres south of Amman, with footage showing three attackers approaching the church and opening fire.

Why it matters: Jordan's Prime Minister Jafar Hassan said the attack was a result of terrorist ideology and blind extremism, and pledged legal measures against those who seek to introduce extremism.

Source: NPR

4. Gunmen kill at least 10 in northeast Nigeria village

Unknown gunmen raided the village of Binnari in the northeast Nigerian state of Taraba on Friday, killing at least 10 people, looting shops and setting houses ablaze, police say. The attackers reportedly arrived on about 50 motorcycles, two men apiece, just after midday Muslim prayers, in a rampage lasting up to four hours. Eyewitnesses reported 20 lives lost and Amnesty International's Nigeria director said bereaved families told the NGO that up to 50 people had died, with an initial funeral said to have included 37 people. Police said officers were continuing to search the bush for further casualties and warned the death toll could still rise.

Why it matters: Amnesty International accused the authorities of leaving rural communities "at the mercy of gunmen that are repeatedly commit atrocities with impunity" and called for an impartial investigation.

Source: BBC

5. Israel shuts West Bank crossings for election day

Israel's military has announced that all West Bank land crossings will be closed during Tuesday's election, and Palestinian workers will be barred from entering all settlements. The restrictions follow a major operation launched by Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu after two Palestinian gunmen killed two Israeli soldiers at a checkpoint, with three other soldiers also injured. Israeli forces have been demolishing homes and businesses in Hebron, with Defence Minister Israel Katz saying about 2,000 residents had been displaced. Netanyahu also threatened a "very big surprise" for the Palestinian Authority in an interview released on Saturday.

Why it matters: Hebron Mayor Youssef al-Jaabari said the operation was being "driven by the elections" and that Netanyahu was attempting to secure the votes of the far right and the settlers.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Storm Boelo batters Scotland with 80mph gusts

Storm Boelo has swept into Scotland with an amber Met Office warning covering the mainland and Western Isles from 21:00 Saturday until 21:00 Sunday, bringing gusts of up to 80mph. Train services have been cancelled on multiple routes including the Borders line, which is suspended due to subsidence, and all 30 CalMac ferry services on the west coast have been cancelled for the rest of the weekend. Energy company SSE has reported power cuts affecting the Western Isles and Argyll, and a number of power cuts in the Glasgow and Edinburgh areas have also been reported to SP Energy Networks. Paisley's Halloween festival and the Enchanted Forest event at Pitlochry on Saturday were both cancelled because of the severe weather.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Companies House accounts filing reforms updated for April 2028

From April 2028, all UK registered companies must file accounts in Inline eXtensible Business Reporting Language (iXBRL) format using commercial software, with web and paper-based filing systems closing for accounts filings on that date. The government has paused the requirement for small companies and micro-entities to file profit and loss accounts, continuing to allow abridged accounts and the option to fillet profit and loss information after 1 April 2028. Additional changes from April 2028 include a strengthened eligibility statement for companies claiming an audit exemption, a requirement to file component parts of accounts and reports together, and a limit on how many times a company can shorten its accounting reference period.

Source: Companies House
