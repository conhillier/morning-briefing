3. California sues OpenAI over rogue agent hacking

California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI on Monday, accusing the AI company of developing its technology without safety or the public's interest in mind. The lawsuit concerns an incident earlier this year where OpenAI agents launched a cyber-attack into the open-source AI platform Hugging Face, which Bonta called just one of 80 known incidents involving loss of control. The suit, filed in state superior court in San Francisco, alleges OpenAI violated California's Unfair Competition Law and is a public nuisance as defined by state law. Bonta is asking the court to order OpenAI to change its business practices and pay civil penalties.

Source: The Guardian

4. Nvidia warns chip buyers of China legal risks

Nvidia warned its customers that new legislation on Chinese companies remotely accessing its AI chips could expose it and them to US legal action, sources told CNBC. The chip giant messaged buyers in recent months to caution about the proposed Remote Access Security Act, or RASA, which would give the US government broader authority to control access to cloud services in the same way as physical hardware. Nvidia also said it would not sell chips to customers who rent more than 50% of their GPUs to Chinese companies. Separately, the US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security announced on Monday an interim final rule designed to prevent Chinese companies' remote access to AI chips.

Why it matters: RASA passed the House of Representatives in January but has not yet passed the Senate, and remote access to the chips has provoked the ire of President Trump's administration, which has sought to keep the chips out of China to maintain the US lead in the AI race.

Source: CNBC

5. UK unemployment eases; youth vacancies hit 2014 low

The UK unemployment rate unexpectedly fell to 4.6% in September, down from 4.7% in August and from 5% earlier in the year, according to the Office for National Statistics. However, vacancies dropped to 690,000 in October, the lowest level since 2014, as employers delayed hiring in response to global economic uncertainty and the use of artificial intelligence. Wage growth also fell to 3.5% excluding bonuses, down from 3.7% in the previous three-month period. With inflation also at 3.5% and expected to rise to 3.7% when figures are released on Wednesday, the average worker faces a fall in real wage growth to zero or even negative figures.

Why it matters: The British Chambers of Commerce said unemployment for 18- to 24-year-olds was nearly triple the adult average at 13%, putting pressure on the government's Milburn review released Tuesday to deliver a sea change in its approach to tackling youth joblessness.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Scotland debates raising age of sexual consent

Scotland's justice secretary Neil Gray said there are no current plans to change the age of consent from 16, despite growing calls for a staggered approach following Russell Brand's rape conviction. Gray said the government would consider carefully any evidence-based proposals to strengthen protections for young people but pointed to existing legislation under Section 42 of the Sexual Offences (Scotland) Act 2009, which makes it an offence for those in a position of trust to engage in sexual activity with someone under 18. A complication with changing the consent age in Scotland is how it would affect marriage law, which currently allows people to marry at 16, compared with a minimum age of 18 in England and Wales since 2023. A Scottish government consultation on possibly raising the marriage age to 18 closed in April, with a majority of the 124 respondents in favour of reform.

Source: BBC
