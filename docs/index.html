1. Jordan strikes Syria after church attack

Jordan has struck what its military says were "terrorist organisations" in Syria in retaliation for an attack on a Christian funeral last week that killed nine people. Syrian health officials told state media five people, including women, children and an elderly man, were killed in the strike on a village in the southern Daraa province. Jordan said it had co-ordinated with the Syrian government, but Syria said it had not been notified of Monday's attack. In response, Syria closed the Nassib crossing - described as the only passenger and commercial border crossing between Jordan and Syria - to all passenger and commercial traffic in both directions.

Why it matters: The strike and Syria's closure of the Nassib crossing escalate tensions between the two neighbours following the Karak church attack.

Source: BBC

2. 60 Christians killed in Ethiopia's Oromia

At least 60 Orthodox Christians were killed in separate attacks on October 20 and 24 in Ethiopia's Oromia region, according to church officials. The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church's holy synod said 14 people were killed on October 20 in Gora Silingo, Angada Kebele in Merti district, while another 46 were killed on October 24 in the villages of Tereta, Chole and Birbo in Shirka district - both in the Arsi Zone. More than 20 people were seriously injured and 20 homes burned down. A local official accused a newly formed rebel alliance that includes the Oromo Liberation Army (OLA) of carrying out the attacks, though the OLA has previously denied allegations of attacking civilians.

Why it matters: The holy synod condemned the killings as a violation of constitutional protections for life and religious freedom, and called on government institutions to publicly condemn the attacks and ensure those responsible are brought to justice.

Source: Al Jazeera

3. US and 30 nations warn of Sudan famine risk

The United States, the European Union and 30 countries have warned of an "alarming deterioration" in food security in Sudan, calling for "urgent action" to prevent famine. In a joint statement released by the US State Department on Monday, the signatories called on the groups fighting Sudan's civil war "to lift all impediments on humanitarian assistance". Sudan's army has been fighting the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces since April 2023, a conflict that has killed tens of thousands of people and displaced more than 13 million.

Why it matters: The signatories called on all members of the international community to "urgently scale up funding" to ensure assistance reaches those most in need.

Source: Al Jazeera

4. Serbia's students savour election victory

Serbia's student-led protest movement is celebrating after its Student List claimed more than half the votes in Sunday's parliamentary elections, pushing longtime leader Aleksandar Vucic and his Serbian Progressive Party (SNS) into a distant second place. Vucic conceded defeat shortly after polls closed, triggering celebrations in Belgrade with champagne corks, fireworks and crowds in the streets. The Student List had no hierarchy, no leader and no political experience, and most of Serbia's opposition parties stood aside to give it a clear run. Analyst Kurt Bassuener, co-founder of the Democratization Policy Council, warned that "the danger of a broad coalition like this is that the fissures will show soon after victory" and that it was "far from clear" who would fill what government positions.

Source: NPR

Closer to Home

Third storm threatens Scotland after Boelo chaos

Two new yellow wind warnings have been issued for Scotland as a third storm in a matter of days approaches, with a warning covering most of the country as well as parts of northern England and Northern Ireland running from 03:00 to 21:00 on Tuesday. Thousands of homes across Scotland remain without power in the aftermath of Storm Boelo, which brought wind gusts of up to 85mph (137km/h) and killed a 60-year-old man - later named as Richard Baines, curator of Logan Botanic Garden - after he was struck by a falling tree at Mid Kelton, near Castle Douglas. Scottish and Southern Energy Networks (SSEN) said about 8,600 homes in the north of Scotland remained without power on Monday evening, while Scottish Power Energy Networks said all but a "very small number" of its 50,000 affected customers in Ayrshire and Dumfries and Galloway would have power restored by 02:00 on Tuesday. All schools and nurseries in the Western Isles will remain shut until Wednesday at the earliest, and ScotRail warned speed restrictions would be in place on several routes until 16:00 on Tuesday.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Vaping Products Duty of PS2.20 per 10ml effective 1 October 2026

Vaping Products Duty comes into force from 1 October 2026, charged at PS2.20 per 10ml of vaping liquid, payable by manufacturers, importers and warehousekeepers approved by HMRC. All vaping liquids manufactured in or imported into the UK from that date are subject to excise duty. Tobacco duty also increased by a one-off pro-rata amount of PS2.20 per 100 cigarettes or per 50 grams of tobacco in addition to the standard escalator.

Source: HMRC

Vaping Duty Stamps mandatory from 1 April 2027

From 1 October 2026 vaping duty stamps must appear on retail packaging of newly manufactured or imported products, with a transitional period allowing unstamped non-duty-liable stock to be sold by wholesalers and retailers until 31 March 2027. From 1 April 2027 all vaping products sold in the UK must carry a valid vaping duty stamp. Digital stamp functionality becomes mandatory for new products manufactured in or imported into the UK from 1 January 2027.

Source: HMRC

Companies House pauses profit and loss filing for small firms

The government has decided to pause implementing the requirement for small companies and micro-entities to file profit and loss accounts with Companies House from April 2028, following recommendations of the Small Business Regulatory Taskforce. Companies will continue to be allowed to file abridged accounts and retain the option to remove profit and loss information from filings after 1 April 2028. Mandatory iXBRL software filing for all UK registered companies from April 2028 remains confirmed, with web and paper-based filing systems closing for accounts filings from that date.

Source: Companies House
