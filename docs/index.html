1. Trump waives Russia sanctions, deepening Zelensky rift

Donald Trump used his presidential authority to block numerous sanctions on Russia two days before they were due to take effect, issuing an executive order on Friday night saying withholding some was in the US "national interest". The sanctions were passed by the US Congress and signed into law by Trump in September, and included powers to levy tariffs of up to 100% on imports from the top five purchasers of Russian oil and gas, as well as sanctions against senior Russian figures including Putin and Moscow's so-called "shadow fleet".

Why it matters: Zelensky has long stressed the need for tighter international sanctions to limit funding Russia can use in its war effort, and the waiver removes key financial pressure on Moscow at a critical moment in the conflict.

Source: BBC

2. Russian attacks kill 8, cut power to 1 million in Ukraine

Russian attacks killed at least eight people and injured 60 across Ukraine on Friday, striking nine regions and cutting power to more than a million people. In Kharkiv, a 71-year-old woman and a 58-year-old man were killed and 12 people wounded in strikes on three districts, while two people were killed in Kramatorsk and two more in the Dnipropetrovsk region. A drone strike on a food-processing facility in the northwestern Volyn region killed one person and wounded eight, and in Volyn about 370,000 households lost power. The UN said civilian casualties in Ukraine had climbed steeply, with at least 328 civilians killed and 2,105 wounded in September alone - 85 percent more than in September 2025.

Why it matters: Russia's military chief told President Putin on Friday that the military had allocated the assets needed to press its aerial campaign against Ukraine's energy system, signalling further attacks are planned.

Source: Al Jazeera

3. Ukraine out of Patriot missiles as ceasefire collapses

Ukraine's air force confirmed to NPR that they are now out of the US-made Patriot missiles that are Kyiv's only means of stopping Russian ballistic missiles from continuing to pound Ukraine. The collapse of Trump's "energy ceasefire" was confirmed when Ukraine struck a major oil refinery in Ryazan, southeast of Moscow, after Zelensky said he was "done" trying to abide by the truce given that Russia had never ceased its attacks on Ukrainian electrical and heating infrastructure. Putin played down the seriousness of the strike on the oil facility but said Ukraine was constantly hitting targets Russia "frankly hadn't thought of", and that Russia would avenge them "twice over". Trump, meanwhile, told Newsmax: "They're not going to do it with him. He doesn't want to settle... No, I'm not a big fan of Zelenskyy."

Why it matters: The Patriot missile shortage leaves Kyiv with no means of intercepting Russian ballistic missiles, while Trump's deal to buy Russian diesel has framed Ukraine's retaliatory strikes as attacks on US investments, deepening the diplomatic crisis.

Source: NPR

4. Brent crude hits $110 as Hormuz attacks escalate

Brent futures hit a session high of $110 per barrel for the first time since May before easing slightly to close at $109.86, a gain of 3.7%, as Iran escalated attacks in the Strait of Hormuz and Houthi militants fired missiles at Saudi Arabia. US West Texas Intermediate rose 3.5% to settle at $101.41 per barrel, with WTI adding more than 10% this week. Saudi Arabia intercepted several missiles fired over the Red Sea on Friday, including one heading toward the oil export terminal Yanbu, while oil giant Aramco came under attack when one of its administrative buildings in Dhahran was hit on Tuesday. Diesel now costs an average of $6.33 a gallon in the US, more than 70% above the same period last year, according to AAA data.

Why it matters: Diesel prices have surged this year due to Ukraine's attacks on Russian refineries and Iranian strikes on energy infrastructure in the Middle East, adding to political pressure on Trump ahead of US midterm elections.

Source: CNBC

5. Russian hacker arrested over deadly London hospital hack

German police have detained a 28-year-old Russian man named as Vladimir K., accused of being one of the core members of the Qilin cyber-crime gang, which hacked blood testing company Synnovis in 2024 and caused chaos across several London hospitals. Vladimir K. was arrested in Japan in May when he travelled there on holiday, with his plans intercepted by German officials who then had Japanese police detain him on arrival. The Qilin gang has allegedly hacked more than 4,000 organisations since 2024 and extorted more than $140m from victims, and the Synnovis hack disrupted more than 10,000 appointments. The BBC has learned British police are examining whether their inquiry may lead to an unlawful killing case - potentially a world first in cyber-crime - after King's College Hospital confirmed one patient had "died unexpectedly" following the hack.

Why it matters: German detectives are now including the Synnovis incident in their investigation into Vladimir K., and if British police pursue an unlawful killing case it would be the first time a cyber attack has led to such a charge anywhere in the world.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

UK banks brace for tax hikes in Budget

Chancellor John Healey is planning to increase taxes on the banking sector, with senior banking executives telling the BBC the sector will be "on the menu" at the Budget in two weeks' time. Shares in Lloyds and NatWest both dropped more than 2% on Friday on expectations of tax hikes, while Barclays fell only slightly and HSBC rose as both have large international businesses that would be less affected. One option under consideration is reversing the reduction in the bank surcharge on corporation tax from 8% to 3% introduced in 2023, which could raise around £2bn, though the Treasury is also weighing a moratorium on banks using past losses to offset current profits, which could yield an estimated £1bn per year. The biggest four banks collectively made about £15bn profit in the first six months of this year.

Source: BBC
