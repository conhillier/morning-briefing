2. Trump tells Zelensky to swap out his negotiator

Zelensky told US senators at a meeting on Monday that decisions taken by the Trump administration "are hard to explain between friends," adding that arms shipments had been delayed and that one missile package was waiting for the signature of US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. Trump's special envoy Steve Witkoff is set to travel to Norway this week for the next round of negotiations on ending the war, with Zelensky also expected to attend.

Why it matters: The deepening rift comes as Trump announced plans for Russia to supply more than 300,000 metric tonnes of diesel to US and global markets, a decision that has angered Kyiv, European partners, and US officials who say it rewards Moscow while it continues its war on Ukraine.

Source: Al Jazeera

3. Drone strike kills 80 near Sudan market

A drone strike near a market in southern Sudan on Sunday evening killed 80 people, mostly commuters catching rides home, in one of the deadliest single strikes since war broke out in April 2023. The RSF-declared administration accused the army of carrying out the strike; all of West Kordofan state is under RSF control.

Why it matters: Between January and April, drone strikes accounted for at least 880 civilian deaths in Sudan, more than 80% of all conflict-related civilian deaths in the country during that period.

Source: The Guardian

4. Alberta votes to stay in Canada by wide margin

Voters in Canada's Alberta have overwhelmingly rejected a proposal to begin a process toward independence, with CBC projecting that about 76% supported remaining in Canada compared with 23% who backed beginning a process that could lead to separation, based on more than 1 million votes counted early Tuesday. Elections Alberta said the remaining results could take days to count.

Why it matters: Members of the separatist movement told the Guardian they would continue their push for independence, and the Calgary mayor said "no result will permanently put this to bed" as "the separatist movement sees this as a long-term project."

Source: The Guardian

5. US bans DeepSeek from government agency systems

The Department of Homeland Security has banned all use of DeepSeek within civilian government agencies and any technology built with the aid of its AI models, making public on Monday an order that took effect from the end of July. The order applies to federal civilian agencies including the Department of the Treasury, the Department of State, and the Department of Justice, but agencies within the Department of Defense are excluded.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Hoax bomb threats hit hundreds of Scottish schools

Hundreds of schools across Scotland received hoax bomb threat emails, prompting more than 300 calls to Police Scotland and triggering a specialist cyber crime investigation. The warnings were quickly identified as hoaxes and learning was unaffected in schools where pupils had returned from the October break. Similar hoax emails warning of an "explosive device" were also sent to around 300 schools in Northern Ireland. BBC Scotland contacted all 32 Scottish local authorities and more than half confirmed that schools in their area had received a warning email, with affected areas including Glasgow, Edinburgh, South Lanarkshire, North Lanarkshire, and Inverclyde.

Source: BBC
