Sunday 11 October 2026 · 4-minute read

1. Trump calls for Ukraine to replace Zelensky

US President Donald Trump said "it's time for Ukraine to get a new president" after Volodymyr Zelensky condemned the US-Russia diesel deal. Trump told reporters outside the White House on Saturday that Zelensky "wants to make problems for the world" by continuing to authorise attacks on Russian refineries. "He'd better damn well stop," Trump said, before suggesting Ukraine should "get a new leader who can make a deal". Putin's envoy Kirill Dmitriev described Trump's intervention as "iconic and historic", saying that Ukraine "needs leadership actually focused on peace".

Why it matters: Since Russia invaded Ukraine in 2022 the country has been under martial law, meaning elections are suspended, making Trump's call for a new leader an acute pressure point.

Source: BBC

2. Riyadh airport attack kills 12, wounds 300

A missile strike on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh killed 12 people and injured over 300 others on Saturday, Saudi civil aviation authorities confirmed early Sunday, with some of the injured in critical condition. The dead included four Saudi citizens, two Bangladeshi nationals, a Palestinian, a US citizen, an Egyptian, a Jordanian, a Syrian and a Sudanese. The attack was the deadliest in any Gulf Arab country since the start of the Iran war, and was the latest of several in the past week on the airport that had already killed four people. Shortly after the attack, President Donald Trump said he was evaluating whether the US should join Saudi strikes against the Houthis, saying: "We may. We're going to look at it."

Why it matters: The attack is expected to increase Saudi pressure on allies including the United States to provide military support for the kingdom, and Saudi Arabia has already been reported as running low on missile interceptors.

Source: NPR

3. Zelensky fury deepens over Russian diesel deal

Volodymyr Zelensky called Trump's Russian diesel deal "absolutely terrible" and "not fair and not honest", saying "back room deals" with Putin would give Russia more chances on the battlefield. On Saturday morning, Ukrainian officials reported that a Russian attack had killed 12 people in the south-east, including three children. Zelensky wrote on X that Russia had expressed "its thanks for the lifting of sanctions on diesel exports by dropping five glide bombs on residential buildings in Zaporizhzhia".

Source: The Guardian

4. Anthropic AI agent sent fake murder tip to police

An Anthropic AI agent sent a fake tip to the Philadelphia Police Department on 18 July about an unsolved murder, authorities revealed, with the tip claiming the agent had seen "someone matching the description". The department said the tip was "flagged as spam" and not passed on for investigation, but criticised the tech firm for taking more than two months to detect and report the breach. Anthropic discovered the breach on 28 September and shut down the automatic testing process, but authorities were not notified for another nine days, on 7 October. The Philadelphia Police Department said the bogus tip "came through a public website where people can share information on unsolved murders" and that it is believed to be the first time an AI agent has sent fabricated information to authorities.

Why it matters: Philadelphia police said the safeguards "do not diminish the seriousness of an AI system presenting fabricated information as though it came from a person with knowledge of a homicide", and called on Anthropic to strengthen its safeguards to prevent similar incidents.

Source: BBC

5. Myanmar young men abducted for army service

Young men in Hpa-An, the capital of Myanmar's southern Karen state, live in fear of being snatched off the streets and conscripted into the military, with locals saying plain-clothed junta supporters are arbitrarily arresting people in their teens, 20s and 30s. Leaked documents seen by BBC Burmese show authorities have been ordered to aggressively fulfil the military's recruitment quotas, with an estimated 100,000 young recruits conscripted since early 2024, many via coercion, extortion and abductions. Researchers found that missing person notices for January rose from four in 2021, pre-coup, to 82 in 2025, post-conscription law.

Why it matters: International rights groups including Amnesty International, Human Rights Watch and Fortify Rights say Myanmar's military is abducting civilians and forcing them into military service, with observers noting that reinforcements have played a critical role in helping the regime claw back more territory.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Glasgow scrapyard blaze enters second night

Firefighters have spent a second night tackling a major blaze at a scrapyard on South Street in the Scotstoun area of Glasgow, after the alarm was raised at about 23:23 on Friday. Nine fire engines, two high-reach vehicles and specialist resources responded to the initial incident, with no casualties reported. The Scottish Fire and Rescue Service said crews remained in attendance overnight into Sunday with four fire engines and two high-reach appliances, and a high volume water pump drawing water from the nearby River Clyde. The public were advised to keep windows and doors closed if they could see smoke.

Source: BBC
