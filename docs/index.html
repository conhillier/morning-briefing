1. Binance hit by $6.3bn hack, largest ever

Cryptocurrency giant Binance said $6.3bn (£4.7bn) of digital assets have been impacted by what could be the largest hack in history. Early checks show the incident may have involved an engineer's laptop that had been compromised, which allowed an attacker to make unauthorised withdrawals. Two analytics firms, TRM Labs and Chainalysis, have attributed the incident to a North Korean hacking unit called TraderTraitor, with Chainalysis saying it has "high confidence" in that link. The incident eclipses a record-setting $1.5bn hack on the Bybit exchange in 2025, and Binance said it will "cover all losses".

Why it matters: Western security agencies have previously said stolen funds are used to finance North Korea's nuclear weapons and missile development programs.

Source: BBC

2. Google loses $500bn as AI costs bite

Google parent Alphabet lost just over $500bn (£374bn) in value on Thursday after its stock hit a six-month low, falling by almost 13%. The company revealed its AI summaries on search were affecting ad revenue, while profits fell by a considerable 25% during the quarter and it showed negative free cash flow in excess of $9bn - almost twice the cash loss of three months ago. Total revenue grew 25% to $127.6bn, buoyed by Google Cloud revenue growing 103% to almost $31bn. By the end of Thursday, Alphabet's total stock market value had fallen to $3.46 trillion from $3.97 trillion the day before.

Source: BBC

3. North Korea fires missiles as tensions rise

North Korea launched ballistic missiles off its east coast into the Sea of Japan on Thursday, South Korea said, as tensions continued to rise between the two countries. South Korea's joint chiefs of staff said around 10 short-range ballistic missiles were fired from the North's eastern coastal town of Wonsan and flew for about 155 miles. The launches came days after the North threatened to resume frontline propaganda loudspeaker broadcasts and deploy new weapons along its border with the South, and followed leader Kim Jong Un's surprise visit to China. Tensions have increased since last month's land mine blasts at the border, in which three South Korean soldiers were injured in two explosions on 21 September in the DMZ.

Why it matters: South Korea's military said it maintained its readiness to repel any provocations by its rival under an alliance with the United States.

Source: Sky News

4. US and Japan agree joint missile production plant

The United States and Japan have agreed to establish a Japanese facility for the joint production of advanced medium-range air-to-air missiles, Japanese Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi announced. The two sides agreed to build a final assembly and inspection plant for the Advanced Medium-Range Air-to-Air Missile, or AMRAAM, in Japan, as part of a joint effort called "Operation Supercharge". The missiles are essential for the F-35 fighter jets used by both countries, Koizumi said.

Source: NPR

5. Healey budget slammed over PS28bn shortfall

Chancellor John Healey has backed away from cutting the cost of welfare this parliament, amid warnings his first budget could lead to brutal spending cuts because of a £28bn shortfall in public finances. The Institute for Fiscal Studies said Healey would need to have set aside an additional £28bn a year to offset the impact of inflation on future spending plans and hit the target of spending 3% of GDP in defence by 2030. IFS director Helen Miller warned of "a spending review that looks pretty brutal for some departments" including real-term budget cuts, saying "public service spending plans look extremely tight." The Office for Budget Responsibility has said total welfare spending will grow from £334bn last year to £431bn by 2030.

Why it matters: The Resolution Foundation said Healey had failed to target energy support to households that need it most, warning that even families receiving help face higher energy bills than during the last energy price shock, with bills expected to rise sharply in January.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Scottish police target online gangs coercing girls

Detectives in Scotland have joined international law enforcement in targeting online gangs which groom and coerce children - mostly girls aged between 11 and 17 - into acts of self-harm. Officers are hunting members of loose online networks known as "The Com", which mainly targets girls on gaming platforms and social media, and are mostly comprised of teenage and young adult males. So far there has been one arrest in Scotland, while there have been a handful of convictions in England; in August, 20-year-old Justin Swaddle was jailed for two years for blackmailing and coercing more than 100 victims.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Companies House accounts filing reforms updated for April 2028

From April 2028, all UK registered companies must file accounts in Inline eXtensible Business Reporting Language (iXBRL) format using commercial software, with web and paper-based filing systems closing for accounts filings on that date. The government has paused the requirement for small companies and micro-entities to file profit and loss accounts, meaning companies may continue to file abridged accounts and retain the option to remove profit and loss information after 1 April 2028. Additional changes from April 2028 include a strengthened eligibility statement for companies claiming an audit exemption, a requirement to file component parts of accounts and reports together, and a limit on how many times a company can shorten its accounting reference period.

Source: Companies House
