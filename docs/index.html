1. Witkoff arrives in Russia to meet Putin

US special envoy Steve Witkoff arrived in St Petersburg on Wednesday to meet President Vladimir Putin and discuss proposals for ending the Russia-Ukraine war. Finnish President Alexander Stubb, who also took part in the Oslo talks, said "genuine progress" was made on proposals to achieve peace but warned that patience would be needed. Putin is yet to publicly comment, but Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov said the Kremlin would listen to what Witkoff would offer while ruling out "any political concessions".

Why it matters: Zelensky and his European allies said it was now up to Russia to respond to the proposals agreed in Oslo, making Witkoff's St Petersburg meeting a pivotal moment in efforts to end the conflict.

Source: BBC

2. Sudan drone strikes kill over 300 people

Sudan's army has been accused of launching several drone strikes killing more than 300 people across two areas of West Kordofan state and the disputed border region of Abyei. In Abyei, 153 people were killed when drones hit a market on Monday, according to the region's information minister, with a military source telling the BBC the army carried out the attack, saying the market was a fuel supply site for the RSF. A separate strike in West Kordofan on Sunday evening killed 150 people, including at least 145 from a truck carrying around 200 mourners, according to the Sudan War Observatory. South Sudan has summoned Sudan's ambassador to lodge a formal protest, and there are fears the conflict could spill across the border.

Why it matters: The UN said the rising use of armed drones came as 20 million people faced acute hunger, with a rising risk of famine in parts of western Darfur and Greater Kordofan.

Source: BBC

3. Musk eyes Tesla-SpaceX mega-merger

With Tesla valued at roughly $1.5tn and SpaceX at $2tn, a combination would mark the largest merger ever. Musk said he saw "a lot to be gained" in bringing the businesses together, specifically combining Tesla's cars, robots and self-driving technology with SpaceX's rockets, data centres, satellites and telecommunications operations. Any deal would require backing from half of Tesla's shareholders, a threshold Musk described as "quite a high hurdle", and he stressed nothing has been agreed.

Why it matters: SpaceX shares rose 8% in after-hours trading while Tesla shares climbed 1.5% following the news, though analysts note Tesla shareholders would likely hold a "smaller slice of a much larger and more complex business".

Source: BBC

4. UK inflation hits 3.5%, rate hike near-certain

UK inflation rose by a hotter-than-expected 3.5% in the 12 months to September, driven by surging motor fuel costs, according to the Office for National Statistics. Economists polled by Reuters had forecast a rate of 3.4%, and the September reading was the highest annual inflation figure in a year, continuing an upward trend from 3.1% in August and 2.9% in July. UK motor fuel prices surged 29.8% in the year to September, with the ONS saying the motor fuel inflation rate hit its highest level since August 2022. Markets are now pricing in a 98% chance that the Bank of England raises its key interest rate by 25 basis points to 4% next month, according to LSEG data.

Why it matters: Wednesday's inflation data also complicates the fiscal picture for Chancellor John Healey, who is set to unveil his first Autumn Budget next week, with the UK's national debt estimated to have exceeded £3 trillion in September for the first time.

Source: CNBC

Closer to Home

UK approves Jackdaw North Sea gasfield

The UK government approved the controversial Jackdaw gasfield in the North Sea, with Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh saying the field will begin supplying gas to homes and businesses from the end of this month. Environmental campaigners condemned the move, with Uplift's Tessa Khan saying "Jackdaw will provide around just 2% of our gas demand for roughly 10 years" and the Green party leader Zack Polanski calling it "climate vandalism". A poll by Savanta for climate group E3G found that nearly a third of voters said they would be less likely to vote Labour if it approved more North Sea oil drilling.
