2. Congo Ebola outbreak tops 12,000 cases

The Ebola outbreak in eastern Congo has reached 12,021 confirmed cases, making it the fastest-growing Ebola outbreak in history, according to Congo's Ministry of Health. Nearly half of those confirmed cases have died, making it also the deadliest of Congo's 17 outbreaks, and it is on track to surpass the death toll from the West African outbreak that killed 11,000 people between 2014 and 2016. The outbreak, declared in May, is caused by the rare Bundibugyo virus, for which there is no approved vaccine or treatment. Fighting between the army and the Convention pour la revolution populaire rebels has disrupted response efforts, with about a dozen people including two WHO personnel evacuated after being trapped for three days in a hospital in Akwe.

Why it matters: Response teams are battling armed conflict, poor roads, and violence from residents fueled by mistrust and misinformation, with fighting preventing teams from reaching some communities and leaving some treatment centers with fewer staff.

Source: NPR

4. Nvidia pauses chip shipments to Southeast Asia

Nvidia has told manufacturing partners, including Dell and Super Micro, to pause server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Indonesia for an unspecified period while it assesses whether regional partners are meeting US export requirements, according to a Wall Street Journal report. The pause follows Chinese companies tapping Nvidia chips remotely from data centres in those countries, a loophole the US Commerce Department closed earlier this month. The think tank Institute for Progress estimates remote data centres like these supply about 24% of China's AI computing power.

Why it matters: The move signals growing US pressure on Nvidia to enforce export compliance as Washington seeks to limit China's access to advanced AI computing infrastructure.

Source: TechCrunch

5. Thanksgiving storm batters US with 460 cancelled flights

A massive Thanksgiving storm struck the eastern US with winds of up to 70mph, torrential rain, and major coastal flooding, with 460 flights cancelled and 2,508 flights delayed on Thursday according to tracking site FlightAware. In New York City, organisers of the annual Macy's Thanksgiving parade postponed the event to Saturday. On the West Coast, water levels along California's coastline could reach over 8ft in some areas, with both San Diego and Los Angeles reporting high tides of over 8ft earlier in the week.

Why it matters: The American Automobile Association predicted that 81.5 million Americans will travel at least 50 miles from home for the Thanksgiving holiday over a seven-day period, making the widespread disruption unusually impactful.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Scotland's chief medical officer resigns on mental health grounds

Sir Gregor Smith, Scotland's chief medical officer since December 2020, has resigned citing mental health grounds, saying he did not "currently enjoy the good mental health necessary" to continue in the role. The 55-year-old said that guiding the country through the Covid pandemic and its aftermath had "taken its toll," and that "events over the last few days" had made his position untenable. His resignation follows questions over his role in the handling of avoidable deaths at NHS Grampian, after surgeon Dr Mohammed Habib leaked an unpublished 2022 review finding 19 avoidable deaths among 45 patients in liver and pancreatic surgery at Aberdeen Royal Infirmary. Dame Anna Dominiczak, the Scottish government's chief health scientist and regius professor of medicine at the University of Glasgow, has been named as interim chief medical officer.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

HMRC late payment interest rate rises from 24 November 2026

The Bank of England base rate increased to 4.00% from 3.75% on 5 November 2026, triggering a rise in HMRC interest rates for late payment and repayment. Late payment interest is set at base rate plus 4%; repayment interest is set at base rate minus 1%, with a minimum floor of 0.5%. Changes take effect on 16 November 2026 for quarterly instalment payments and 24 November 2026 for non-quarterly instalment payments.

Source: HMRC

Companies House pauses P&L filing; mandates iXBRL software from April 2028

The government has decided to pause implementing the requirement for small companies and micro-entities to file profit and loss accounts with Companies House from April 2028, and will continue to allow companies to file abridged accounts and retain the option to 'fillet' profit and loss information after 1 April 2028. From April 2028, all UK registered companies must file accounts in Inline eXtensible Business Reporting Language (iXBRL) format using commercial software, with web and paper-based filing systems closing for accounts filings on that date.

Source: Companies House
