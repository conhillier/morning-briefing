1. Trump defends Russia sanctions pause as peace bid

US President Donald Trump said his decision to suspend several Russian sanctions is an effort to keep Moscow at the negotiating table, posting on Truth Social that he didn't want to "do anything that will drive, foolishly, Russia away from the bargaining table". Trump blocked numerous sanctions on Russia on Friday night, two days before they were due to take effect. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio described the suspension as a 60-day pause during which the potential impact on the US and global economies would be analysed. Zelensky told NBC News on Sunday that siding with Russian President Vladimir Putin would be a "historic mistake", while Russian envoy Kirill Dmitriev called Trump's order "an important and truly historic moment".

Why it matters: Zelensky said he believed the Trump administration would lift more sanctions against Russia, and described the US deal to import Russian diesel as tantamount to investing in Russia's war effort.

2. WFP slashes Gaza food aid by a third

The World Food Programme says it is being forced to "drastically reduce lifesaving assistance" to Gaza due to funding shortages, with the number of people helped dropping by about a third - from 1.6 million to 1.1 million. Hot meals provided by community kitchens will be cut from 250,000 to 150,000 each day, and families in hard-to-reach areas will receive a meal every other day instead of daily. The WFP received less than half as much funding for its Palestinian operations in 2026 as in previous years, and says it urgently needs $450m (£338m) to maintain critical operations over the next six months. The Palestine director for the WFP, Shaun Hughes, was clear the cuts were being made because of finances, "not because the needs are coming down, or because we don't have access - we do."

Why it matters: Hughes told the BBC that "cuts to food assistance are going to compound that hardship that families have been facing for three years now", with nearly all of Gaza's 2.1 million population displaced and two-thirds relying on food aid to survive.

Source: BBC

3. China Q3 GDP grows 4.5%, beats forecasts

China's gross domestic product expanded 4.5 percent in the July-September period from the same quarter a year earlier, picking up from 4.3 percent growth in the second quarter and beating most analysts' forecasts, according to figures released on Monday by the National Bureau of Statistics. During the first nine months of the year, GDP grew 4.6 percent year-on-year. Chinese exports surged more than 16 percent in the first three quarters of the year to reach 23.19 billion yuan ($3.47bn), largely due to strong demand for semiconductors and other tech-related goods. However, growth has been dampened by weak domestic demand and a sagging property market, with investment in fixed assets contracting 7.2 percent during the January-September period.

Why it matters: China's ruling Communist Party has set a GDP growth target of 4.5 to 5 percent for 2026; Stanford finance professor Zhiguo He told Al Jazeera he expects Beijing is "getting 4.5 percent as wanted" but warned that "a managed slow slowdown is the best Beijing can hope for".

Source: Al Jazeera

4. Anthropic pledges 5% of shares to public fund

AI company Anthropic announced it will commit 5% of its shares to a fund that will make donations for the "public good", with the announcement coming as the firm lays the ground for a potential stock market listing that could value it at more than $1tn (£750bn). The proposed Public Benefit Fund could support nonprofit organisations and efforts to address AI's impact on the jobs market, and none of the shares would be used for lobbying or political donations. Anthropic's board could also direct a further 2% of the firm's shares to the fund at its discretion. Last week, Anthropic told prospective investors that its revenue surged by 45% to $16.8bn (£12.6bn) in the three months to the end of September.

Why it matters: Anthropic reportedly said in its IPO filing that AI could pose "catastrophic or existential risks to humanity", and the announcement comes amid intense scrutiny of the AI industry over safety concerns and fears about its impact on society and the economy.

Source: BBC

5. Four Canadian soldiers killed in Alberta base fire

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said four service members were killed and dozens more seriously injured in a fire at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright in Alberta, describing it as one of the worst peacetime tragedies for Canada's military on domestic soil. Fire crews scrambled late Saturday as fierce winds with gusts up to 100 kph (62 mph) whipped flames beyond the base and into the surrounding rural area. The Canadian military said there were no indications that the incident was the result of terrorism, but the cause remained under investigation. Several hospitals across the province activated a "Code Orange" emergency designation, with Acute Care Alberta saying emergency medical resources were stretched so thin that extremely few ambulances were available in the Edmonton area.

Why it matters: An emergency department physician at the Royal Alexandra Hospital in Edmonton described the situation as "a one in every 15- to 25-year disaster", with patients taken to hospitals across the city as healthcare facilities around the base became "completely overwhelmed".

Source: NPR

Closer to Home

UK home sellers cut prices on 3,000 homes daily

Home sellers cut asking prices on more than 3,000 properties a day on average in Britain during September, according to figures from Rightmove shared exclusively with the BBC - the highest total of any month for 15 years. The average price reduction was nearly 7%, equivalent to nearly £21,000 cut from a typically valued home, and homes that had a price reduction typically took 119 days to find a buyer compared with 39 days for those listed at one price. The proportion of listed homes that had a price reduction stood at 34% last month, only slightly lower than the recent peak of 36% seen in September 2023. Zoopla said the number of homes for sale was up 5% in September compared with a year earlier, but the number of sales agreed had fallen by 9% over the same period.

Source: BBC
