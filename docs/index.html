1. Trump, Xi, Putin hold first-ever trilateral summit

US President Donald Trump met Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin on Thursday for an unprecedented trilateral gathering at the APEC summit in Shenzhen. Putin said Russia, China and the US can strengthen global stability if they cooperate constructively, and made clear that the war in Ukraine would be a focus of his conversation.

Source: CNBC

2. Trump calls for nuclear arms reduction at summit

In his opening remarks at the trilateral meeting, Trump said the US, Russia and China are "uniquely positioned" to discuss how to reduce the number of nuclear weapons on earth and also to lessen the risk that those weapons will ever be used. "The power is unbelievable, unthinkable," he said, adding that it is "a very hard thing" for countries to reduce nuclear weapons. Trump said that would include not letting Iran have nuclear weapons, and also said the three leaders would discuss the war between Russia and Ukraine. Xi echoed Trump's comments on nuclear weapons, calling for such arms to be comprehensively prohibited and destroyed, adding that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought.

Source: CNBC

3. Crimean Bridge struck for third time this week

The Crimean Bridge has been attacked by Ukraine for the third time this week, Russia has said. The Moscow-installed head of Crimea, Sergei Aksyonov, confirmed the strike in the early hours of Thursday via Telegram, saying traffic was temporarily suspended following the attack.

Why it matters: While Ukrainian forces have struggled to close the bridge for good, recent attempts have brought them closer to destroying the bridge's crucial road and rail route for Russian soldiers heading towards the front line.

Source: BBC

4. Anthropic debuts on Nasdaq at $1.5 trillion valuation

Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share, above its initial range of $70 to $78, generating $81.3 billion in proceeds and surpassing SpaceX's $75 billion record to become the largest IPO in history by proceeds raised. The Claude maker sold 980 million shares in the offering, up from the 825 million initially planned, and will begin trading on Nasdaq under the ticker symbol "ANT" on Thursday. At the IPO price, Anthropic is valued at roughly $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis, a significant increase from the $965 billion the company reached in May when it raised $65 billion in its last private market round. The company's seven co-founders, including CEO Dario Amodei, each sold nearly 3.4 million shares in the IPO, bringing in about $282 million for each.

Source: TechCrunch

5. North Korea expands Yongbyon uranium enrichment facility

North Korea is expanding a nuclear facility for enriching uranium, according to IAEA Director General Rafael Grossi, who said an existing facility at the Yongbyon nuclear centre has been further expanded in the last couple of months. "Since mid-September 2026, a further extension to the original Yongbyon enrichment facility has been under construction with the new extension externally complete by late October 2026," Grossi said in a statement to the IAEA's board of governors. North Korea has called its Yongbyon complex "the heart" of its nuclear programme, and the country has facilities to produce both highly enriched uranium and weapons-grade plutonium there. Pyongyang has not allowed IAEA inspectors into the country for years, with the agency keeping watch through satellite imagery and open-source information.

Source: Al Jazeera

Closer to Home

Falkirk asylum hotel closed permanently by Home Office

A hotel in Falkirk which was previously used to house asylum seekers has been closed and handed back to the community, the Home Office has confirmed. The Cladhan Hotel on Kemper Avenue has not been used for accommodating asylum seekers since August, and a similar hotel outside Dumfries is to close in February. Border Security and Asylum Minister Anna Turley said the government was "restoring fairness and control" to an immigration system that had "spiralled out of control," adding that asylum seekers are being moved to alternative and "more basic forms of accommodation", including former military barracks. The hotel was first used as asylum accommodation under the previous Conservative government in 2021 and was the site of several demonstrations last summer involving pro and anti-immigration demonstrators.

Source: BBC
