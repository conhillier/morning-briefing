1. Spain train crash kills at least 68

A high-speed train travelling from Madrid to Seville crashed into a stationary train on Wednesday morning, killing at least 68 people and injuring dozens more. The stationary Iryo train had 147 passengers on board and had stopped because of a computer failure, while the Renfe train that struck it was carrying 397 passengers. Spain's Transport Minister Oscar Puente said a computer failure followed by human error caused the crash, which he described as one of the worst in Europe's modern history and Spain's deadliest train disaster in 13 years. The crash happened just after 09:00 around 140km south of Madrid, between the municipalities of Mora and Orgaz.

Why it matters: Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez called it "unacceptable" for Spain to have two serious train crashes in one year, vowing to determine if there was any negligence, after a separate high-speed rail disaster near Cordoba in January killed 46 people on the same line.

Source: BBC

2. Ebola outbreak hits 10,000 cases, may spread globally

Africa's Ebola outbreak has reached 10,091 cases and nearly 5,000 deaths, with the Africa CDC's director general Dr Jean Kaseya warning the disease could reach the UK and other countries if preparedness measures are not taken. Kaseya described the outbreak as "unprecedented", noting it is the first time three African regions have been affected simultaneously, spanning from Nigeria to Kenya. The Bundibugyo virus, which has no approved treatment, caused the outbreak when DR Congo's health ministry declared it in Ituri province on 15 May, though sequencing shows it had begun in February. France is the only non-African country to have confirmed a case so far - a doctor who had returned from DR Congo in June.

Why it matters: Kaseya warned that governments had pledged $3bn but more than $2bn was still outstanding, cautioning that failure to fund the response now could eventually require trillions of dollars if the outbreak continues to spread.

Source: The Guardian

3. Israel election deadlock as Netanyahu leads narrowly

Partial results from Israel's general election show Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud party and its far-right and ultra-Orthodox allies on track to win 53 seats in the 120-seat Knesset, while rival Gadi Eisenkot's bloc was set for 48 seats, with 85% of ballots counted. Arab-led parties are on course to win an unprecedented 19 seats and could emerge as kingmakers, though all but one of the Jewish parties have pledged not to work with them. Between 430,000 and 480,000 absentee ballots from soldiers, diplomats and hospital patients have yet to be counted, meaning final results could take a day or two. President Isaac Herzog, who praised the 77.7% turnout as the country's highest in more than two decades, said he expected official results next week before beginning coalition discussions.

Why it matters: Tuesday's election was the first since the deadly Hamas-led attacks on Israel on 7 October 2023 and the wars that followed in Gaza, Lebanon and Iran, and was seen by Israelis as one of the most consequential in the country's history.

Source: BBC

4. Azure revenue hits $33.2bn, up 48%

Microsoft disclosed for the first time that its Azure cloud service brought in $33.2 billion in revenue for the July-September quarter, up 48% from a year earlier. The figure came in about $850 million more than the $32.3 billion analysts had expected, and beat the 44% to 45% growth rate that Microsoft CFO Amy Hood had forecast. Azure now accounts for roughly 36% of Microsoft's $92.3 billion in total quarterly revenue, making it the company's largest and fastest-growing product line. The disclosure allows investors to directly compare Azure's performance against Google Cloud, which brought in $30.7 billion in the same quarter, and Amazon Web Services, whose results are due Thursday.

Why it matters: The new Azure revenue disclosure ends years of Microsoft only sharing vague growth percentages, putting all three major cloud providers on a comparable footing for the first time.

Source: TechCrunch

5. Microsoft earned $10bn from OpenAI last quarter

Microsoft generated $10 billion in revenue from commercial arrangements with OpenAI, including revenue-sharing payments, in the quarter ended 30 September, representing nearly 11% of Microsoft's total quarterly revenue of $92.3 billion. For the full 2026 fiscal year, revenue from OpenAI hit $24.1 billion, and at the latest quarterly pace Microsoft would pull in $40 billion annually from OpenAI-related arrangements. Microsoft also reported $7.2 billion in outstanding receivables from OpenAI at the end of September, up from $6 billion at the end of June. Microsoft's ownership stake in OpenAI has also declined slightly from about 25% in June to 24% in September.

Why it matters: The figures highlight the enormous financial ties that remain between the two companies even as they increasingly compete for the same customers.

Source: TechCrunch

Closer to Home

Renfrew Bridge shut for months after Storm Boelo

The Renfrew Bridge, which opened to road traffic across the River Clyde last year as part of a £117m project, will be closed for "a number of months" after Storm Boelo caused serious water damage to the electrical systems in one of the bridge's two operating plant rooms. Renfrewshire Council said the tidal surge on Saturday night saw the Clyde's water levels reach 6.7m at Renfrew, severely damaging the electronics that control hydraulics on the Renfrew side of the bridge. Because the council is legally obligated to keep the Clyde open to river traffic, the swing bridge will remain in an open position, cutting off the route often used to reach the Queen Elizabeth University Hospital. The council was also criticised for not raising flood barriers at the weekend, saying its data suggested predicted tide levels would not meet the threshold, only for the tide to be higher than predicted.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Vaping Products Duty at PS2.20 per 10ml from 1 October 2026

Vaping Products Duty comes into force from 1 October 2026, charged at PS2.20 per 10ml of vaping liquid, payable by manufacturers, importers and warehousekeepers approved by HMRC. Tobacco duty rates have also increased by a one-off pro-rata amount of PS2.20 per 100 cigarettes or per 50 grams of tobacco, in addition to the standard tobacco duty escalator. From 1 April 2027, all vaping products sold in the UK must carry a valid vaping duty stamp; a six-month grace period allows wholesalers and retailers to sell existing unstamped non-duty-liable stock until 31 March 2027.

Source: HMRC

Companies House pauses profit and loss filing for small companies

The government has decided to pause implementing the requirement for small companies and micro-entities to file profit and loss accounts with Companies House from April 2028, following recommendations of the Small Business Regulatory Taskforce. Companies will continue to be allowed to file abridged accounts and retain the option to remove, or fillet, their profit and loss information from accounts filed at Companies House after 1 April 2028. The mandatory requirement for all UK registered companies to file accounts in iXBRL format using commercial software from April 2028 remains in place, with web and paper-based filing systems closing for accounts filings from that date.

Source: Companies House
