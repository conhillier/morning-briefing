1. Serbia's Vucic concedes to student protest movement

Serbia's long-standing leader Aleksandar Vucic has admitted defeat to the student-led protest movement in snap parliamentary elections. Preliminary figures from Serbia's election commission indicated a record high turnout, with 64.9% having cast their vote by 18:00 local time, two hours before polls were set to close. The Student List's lead candidate Ilija Srdanovic told BBC Serbian: "This will be a free state." Vucic called the snap elections after two years of protests triggered by the collapse of a train station canopy in the city of Novi Sad, which killed 16 and was seen as emblematic of corruption and negligence.

Why it matters: The anti-corruption movement campaigned on a manifesto that included purging officials who broke rules cracking down on protesters, stopping party loyalists from leading public enterprises, and freeing the public broadcaster of political control.

Source: BBC

2. Lula wins historic fourth presidential term in Brazil

Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva has been elected president of Brazil for a fourth, nonconsecutive term, defeating far-right senator Flavio Bolsonaro in Sunday's runoff. Lula secured 51.79% of the ballots, or 64,256,232 votes, while Bolsonaro received 48.21% or 59,826,041 votes. Lula, who turns 81 on Tuesday, told supporters in Sao Paulo: "We are going to change the way that this country is governed... This election was a defeat for denialism." Bolsonaro said it was "impossible to concede the election result" and that his legal team would seek to have the election annulled due to a supreme court ruling that extended voting for two hours.

Source: The Guardian

3. Jordan arrests three suspects in church attack

Security forces in Jordan have arrested three armed suspects in connection with a deadly attack on a Greek Orthodox church in Karak that killed eight people and injured 40. The suspects were located on Monday while hiding in an abandoned house in the village of Al-Jadeeda in Karak, and refused to surrender before attacking security forces, who returned fire and arrested all three after wounding them. One suspect was in a critical condition after fighting that lasted about three hours, a Jordanian government source told Al Jazeera. Of the injured from Friday's attack, 27 remained in hospital on Monday, with one person in critical condition after undergoing surgery.

Why it matters: King Abdullah II said those behind the attack would face the "harshest penalties" and has ordered a review of the country's security procedures for responding to attacks.

Source: Al Jazeera

4. Venezuelan opposition leader Machado returns from exile

Venezuelan opposition leader Maria Corina Machado has returned home after more than 10 months in exile, arriving in Caracas late on Sunday to cheering supporters. "I am here. With you," Machado wrote on X on Sunday, after prior efforts to re-enter the country had repeatedly come to no avail - an advisor said she had attempted to return seven times in September alone.

Why it matters: US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the conditions allowing her safe return were "the direct result of the US supported reconciliation talks which has already seen hundreds of political prisoners freed and hundreds of political opposition figures return from exile".

Source: BBC

5. SpaceX closes $40bn debt deal for AI chips

Elon Musk has announced that SpaceX has closed $40 billion in debt financing to purchase graphics processing units used to power artificial intelligence systems. In a post on X on Sunday, Musk said the financing primarily consisted of callable debt for GPUs expected to arrive in the first and second quarters of 2027, with Goldman Sachs leading talks with investors including Apollo Global Management. SpaceX's AI segment, which it refers to as "Super Intelligence," has become the company's largest business unit, with third-quarter revenue surging ninefold to $7.56 billion from $813 million a year earlier. Musk also warned that more than 200 so-called neocloud companies would face widespread failures and consolidation as debt markets have largely shut off access to financing for companies without substantial revenue outside AI data centres.

Why it matters: The financing underscores the enormous capital requirements of the AI boom, as technology companies pour billions of dollars into advanced chips and data centers to support increasingly powerful AI models.

Source: CNBC

Closer to Home

Storm Boelo kills man, cuts power across Scotland

A 60-year-old man has died after being struck by a falling tree on a minor road at Mid Kelton, near Castle Douglas, in Dumfries and Galloway, as Storm Boelo brought strong winds and heavy rain to Scotland. The storm left more than 50,000 properties without power, with winds gusting to 85mph (137km/h) at Machrihanish in Argyll and Bute. Two women were also injured by a falling tree in Aberfeldy, Perth and Kinross, and were taken to hospital. Network Rail reported that trees had come down on tracks near Dumfries and Partick in Glasgow, and Kirkwall Airport in Orkney was closed until Monday.

Source: BBC
