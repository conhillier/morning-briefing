1. US charges trio over RAF Fairford terror plot

The US Department of Justice has charged three Iran-backed operatives allegedly involved in a plot targeting RAF Fairford, a UK air base where US forces were stationed. Iranian Mohsen Mohajeri, Iraqi national Mohammad Ali al-Hassani and dual Iranian-Iraqi citizen Mohammad Baqer Saad Dawood al-Saadi were named in an indictment unsealed on Tuesday. Al-Hassani allegedly directed and paid associates to conduct surveillance of the base's perimeter on 22 August, and later arranged for three vans loaded with barrels of fuel to be driven toward the base on 26 September.

Why it matters: FBI Director Kash Patel said the eight-count indictment "shows the determination of Iran to commit terrorist attacks in Europe and the US", and the US has already withdrawn all its bombers from RAF Fairford following the incident.

Source: BBC

2. US soldier arrested in UK on Russia spy charges

A 41-year-old senior US Army soldier has been arrested near Gloucester on suspicion of spying for Russia, British counter-terror police said on Tuesday. He was detained under section one and section three of the National Security Act 2023, which relate to obtaining or disclosing protected information and assisting a foreign intelligence service respectively. The BBC understands the soldier was based at Imjin Barracks in Innsworth, Gloucestershire, which serves as the headquarters of NATO's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps. He is currently held at a London police station and can be detained without charge for up to 14 days.

Why it matters: FBI Director Kash Patel said the arrest "involves allegations of espionage, which is of the utmost concern to the United States and our allies", while the commanding general of US Army Counterintelligence Command warned that "insider threats are real and persistent".

Source: BBC

3. Abu Dhabi Ukraine peace talks last four hours

Russia and Ukraine held their first US-mediated talks in nine months on Tuesday in Abu Dhabi, with the session lasting four hours and a second day of talks scheduled for Wednesday. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio called the meeting "very productive" and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy described it as "constructive and quite substantive", saying talks focused largely on humanitarian issues including prisoner exchanges and Ukrainian civilians in captivity. A senior Ukrainian official said Ukraine and Russia discussed a large-scale prisoner swap, with details to be worked out in coming days.

Why it matters: Despite the positive tone, Rubio cautioned that "there's a lot of work to be done" and that the situation was "not like something that's on the verge of being solved in the next 24 hours or anything like that".

Source: NPR

4. Iran says Hormuz stays shut pending US response

Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei said on Tuesday that the Strait of Hormuz will not reopen until the US meets Tehran's seven conditions, dismissing reports of ongoing negotiations as "a pure lie". Rezaei told Lebanon's Al Mayadeen TV that Washington was talking up negotiations to ease economic pressure and bring down oil prices, adding that the US initial response was "insufficient and deceptive". Meanwhile, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps claimed that three oil tankers caught fire after striking naval mines in the strait, with maritime monitoring firms reporting at least 37 attacks on ships in and near the Strait of Hormuz in November. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said the conflict "will not be resolved through military means" and that Hormuz could reopen within two days if demands are met.

Why it matters: The war, now in its ninth month, has roiled global markets and disrupted oil and gas supplies, pushing up prices around the world, while US Secretary of State Rubio said Washington was "open to diplomacy" but that "the economic campaign is so devastating that right now there is no need for major combat operations".

Source: Al Jazeera

5. Nvidia hires Wachtell law firm for spy probe

Nvidia has hired New York law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to lead what it called "a thorough and independent investigation" into the affair between CEO Jensen Huang and alleged Chinese spy Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le. The company's share price has fallen almost to a yearly low, with $500bn (PS375bn) wiped off its stock market value since Friday. Congressional leaders of the House Committee on Foreign Affairs have asked Huang to appear next week for a closed-door briefing, and the committee has the authority to subpoena him if he declines. The Nvidia boss has yet to say whether he will attend, according to a person familiar with the committee's demand.

Why it matters: The investigation will likely be treated as urgent because Jensen Huang's public image is tightly intertwined with the company, which is the world's second most valuable company after Apple and whose chips have become a matter of national security for US officials.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Scotland's housing crisis deepens as builds fall

Scotland's housing shortage is worsening, with homelessness reaching record levels and more than 19,000 households in temporary accommodation - a 10% rise on the previous year - including 10,715 children. In the 2025-26 financial year, only 17,640 new homes were completed, the third consecutive year of decline, and new project starts over the last two years were the lowest since 2012-13. Glasgow is spending almost PS70m a year putting people up in hotel rooms and has been declared in a state of "systemic failure" by the Scottish Housing Regulator, while 44% of the city's 8,025 homelessness applications last year came from people with refugee status or leave to remain. Plans have been announced for 6,500 new affordable homes on brownfield sites over the next six years, and the council has identified 59 sites for another 12,173 new homes.

Source: BBC
