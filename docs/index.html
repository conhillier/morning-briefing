1. Jensen Huang returns, Nvidia surges $1 trillion

Nvidia's market value rose by nearly $1 trillion as its share price skyrocketed after the tech firm reinstated Jensen Huang as its chief executive with immediate effect. Nvidia shares rose by more than 31%, lifting the tech-heavy Nasdaq by 6.5% and the S&P 500 by 4.75%. Other chipmakers also surged, with Micron Technology jumping 19% and AMD shares rising nearly 7%. Nvidia's board of directors voted unanimously to reinstate the firm's co-founder following "a series of discussions and meetings" with him, though US President Donald Trump claimed he "convinced" the board to reinstate Huang.

Source: BBC

2. NATO chief vows space defence after nuclear satellite reports

NATO chief Mark Rutte has said the alliance will defend against any threats in space after German reports that Russia is preparing to launch a nuclear-armed satellite. Intelligence reports about a possible launch in the next 60 days had been shared among NATO countries this month, according to German media. German public broadcasters NDR and WDR and newspaper Suddeutscher Zeitung reported the Russian satellite was dubbed "Sputnik-S" and was equipped with a nuclear warhead that could be used to disrupt or destroy other satellites. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said the reports "reveal the level of escalation Russia is evidently prepared to pursue", while the Kremlin dismissed stories citing "unknown sources" as "hardly worth taking seriously".

Why it matters: Russia is a signatory to the 1967 Outer Space Treaty, which bans weapons of mass destruction from orbit.

Source: BBC

3. Russia-Ukraine war spreads beyond Ukrainian borders

Three Russian cruise missiles struck a rural part of southeastern Poland, about 30 miles from the Ukrainian border, with Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk calling it an "intentional provocation". On Saturday, a sanctioned Russian cargo ship in the Baltic Sea was hit by what is suspected to have been a Ukrainian maritime drone. On Sunday, Ukraine took credit for sinking a Russian warship docked in the Libyan port of Benghazi, thousands of miles from the front line, with Libyan authorities condemning the attack and reflecting broader concerns the conflict could spread far beyond Europe.

Why it matters: NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte acknowledged the danger of cruise missile incursions but urged that Polish authorities finish their investigations to establish whether they were intentional, while also appealing for continued public support for Ukraine.

Source: NPR

4. US to blacklist Singapore AI firm Megaspeed

The US Department of Commerce announced it planned to add Megaspeed Ltd, a Singaporean AI company, to a list of entities blacklisted from buying or selling goods in the US. The company became prominent after a former Megaspeed executive, Alice Huang, was alleged by US intelligence to be working as an asset for Beijing while having an affair with Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, raising concerns that Megaspeed was funnelling Nvidia's advanced AI chips to China. The commerce department said it had been investigating Megaspeed for almost two years over suspected violations of US export controls. Megaspeed's subsidiary Speedmatrix has also been added to the Commerce blacklist.

Why it matters: China has been blocked from buying such Nvidia technology since 2022, and the blacklisting reflects heightened concern that advanced AI chips were reaching Chinese companies such as Alibaba, reported to be one of Megaspeed's largest customers.

Source: BBC

5. Iraq seizes Kurdish oilfields in budget dispute

Iraq's Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi has ordered the federal Ministry of Oil to manage oilfields in areas under Kurdish security control, covering Diyala, Kirkuk, Salahuddin and Nineveh provinces. Al-Zaidi also ordered the border crossings authority to take over all crossings in the Kurdish region and directed that crude oil exports be approved only through the federal Oil Ministry. The prime minister removed the head of the border crossings authority and suspended North Oil Company's director general and his deputies pending investigation. A dispute over Kurdish authorities selling oil without Baghdad's approval had halted pipeline shipments to Turkiye for more than two years before the sides agreed in September 2025 to restart them.

Why it matters: Al Jazeera's reporter in Baghdad said the development might exacerbate tensions and disputes over control of oil and the sharing of budgetary resources between the national government and the autonomous regional government.

Source: Al Jazeera

Closer to Home

NHS Scotland's "revolving door" of managers under fire

Following the NHS Grampian crisis, questions are being raised about a pattern in which senior managers who presided over failures at one Scottish health board are moved to leadership roles at others. Health Secretary Angela Constance has sent in a team of four expert leaders to help drive change at NHS Grampian, but three of them previously held senior positions at boards that also faced serious criticism. The team is led by Jim Crombie of NHS Lothian, who in 2018 was forced to apologise after an investigation found a culture of "bullying and harassment" at that board. Lawyer Aamer Anwar, representing a whistleblower at NHS Grampian, likened the situation to "shuffling of deckchairs on the sinking Titanic, where you have one individual, who hasn't done a good job in one place, is then shuffled off to another place."

Source: BBC
