1. Finland and Norway hold rare call with Putin

The leaders of Finland and Norway held a joint call with Vladimir Putin, described as rare contact between the Russian president and European leaders. Stubb and Store are the first European leaders to contact Putin since French President Emmanuel Macron's conversation with him in July 2025.

Why it matters: The first trilateral US-Ukraine-Russia talks since the start of the year are tentatively planned for early November in Abu Dhabi, and Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said Europe's position on those upcoming talks is "moderately optimistic".

Source: BBC

2. Ukraine ceasefire expands across Kherson region

A ceasefire that began to get food and medicine to the Russian-occupied city of Oleshky has been extended across the wider southern region of Kherson, with no breaches reported. US First Lady Melania Trump, who helped broker the ceasefire along with Pope Leo XIV, said on Tuesday the area had expanded to provide "safe passage" for humanitarian convoys. An estimated 1,700 to 2,000 people remain in Oleshky itself, with a few thousand more in surrounding areas, many having resorted to cooking garden weeds to survive. A key official in President Zelensky's parliamentary party, David Arakhamia, said the truce would continue until 22:59 Ukrainian time on Sunday 1 November and described it as the "largest de-escalation since 2022".

Source: BBC

3. Apple replaces faulty iPhone Duo screens

Apple has acknowledged that some of its iPhone Duo handsets have developed problems with their screens, just days after the highly-anticipated smartphone went on sale. Photos posted on social media show lines appearing on the screens of the company's first ever foldable device, which has a starting price of £1,999 ($2,668) in the UK, while some owners also reported cracks along the screen's centre crease and a crunching noise when opening the device. Apple said it was aware of "an issue impacting the inner display on a small number" of the phones and urged affected users to contact the company for a replacement. Technology analyst Francisco Jeronimo of IDC said the reports showed "that even a company with Apple's engineering resources and supply chain discipline faces a steep learning curve in foldables", and warned that with 28 more markets launching on 30 October, Apple needs to clarify its warranty position quickly.

Source: BBC

4. Anthropic IPO reveals explosive but costly growth

Anthropic has filed to go public on the US stock market, revealing for the first time the AI research lab's finances in a filing known as an S-1. The five-year-old firm, founded in 2021, brought in $16.3bn in revenue but lost $80bn in just the first half of 2026, though much of that was due to a $72.4bn one-off charge; in 2025, the company brought in $4.585bn and lost roughly $33.9bn. Securing computing power accounts for the vast majority of costs, with the company spending nearly $9bn on AI infrastructure in the first half of this year, up from $2.3bn in the same period last year. The company has been valued between $1.5 and $2tn by some investors but did not list a target valuation in its S-1.

Source: The Guardian

5. UBS profits jump 27% on wealth inflows

UBS reported a pre-tax profit of almost $3.6 billion in the third quarter, up 27% year over year and broadly in line with consensus forecasts, with net profit of $2.7 billion. Strong net inflows into its global wealth management business supported performance, with net new assets reaching $55.1 billion by the end of the quarter. The bank said the integration of Credit Suisse is "firmly on track" to be completed by year-end, delivering $13.7 billion in cumulative cost savings, above earlier expectations of $13.5 billion.

Why it matters: The Swiss parliament's upper house voted last month to require UBS to hold 90% of CET1 capital - about $16 billion in extra shares - as collateral for its foreign subsidiaries, up from the current level of around 45%.

Source: CNBC

Closer to Home

Chancellor scraps gas standing charges for 23m homes

Chancellor John Healey is abolishing standing charges on gas bills for 23 million homes, calling the fixed daily fee "unfair and outdated" ahead of his budget statement on Wednesday. He will ask Ofgem, the energy regulator for Great Britain, to remove the charges - which cost the typical household £108 a year - next year or as soon as possible after. The Treasury said the move could save 60% of account-holding households an average of £45 off their annual bill. The announcement follows the government's VAT cut on electricity bills that came into effect at the beginning of this month.

Source: The Guardian

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Vaping Products Duty at 2.20 per 10ml from 1 October 2026

Vaping Products Duty came into force on 1 October 2026, charged at 2.20 per 10ml of vaping liquid, payable by manufacturers, importers and warehousekeepers approved by HMRC. A six-month grace period allows wholesalers and retailers to sell existing unstamped, non-duty-liable stock until 31 March 2027; from 1 April 2027 all vaping products sold in the UK must carry a valid vaping duty stamp. Tobacco duty rates also increased on 1 October 2026 by a one-off pro-rata amount of 2.20 per 100 cigarettes or per 50 grams of tobacco, in addition to the standard tobacco duty escalator.

Source: HMRC

Mandatory iXBRL software filing for Companies House from April 2028

From April 2028, all UK registered companies must file accounts in Inline eXtensible Business Reporting Language (iXBRL) format using commercial software; web and paper-based filing systems will close for accounts filings from that date. The government has simultaneously paused the requirement for small companies and micro-entities to file profit and loss accounts with Companies House from April 2028, retaining the option to file abridged accounts and to fillet profit and loss information.

Source: Companies House
