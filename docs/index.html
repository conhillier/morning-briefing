1. UK energy bills to soar 26% in January

Energy bills will rise by 26% for millions of households in January after a surge in gas prices sparked by the Iran war. Regulator Ofgem said typical bills will increase by about £37 a month in the first three months of the new year, pushing annual bills from £1,723 to £2,166 if sustained for a year. The government will pay millions of households £100 from 4 January to help cover winter costs, on top of an existing VAT cut on electricity. Ofgem said the rise had been driven entirely by higher wholesale costs, with wholesale gas prices paid by suppliers up 33% in the last three months.

Source: BBC

2. BBC reveals 490,000 Russian war deaths

At least 490,334 Russian soldiers have died since the start of the war in Ukraine in 2022, according to Russian government statistics analysed by the BBC. The leaked Rosstat data came via a hack by Belarusian group Cyber Partisans BY and Ukraine's Silent Crow, giving BBC Russian access to more than 11 million death records from 2021 to September 2026. The records show that 65% of 18 to 24 year-olds in Russia who lost their lives in 2025 died in the war, and that some 20,000 Russian soldiers died fighting for control of 10 Ukrainian villages whose combined pre-war population was just 12,000. Military experts estimate the true death toll could range from 527,295 to 644,471.

Why it matters: The data reveal the full human cost of a war Vladimir Putin had assumed would last only days but is now almost five years old, with men from Russia's poorest eastern regions 20-25 times more likely to die than residents of Moscow and St Petersburg.

Source: BBC

3. US to announce sweeping new tariffs today

The US is preparing to announce plans for new tariffs on several countries as early as Wednesday, with China, the European Union and Japan among at least 14 jurisdictions affected. The move follows the White House's "Section 301" investigation, launched in March after the US Supreme Court ruled that President Trump's Liberation Day tariffs were illegal. Countries will face different import tax rates depending on deals previously struck with Trump. The announcement is likely to increase tensions between the US and China, whose interim agreement is set to expire on 10 January.

Why it matters: The Trump administration's tariff policy has been criticised for putting up prices for American consumers and damaging the global economy.

Source: BBC

4. Huawei convicted on 10 counts in US trial

Huawei has been found guilty of conspiracy to steal trade secrets and bank fraud, but not guilty of the more serious overarching racketeering charge in a landmark US case. Jurors in a Brooklyn court found Huawei guilty of 10 out of 12 counts, which included conspiracy to violate US sanctions.

Source: BBC

5. China investigates Shanghai party chief Chen Jining

Chinese Communist Party authorities are investigating Shanghai party secretary Chen Jining for suspected violations of party discipline, China's anti-corruption watchdog announced Tuesday. Chen, a Politburo member, is under investigation by the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection, though the brief announcement provided no further details. Analyst Mingda Qiu of Eurasia Group called it "the fourth Politburo member to fall, and the fourth sitting member brought down in the current cycle," describing the pattern as "unprecedented." Chen has served as Shanghai party secretary since 2022 and previously headed the Ministry of Environmental Protection.

Source: NPR

Closer to Home

NHS Grampian crisis becomes Swinney's gravest political test

The NHS Grampian scandal has deepened into what Scotland political editor Glenn Campbell describes as potentially the most dangerous political moment for First Minister John Swinney since he took office in 2024. Surgeon-turned-whistleblower Dr Mohammed Habib released a previously unpublished 2022 review of 45 patients in a specialist liver and pancreatic unit, finding that 31 had died and 19 were considered to have died of avoidable consequences of surgery or intervention - findings that were not made public by NHS Grampian or the Scottish government. Dr Habib said he wrote to the first minister on three occasions, but Swinney's office did not make him personally aware of the correspondence. Health Secretary Angela Constance has since announced an independent review to be led by an experienced King's Counsel, likely from outside Scotland, with findings to be published in full.

Source: BBC
