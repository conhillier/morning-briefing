1. Anthropic revenue surges 45% before IPO

Anthropic saw its revenue surge by 45% to $16.8bn in the three months to the end of September, figures revealed in a briefing for prospective investors on Wednesday as it lays the groundwork for a stock market listing expected before the end of the year. The company also reportedly told investors it had made a profit for the second quarter in a row. Anthropic's revenue has risen in successive quarters this year, from $4.7bn in the first three months to $11.6bn the following period.

Why it matters: The revenue figures offer an important gauge of the AI industry's growth and ability to finance the infrastructure needed to develop the technology, alongside those of rival OpenAI.

Source: BBC

2. AI sell-off deepens across Asian markets

Tech stocks fell sharply in Asia on Thursday, with shares in Japanese tech investment firm Softbank falling by more than 9%, Samsung falling by 9.4% and SK Hynix dropping 10.8%. Trading on South Korea's Kospi and Kosdaq was temporarily halted after falling by more than 8%, while Japan's Nikkei 225 fell by over 5%. The falls deepened after US inflation data released on Wednesday showed price rises accelerated by more than expected in September, raising concerns the US Federal Reserve is more likely to put up the cost of borrowing.

Why it matters: Investors are concerned that higher inflation will mean the US Federal Reserve is more likely to put up the cost of borrowing, adding further pressure to already rattled tech markets.

Source: BBC

3. TSMC posts sixth straight record profit

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co reported a record third-quarter net profit of NT$763.1 billion, up 68.7% from a year earlier, beating analyst estimates. The company's third-quarter operating margin came in at 58.7%, exceeding the upper end of its guidance range of 58%. High-performance computing accounted for the largest share of revenue at 68%, with 3-nanometer chips making up the biggest slice of revenue by technology at 33%.

Why it matters: The result showcases the pricing power enjoyed by TSMC as the AI boom fuels demand for semiconductors, even as broader tech stocks fell sharply this week.

Source: CNBC

4. Nvidia donates $250m to UK AI safety research

The $5.5tn US chipmaker Nvidia is donating $250m worth of computing power to the UK to support AI safety research, spanning five years. Nvidia said the donation would "support agentic safety and security research across the UK", with a proportion going to the British Open-Ended Learning and Discovery Lab, the Science of Fundamental AI Research Lab - run by Edinburgh, Cambridge, Oxford and University College London - and Imperial College London. The computing power will be delivered via UK-based datacentres using Nvidia chips.

Why it matters: The donation comes as the UK government confirmed its AI Security Institute will be able to examine cutting-edge AI models from companies such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Google before they are released.

Source: The Guardian

5. UK economy grows 0.1% in August

The UK economy grew by 0.1% in August, according to the Office for National Statistics, following growth of 0.4% in July. City economists had forecast the economy would shrink by 0.1%.

Why it matters: The figures arrive less than a fortnight before Chancellor John Healey's Budget on 28 October, with the global economy rattled by the war in the Middle East and a surge in energy costs hitting British households and businesses.

Source: The Guardian

Swinney: UK not safe from Russian threat

First Minister John Swinney said he does not have confidence the UK is safe from potential Russian threats, saying the UK government did not have a sufficient "presence" in vulnerable areas off Scotland's coast. Speaking to BBC Scotland News ahead of the SNP's annual conference in Aberdeen, Swinney said he was "very worried" by security fears highlighted by MI5 chief Sir Ken McCallum, who named Russia as "aggressor number one".

Source: BBC
