1. Paraguay president impeached, under house arrest

Paraguay's President Santiago Peña was impeached and placed under house arrest on Friday, minutes after lawmakers including from his own party unanimously voted to remove him from office. The vote followed the collapse of Ueno Bank, which left more than half the country's population without access to their accounts. Peña's impeachment passed with 44 out of 45 senators voting in favour, and he now faces questioning on Saturday over allegations of breach of trust, influence peddling, and misappropriation of funds. His deputy, Pedro Alliana, has been sworn in as interim president and promised those affected by the collapse would be "fully compensated".

Source: BBC

2. Kenyan cult leader jailed 200 years for 191 murders

Pastor Paul Mackenzie, leader of the Good News International Church, was sentenced to 200 years in prison by the High Court in Kenya's coastal city of Mombasa on Friday. He was convicted of murdering 190 children and one adult after ordering his followers to starve themselves to death in order to meet Jesus. Justice Diana Kavedza said the killings were "cold and calculated" and that Mackenzie bore the "primary criminal culpability" for relocating his followers to a remote location and ordering them to fast to death. Mackenzie's assistant pastor, Smart Mwakalama, was sentenced to 160 years in prison.

Why it matters: In 2023, more than 400 bodies were exhumed from shallow graves on the 800-acre Chakama Ranch in coastal Kilifi County; Mackenzie is also facing terrorism and manslaughter charges for the more than 200 other victims.

Source: NPR

3. Europe hit by third wave of airline bomb threats

European airlines were struck by a third wave of bomb threats on Friday, with multiple flights to and from Lisbon, Rome, Prague and Sofia targeted. Three planes were escorted by Hungarian fighter jets, and a WizzAir flight from Prague to Rome was diverted to Venice where passengers were evacuated. The EU's transportation commissioner, Apostolos Tzitzikostas, confirmed there were 11 more threats on Friday, and said security experts would meet in Brussels next week to discuss measures. Several media organisations were also evacuated, including Ireland's public broadcaster RTÉ, the Irish Independent, NewstalkFM and TodayFM, though no concrete link between the airline and broadcaster threats has been established.

Why it matters: The Irish government warned it is a "likely coordinated campaign of disruption," and Irish Prime Minister Micheál Martin described the situation as "worrying," noting Ireland is scheduled to host two high-profile EU meetings in Dublin next week.

Source: BBC

4. Global stocks plunge on AI regulation fears

Global stock markets tumbled after investors sold tech shares on fears that the US midterm election results would slow the development of artificial intelligence. The tech-heavy Nasdaq sank 5.5% on Thursday, with the so-called magnificent seven tech companies including Apple, Nvidia and Tesla shedding a trillion dollars in value in a single trading day, with Nvidia shares sliding more than 9%. The sell-off spread to Asia on Friday, with Japan's Nikkei closing down 6% and South Korea's Kospi plunging more than 8% at one point, triggering a circuit breaker. The UK's FTSE 100 fell nearly 2% while Germany's Dax was down 2.4%.

Why it matters: Democrats won both the US Senate and House of Representatives in the midterms, taking control of Congress, and analysts say their opposition to data centres could slow the pace of AI development; the Nasdaq has now entered what market watchers call correction territory.

Source: BBC

5. OpenAI raises $11bn at $1.25 trillion valuation

OpenAI has reportedly raised $11 billion in a new funding round that values the AI lab at $1.25 trillion, according to Bloomberg. The $11 billion is part of a larger round that has not yet closed and could total about $20 billion, a source told Bloomberg. This falls well short of the $30 billion OpenAI was reportedly seeking when it began exploring the round in late September. The news comes as Bloomberg also reported that potential buyers of shares in the IPO of OpenAI's chief competitor, Anthropic, are eyeing a valuation as low as $1.1 trillion, compared with the $2 trillion investors were expecting roughly a month ago.

Why it matters: The Democratic landslide in the US midterms and overwhelmingly negative voter sentiment around data centres has caused jitters throughout the AI market, with many anticipating potential regulatory challenges in the future.

Source: TechCrunch

Closer to Home

M8 closures begin in Glasgow this weekend

Part of the M8 motorway will close in both directions in Glasgow between Junction 19 (Anderston) and Junction 24 (Helen Street) over the next two weekends to allow footbridges to be lifted back into place after refurbishment work. The first closure runs from 18:00 on Saturday until 13:00 on Sunday, with drivers diverted through Glasgow's south side via Paisley Road West, Paisley Road, Helen Street and Dumbreck Road. Engineering company Amey said road users are "strongly advised to avoid the area" during the closures. Similar works in 2024 led to delays of up to an hour, and officials warned similar disruption is possible; emergency vehicles will not be able to pass through the closure area.

Source: BBC
