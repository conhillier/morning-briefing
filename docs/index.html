1. Trump waives Russia sanctions, Zelensky condemns move

President Donald Trump signed an executive order late Friday waiving key parts of new U.S. sanctions on Russia, coming two days before the law's implementation deadline. The legislation, the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026, had required Trump to increase duties on all Russian goods by up to 500% and impose tariffs of up to 100% on top purchasers of Russian crude oil or gas. Zelensky posted on X: "The decision to undo all of this, to block all of it, seems wrong to me.

Why it matters: Zelensky said he expects further sanctions relief to follow, warning: "Sanctions will be lifted after gas. They will be lifted on something else. Then this will come to the banking system, certain individuals, chemical industry."

Source: CNBC

2. France erupts: 1,200 arrested in protest violence

Violence broke out across France on Saturday during the latest day of demonstrations against hardship in schools and the cost of living, with at least 1,200 people arrested. The French government described events as an "escalation in violence" alongside an estimated 550,000 taking to the streets. Buildings were set alight in the capital and other areas, including in Limoges where a large fire in the town hall caused the roof to partly collapse, while looting broke out in several cities. Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu said 150 police officers were injured in the unrest.

Why it matters: Lecornu warned that some groups were using the protests as a "warm-up" for the presidential elections, adding: "No-one will impose their political agenda through violence."

Source: BBC

3. Ukrainian drones strike St Petersburg port

Video posted on social media shows multiple explosions and fires around the port of St Petersburg, Russia after a suspected Ukrainian drone swarm. Witnesses say an oil terminal and data centre were hit in the attack.

Source: Al Jazeera

4. Zelensky urges allies to back stalled Russia sanctions

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky urged supporters Saturday to speak out in defence of new U.S. sanctions against Russia, hours after Trump signed an executive order pausing the restrictions. Russian attacks overnight killed at least four people, including an ambulance driver, and wounded dozens, while Ukrainian drones killed a woman in a Russian border region and disrupted flights at Moscow's airports. Ukraine's air force said Russian forces launched four cruise missiles and 332 attack drones overnight, with all missiles and 289 of the drones suppressed or destroyed. Zelensky also said Moscow had carried out a massive strike on Ukraine's energy infrastructure, targeting more than 15 sites across the country.

Why it matters: Zelensky said blocking the sanctions "would encourage Russia to continue killing and do nothing to bring the war to an end," and warned that limiting Ukraine's ability to retaliate against Russian attacks would not bring peace.

Source: NPR

5. Bank of England chief walks out of IMF meeting

Bank of England governor Andrew Bailey joined finance leaders from France, Germany, and a number of Scandinavian and Baltic countries in walking out of an IMF meeting in Bangkok when Russian finance minister Anton Siluanov addressed the group. The president of the German Bundesbank, Joachim Nagel, said nearly half of all delegates took part in the joint departure. One European diplomat told Reuters: "The Ukraine war is existential for us in Europe. We cannot normalise relations with Russia." The US, however, was supportive of Russia's presence, with treasury undersecretary Erin Browne telling Reuters that Washington supported Russia's attendance despite European concerns.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Search narrows for two missing Lewis fishermen

Police have identified an "area of interest" in the search for two fishermen whose boat went missing off north Lewis in the Western Isles on Tuesday. The Marine Accident Investigation Branch has put an exclusion zone in place for divers in an area between the Butt of Lewis and Port of Ness, though vessels can still operate there. Douglas Craigie's body was found on Wednesday - the day before his 50th birthday - but Norman Murray, 40, and Murdo Morrison, 23, remain missing along with their boat, the Kilda. HM Coastguard stood down its operations on Thursday evening, pending any new information.

Source: BBC
