1. Huang summoned to Congress after Nvidia exit

Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has been summoned to testify before a Senate committee about his alleged affair with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy. Senate Banking Committee Chairman Tim Scott requested that Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick appear at a public hearing on December 3, threatening subpoenas if they refuse. Three House committees have separately asked Huang and Nvidia General Counsel Tim Teter to appear for hearings on December 1 and 2. Nvidia's market value has fallen below $4 trillion for the first time since July 2025 amid the scandal.

Why it matters: Lawmakers say "there are clear national security concerns that need to be addressed immediately," with scrutiny also falling on whether Nvidia's advanced semiconductor exports to China received proper oversight.

Source: Al Jazeera

2. Nvidia heads for longest losing streak since 2012

Nvidia shares are down 11% this week as of Thursday's close, putting the stock on course for its sixth straight weekly decline - the longest losing streak since 2012. The chip giant has lost roughly $1.6 trillion in market cap since the slide began in early October, a fall of 29% over that stretch. Huang, who co-founded Nvidia and led it for 33 years, resigned on Thursday after two separate extramarital affairs were made public, with the board naming finance chief Colette Kress as interim CEO. Analysts at D.A. Davidson lowered their price target to $200 from $300, while Oppenheimer warned that "the departure of Mr. Huang could, in our view, translate to a lingering share overhang".

Why it matters: Nvidia is scheduled to report quarterly results on Tuesday, with analysts projecting 91% year-over-year revenue growth to $108.9 billion, but Oppenheimer said it expects "fundamentals to take a backseat near term".

Source: CNBC

3. Zelensky welcomes Europe joining Ukraine peace talks

Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelensky has welcomed a pledge from US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff to include European Union delegates in the next round of peace talks with Russia. The envoys met Zelensky in Dublin on Thursday evening after earlier meeting Vladimir Putin in Moscow, where Putin said Russia's position on the war and its demand for territory has not changed. Thursday also saw Russia and Ukraine agree to another prisoner exchange, described by Kushner as the "largest swap yet" since Russia launched its full-scale invasion in 2022, with Kushner saying it would return "thousands of people to their families". Putin told the US envoys he was willing to "give it a try" on European participation, though Moscow has previously been dismissive of European involvement.

Why it matters: Approximately one-fifth of Ukraine remains under control of Russian forces, and Zelensky said bringing European delegates into the talks would ensure the EU has a "voice" in negotiations over the continent's longer-term security arrangements.

Source: BBC

4. Ukrainian drone hits Russian warship in Baltic

Finnish officials have confirmed that a Russian warship, the Naro-Fominsk, was attacked in the Gulf of Finland on Wednesday in what is thought to be the first time Ukrainian drones have attacked a Russian ship in an area of the Baltic Sea open to civilian maritime traffic. Ukraine's SBU security service said it targeted the ship as part of a broader drone operation against Russia's Ust-Luga oil export depot on the Baltic coast. Estonia's defence minister said the ship was hit at about 05:30 and that "smoke came from the ship afterwards" before it headed back towards St Petersburg. The extent of casualties or damage sustained remains unclear.

Why it matters: Finland's Foreign Minister said the situation in the Baltic Sea remained stable and safe with no threat to marine traffic, but defence officials stressed to Kyiv that the Gulf is a narrow waterway used by oil tankers, civilian vessels, and subsea data cables.

Source: BBC

5. North Korea stole $400m in fake video call scam

State-sponsored hackers from North Korea have taken more than $400 million in cryptocurrency from over 900 victims in the last 18 months using a scheme the FBI calls FaceStealer, according to US authorities. The hackers use fake or hijacked social media accounts to invite victims into video calls hosted over imposter versions of Zoom, Microsoft Teams, and Google Meet, then play pre-recorded videos of people the target knows to simulate a legitimate call. A message then prompts the target to click a link to "fix" apparent audio problems, which downloads malware giving hackers access to contact lists and apps. The FBI has described FaceStealer as "a highly effective social engineering scheme capable of deceiving even the most experienced technical and security conscious computer user".

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

76,000 sewage spills recorded across Scotland

BBC analysis of new monitoring data shows more than 76,000 sewage spills were recorded after 1,009 new sewer monitors were installed in 2024, increasing the proportion of the Scottish Water network being monitored from 6% to 30%. Glasgow's River Clyde was one of the most impacted waterways, receiving almost 6,150 spills - the equivalent of 17 a day - with the Clyde Estuary recording the longest duration of spills at more than 25,000 hours. Scottish Water said the vast majority of the spills were not harmful to the environment, and that increased monitoring had improved understanding of the sewer network. However, only just under 12% of overflows are legally required to report their data to the regulator, the Scottish Environment Protection Agency.

Source: BBC

The MPC voted 8-1 on 4 November 2026 to increase Bank Rate by 0.25 percentage points to 4%; one dissenting member preferred a 0.5 percentage point rise to 4.25%.
