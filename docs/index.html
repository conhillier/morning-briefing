1. Tibet earthquake kills 16, injures dozens

A magnitude 7.0 earthquake struck Baqen County in the Nagqu region of Tibet on Thursday night, killing at least 16 people and injuring 65 others, including 11 with severe injuries. Authorities declared an emergency and deployed rescue teams to the high-altitude affected area, which sits at around 4,800 metres. More than 12,400 people have been relocated, and 339 homes have collapsed, though power was restored in some areas by Friday morning. Chinese Premier Li Qiang called for urgent rescue operations and the government allocated 50 million yuan (£5.6m, $7.5m) for disaster relief.

Why it matters: Rockfall has closed roads in the region, making it more difficult for crews to reach impacted high-altitude areas, and Chinese state media reported more than 400 aftershocks since the quake occurred.

Source: BBC

2. Trump says Musk restored Ukraine Starlink access

US President Donald Trump said Elon Musk has restored Ukraine's Starlink internet access after restrictions hampered Kyiv's armed forces. Trump refused to be drawn on reports he called Musk to ask him to lift restrictions, but said: "Bottom line, he put it back." Earlier on Thursday, Ukrainian President Zelensky said the speed of the system "has not been restored to 400 [km/h; 250mph] yet" and was still awaiting a response from US Secretary of State Marco Rubio. The Wall Street Journal reported the system was working more normally on Thursday, shortly after a Trump-Musk conversation, though BBC News has not confirmed the report.

Why it matters: The speed limit had made Ukraine's offensive drones and interceptors powerless against Russian attacks, meaning full restoration is critical to Kyiv's battlefield capabilities.

Source: BBC

3. US-Israel planning early November Iran strike

The US and Israel are preparing plans for a possible attack on Iran in early November, after the US midterm elections, according to a person briefed on the war planning who spoke to NPR on condition of anonymity. Israel had initially planned to strike Iran in mid-October, about two weeks before Israeli elections on October 27, but President Trump objected. Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told Iranian state media: "We are just as prepared for diplomacy as we are for war," adding that "any scenario is conceivable, including the possibility of an attack by the U.S. and Israel." Iranian state media also quoted a security source warning that if Iranian officials or civilian infrastructure were attacked, Iran would retaliate by targeting US interests in the region.

Why it matters: The IMF warns that if the US-Israeli war with Iran continues and energy supply disruptions persist, the price of oil will average $130 a barrel in 2027, with Iraq, Kuwait and Qatar all expected to experience contractions of around 16% to their economies this year.

Source: NPR

4. PHP cyber flaw sparks global scramble

Governments and organisations worldwide are still scrambling to patch critical vulnerabilities in PHP, the programming language that powers 70% of the world's websites, after the flaws were disclosed on Wednesday. The flaws, dubbed "php2shell", were inherent in the language itself and affected versions released over the last decade; under the right conditions an unauthenticated attacker could remotely execute code on affected servers. Cloudflare boss Matthew Prince wrote that the company saw "thousands" of exploit attempts in the 10 hours before public announcement, rising to "millions" within two hours of disclosure. The UK government said 32 government services were unavailable and four were partially unavailable, though no large public-facing critical services were affected.

Source: BBC

5. Barclays cuts 5,000 jobs, cites AI

Barclays is cutting around 5,000 staff, amounting to roughly 5% of its global workforce, as it introduces more artificial intelligence into its work processes. Some 1,000 posts have already been closed, with a further 4,000 cuts expected by the end of this year; around 60% of the cuts will fall within support functions, including among developers. The bank said the changes were the result of an ongoing modernisation plan, with AI being the "icing on the cake" of its strategy. Barclays also reported a £2.3bn profit in the three months to September, higher than the same period a year ago but slightly below forecast.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Two storms to batter Scotland this weekend

Storm Austen is sweeping across Scotland today with the Met Office issuing a yellow warning for rain in central and eastern areas from 06:00 on Friday until midnight, with forecasters warning some places could see 60-80mm of rain with a risk of flooding and travel disruption. Network Rail Scotland has slowed trains and imposed speed restrictions on parts of the West Coast Main Line, East Coast Main Line, and routes across southern and eastern Scotland. Storm Boelo will follow on Saturday with a yellow wind warning for the whole of mainland Scotland, the Western Isles and Orkney from 18:00 Saturday until 21:00 Sunday, with gusts of 50-65mph widely forecast and up to 80mph in exposed locations. Power cuts are possible, which could also affect mobile phone coverage, and there is a small risk of damage to buildings including roof tiles being blown off.

Source: BBC
