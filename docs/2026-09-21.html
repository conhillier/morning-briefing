1. US-China agree AI safety dialogue before summit

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent called his meeting with Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng "successful" ahead of the September 24 Trump-Xi summit in Washington.

Why it matters: The AI notification mechanism and trade discussions lay groundwork for Trump and Xi's high-stakes summit, while a key US-China trade truce covering suspended reciprocal tariffs is scheduled to expire on November 10.

Source: CNBC

2. Ukraine fires over 1,000 drones at Russia

Ukrainian forces fired more than 1,000 drones at Russia overnight, including hundreds launched toward Moscow, in what Moscow's mayor described as the "largest ever" attack on the Russian capital. The attack across the wider Moscow region killed two people and wounded 20, with the dead identified as a 74-year-old man and a 44-year-old woman.

Why it matters: Kyiv is aiming to make the Russian public feel the war's consequences and pressure President Putin into negotiating a peace settlement, though Putin has so far shown no signs he intends to stop the invasion.

Source: NPR

3. Iran warns US over plans to resume strikes

Iran's central military command, the Khatam al-Anbiya Central Headquarters, said on Sunday it had received information that the United States is preparing to resume strikes on Iran. Tehran warned that any renewed US assault would bring retaliation against American bases and interests in the Middle East, and that states allowing their territory to support US attacks would be "considered complicit" and could face an Iranian response. President Trump told Fox News on Sunday that he was in a "deciding mode" over Iran, with options including further military action, economic pressure or a deal, and said he would be open to meeting Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian at the UN General Assembly in New York this week. Shipping data showed 12 commodity vessels crossed the Strait of Hormuz over the weekend, down from 35 the previous weekend.

Why it matters: The Strait of Hormuz disruption is already affecting global energy shipments, with Qatar mediating between the two sides and Doha having heard assurances from US officials that Washington wanted an agreement.

Source: Al Jazeera

4. Nvidia boss dismisses AI extinction fears

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has rejected warnings that AI could lead to humanity's extinction by 2030 as "doomsday narratives", saying "There is 0% chance that's going to be the end of the world" in an interview with CBS News. Huang said predictions of an extinction-level event caused by AI were "not grounded in science" and called scaremongering "unnecessary" and "irresponsible." His remarks followed social media claims by former Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon that AI developers believe the technology "could kill us all by the end of the decade," which prompted Anthropic boss Dario Amodei and OpenAI's Sam Altman to urge a slowdown in AI development. Huang suggested AI firms calling for a "slow down" could be seeking liability protections, saying: "They're actually not asking for more laws. They're asking to be relieved of the laws we do have."

Source: BBC

5. Meta challenges UK online safety regulator

Meta has launched a fresh legal challenge against Britain's media regulator Ofcom in an appeal against placing WhatsApp and Instagram in a category that subjects them to additional duties, including greater transparency, more user controls, and increased recording of illegal content. Lawyers for Meta served notice during the week, with a spokesperson saying the company was "challenging specific aspects of implementation" and that these were "not challenges to the law itself, but how Ofcom has interpreted it." Meta had previously launched a legal challenge in London in May, claiming Ofcom's methodology for calculating charges was flawed and should not be based on a company's global revenue. Breaches of the Online Safety Act can be punished by fines of up to 10% of qualifying worldwide revenue or £18m, whichever is higher.

Why it matters: A senior Ofcom director warned the regulator was "operating in a highly litigious environment," with TikTok and X also taking legal action, potentially slowing down implementation of the Online Safety Act.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Dundee University lecturers begin five-week strike

Lecturers at the University of Dundee began an unprecedented five-week strike today, the first day of the new term, in a dispute over the threat of compulsory redundancies. The latest wave of cuts proposed closing 187 posts, reduced to 154 by August, but UCU bosses said 149 voluntary redundancy applications should now rule out the need for compulsory redundancies.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Bank Rate held at 3.75%, QT pace set

The MPC voted 6-3 to maintain Bank Rate at 3.75% at its meeting ending 16 September 2026, with three members preferring an increase to 4%. The Committee also voted unanimously to reduce the stock of UK government bond purchases to zero via a multi-year plan, unwinding at an annual average pace of 46 billion pounds by end of 2034 through annual sales of 20 billion pounds alongside maturing gilts. UK CPI inflation rose to 3.1% in August and the Committee judged risks to the inflation outlook are tilted to the upside.

Source: Bank of England
