1. US envoys fly to Moscow after Abu Dhabi talks

US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff arrived in Moscow on Thursday morning for talks with Russian President Vladimir Putin, accompanied from the plane by Putin's special envoy Kirill Dmitriev. The visit follows two days of trilateral peace talks in Abu Dhabi that concluded on Wednesday with agreement on a major prisoner exchange but no breakthrough on ending the nearly five-year war. The US said differences remained on major issues including territory, but some progress had been made on post-war security guarantees. After Moscow, Kushner and Witkoff are scheduled to meet Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Dublin on Friday.

Why it matters: One goal of the Moscow visit is for the US team to persuade Putin to allow European countries into the negotiation process, as Ukraine and the US want European partners involved while Moscow remains adamant about talks without them.

Source: Al Jazeera

2. Abu Dhabi talks end with prisoner exchange deal

Russia and Ukraine agreed to a large exchange of prisoners of war as two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi concluded on Wednesday with no major breakthrough on ending the conflict. Jared Kushner said the sides had agreed to "the biggest humanitarian release of prisoners," with final numbers to be confirmed, while Russian delegation head Vladimir Medinsky confirmed the agreement was "in principle." Alexander Zorin, a deputy chief of the General Staff's information department, said it would be one of the biggest such swaps after the 1,000 soldiers returned home last year, and that the exchange could take place in the next two or three weeks. The talks lasted just under two hours on Wednesday, after a four-hour session the previous day.

Why it matters: Kushner posted that the POW exchange "will reunite families, build trust and create momentum toward peace," though the US acknowledged there is currently no trust between the two sides.

Source: NPR

3. Nvidia board weighs removing CEO Jensen Huang

Nvidia's board was reportedly meeting on Wednesday evening to decide whether CEO Jensen Huang should remain with the company, after its stocks plummeted following US media reports of a second alleged extramarital affair. Huang had already admitted earlier this week to being "unfaithful" to his wife and apologised for hurting her and their children. The first affair involved Alice Huang, a woman US intelligence officials believed was recruited to spy by China's top intelligence agency; a New York Times report then revealed a second alleged relationship with a Nvidia executive who reports directly to him. Nvidia has 11 directors on its board including Huang, and the CEO can be removed with or without cause by a majority vote of directors.

Why it matters: Nvidia is at the heart of the US and China AI race, and the affair has sparked national security fears among American lawmakers who worry that if China gains the upper hand in AI it will supercharge the country's military and surveillance capabilities.

Source: BBC

4. UK economy grows fastest in over two years

The UK economy grew by 0.7% in the three months to September, its fastest rate for more than two years, according to official figures. Strong growth in the services sector helped drive the expansion, with computer programming registering its fastest growth for the past three years, helped by AI-linked spending. The latest figures put the UK on course to be the second fastest growing economy across the major G7 countries. However, PwC senior economist Jake Finney cautioned that growth was likely to slow over the coming months "unless energy markets stabilise," with a sharp rise in energy bills forecast for January.

Why it matters: Despite the strong headline figure, the ONS chief economist noted that households may not feel better off as inflation and wages figures have a more direct impact on people.

Source: BBC

5. Israel strikes Nasser Hospital, killing Palestinians

At least five Palestinians were killed and twenty others injured after an Israeli airstrike struck a building at Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis in southern Gaza on Thursday, according to an official from the Hamas-run Civil Defence agency. Eyewitnesses said a single missile fired from a warplane struck a building housing the hospital's surgical wards and intensive care units, and a surgeon is reported to be among those killed. The Israeli military said it struck a senior Hamas member and other militants who were using the hospital "as a command center for military and terrorist activities," and said precision munitions and aerial surveillance were used to mitigate harm to civilians. Nasser Hospital, the largest in southern Gaza, was previously hit by a double Israeli strike in August 2025 which killed at least 20 people.

Why it matters: Israeli strikes on Gaza have continued despite a ceasefire agreement in October last year, and more than 1,600 people have been killed since then according to Gaza's Hamas-run health ministry, whose figures are considered reliable by the UN.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

NHS Grampian leadership slammed by watchdog

Healthcare watchdog Healthcare Improvement Scotland (HIS) has found "major deficiencies" in NHS Grampian's handling of the death of a patient during surgery, with the report identifying failures to learn from mistakes that caused repeated and avoidable harm to patients. The watchdog said it had lost confidence in NHS Grampian's management after the board was ordered to halt all paediatric surgery at Dr Gray's Hospital in Elgin but continued to operate on some children until early August. The report identified serious weaknesses in all aspects of leadership and management of the medical workforce, including a lack of workforce plans, poor recruitment controls, and significant concerns in how doctors are managed. NHS Grampian chief executive Laura Skaife-Knight, who commissioned the review, accepted the findings and apologised to patients.

Source: BBC
