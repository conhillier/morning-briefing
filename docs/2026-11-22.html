1. COP31 third draft fails to end deadlock

A new draft agreement was published in the early hours of Sunday at the COP31 UN climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, as efforts to avert collapse of the talks entered a second day of overtime. The draft still includes no specific reference to "transitioning away from fossil fuels" but does acknowledge a previous commitment made by countries to do just that three years ago. The EU, which the BBC understands considers the new text to be a backward step, had been arguing against the removal of fossil fuels from the draft text. COP31 President and Turkish Environment Minister Murat Kurum told delegates he believed concerns had been addressed, saying "We are closer than ever to agreement on the remaining issues."

Why it matters: If countries cannot reach consensus, the negotiations could be suspended until next year - something that has only happened once before since countries started holding COP climate meetings in 1995.

Source: BBC

2. Riemann hypothesis proof independently verified

Two organisations independently verified on Saturday that Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpoge used the company's Claude AI software to prove the Riemann hypothesis, one of the most important unsolved problems in mathematics. Alpoge posted a note on his personal website on Friday saying Claude helped develop a solution during the course of about six weeks.

Why it matters: The proof marks another breakthrough in the use of AI for complicated math problems, even as mathematicians express uneasiness around extremely lucrative private companies using the technology to solve problems.

Source: The Guardian

3. Anthropic IPO collides with worst tech week since 2020

Anthropic raised a record $93.5 billion in its IPO and an additional $26 billion from tech giants that bought at the offer price, with the stock gaining 17% on its debut Thursday and lifting Anthropic's market cap to $1.8 trillion. However, the debut collided with a brutal week on Wall Street, led by Nvidia's 23% plunge - the stock's sharpest weekly pullback in 18 years - as the Nasdaq slid 10% over the past five trading days in its worst week since the early days of Covid in 2020. Anthropic opened at $111.50 on Thursday but closed on Friday at $88.91. At week's end, the Claude creator's $1.6 trillion fully diluted market cap put it behind just six US companies: Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, Nvidia, Amazon and SpaceX.

Why it matters: Friday's market action showed that Wall Street is skeptical of Anthropic's ability to grow into its lofty valuation, with investors debating whether it will trade alongside AI hardware or stand alone as the first public pure-play AI developer.

Source: CNBC

4. Russia-bound ship hit by drone in Gulf of Finland

A Russian cargo vessel, the Sparta II, bound for the Russian exclave Kaliningrad was hit by a drone attack in the eastern Gulf of Finland on Saturday morning, catching fire and drifting into Finnish waters. Finnish defence forces said Ukraine was likely behind the attack, though Kyiv has yet to comment. Kaliningrad Governor Alexei Besprozvannykh said two drones had been used based on preliminary information and there was "no threat" to the territory's supplies. The Finnish Border Guard later said the crew had been evacuated to another Russian vessel, and that no environmental damage had been detected and there was no danger to maritime traffic or people.

Why it matters: Finland's defence ministry said a Russian helicopter was suspected of violating the country's airspace, potentially linked to rescue or tracking operations, and NATO has also been informed.

Source: BBC

5. Mexican cartel boss captured after bloody firefight

Mexican authorities captured cartel boss Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as "El Chapo Isidro" and one of the FBI's 10 most wanted fugitives, after a firefight in Sinaloa state that left at least 11 people dead, including seven alleged cartel gunmen and four Mexican soldiers. The operation in the city of Guasave triggered widespread violence, with military helicopters and drones flying overhead, burning cars and businesses, and cartel members maintaining at least 10 roadblocks with burning vehicles on the city's streets. Mexico's government said intelligence from the Trump administration helped authorities capture Meza Flores, though few details were provided about how the two countries coordinated. The United States had offered a $5 million reward for information leading to his arrest, and he was wanted for trafficking drugs to the US and illegally carrying firearms.

Why it matters: Experts warned that picking off capos could set off another wave of violence in a region already caught up in a power struggle between warring cartel factions.

Source: NPR

Closer to Home

Scottish government orders NHS Grampian surgery review

The Scottish government has ordered an independent review into hepatobiliary surgery at NHS Grampian after whistleblower Dr Mohammed Habib leaked a 2022 report showing 19 of the 31 deaths in liver and pancreatic surgery cases were avoidable. Habib said he had contacted the Scottish government on four separate occasions and had also gone to the police and the lord advocate with his patient safety concerns. Health Secretary Angela Constance said the review would assess how concerns raised about liver, gallbladder and bile duct surgery were handled by both the board and the Scottish government, and would identify weaknesses in governance, missed opportunities, unreasonable delay, and gaps in accountability. Constance will make a statement in Holyrood on Tuesday, where she is expected to lay out the timetable and announce who will lead the review.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Bank Rate raised to 4% in November 2026

The Bank of England's Monetary Policy Committee voted 8-1 on 4 November 2026 to increase Bank Rate by 0.25 percentage points to 4%. UK CPI inflation stood at 3.5% in September and is now expected to rise to around 5% by the middle of next year before falling back. The MPC cited sufficiently elevated inflationary pressure in the pipeline and stronger-than-expected activity data as warranting tighter monetary policy.

Source: Bank of England
