1. AfD wins big in Saxony-Anhalt, falls short of majority

Germany's far-right Alternative for Deutschland has won a decisive victory in the eastern state of Saxony-Anhalt, taking 43.8% of the vote compared to the conservative CDU's 17.2%. Preliminary results show the AfD is heading for 39 seats in the 83-seat state parliament, falling short of the 42 seats needed for an outright majority.

Why it matters: No far-right party has controlled a German state since World War Two, and governing at state level would mean powers over policing, local education and culture.

Source: BBC

2. Zelensky expects war to continue into winter

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has said he expects the war with Russia to continue into winter, following talks in Kyiv with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner that ended without a major breakthrough. Zelensky stressed Ukraine would need air defence and energy support if fighting continued, and said officials from Ukraine, Europe and America would meet in the near future to continue discussions, while also calling for a trilateral meeting with Russia and the US. Witkoff said he was "encouraged" by what he called "substantive" and important talks, adding "we're confident there'll be a good resolution here" and that the negotiators had "made a lot of progress in Moscow". Putin and Zelensky agreed to halt strikes on each other's capitals for three days while the negotiators carried out their trip, though Russian and Ukrainian forces continued to exchange strikes overnight.

Why it matters: Ukraine has relied on American-made Patriot interceptors to combat Russian ballistic missiles, but the US has used many of its stockpiles during its war with Iran, making air defence support for Ukraine an increasingly urgent issue.

Source: BBC

3. Anak Krakatau eruption strands 270,000 passengers

Indonesia's Anak Krakatau volcano, located 150km from Jakarta, began erupting late on Friday, sending lava fountains and volcanic ash into the capital and Lampung province, grounding more than 2,000 flights and leaving 270,000 passengers stranded. Jakarta's Soekarno-Hatta International and three other airports extended closures until 18:00 local time on Monday after volcanic ash was detected in nearby airspaces. Indonesia's Geological Agency warned on Monday that "the probability of further eruptions in the coming periods remains high", with volcanic ash reaching as high as 6,000m on the Java side and 15,000m on the Sumatra side.

Why it matters: Authorities have warned against approaching within a 3km radius of the active crater, and ships passing through the Sunda Strait have also been prohibited from entering an exclusion zone of 3km radius, with no tsunami warning currently linked to the volcano's recent activity.

Source: BBC

4. Amazon cargo plane crashes at Miami airport, five dead

An Amazon Air cargo plane, a Boeing 767-300 operated by charter company 21 Air, overshot the runway while attempting to land at Miami International Airport on Sunday, killing five people and seriously injuring five more, three of whom are in critical condition. The aircraft departed from Luis Munoz Marin International Airport in San Juan, Puerto Rico, and crashed just before 14:00 local time, also hitting several vehicles on the ground, with flight data showing it was travelling at 112 knots as it exited the "useable runway". Over 200 firefighters responded to the crash; the flames were extinguished, but a fuel leak remained at the site. The National Transportation Safety Board will investigate the cause of the crash and plans to hold a news conference on Monday.

Why it matters: The crash occurred over the Labor Day weekend, one of the busiest travel times of the year in the US, with the TSA predicting 2.3 million people would fly on Sunday alone, causing widespread cancellations and delays at one of America's busiest international airports.

Source: BBC

5. China injects $54bn into state banks and insurers

China is injecting 360 billion yuan ($53.6bn; PS39.7bn) into eight state-owned banks and insurance companies, led by China's finance ministry, to shore up the country's financial system and boost its slowing economy. The package will benefit three big lenders and five insurers, including the Industrial and Commercial Bank of China, the Agricultural Bank of China and China Export and Credit Insurance Corporation. State news agency Xinhua said the move "will help further enhance their sound operating capabilities, risk resistance capabilities, and ability to serve the real economy".

Why it matters: China's economic growth slowed sharply between April and June as weak domestic demand and the Iran war's impact on oil prices overshadowed strong exports, and Beijing is aiming to reshape the economy in the face of a shrinking workforce, a years-long property market slump and ongoing trade and technology rivalry with the US.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

PS2 bus fare cap launches across west Scotland

Scotland's PS2 bus fare cap came into force on Monday across Glasgow, Renfrewshire, Ayrshire, Dunbartonshire, Lanarkshire and Inverclyde, as part of First Minister John Swinney's programme for government. Participating bus operators will be reimbursed by public body Transport Scotland, and the Scottish government has already invested PS10m in a pilot of the scheme in the Highlands, Western and Northern Isles, Moray, and Argyll and Bute over the past year. However, electric bus company Ember called the cap "a good slogan" and raised concerns about how it would work on routes that cross between capped and non-capped areas, such as Glasgow to Dundee. Campaigner Ellie Harrison said the fare cap was only "half a solution", warning it would do nothing to improve service quality, add new routes to underserved communities, or integrate the bus network with the subway, train and active travel.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Business rates review launched for pubs and hotels

On 24 August 2026, HM Treasury launched an independent review of business rates valuations for pubs and hotels, to be led by business rates expert Jerry Schurder, who will report back to the Treasury by the end of March 2027. This follows a previously announced 20% cut to business rates bills for pubs, social clubs and live music venues from April 2027, on top of a 15% cut introduced from April 2026 that saved the average pub an additional £1,650 in 2026/27.

Source: HM Treasury
