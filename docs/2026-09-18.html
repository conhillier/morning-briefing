1. UN says US committed war crimes in Iran

UN human rights experts have found reasonable grounds to believe the US committed war crimes in two strikes in Iran that killed at least 177 civilians. On 28 February, Tomahawk missiles struck Shajareh Tayyebeh Primary School in Minab, killing at least 156 people, including 120 children, according to local authorities. The experts concluded the school was "the intended point of impact" and not collateral damage from an adjacent IRGC complex also struck that day. A separate strike using Precision Strike Missiles dispersed tungsten pellets over a sports complex in Lamerd, killing at least 21 civilians, including seven children.

Source: BBC

2. UN also finds Iran guilty of crimes against humanity

The same UN fact-finding mission report found Iranian authorities committed crimes against humanity during a crackdown on mass anti-government protests in December 2025 and January 2026. The experts said killings by security forces occurred on a "staggering and unprecedented scale" across all 31 provinces, with security personnel firing assault rifles into crowds from rooftops and elevated positions. Tens of thousands of people detained during the protests faced torture, solitary confinement, and denial of legal counsel. At least 29 men were executed following expedited trials on charges connected to the unrest.

Why it matters: The experts warned that the government had intensified its use of the death penalty against protesters, and that the true death toll from the crackdown was believed to be significantly higher than official figures.

Source: BBC

3. Iran delegation allowed into UN General Assembly

The US government has granted visas to Iranian officials, including President Masoud Pezeshkian and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, to attend the UN General Assembly in New York next week. The delegation will be placed under travel restrictions and banned from purchasing certain American products. State Department Spokesman Tommy Pigott warned New York shops to "be on guard" against selling goods to the delegation, saying the US would prevent diplomats from "going on shopping sprees" while Iranian people deal with water and electricity shortages. The General Debate is due to begin on Tuesday, with speeches also expected from UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham and France President Emmanuel Macron.

Why it matters: The move comes as the war between the US and Iran shows no sign of ending, having driven up global energy prices and expanded into neighbouring countries since it began in February.

Source: BBC

4. Bank of Japan raises rates to 31-year high

The Bank of Japan (BOJ) raised its main interest rate from 1% to 1.25% on Friday - a level not seen since 1995 - as it continues moving away from decades of ultra-low borrowing costs. The BOJ has now hiked rates six times in the last two and a half years, having started raising from minus 0.1% in 2024. The move follows the US Federal Reserve raising its benchmark rate for the first time in over three years on Wednesday, and the European Central Bank also increasing borrowing costs earlier this month. Official figures published Friday showed core inflation eased slightly to 1.7% in August from 1.8% the previous month, remaining close to the bank's 2% target.

Why it matters: Japan is particularly vulnerable to global energy price rises caused by the Iran war, as it is heavily reliant on energy from the Middle East, and the country's yen also fell to a fresh 40-year low in August before a joint Tokyo-Washington intervention.

Source: BBC

5. King Charles warns of AI 'existential danger'

King Charles convened a summit at Dumfries House in Ayrshire to discuss how AI can benefit society, warning executives of the "existential dangers" posed by the technology falling into the wrong hands. Attendees included Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, Google DeepMind co-founder Sir Demis Hassabis, OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar, and UK AI Minister Kanishka Narayan. Sir Demis warned that artificial general intelligence was "probably only a few short years away" and could have an impact "ten times that of the Industrial Revolution", adding that the chance of something going wrong was "definitely non-zero". Nvidia's Jensen Huang, whose company has a stock market value of around $5.15tn, told the room he believed safety was "paramount" and that companies should "keep engineering" if a product was not safe enough.

Why it matters: The summit was called to consider whether a "shared set of principles" can be developed to "help guide the future application" of AI, amid a wave of warnings from inside the industry about rapidly evolving capabilities of advanced models.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Detained Scot Jagtar Singh Johal granted bail

The family of Jagtar Singh Johal, a Scottish Sikh activist from Dumbarton who has been held in India for almost nine years without conviction, say he has been granted bail by an Indian court. Johal, aged 39, was arrested in 2017 in connection with alleged terror offences, including an alleged role in a series of targeted killings of religious and political figures. He was acquitted last year in a case in which he was accused of financially supporting a terror group, but still faces federal charges lodged by Indian authorities. His family said they were still waiting for details of what happens next but are "relieved" by the court's decision.

Source: BBC
