1. COP31 ends with no deal on fossil fuels

Countries failed to reach a major new deal to tackle climate change after deep divisions over fossil fuels and money at UN COP31 talks in Turkey. Negotiations ran late into Sunday, becoming the longest UN climate conference in history. The final deal proved unacceptable to the EU, Pacific island nations and others because it failed to directly mention fossil fuels.

Source: BBC

2. Russia strikes Kyiv region, killing 15

Overnight Russian strikes on the Kyiv region killed at least 15 people, with an attack on a five-storey building in Dymer, about 45km north of Kyiv, killing at least 11 people. A Russian strike on a supermarket killed three other people and wounded 15, including three children, in Vyshneve. A strike on a private home in nearby Bila Tserkva killed one more person and set the building ablaze. Russia also struck a power substation in the Dnipropetrovsk region.

Why it matters: Putin had promised on Thursday that Russia would respond to Ukrainian attacks on the Kerch Bridge with "much more painful" attacks, and Moscow has been pummelling Ukraine with nightly drone and missile barrages as the country braces for another winter.

Source: Al Jazeera

3. Ukraine sinks Russian corvette near Libya

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukraine's forces sank the Russian corvette Merkury, one of Russia's newest ships commissioned in 2023, near the coast of Libya using seven Ukrainian-made naval drones. Zelenskyy said they also hit a second target, the Russian dry cargo ship MV Angara, which he said was used by Russia to transport weapons. A Russian Ka-27 combat helicopter was also destroyed along with the corvette. "We continue operations in designated waters," Zelenskyy said on Telegram.

Source: Al Jazeera

4. Capita boss quits as shares crash 67%

Chief executive Adolfo Hernandez has departed Capita with "immediate effect" after the outsourcing group admitted it has "not delivered" for retired civil servants left waiting for pension payments. Shares in Capita plunged by 67% to as low as 80p, the company's lowest share price since October 1991, as it cut profit forecasts and proposed raising £190m in extra capital. Capita said adjusted operating profit in 2027 is expected to be £85m to £95m lower than previously expected, due to extra costs related to the UK Civil Service Pension Scheme (CSPS) contract. Hernandez will be replaced by chief financial officer Pablo Andres.

Why it matters: Pensioners have reported being forced to borrow money from family to pay for food and heating, with some saying they feared losing their homes because they could not afford their rent or mortgage.

Source: The Guardian

5. Europe gas prices surge 8.6% on cold snap fears

European gas prices surged on Monday as a forecast cold snap fed fears over depleted supply, with the Dutch month-ahead contract rising by as much as 8.6% to €96.08, before settling up 4% at €92.50. Europe's gas supplies have been hit by a slowdown in exports from Qatar due to the war in the Middle East, as well as increased summer consumption during a prolonged heatwave. Storage levels are now at about 69% full, against a typical level of 80%. In the UK, energy regulator Ofgem is expected this week to announce a 26% rise in its quarterly price cap from January, adding an extra £443 to an average annual bill for a dual-fuel household.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

NHS Grampian cover-up denied by health secretary

Health Secretary Angela Constance has denied a cover-up surrounding an unreleased report showing that 19 deaths at NHS Grampian could have been avoided, saying she was "not satisfied that all questions have been asked or answered" about how concerns over hepatobiliary care were handled. The leaked 2022 report, carried out into liver and pancreatic surgery at Aberdeen Royal Infirmary, found 19 avoidable deaths among 45 patients. Constance has ordered an independent review and said current and past ministers, NHS and Scottish government officials will all be within its scope.

Source: BBC
