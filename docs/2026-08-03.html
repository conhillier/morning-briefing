1. Trump says Iran talks begin Monday

US President Donald Trump said new talks with Iran will begin on Monday, after calling off what he described as a planned "massive" strike on the country. Speaking to reporters on Air Force One on Sunday, Trump said: "We're talking to them in the form of a negotiation. It begins tomorrow afternoon." Trump said Saudi Arabia's Crown Prince Mohammed Bin Salman, as well as US partners including the United Arab Emirates and Qatar, had urged him against conducting strikes over the weekend.

Why it matters: Global oil prices fell on Monday morning in Asia trading after Trump's announcement, with Brent Crude down 4.5% at $83.98 and US-traded oil 4.6% lower at $80.74.

Source: BBC

2. Israel strikes Gaza after Hamas disarmament deal

At least 13 people, including several children, have been killed in Israeli strikes on Gaza, Palestinian officials said. Gaza's Civil Defence Authority said Israeli military aircraft carried out attacks on Gaza City, Khan Younis and Deir el-Balah, the second successive night of strikes. The strikes came days after Hamas accepted a disarmament plan, which would see the Palestinian group hand over its weapons to US President Donald Trump's Board of Peace. Israeli Energy Minister Eli Cohen said on Sunday that no deal had been reached to stop attacks on the Gaza Strip, a territory now under 70% Israeli control.

Source: BBC

3. US and Japan confirm joint yen intervention

Japan and the United States have confirmed a rare, coordinated yen-buying intervention to halt the Japanese currency's slide to 40-year lows, with Tokyo signalling it is willing to take further action if needed. The yen gained as much as 1.4% to hit a nearly three-month high of 155.20 per US dollar, compounding a 3.8% surge over the previous two sessions. Tokyo may have sold as much as $58.97bn to buy yen when it intervened in New York markets on Thursday, Bank of Japan data indicated. Japan's Finance Ministry said it "will not hesitate to conduct further joint intervention", and US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent repeated calls for further interest rate hikes by the Bank of Japan.

Why it matters: The joint intervention is the first since a 2011 coordinated action to weaken the yen after the devastating earthquake in eastern Japan, and the Nikkei share average tumbled immediately after the rapid yen appreciation.

Source: Al Jazeera

4. China chip advances rattle global markets

Last week was particularly volatile for AI-linked shares after Chinese memory chipmaker CXMT floated on the Shanghai stock market, soaring by 466% in value to 3.3tn yuan (£365bn). That same day it was reported that China had developed its own tools to carry out deep-ultraviolet lithography, a technique over which Dutch company ASML had held a monopoly. South Korea's main share index, the Kospi, fell 11.5% on Tuesday and a further 6% on Wednesday, dragged down by semiconductor maker SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics. The main US tech index, the Nasdaq, fell into correction territory at one point after dropping more than 10% from its recent high, before easing back after strong results from Amazon and Microsoft.

Why it matters: Analysts said the sell-off was an "overreaction" given a global memory chip shortage likely to continue until 2030, but experts warned China appears "poised to do to the big chipmakers what they have done to steel, automobiles and a host of other industries, namely undercut them and outcompete them on price."

Source: The Guardian

5. Solar hits record 14.4% of UK electricity in July

Solar energy supplied more power to Great Britain's grid in July than in any previous month, with solar farms and rooftop panels providing 14.4% of the country's electricity. This smashed the previous monthly record of 12.4%, set in May, and is 50% higher than a year ago when only 9.6% of generation was from solar, according to data from the National Energy System Operator. The surge comes after official figures recorded more new solar power installations in the first half of 2026 than in any six-month period since 2011, with 142,536 solar installations tracked. The extra solar power helped to meet higher-than-usual demand for electricity to run air-conditioning units and fans during heatwaves.

Why it matters: Energy minister Michael Shanks said: "The more power we generate here in Britain, the less exposed families and businesses are to global energy price shocks."

Source: The Guardian

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Business rates cut 20% for pubs and live music venues

From April 2027 (the 2027/28 financial year), pubs, social clubs and live music venues in England will receive a 20% cut to their business rates bills, saving the typical pub an estimated £1,100 in the next financial year. This package of support is worth around £100 million a year and will benefit nearly 32,000 pubs, clubs and live music venues. The largest live music venues will not be eligible for the new 20% discount, with details to be set out at the Budget.

Source: HM Treasury

Companies House accounts filing reforms delayed to April 2028

Accounts reforms under the Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023 will now come into effect from April 2028, rather than April 2027, giving all companies one full accounting year plus 9 months (21 months) to prepare. From that date, all UK registered companies must file accounts in iXBRL format via commercial software, with web and paper-based filing systems closed for accounts filings. Small companies and micro-entities will be required to file profit and loss accounts but may opt out of publication on the public register.

Source: Companies House
