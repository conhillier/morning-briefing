1. Trump cancels Iran strikes, cites deal progress

US President Donald Trump announced he has cancelled a planned large-scale military attack on Iran, saying the "perimeters of a deal" have been agreed upon. Trump posted the decision on his Truth Social platform late Saturday, a day after US media reported that Washington and Israel were preparing to strike Iran's energy infrastructure. The US Department of State had earlier issued security alerts for Americans in 10 countries in the region, urging them to exercise increased caution and to consider leaving due to escalating tensions.

Why it matters: Iran had not yet responded to Trump's announcement, and earlier on Saturday Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told his Saudi counterpart that any US or Israeli attack would meet a "proportionate response".

Source: Al Jazeera

2. Russian barrage kills nine in Kyiv overnight

Russian missiles tore into the Ukrainian capital before dawn on Saturday, killing nine people and injuring more than 30 others. The attack included 35 missiles and 185 attack drones, according to Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, with only one of 27 ballistic missiles shot down due to Ukraine's dwindling supply of Patriot air defense missiles. The heaviest toll came in the Darnytskyi district, where seven people died and 14 were hurt. Zelenskyy also said Ukrainian drones struck three oil refineries overnight in Russia's Bashkortostan region, roughly 1,000 miles from the front lines.

Why it matters: Zelenskyy said "every package of ballistic missile interceptors saves the lives of our people," while Trump walked back a promised boost to Ukraine's missile defense systems, saying at a Cabinet meeting that "we have not agreed to that" regarding Ukraine manufacturing its own Patriot systems.

Source: NPR

3. Moscow restaurant bomb kills three

At least three people are dead and more than 20 injured after a bomb exploded outside a restaurant in central Moscow. Russian authorities say a woman carrying an explosive device was stopped by a security guard before it exploded. It is not clear who the target was.

Source: Al Jazeera

4. Ceuta death toll rises to 67

The death toll in Spain's Ceuta border crisis with Morocco has climbed to 67, the Spanish government said Saturday, including some who drowned and some killed in a stampede to cross a breakwater barrier. Spain announced it was installing a 500-meter-long containment barrier along the breakwater fence into the sea after some 60,000 migrants breached the tiny Spanish territory's frontier between Thursday and Friday. Spain's interior ministry said the majority of those who entered the Spanish territory have already returned to Morocco.

Why it matters: Despite the return of most migrants, some remained determined to stay.

Source: NPR

5. Tourist plane crashes near Peru's Nazca Lines

Police in Peru report that 13 people, among them foreign tourists, have been killed in a plane crash in southern Peru, near the Nazca Lines. Local authorities confirmed that 11 tourists and two pilots died when the Cessna Grand Caravan crashed in Pueblo Viejo, approximately 4 miles (6km) from the city of Nazca and roughly 12km from the Nazca Lines site. The passengers were identified as seven Italians, two Germans and two Spanish people. The plane had departed the nearby city of Pisco at about 1pm local time, lost contact with the control tower and crashed at 1.20pm.

Why it matters: The cause of the crash is as yet unknown and local authorities have launched an investigation.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Royal family attends Commonwealth Games in Glasgow

The Prince and Princess of Wales brought their three children to watch sporting events at the Commonwealth Games in Glasgow, with cheering crowds at the city's OVO Hydro witnessing New Zealand's 61-54 win over England in the netball semi-finals. The family also visited the Sir Chris Hoy Velodrome, where they chatted to British cyclist Dame Laura Kenny and sat one row behind Olympic champion Sir Chris Hoy. After leaving the netball, they met gold medal-winning para powerlifter Mark Swan and gymnast Reuben Ward. The Games has hosted a record 47 para-sports events and Glasgow stepped in to host the tournament for the second time in eight years after Australian state Victoria pulled out due to soaring costs.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Bank Rate held at 3.75% by MPC, July 2026

At its meeting ending on 29 July 2026, the Monetary Policy Committee voted by a majority of 6-3 to maintain Bank Rate at 3.75%. Three members voted to increase Bank Rate by 0.25 percentage points, to 4%. CPI inflation has fallen to 2.6% since the previous meeting, although it is expected to rise later this year as the effects of higher energy prices continue to pass through.

Source: Bank of England
