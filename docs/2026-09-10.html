1. Oil hits $100 as Middle East escalates

Oil prices rose to $100 a barrel on Wednesday after the US struck Iranian tankers and Yemen's Houthis hit oil facilities in Saudi Arabia. The US military's Central Command said its strikes destroyed four Iranian oil tankers in the Gulf of Oman - Kaviz, Charminar, Horizon 1 and Riesco - and a fifth tanker, Derya, near Kharg Island. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said their Aerospace Force launched missiles at US maintenance facilities at Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan, while Jordan's armed forces said they intercepted 20 Iranian missiles with no casualties reported. A barrel of Brent crude was priced at $100.70, up 2.83% compared to Tuesday.

Why it matters: The escalating conflicts have pushed Brent crude to its highest level since late July, and the rising oil costs have sparked leaps in petrol prices around the world for motorists.

Source: BBC

2. IRGC plans new Arabian Sea exclusion zone

Iran's Revolutionary Guards announced plans for a new "prohibited zone" for shipping that would extend beyond the Strait of Hormuz into the Arabian Sea. IRGC spokesman Hossein Mohebi told Iranian state TV that it would soon announce co-ordinates covering "part of the Sea of Oman and another part of the Arabian Sea, along the route that can lead to the Strait of Hormuz".

Why it matters: The Strait of Hormuz is the waterway through which 20% of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas shipments usually pass, and any effective exclusion zone would severely disrupt global energy supplies.

Source: BBC

3. Yemen fighting kills children, Houthis claim 54 Saudi raids

Fighting across Yemen intensified on Wednesday, with the internationally recognised government blaming Houthis for an artillery attack that killed three children, including a baby, in the central Marib governorate, with six others wounded. The Houthis meanwhile said the death toll from a Saudi strike on a prison in the northern al-Jawf governorate had climbed to at least 32 people, with seven others wounded. A Houthi military spokesman said Saudi forces had launched 54 air strikes across six provinces over 12 hours, targeting al-Jawf, Marib, Taiz, Hodeidah, Saada and al-Bayda. Pakistan warned on Tuesday that continued Houthi attacks could trigger the recently signed Mecca Joint Defence Agreement, a mutual security pact involving Saudi Arabia, Turkiye and Pakistan.

Why it matters: According to the World Health Organization, the clashes have killed or wounded more than 1,000 people and displaced tens of thousands.

Source: Al Jazeera

4. Anthropic discloses fourth AI hacking incident

Anthropic has reported a fourth incident involving an AI model gaining unauthorised access to external systems, revealing that an early version of its Claude Opus 4.6 hacked into a third-party system in January. The January incident went undetected until last month, despite an earlier company-wide review, Anthropic said. The company's investigation identified two recurring problems: biased reasoning, in which Claude discounted or misinterpreted evidence that it was operating on the live internet, and recklessness, or a willingness to take potentially harmful actions in pursuit of a task. Anthropic said it has engaged independent research firm METR to investigate the incidents.

Why it matters: The disclosure came shortly after researcher Jacob Coxon quit Anthropic, stating publicly that "the people building AI earnestly believe that it could kill us all by the end of the decade" and warning the industry was more focused on competition than implementing safeguards.

Source: Al Jazeera

5. Google makes record 13bn euro Finland investment

Google announced a record 13 billion euro ($15bn) investment in Finland's AI infrastructure, its largest single investment in Europe. The package will fund three new data centres in Kajaani, Muhos and Vaala, expand an existing site in Hamina, and support energy projects. As part of the deal, Google signed a 22-year contract with Finnish utility Fortum to buy up to 50% of the output from the Loviisa nuclear power plant, which currently generates about 10% of Finland's electricity. The investment is expected to support more than 37,000 jobs during construction and boost Finland's GDP by 3.6 billion euros a year, according to Google.

Why it matters: Construction is set to take place in 2027 and 2028, and Fortum said Google's commitment would support an investment programme aimed at extending the life of the nuclear station and increasing its generating capacity.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Family tribute to A8 crash victim Linda Patterson

The family of Linda Patterson, 58, killed in a four-car crash on the A8 near Newbridge on Monday, have described her as a "beautiful soul". Patterson was driving a Hyundai i30 when the collision occurred and was pronounced dead at the scene. Shola Richards, 32, of Broxburn, West Lothian, has since been charged with offences including causing death by dangerous driving, attempting to pervert the course of justice, failing to stop after the incident, and failing to provide driver's details to police. Richards made no plea during a private appearance before Edinburgh Sheriff Court and was remanded in custody for further examination.

Source: BBC
