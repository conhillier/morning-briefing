1. 1,400 Yemenis flee to Djibouti in 24 hours

About 1,400 people have fled Yemen for Djibouti in a 24-hour period, according to Djibouti's economy and finance minister Ilyas Moussa Dawaleh, who posted on X: "1,400 refugees arrived in Djibouti from Yemen in just 24 hours." The IOM said on Saturday that 76,000 people had been displaced by fighting in Yemen since July, with the figure quadrupling following the renewed escalation in hostilities. The Houthis seized the Red Sea port city of Mocha on Thursday and have since overrun the coastline and islands surrounding the Bab al-Mandeb strait, with entire villages reportedly emptied in districts such as Maqbanah, Jabal Habashi and al-Maafer.

Why it matters: The scale of displacement is accelerating rapidly as Houthi gains tighten their grip on the Bab al-Mandeb strait, a critical maritime chokepoint.

Source: Al Jazeera

2. Houthis seize Mayun Island, control Bab al-Mandeb

Yemen's Houthis have seized the strategic Mayun Island, also known as Perim Island, cementing their control over the Bab al-Mandeb Strait. The rebels also now control millions of dollars worth of seized US-made military hardware.

Source: Al Jazeera

3. Oil tops $102 as China's buying intent watched closely

US crude oil prices topped $102 per barrel on Thursday for the highest close since May, having surged about 50% from a summer low of $68.55 reached about three weeks after Washington and Tehran signed their now-failed memorandum of understanding on June 17. Saudi Arabia's crucial East-West oil pipeline has been shut down after multiple attacks, intensifying the week's rally. Global inventories have plunged by 400 million barrels after more than six months of war, eroding a key buffer that had prevented prices from shooting up earlier, according to the US Energy Information Administration. China's crude imports fell to a wartime low of around 6 million bpd in June, a nearly 50% decline compared with 11.5 million bpd in February, but have increased to around 7 million bpd in July and August, according to Kpler data.

Why it matters: Whether China ramps up crude imports further could determine if prices rally toward their wartime closing high of $112.95, with analysts warning demand from Chinese refiners - attracted by soaring diesel margins - may yet tighten the market further.

Source: CNBC

4. OpenAI delays IPO; AI chiefs call for slowdown

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman told Fortune that an IPO in 2026 would be "ill-advised," pushing one of the most anticipated listings in history until at least 2027, despite OpenAI CFO Sara Friar telling employees just last month the company would likely go public in 2027 or sooner.

Why it matters: The sudden alignment among Altman, Amodei and Musk shows how quickly concern over AI has moved from the margins to the centre of the industry and is now beginning to collide with its enormous commercial ambitions.

Source: CNBC

5. BRICS summit tests bloc unity over wars

Leaders of the BRICS nations met in New Delhi on Saturday for a summit focused on trade, investment and global conflicts, with Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi hosting Russian President Vladimir Putin, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian, among others. Reducing reliance on the US dollar is a priority, with members promoting greater use of their own currencies in trade and payments, and Iranian President Pezeshkian backing expanding trade in national currencies. A key test for India will be whether it can secure a joint statement from leaders, after BRICS foreign ministers failed to agree on one in May over the Iran war.

Why it matters: The summit highlights deep divisions within the bloc, with China and Russia seeking to use BRICS to reduce Western dominance while India tries to balance ties with Moscow and Tehran against its deepening relations with Washington and Europe.

Source: NPR

French train derailment probed as possible sabotage

Officials in northern France are investigating whether the derailment of a passenger train on Friday evening was the result of a malicious act. Prosecutor Sebastien Gallois said: "At this stage, the leading theory is that a piece of rail was present on one of the tracks, potentially causing the derailment. Some 140 firefighters and other emergency teams were deployed to the crash scene near Cleon, just south of Rouen.
