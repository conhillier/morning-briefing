1. Three children and teacher killed in Oregon school shooting

A 16-year-old shot dead three children and a teacher at Silver Rail Elementary School in Bend, Oregon on Thursday afternoon before being killed by a bystander. The suspect forced his way into the school by shooting at locked doors, Bend Police Chief Mike Krantz said. The three children killed were aged five, eight and 11; a seven-year-old and a 54-year-old teacher were taken to hospital in a critical condition. Around 450 children attend Silver Rail Elementary School, which has more than 40 adult staff members.

Why it matters: Schools in Bend have been closed on Friday, with officials saying the community needed time to heal. According to the K-12 School Shooting Database, there were 235 shooting incidents at American schools last year and at least 140 so far this year.

Source: BBC

2. Ukraine resumes strikes on Russian oil refineries

Russia's defence ministry said four missiles and dozens of drones were intercepted, with falling debris causing a fire at the Ryazan refinery site.

Why it matters: The strike risks further complicating Kyiv's relations with US President Trump, which have soured in recent days, and Zelensky said Ukraine would only halt strikes on Russian oil infrastructure if the US supplied more Patriot air defence missiles.

Source: BBC

3. Ship attacks near Hormuz hit record high

The number of ships reporting attacks in and around the Strait of Hormuz has hit a record high, with twelve vessels struck between 5 and 11 October - the largest weekly total reported to the UK's Maritime Trade Operations agency since the US-Israeli war with Iran began. Six vessels were struck on a single Sunday, a one-day high, and four more attacks were reported in the days that followed. Oil shipments through the strait have slowed as a result, with about 12.6 million barrels per day passing through on 13 October, down from 15 million at the end of September and below a pre-war baseline of some 17 million barrels per day.

Why it matters: Martin Kelly of Orbital Risk Intelligence told BBC Verify that Iran has "evolved its strategy" and is now carrying out "near daily or multiple attacks every single day", with the IRGC navy warning strikes would not be "limited to the Strait of Hormuz".

Source: BBC

4. Anthropic grants independent researchers employee-level access

Anthropic is giving employee-level access to six researchers from METR, a nonprofit that tests frontier AI models, to independently assess Anthropic's models, safety practices, and risk management. Under the arrangement, the METR team has Anthropic-issued laptops, office space, and access to internal tools roughly on par with those used by Anthropic's own risk assessment teams. Anthropic has also given up the right to block METR's publications, though it can require redactions in narrow pre-defined categories such as customer data or attorney-client privileged information. METR is the only evaluator so far at the top of a three-tier independent review system Anthropic is building.

Why it matters: The move fulfils a promise made by CEO Dario Amodei in a September essay proposing that outside evaluators be embedded inside AI labs, and follows a White House accord signed by Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta, Nvidia, and xAI committing to independent review.

Source: TechCrunch

5. Meta launches open-weight AI model for Macs

The model leverages 816 billion parameters and a 1 million token context window, and has been designed to run locally on Apple computers, specifically the Mac Studio equipped with the M5 Ultra chip, though it requires at least 1 terabyte of RAM. The model is natively multimodal and built for complex coding tasks and agentic work, and Meta paired it with its AI coding terminal Muse Code to deploy it across agentic workflows. Meta is making Muse Spark Open available through a number of different API platforms.

Source: TechCrunch

Closer to Home

Nandy considers blocking Sky's £1.6bn ITV deal

Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy has said she is "minded to intervene" in the proposed £1.6bn deal for Sky to buy ITV's media and entertainment division, citing concerns about free expression and the variety of Britain's news landscape. Nandy noted that Sky News and ITV News together hold a joint 26% share of those viewing news on linear TV, and warned that over time the tie-up could result in 86% of the linear TV news market being controlled by the BBC and a combined Sky/ITV. Sky and ITV have been given until 20 October to respond to the culture secretary's concerns. Nandy said if she decides to intervene she will bring forward legislation to extend public interest considerations to on-demand programmes, as current laws do not cover streaming.

Source: BBC
