1. DRC Ebola outbreak spreads to sixth province

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo has spread to a sixth province after a man died in Bas-Uele having travelled from Isiro, in Haut-Uele province, to Buta, the capital of Bas-Uele. The current outbreak has killed more than 2,100 people out of more than 4,500 cases, according to the latest government figures released on Tuesday. The death toll has been reached almost three times faster than in the 2014-16 outbreak, and WHO director general Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said the current outbreak would surpass that one based on its current pace. There are no approved vaccines or treatments for the rare Bundibugyo strain responsible, though clinical trials of two possible treatments began last month in Ituri.

Why it matters: Health authorities have said between 60% and 70% of new cases are recorded outside contacts being monitored, and the WHO's Africa regional director warned: "We are chasing the virus; the virus is ahead of us."

Source: The Guardian

2. UK GDP growth slows as energy prices bite

UK economic growth slowed in the second quarter of 2026, with the Office for National Statistics reporting that GDP expanded by 0.4% in the three months to June, down from 0.6% in the first quarter. The slowdown was attributed to disruption from the Iran war, though analysts said the economy had been more resilient than feared. Services output expanded by 0.5% across the second quarter, with information and communication businesses leading at 2.7% growth, while industrial production was flat. Inflation figures for July, due next week, are expected to show a higher reading than June's 2.6%, reflecting rising utility bills, which jumped by 13% when the energy price cap lifted in July.

Why it matters: Economists still expect rising energy costs to dampen growth through the remainder of 2026, and sustained high inflation will increase pressure on the Bank of England to raise interest rates.

Source: The Guardian

3. Putin visits disputed Kuril Islands, Japan protests

Russian President Vladimir Putin made his first visit to the Kuril Islands on Thursday, visiting a fish plant, a hospital and a school on Iturup, one of the chain's southern islands. Japanese Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi said his country "strongly protests" Putin's visit and summoned Russian Ambassador Nikolay Nozdrev to the ministry in Tokyo.

Why it matters: The Soviet Union took the four southernmost islands from Japan in the final days of World War II, and the dispute has kept the two countries from signing a peace treaty formally ending their hostilities.

Source: NPR

4. Taiwan hit by AI-assisted government cyber-attack

Taiwan's Ministry of Digital Affairs said its cybersecurity monitoring units detected an "abnormal attack" targeting government agencies that began on 20 July, described as a first-of-a-kind AI-assisted breach.

Why it matters: Chinese cyber-attacks on Taiwan's key infrastructure rose 6% in 2025 to an average of 2.63 million attacks a day, according to Taiwan's National Security Bureau, with some hacks synchronised with military drills.

Source: The Guardian

5. Uber and Pony.ai to deploy 2,000 robotaxis across Europe

Uber and Chinese autonomous vehicle company Pony.ai announced plans to deploy 2,000 self-driving taxis across Europe and expand their partnership to the Middle East. The companies had already launched what they claim is Europe's first commercial robotaxi service in Croatia's capital Zagreb in late March, and the new partnership will roll out vehicles to four other European cities, though the cities and exact timeframe were not named. Alphabet-backed Waymo remains the global leader with a fleet of around 5,000 vehicles, primarily in the US.

Why it matters: Having a large-enough vehicle fleet is critical for commercialisation: the more cars available, the more data can be collected to demonstrate safety to regulators and boost operational efficiency.

Source: CNBC

Closer to Home

Train derails near Lewes, two seriously hurt

Two people were taken to major trauma centres with severe injuries after a Southern passenger train derailed between Haywards Heath and Lewes in East Sussex on Thursday afternoon. British Transport Police said a further nine passengers sustained less serious injuries, and a major incident was declared with all passengers evacuated. An aerial photograph showed three of the train's eight carriages on their side; the train was the 14.24 Southern service from Victoria to Eastbourne. The Rail Accident Investigation Branch has launched an inquiry, and passengers travelling to Gatwick Airport were advised to allow at least an extra 150 minutes for their journey.

Source: The Guardian

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Companies House accounts reforms delayed to April 2028

From April 2028, small companies and micro-entities must file profit and loss accounts with Companies House but may opt out of publication on the public register. All UK registered companies will be required to file accounts in iXBRL format via commercial software only, with web and paper-based filing systems closing for accounts filings from that date. The implementation date has been moved from April 2027 to April 2028, giving companies one full accounting year plus 9 months (21 months) to prepare.

Source: Companies House
