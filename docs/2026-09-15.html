1. Houthis seize Red Sea islands, oil hits $107

Yemen's Houthi militants have seized the islands of Greater and Lesser Hanish in the Red Sea, reinforcing their ability to control shipping through the Bab al-Mandab strait. The islands lie 160km north of the strait, which connects the Red Sea to the open ocean and Saudi Arabia's key Asian markets. Brent crude rose 1.17% to $106.92 a barrel on Tuesday morning, while the average price of diesel in the US hit $6 per gallon for the first time last week. Nearly 94,000 people in Yemen have fled their homes since fighting escalated this month, and about 200 schools have been converted to shelters in south-western Yemen.

Source: The Guardian

2. NATO jets shoot down drone over Lithuania

NATO fighter jets shot down a drone that entered Lithuanian airspace overnight on Tuesday, according to Lithuanian President Gitanas Nauseda. The drone likely entered southern Lithuania, near the second-largest city of Kaunas, from neighbouring Belarus shortly after midnight. Drone alerts sounded across the capital Vilnius and the surrounding region, with warnings sent to phones telling residents to "find a safe place" and "wait for the all clear". Lithuania's armed forces and Italian pilots serving in the NATO Air Defence Mission were credited with a "swift and responsible response" to the incursion.

Why it matters: President Nauseda stated: "With Russia intensifying its aggression against Ukraine, such readiness is vital for our region." The origins of the drone have not yet been determined.

Source: BBC

3. US confirms first-ever deployed space weapon

The US has confirmed it deployed a space weapon in Earth's orbit, the first time it has acknowledged such offensive capabilities. US Secretary of the Air Force Troy Meink made the announcement at the Air, Space and Cyber Conference on Monday, saying the "on orbit" weapon was necessary to safeguard US forces against hostile enemy action. Meink said the US was ready for "evolving threats" but did not elaborate on the weapon's capabilities or when it was put into orbit. Senior military leaders have previously cited Russian and Chinese programmes developing ways to attack and disrupt US satellites as justification for boosting military capabilities in space.

Why it matters: The US Space Force states that space control encompasses employing "kinetic and non-kinetic means to affect adversary capabilities through disruption, degradation and even destruction, if necessary" for both offensive and defensive purposes.

Source: BBC

4. Treasury yields hit highest since 2007

The benchmark 10-year Treasury yield climbed to its highest level since 2007 on Tuesday, jumping more than 6 basis points to 5.025%. The move comes ahead of the Federal Reserve's two-day policy meeting beginning Tuesday, with markets pricing in a more than 92% chance that the Fed will raise rates by 25 basis points, according to the CME FedWatch tool. The yield on the longer-dated 30-year Treasury bond was up over 5 basis points at 5.384%. Experts noted the one-month rolling correlation between front-month West Texas Intermediate crude and the 10-year Treasury yield has climbed to 0.96, according to BMO Capital Markets.

Why it matters: "As long as oil prices remain firm and continue to drift higher, this will add pressure to interest rates," said Steve Sosnick, chief strategist at Interactive Brokers.

Source: CNBC

5. Trump dismisses AI safety fears as hoax

US President Donald Trump has said fears about the safety of AI are a "hoax", criticising calls for greater guardrails on the technology. In a series of social media posts on Monday, Trump compared AI safety warnings to the "Global Warming Scam" and called himself "the Hoax Buster". He also posted that the only "guardrails" needed for AI was a "strong and smart" president, and warned: "WHOEVER WINS AI, WINS!" Trump's remarks came after Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark told the BBC that a "kill switch" for dangerous AI that can be checked by a third party may need to be mandatory, and after the bosses of OpenAI and xAI said they agreed with Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's proposal for industry-wide deceleration.

Why it matters: Concerns over AI safety triggered a sell-off in shares of some tech firms on Monday as investors weighed the impact of a potential slowdown in AI development.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

North Sea bosses say 100 projects could be unlocked

Industry body Offshore Energies UK (OEUK) says changes to the windfall tax and approval of the Rosebank and Jackdaw fields could unlock 111 projects, attracting investment worth £50bn. The report argues the UK could supply half of its oil and gas needs without imports if the government showed more support for the sector. OEUK said the oil and gas industry supported 180,000 jobs in 2024 across the UK, and called for the current Energy Profits Levy to be scrapped by next January. Environmental group Uplift branded the report a "fantasy", saying the North Sea is an "ultra-mature" basin with very little reserves left.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Strike-off objections must be submitted online from 1 December 2026

From 1 December 2026, objections to a limited company being struck off must be submitted through the Companies House Make an objection online service. Objections by email will no longer be accepted after this date.
