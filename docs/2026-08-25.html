1. US launches 'Operation Economic Outcast' against Iran

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent unveiled sweeping new sanctions against Iran on Monday under what he called "Operation Economic Outcast", describing it as "the single greatest financial offensive ever". The Treasury Department issued determinations against five sectors - digital assets, technology, gold, aviation and shipping - and imposed sanctions on almost 60 entities, individuals and vessels. Bessent warned that any nation financially partnering with Iran would also be isolated, saying the US was "no longer managing the Iranian threat, we are ending it". Iran's economy minister Ali Madanizadeh said the country had a two-year plan to counter the sanctions and predicted defeat for Washington, while China's Foreign Ministry said sanctions and pressure tactics did not help.

Why it matters: One fifth of the world's oil and gas usually passes through the Strait of Hormuz, which has been effectively blocked since the conflict began, and higher oil prices have fuelled cost-of-living concerns globally, with US gasoline prices surpassing $4 a gallon.

Source: BBC

2. Trump threatens 50% auto tariff on Canada

President Trump threatened on Monday to raise tariffs on Canadian cars, trucks and auto parts from 25% to 50% as of 1 January, escalating the trade war after US-Canada talks collapsed late on Friday. Prime Minister Mark Carney called the threat unsurprising and accused Trump of wanting to destroy Canada's auto industry, saying Canada would only resume talks if the US came with the "right attitude". Ontario Premier Doug Ford told Trump to "kiss my ass" and suggested Canada should charge the US extra for oil, gas, electricity and critical minerals. Oxford Economics warned the risk of the USMCA unravelling had increased and that this "would plunge Canada into recession and leave it on a permanently lower growth path".

Why it matters: Canada accounts for 60% of total US crude oil imports and close to 100% of US natural gas exports, meaning an escalating trade war carries serious energy supply risks for the United States.

Source: BBC

3. US removes Syria from terrorism sponsors list

The US has removed Syria from its list of state sponsors of terrorism after 47 years, lifting what it described as the "final major barriers" for private sector investment in the country. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the move recognised the Syrian government's positive actions to fully distance itself from international terrorism, while Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said it would "help foster additional investment in Syria to promote political and economic stability". The Trump administration also removed Syria's ruling Islamist group Hayat Tahrir al-Sham from the US list of global terrorist organisations. Syria's removal leaves just three states remaining on the list: Cuba, North Korea and Iran.

Why it matters: The listing carries financial sanctions, restrictions on US foreign aid and defence exports and sales, and can penalise people and other countries that trade with the sanctioned state, so removal opens significant economic pathways for Syria.

Source: BBC

4. Lockerbie trial postponed a third time

The trial of Libyan suspect Abu Agila Mohammad Kheir Al-Marimi over the 1988 Lockerbie bombing has been postponed for a third time after "newly discovered evidence" previously unknown to both the defence and prosecutors emerged on Friday 21 August. Jury selection had been due to begin in Washington DC on Wednesday, but a new start date has been set for January next year. Al-Marimi has denied building the bomb that destroyed Pan Am Flight 103, which killed 270 people. Trial judge Dabney Friedrich agreed to set aside the start date, underlining the potential importance of the new evidence.

Why it matters: For the relatives of those who died and Al-Marimi himself, this latest delay means a verdict might not come until next spring.

Source: BBC

5. UK energy debt could hit £7bn this winter

Households in Great Britain could owe their energy suppliers as much as £7bn by the end of the year after higher gas and electricity prices forecast for winter kick in, industry group Energy UK has warned. Domestic energy debt and arrears already climbed by about £500m over the past year to a record £6bn at the end of June, as the Middle East conflict continued to stoke rising gas market prices. The regulator is expected to confirm later this week a 4% increase on the energy price cap from October, which forecasters at Cornwall Insight say would leave typical households facing a bill equivalent to £1,729 a year over the final three months of 2026. Energy UK chief executive Dhara Vyas urged the government to consider a "targeted and more permanent" solution including a social discount tariff.

Why it matters: The forecast price cap rise would more than wipe out the impact of the prime minister's promise to cut VAT from household electricity bills from October, which aimed to shrink bills by an average of about £45 a year.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

UK to train AI on Ukraine battlefield data

AI models trained on Ukrainian battlefield data will be used to stop protesters and foreign states targeting UK defence sites, railways and energy plants under a deal struck between London and Kyiv. The scheme will be piloted at a UK defence site using AI-optimised sensors in buried fibre-optic cables to detect different kinds of movement, with pilot projects already approved with three British AI companies: Sintela, Mind Foundry and Skyral. Private companies will be given access to data from Ukraine's Avengers AI lab - the first agreement of its kind in the UK - once approved by the MoD on a secure platform. Prime Minister Andy Burnham said it would be an opportunity for British companies to combine their AI expertise with Ukraine's battlefield data, which would "accelerate the development of capabilities that not only strengthen our national security but better protect our critical infrastructure and growth in every postcode here at home".

Source: The Guardian

Business rates cut 20% for pubs, clubs, live music venues

From April 2027 (the 2027/28 financial year), pubs, social clubs and live music venues across England will receive a 20% cut to their business rates bills, saving the typical pub an estimated £1,100 in the next financial year. The package affects nearly 32,000 businesses and is worth around £100 million a year. The 20% cut comes on top of a 15% relief already announced in January for pubs and live music venues for their 2026/27 bill.

Source: HM Treasury

Bank Rate held at 3.75% by MPC, July 2026

At its meeting ending 29 July 2026, the MPC voted 6-3 to maintain Bank Rate at 3.75%; three members voted to increase Bank Rate by 0.25 percentage points to 4%. CPI inflation has fallen to 2.6% since the previous meeting, although it is expected to rise later in the year as higher energy prices continue to pass through. The next decision is due 17 September 2026.

Source: Bank of England
