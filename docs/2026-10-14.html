1. Russia pounds Kyiv energy despite ceasefire

Russia launched a massive ballistic missile attack on Kyiv overnight, striking the Darnytska CHP-4 power plant and a residential apartment block. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said two people were killed and 21 injured in the strike on the apartment block, and that 'massive missile attacks' had knocked out power to parts of Kyiv. Zelensky said: 'Ukraine supported an energy ceasefire. Russia is targeting yet another power plant and the lives of our people.' It is the second night in a row Kyiv has been hit by Russian missiles, and Zelensky prepares to meet UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham at Downing Street on Wednesday to discuss accelerating air defence supplies.

Why it matters: Zelensky said the strike came with 'no early warning from our partners' and called on the United States to 'really see what is happening and who is responsible for this war continuing.'

Source: BBC

2. Russia leaves 600,000 without power in Ukraine

Russia's overnight assault on Ukrainian energy infrastructure left at least 600,000 people without power in the Kyiv and Kharkiv regions, President Zelensky said on Wednesday. Zelensky reported 'significant damage to facilities and equipment - at 26 facilities of our power system and four facilities in the oil and gas sector.' He described the strike as 'entirely deliberate and demonstrative in its scale', adding: 'Russia has never before shown such ostentatious disrespect toward the United States.' The attack came after Trump announced an 'energy ceasefire' on Sunday, which both Russian and Ukrainian authorities initially said they had no knowledge of.

Why it matters: Zelensky highlighted that the targeted ballistic missile strike should have been covered by the energy ceasefire announced by President Trump on Sunday, deepening questions about whether any agreement is holding.

Source: CNBC

3. Trump threatened Ukraine intel cut-off, Zelensky tells NPR

The Trump administration warned it could cut off intelligence-sharing with Ukraine unless Ukrainian forces stop long-range drone strikes on oil refineries inside Russia, Zelensky told NPR in an interview on Tuesday. Zelensky also refuted Trump's claim that Ukraine was to blame for rising diesel prices, pointing instead to the blocking of the Strait of Hormuz. In the US, diesel fuel costs an average of $6.27 per gallon, more than 70% above what it was a year ago when it was a little over $3.65 a gallon, according to AAA. Zelensky said Trump's decision to lift sanctions on Russian diesel would not bring down US fuel prices because 'the volume is not enough', but would give the Kremlin more money to spend on the war.

Why it matters: The White House did not confirm or deny the intel threat, referring NPR to Trump's comments that 'we are helping them and we are sharing intel.'

Source: NPR

4. Nord Stream trial opens in Hamburg

A Ukrainian man identified as Serhii K went on trial in Hamburg on Wednesday, accused of plotting with other Ukrainian military personnel to blow up Russia's Nord Stream gas pipelines to Germany in September 2022. He will become the first suspect to stand trial for the blasts, which destroyed three of the four gas pipelines in the Baltic Sea off the island of Bornholm on 26 September 2022. German prosecutors allege he was a serving officer in the Ukrainian army who led a group of professional divers, a boat captain, and an explosives expert. Serhii K, extradited from Italy late last year, denies the charges, as does a second suspect fighting extradition from Croatia.

Why it matters: The case is described as highly sensitive in Germany, seen as one of Ukraine's closest allies, with German prosecutors alleging the saboteurs acted 'by order of Ukrainian state agencies.'

Source: BBC

5. Bank of America fined $1.2bn for money laundering

Bank of America has been fined $1.2bn for failing to detect potential money laundering and other illegal activity, the US Treasury Department said on Tuesday. The bank failed to report over $5bn in suspicious activity to the government, $1bn of which posed national security and illicit finance risks. Failures included not flagging transactions possibly involving Chinese money laundering networks or trade-based laundering 'with a substantial nexus to the southwest border'. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the fine was the third-largest the department has ever imposed, and the OCC's $750m component marked the biggest penalty it has ever levied.

Why it matters: As part of the settlement, Bank of America admitted it 'willfully violated' the Bank Secrecy Act, and the bank said its third-quarter results will include a non-tax-deductible charge of $1.2bn impacting net income by approximately 17 cents per share.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Gales up to 70mph to hit Scotland today

A Met Office yellow wind warning is in force for parts of the Highlands and Western Isles on Wednesday from 10:00 until 19:00 BST, with gusts potentially touching 70mph in exposed coasts and hills around the middle of the day. The system carries the remnants of Hurricane Isaias, which made landfall in the southern United States last Friday before its remnants were picked up by a powerful jet stream. Widespread gusts of 50-60mph are likely across the warning area, with disruption possible to rail, air and ferry transport, delays on roads and bridges, and possible short-term power cuts. Outside the warning area, Shetland is also anticipated to see 50mph gusts, and 40mph gusts are possible in other parts of Scotland.

Source: BBC
