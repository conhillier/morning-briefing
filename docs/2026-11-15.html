1. US floats Donbas-for-NATO deal to Ukraine

US negotiators raised the idea of Ukraine withdrawing its remaining forces from the eastern Donbas region in exchange for NATO membership, President Volodymyr Zelensky has said. "The Americans raised a question: 'What if Ukraine joins Nato? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?' This was the first time this had been raised," Zelensky said in remarks released by his office on Saturday. He added that if he received a specific proposal containing security guarantees, no constraints on Ukraine's military, and funds to rebuild the country, he would put it to a referendum. Hours after Zelensky's remarks, Jared Kushner urged "confidentiality" in a sensitive process and referred to "private discussions" aimed at exploring new ideas about ending what he called a "horrible war".

Why it matters: Russia currently occupies roughly a fifth of Ukrainian territory and has insisted it will not agree to a ceasefire until a final peace deal is in place, including Ukraine ceding control of Donbas areas Russian troops have not yet captured, making any NATO-for-territory swap an extraordinarily difficult negotiation.

Source: BBC

2. Israel kills Hamas Rafah commander in Gaza

A senior Hamas figure who helped plan and oversee the 7 October 2023 attack on Israel has been killed in an Israeli airstrike on Al-Mawasi in the southern Gaza Strip. Samir Abu Younis, the newly appointed commander of the Rafah Brigade, was killed on Saturday, according to Hamas's Al-Qassam Brigades military wing. Younis, 53, had held his position since 15 September, following the death of the brigade's previous commander, Nael Abu Obeid, and had joined Hamas's armed wing in 1991. The IDF and Israel's security agency confirmed they had "eliminated" Younis, describing him as a "central figure" who had "acted with the aim of trying to restore the strength of the brigade and the Hamas terrorist organisation".

Why it matters: Since a ceasefire was reached last October, Israel has continued occasional strikes it says are targeting remaining Hamas militants, which it argues it has the right to do under the terms of the ceasefire agreement.

Source: BBC

3. SpaceX catches Starship upper stage from orbit

SpaceX successfully caught the upper stage, or spacecraft, portion of its massive Starship rocket from orbit on Saturday, a historic first for the company. The catch was made using "chopsticks" - mechanical arms attached to a launch tower - at SpaceX's facility in Starbase, Texas, during Test Flight 15, described as the most ambitious Starship test yet. The rocket's upper stage flew around the Earth 14 times before successfully landing on SpaceX's launch tower, nicknamed Mechazilla. During the first phase of the test on Friday, SpaceX failed to catch the booster and diverted it to splash down in the Gulf of Mexico, where it exploded before hitting the water.

Why it matters: Making Starship a reliable and fully reusable rocket is critical to SpaceX's ambitions, including sending more Starlink satellites into orbit, bringing astronauts and cargo to the Moon's surface for NASA, and Musk's vision to colonise Mars.

Source: CNBC

4. Bank of England signals further rate rises ahead

The Bank of England will probably need to raise interest rates next month and again in early 2027 to combat rising inflation, its chief economist Huw Pill has said. The earlier move nudging rates from 3.75% to 4% was described as a "significant step" but Pill said "we have further to go", with inflation expected to rise to 5% in spring next year driven by high oil and gas prices as a result of the Iran war. The signal affects an estimated 2.3 million homeowners who will have to refinance their loans next year.

Source: The Guardian

5. Microsoft services restored after global outage

Microsoft said early on Saturday morning that its services had been fully restored after a worldwide outage affected several of its tools, including Outlook, Teams and Copilot. The outage hit more than 70 countries, disrupting business operations, government services and personal users, with more than 430 million people using the affected products worldwide. Microsoft said the issue was introduced during a "network capacity upgrade" and was not caused by a cyber-attack or an artificial intelligence agent. Services began recovering by Friday afternoon UK time, nearly 24 hours after issues first emerged, with affected institutions ranging from the Japanese government and the Philippines Supreme Court to the Australian spy agency.

Why it matters: Experts said the event was a "stark reminder" of how heavily businesses and vital services rely on a small group of big tech providers, with one analyst noting it is "almost impossible for a business to function without tools such as Microsoft".

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Thousands march in Edinburgh against AI data centres

Thousands of people joined a protest in Edinburgh against the building of large-scale AI data centres, with demonstrators marching down the Royal Mile towards the Scottish Parliament. Protesters carried slogans such as "AI data centres threaten humanity" and voiced opposition to specific planned sites including in Cockenzie, East Lothian, and Larbert, Falkirk. A planned data centre at Chapelcross, Dumfries and Galloway, would have a capacity of 1,000MW, compared with the biggest current data centre in Scotland - DataVita's site in Airdrie, North Lanarkshire - which has a capacity of only 12MW. The Scottish government confirmed it would publish new planning guidance on data centres before the end of the year and said all new data centre applications with a power capacity exceeding 50 megawatts must now submit an Environmental Impact Assessment.

Source: BBC

Bank Rate raised to 4% in November 2026

The MPC voted 8-1 to increase Bank Rate by 0.25 percentage points to 4% at its meeting ending 4 November 2026; one member voted for a larger 0.5 percentage point rise to 4.25%. UK CPI inflation increased to 3.5% in September and is now expected to rise to around 5% by the middle of next year before falling as energy price effects fade. The next rate decision is due 17 December 2026.
