1. Six killed in Krakow gun attack

At least six people were killed and more than a dozen others injured when a gunman opened fire in central Krakow, Poland's second largest city. The attack began shortly before 15:00 local time in Krakow's Main Market Square, with police saying a man drove into the square before firing "at random people from his car". A 25-year-old Polish man was shot by armed officers within three minutes of the attack beginning and remains in hospital after surgery. Polish police confirmed that three further bodies found at the gunman's home were his parents and his 21-year-old sister.

Why it matters: Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said: "Until now in Poland we haven't had such drastic cases of terrorism...that's what we have to call this cruel attack on people's lives here in Krakow." Krakow Mayor Lukasz Gibala has signed an order declaring a period of mourning lasting until Wednesday, 11 November.

Source: BBC

2. Israel opposition leader tasked to form government

Israeli President Isaac Herzog has tasked opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot with forming a new coalition government after last week's election left neither Eisenkot's bloc nor Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc with a majority. Eisenkot will have 28 days to try to form a new administration, with a potential 14-day extension. Netanyahu's right-wing Likud party and its allies won 53 seats in the 120-seat Knesset, while Eisenkot's centrist Yashar party and three other opposition parties won 48 seats. Two Arab-led alliances together won an unprecedented 19 seats, following a surge in turnout among Arab voters, positioning them as potential kingmakers.

Why it matters: Eisenkot received 67 recommendations for the premiership compared to Netanyahu's 46 during the president's consultations, though forming a majority coalition remains complex given pledges by most Jewish parties not to work with Arab parties.

Source: BBC

4. Houthis say 33 killed in Saudi air strike

Media linked to Yemen's Houthi movement reports that at least 33 people, including women and children, were killed in air strikes by Saudi Arabia on the town of al-Shariyah, east of the capital Sanaa. Houthi-run Saba news agency said the attack was carried out by Saudi Arabia on Saturday and that 39 people were injured. Live video footage on Houthi-run Al Masirah television showed rescuers pulling bodies from rubble, with the bodies of young children visible among those recovered. Houthi leader Mahdi al-Mashat has vowed the group will take revenge for the latest attacks.

Why it matters: The United Nations says more than 1,600 people have been killed and more than 300,000 displaced since fighting resumed in Yemen.

Source: BBC

5. Anthropic IPO faces fierce AI backlash

AI company Anthropic is preparing for a Nasdaq IPO likely targeting mid-November, with investors seeing the company aiming for a valuation of $1 trillion to $1.5 trillion, down from a target of $2 trillion floated in recent months. The listing comes as voters in the US midterm elections opposed data center-related ballot measures in 40 out of 41 cases, and more than half of voters said AI data centers were a "crucial" or "major factor" in who they supported for Congress, according to an NBC News exit poll. Anthropic said in an amended IPO prospectus this week that it has entered into purchase commitments and lease agreements with an aggregate committed spend of about $895.4 billion, substantially all related to compute and infrastructure. The Nasdaq suffered its steepest drop in 19 months on Thursday, with chipmakers and neoclouds hit hardest.

Why it matters: Anthropic would become the first pure-play AI company to hit the public market, and in its prospectus the company warned that opposition to data center development has become more organised across jurisdictions and that its public profile could draw particular scrutiny to associated projects.

Source: CNBC

Closer to Home

Ukrainian refugee faces PS9,000 Scots property tax

A Ukrainian woman who fled to Scotland in 2023 has been told she could face a PS9,000 Additional Dwelling Supplement (ADS) charge because she still owns a flat in Kyiv that she bought in 2018, before Russia's invasion. Kateryna Stepanovych, 38, and her Scottish partner Mattias Lindgren discovered the charge when they tried to buy a house in North Ayrshire, and had to withdraw their offer as they could not afford the additional cost. Under Revenue Scotland's rules, the ADS applies an additional 8% charge if buyers already own another property, including overseas. Deputy First Minister Jenny Gilruth responded to the couple's MSP with a letter stating there are "no provisions within the legislation that allow either the Scottish government or Revenue Scotland to disapply ADS on a discretionary or case-by-case basis."

Source: BBC
