1. Iran launches multi-country missile barrage at US

Tehran launched missile and drone attacks at US targets in Jordan, Kuwait, Iraq and Bahrain after American forces resumed strikes against Iran on Tuesday. The IRGC fired a barrage of ballistic missiles at a US Marine base in Jordan's Aqaba, of which the Jordanian Armed Forces said it intercepted 13 ballistic missiles - 10 destroyed, with three falling in remote areas and no deaths or injuries. Iran's IRGC also claimed to have struck US bases in Iraq's autonomous Kurdistan region, destroying a maintenance centre, warehouses and equipment. Several explosions were also reported in Bahrain, according to Iranian state media.

Why it matters: The US-Iran ceasefire agreement signed in June has expired, leading to a resumption of hostilities, with both sides striking military and reportedly civilian targets across the region.

Source: BBC

2. US strike kills five at Iranian wedding party

The Iranian Red Crescent said five people, including a child, were killed and more than 50 injured when shrapnel from a US missile attack hit a home where a wedding celebration was taking place in Sirik in southern Iran. Iranian state media and the Red Crescent reported that civilian sites on Iran's southern coast had been bombed, with a fishing pier and a Ports and Maritime Organization tower in Sirik also among locations struck. US Navy Captain Tim Hawkins, speaking on behalf of Centcom, told the BBC it was aware of reports from Iranian state media of the attack in Sirik, adding: "The US military never targets civilians, unlike the IRGC." US Central Command said the strikes followed recent attempted attacks by Iran's IRGC against commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz.

Why it matters: The reported civilian deaths at the wedding have directly triggered Iran's latest multi-country missile barrage, threatening to further escalate the conflict.

Source: BBC

3. UK 30-year borrowing costs hit 28-year high

The yield on a 30-year gilt rose to 5.89% on Tuesday, the highest since 1998, putting further pressure on Prime Minister Andy Burnham ahead of his first Budget next month. The yield on the benchmark 10-year gilt also rose to 5.22%, its highest rate since June 2008 at the height of the global financial crisis. Burnham told the House of Commons that the economy and cost of living were "the biggest issues facing the country", pledging "fiscal responsibility" as his government's bedrock. US borrowing costs also hit a fresh high on Tuesday as renewed strikes in the Middle East pushed up oil prices and heightened concerns over inflation.

Why it matters: Higher borrowing costs will reduce the amount of headroom the government has against its self-imposed fiscal rules, limiting what Chancellor John Healey can spend on consumer-friendly measures to ease the cost of living.

Source: BBC

4. Israel expanding Lebanon occupation, BBC finds

A BBC team visiting southern Lebanon found that Israel has established four new bases in invaded areas, bringing the total number of such positions to nine, according to Unifil.

Why it matters: Human rights groups say some of Israel's actions in southern Lebanon amount to the deliberate destruction of civilian infrastructure, a possible war crime, with hundreds of thousands of Lebanese remaining displaced.

Source: BBC

5. AI poses global economic downturn risk, Bailey warns

Bank of England governor Andrew Bailey warned G20 finance ministers that a collapse of growth in the AI sector could lead to a "future market correction" that spreads worldwide. Writing in his capacity as chairman of the Financial Stability Board, Bailey said a combination of highly priced stock markets, increased borrowing by investors, and the growing concentration of money into a small number of major technology companies could amplify any future correction.

Why it matters: There is growing concern that AI companies are increasingly developing models that can easily override the safeguarding systems of banks and financial centres, compounding the systemic risk Bailey describes.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Glasgow gets PS2 bus fare cap from Monday

Bus fares in the west of Scotland will be capped at PS2 from Monday, First Minister John Swinney has announced, covering journeys in Glasgow, Renfrewshire, Ayrshire, Dunbartonshire, Lanarkshire and Inverclyde. In his programme for government, Swinney also confirmed plans to cut Scotland's health boards from 14 to two in what he described as the "most significant structural reform in the NHS in the era of devolution". He vowed that by 2031, no patient will wait longer than 26 weeks for the treatment they need. The trade union Unite branded the health board plan a "slash and burn programme for the NHS", with general secretary Sharon Graham warning it would "inevitably result in swingeing cuts and thousands of job losses across the public sector".

Source: BBC
