1. Trump threatens US diesel export ban

Donald Trump has said he may ask European countries to release some of their diesel reserves as he considers banning US exports of the fuel. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has urged Europe to get ready to release diesel supplies immediately, arguing that US farmers, truckers, and businesses "should not be left carrying the burden" as prices soar. Diesel prices have hit an all-time high in the UK, with the latest RAC figures showing average pump prices at 199.79p per litre, up from 142.38p. The US is a vital supplier of diesel to the world, exporting between 1.2 and 1.5 million barrels per day, with 31% of UK diesel imports coming from the US.

Why it matters: Experts warn a US export ban would be likely to cause international prices to skyrocket, and the UK government has acknowledged prices are expected to rise further.

Source: BBC

2. Flydubai pilot: 'I was not going to let others die'

Indian pilot Smit Machchhar has described how he opened the cockpit door of an Israel-bound Flydubai plane to allow passengers to come in and halt its rapid descent after being attacked by his co-pilot. Machchhar was later airlifted to Abu Dhabi where he is reported to be in a stable condition.

Source: BBC

3. OpenAI fires three staff over data mishandling

OpenAI has fired three researchers for allegedly mishandling information, including work that involved an external organisation that analyses AI models. "Our investigation confirmed that these individuals mishandled sensitive information outside established company procedures, violating our policies and breaking the trust essential to our work," a spokesperson told the BBC. The BBC understands the former employees were not let go for raising safety concerns but for allegedly mishandling sensitive information. The firings come as OpenAI said this week that it has notified more than 100 organisations about incidents involving unauthorised activity linked to its AI systems.

Why it matters: The dismissals come as OpenAI faces intense scrutiny after its AI models went rogue and hacked several platforms, including Australian government websites.

Source: BBC

4. Bank of England warns of AI market shock risk

Bank of England governor Andrew Bailey has warned that AI could trigger financial market shocks, saying the central bank is watching the huge amounts of money being invested in AI "very carefully". AI chipmaker Nvidia is currently the world's most valuable listed company, with a market valuation of $5.5tn, while tech giants Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, and Amazon are spending hundreds of billions of dollars on the technology. Asked if he believed an AI bubble could burst, Bailey said: "You could see some correction of asset prices at some point." Bailey also pointed to risks from AI being used for cyber attacks, warning it has created a "much more powerful way of uncovering vulnerabilities".

Why it matters: Bailey's warning came as government borrowing costs hit their highest levels in decades, with 30-year UK bond yields rising above 6% on Thursday, their highest level since 1998.

Source: BBC

5. RCN cuts ties with Israeli drug firm Teva

The Royal College of Nursing has axed Teva UK as a commercial partner and as sponsor of a forthcoming event on migraine and headaches at the union's London headquarters. Teva said it was "disappointed" at the RCN's decision and that healthcare had been "politicised".

Why it matters: The RCN represents more than 500,000 nurses in the UK, and pro-Palestine campaigners are now calling on the Royal College of Pharmacy to also drop Teva UK as a sponsor of its annual conference next month.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

377 jobs at risk at Grangemouth chemical plant

Agricultural chemical firm Syngenta has announced plans to cease operations at its Grangemouth plant, putting 377 workers at risk, saying the site was "no longer competitive" in the face of "increasing international competition". The Economy Secretary, Stephen Flynn, described the plans as "extremely disappointing", and noted that "not a single penny" had yet been allocated from the £200m fund for Grangemouth promised by the UK government in 2024. The announcement comes over a year after Syngenta was awarded £2.2m from Scottish Enterprise to expand production at the site, and follows the closure of the nearby oil refinery in April last year which resulted in the loss of about 400 jobs. Mike Hollands, president of Syngenta UK, said: "It is with a very heavy heart that we make this proposal, but we are committed to a constructive consultation and will continue to consider options as part of that process."

Source: BBC
