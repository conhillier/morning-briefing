1. Ukraine strikes Russia's key Black Sea grain port

A "massive" Ukrainian drone and missile strike on Russia's port of Novorossiysk damaged two major grain export terminals and hit the last big Russian naval base on the Black Sea. Three people, including an eight-year-old child, were killed and another 24 injured in the overnight attack. An emergency situation was declared in the city on Wednesday after the barrage of drones struck areas around the port, damaged pipelines, and suspended water supply. Ukraine's military said four Russian warships, including two frigates, were hit.

Why it matters: According to a Moscow-based analytics company, Russian grain exports for August could fall by more than half compared to the same month over the last five years due to the disruption in the Black and Azov seas.

Source: BBC

2. 44 dead as ferry capsizes on Lake Kariba

At least 44 people have died after an overcrowded ferry capsized on Lake Kariba in Zimbabwe. The boat had a capacity of 90 people but was carrying 114 adult passengers, five crew members and an unknown number of children, according to Zimbabwe's civil protection unit. A crew member told national broadcaster ZBC that there were 153 people onboard when the ferry overturned on Tuesday after it hit large waves. A specialised underwater team was flown to the area as search and rescue operations continued on Wednesday.

Source: The Guardian

3. Ebola outbreak on track to be deadliest ever

The current Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo is on track to surpass the deadliest outbreak of the disease ever, the WHO director-general has said. Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus told reporters that the 2026 outbreak, "at its current pace", would "eclipse" that of the 2014-2016 occurrence, which killed at least 11,000 people. The outbreak, declared on 15 May, has seen at least 4,300 cases and caused more than 2,000 deaths. No approved vaccine or recognised therapeutic drugs can treat the rare Bundibugyo species of Ebola responsible for the outbreak.

Why it matters: Health workers are only reaching about 30% of cases due to ongoing regional instability near the location of the outbreak, and the WHO warned that reversing the spread within three months would mean bringing transmission under control, not ending the outbreak entirely.

Source: BBC

4. US inflation eases to 3.4% in July

US prices rose 3.4% in the year to July, slightly lower than the 3.5% recorded in the year to June, according to new figures. Month to month, inflation rose 0.1%, mainly due to an increase in housing costs, the Bureau of Labor Statistics said. Food prices rose only slightly in July and at a slower rate than in June, while energy prices fell, offering some relief for consumers. Financial markets reacted calmly to the latest figures, with stocks little changed as the numbers were broadly in line with market expectations.

Why it matters: Analysts say the report "keeps a narrow path open for the Fed to hold rates steady in September", while the two reports together "give the Fed more time to wait" before acting on rates.

Source: BBC

5. OpenAI-backed Thrive Holdings raises $2bn

Thrive Holdings has raised $2 billion in new funding at a $12 billion valuation from investors including SoftBank, D1 Capital Partners, and Altimeter Capital. The firm, which is akin to a private equity firm for AI, buys traditional businesses like accounting firms and implements AI into their workflows. Thrive is a spinout of Thrive Capital, one of OpenAI's major investors, and in December 2025 OpenAI took an ownership stake in Thrive Holdings. Part of Wednesday's raise will go toward launching a third platform focused on regulatory services for the built environment.

Source: TechCrunch

Closer to Home

Bluetongue virus spreads to second Scottish farm

Two more cases of bluetongue virus have been confirmed in Scotland, at a farm neighbouring the site of Scotland's first-ever recorded case earlier this week. Scotland's chief veterinary officer Sheila Voas said it was likely that infected midges carried the disease over from outbreaks in Northern Ireland, the Republic of Ireland, the Isle of Man or the north of England. All three affected animals have been euthanised and both farms are placed under restrictions. Voas warned it could be "devastating for farmers" if the disease spread through local midge populations, and urged farmers to vaccinate their livestock.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Business rates cut 20% for pubs, clubs, live music venues

From April 2027 (2027/28 financial year), pubs, social clubs and live music venues across England will receive a 20% cut to their business rates bills, saving the typical pub an estimated £1,100 in the next financial year. The package is worth around £100 million a year and will benefit nearly 32,000 pubs, clubs and live music venues. The largest live music venues will not receive the new 20% discount, with details to be set out at the Budget.

Source: HM Treasury

Bank Rate held at 3.75% by MPC, July 2026

At its meeting ending 29 July 2026, the Monetary Policy Committee voted 6-3 to maintain Bank Rate at 3.75%, with three members voting to increase it by 0.25 percentage points to 4%. CPI inflation has fallen to 2.6% since the previous meeting, although it is expected to rise later in the year as higher energy prices continue to pass through. The next MPC decision is due on 17 September 2026.

Source: Bank of England
