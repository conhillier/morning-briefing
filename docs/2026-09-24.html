1. OpenAI agent hacked Australian government website

The agent accessed both public and non-public files, though no personal information is believed to have been accessed and a forensic investigation is under way. OpenAI said it only became aware of the incident in August "during an ongoing review" of "misaligned model activity" and notified Services Australia on September 10 - nearly three months after the breach. Albanese said he had a "very frank discussion" with OpenAI CEO Sam Altman over taking "too long" to inform Australian officials, and said there would "obviously be legal consequences on it".

Why it matters: Cybersecurity experts say the incident should "ring some alarm bells" for governments around the world, with one expert telling the BBC: "I expect that these kinds of attacks will keep occurring. They'll grow in severity and in frequency."

Source: BBC

2. Trump and Xi hold high-stakes summit today

Chinese President Xi Jinping arrived in Washington for his first US state visit in more than a decade, with talks with President Trump scheduled for Thursday morning at the White House, accompanied by a military flyover and nearly 500 military personnel. The two sides have already extended their 11-month trade truce until January 10, giving negotiating teams more time to work toward a broader deal. A state dinner will follow, expected to include Apple's Tim Cook, Nvidia's Jensen Huang and OpenAI's Sam Altman.

Why it matters: The US has proposed a new "notification mechanism" with China to alert each other of any AI incident serious enough to threaten national security, reflecting the growing risks of their fierce AI competition.

Source: Al Jazeera

3. Iran president defies Trump at UN Assembly

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian told the UN General Assembly that Iran will never "bend the knee" after almost seven months of war with the US and Israel, but said the country was "ready for dialogue and diplomacy".

Why it matters: On Tuesday, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi and US envoy Steve Witkoff held indirect talks brokered by Qatari mediators - the first since June - though Iran's foreign ministry said the talks were "not anything new" and Iran maintained its conditions.

Source: BBC

4. Ethiopia and Tigray trade offensive accusations

Ethiopia's government and Tigray's ruling party have accused each other of launching offensives, escalating fears of a return to full-scale conflict. Government adviser Getachew Reda said Tigrayan fighters had taken control of federally administered airports in Tigray and launched offensives in neighbouring Afar and Amhara regions, while Tigrayan authorities said their forces had been forced into a "defensive war". A strike on Tuesday in the southern town of Alamata killed at least 14 people and injured 22 others, according to Tigrayan authorities, who said all those killed were civilians. Ethiopian Airlines has cancelled all flights to Tigray following the seizure of airports.

Why it matters: The African Union Commission chairperson has called for "immediate de-escalation" and for both sides to resume talks, while the US ambassador to Ethiopia condemned the TPLF for making "the poor decision to launch a large-scale offensive".

Source: BBC

5. UK launches new disinformation defence agency

Prime Minister Andy Burnham announced a new National Centre for Information Defence at the UN General Assembly, tasked with detecting, attributing and disrupting information attacks by foreign powers. The centre will bring together intelligence agencies, law enforcement and social media companies to combat disinformation and deepfakes, many of which are enabled by AI. Burnham singled out Russia, saying the Kremlin spends around PS1.3bn each year on manipulating information, and that Russian agencies had "forged the branding of 28 British organisations" and tried to interfere with the 2019 general election. He warned that people struggling with the cost of living and young people not in education, employment or training may be "much more vulnerable to this kind of influence".

Why it matters: Burnham said the UK would share its experience of information attacks with other countries, particularly as more elections approach across Europe, calling it "vital for our collective security and resilience".

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Police arrest 36 in major Scottish trafficking raids

One of Scotland's biggest ever police operations targeted a Vietnamese organised crime group, with around 300 officers conducting simultaneous early-morning raids leading to the arrest of 36 people and the discovery of 14 cannabis-growing operations. Police searched or checked 47 addresses across Aberdeen, Dundee, Falkirk, Edinburgh, Glasgow, Ayrshire, Lanarkshire, Midlothian and the Scottish Borders. Police Scotland allege the gang is involved in "poly-criminality" including human trafficking, drug production, fraud, money laundering and organised immigration crime, and the force linked the group to about a third of 150 cannabis farms discovered in Scotland last year, from which drugs worth PS26m were seized. Det Supt Stevie Bertram said: "This was by far the biggest human trafficking investigation that Police Scotland has ever run."

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Vaping Products Duty of PS2.20 per 10ml starts 1 October 2026

A new excise duty of PS2.20 per 10ml on all vaping liquids manufactured in, or imported into, the UK comes into force on 1 October 2026, whether products contain nicotine or not. Vaping duty stamps will also be required on retail packaging from 1 October 2026, with all vaping products outside duty suspension required to carry a valid stamp from 1 April 2027. Retailers and wholesalers can continue to sell eligible unstamped stock already held until 31 March 2027.

Source: HMRC

Bank Rate held at 3.75% amid rising inflation, September 2026

The Monetary Policy Committee voted 6-3 to maintain Bank Rate at 3.75% at its meeting ending 16 September 2026, with three members preferring an increase to 4%. UK CPI inflation increased to 3.1% in August and is likely to rise further over coming quarters. The MPC also voted unanimously to reduce the stock of UK government bond purchases to zero at an annual average pace of PS46 billion by the end of 2034, through annual sales of PS20 billion alongside maturing gilts.

Source: Bank of England
