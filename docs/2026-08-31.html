1. US and Iran trade strikes in Hormuz

American forces attacked two rocket launchers on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz, killing two people and injuring two others according to Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, which vowed "response and punishment". US Central Command said it had taken "limited, precise action" against "minelaying forces posing an imminent threat in the Strait of Hormuz". Iran said it struck US military bases in Jordan in response, with Jordan's army saying it intercepted eight missiles early on Monday morning. Separately, Iran's state TV said the Iranian army had targeted an air base in the UAE with drones, though the UAE's Ministry of Defence said reports that Al-Minhad Air Base was targeted were "unfounded".

Why it matters: The US strike is the first known attack on Iran since President Donald Trump paused a renewed military campaign in late July, and Iran is currently demanding that tanker traffic pass by Larak Island for checking, reportedly forcing vessels to pay $2m (£1.5m) to cross.

Source: BBC

2. Nepal flood toll hits 903, 900 workers trapped

The unprecedented deluge of ice, rock, mud and debris on the Nepal-Tibet border has killed 903 people with 4,247 missing in Nepal, according to its disaster authority. Nepal's rescue teams, aided by Chinese and Indian experts, are racing to save more than 900 workers believed to be trapped underground at hydropower projects hit by the floods. Nepal army spokesperson Raja Ram Basnet said "a large amount of debris has been deposited and that is posing us a big challenge to open the tunnels". In addition to the trapped hydropower workers, some 500 workers from the Truck and Container Association are also missing, and the Red Cross has estimated more than 90,000 people are likely to have been affected.

Why it matters: Almost 40 kilometres of roads have been absolutely swept away, creating severe challenges in getting aid to communities, while rescue operations have been suspended several times since Friday because of bad weather and concerns that a lake formed by the disaster could trigger new flooding.

Source: Al Jazeera

3. Russia threatens massive Ukraine energy strikes

Russia's Defense Ministry announced it is planning "massive strikes" on Ukraine's energy infrastructure, framing the planned attacks as a response to continued Ukrainian strikes against Russian energy sites. The death toll from a Russian strike on a warehouse west of Kyiv on Friday has climbed to 38, with four people missing, according to Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. Ukrainian officials said the strike hit an ammunition storage facility in the village of Myla, triggering fires and explosions, and Zelenskyy described it as a "terrible situation" and a consequence of "terrible negligence on the part of those who stored explosives right next to people".

Why it matters: Ukrainian officials, drone executives and national security experts have previously warned that the country could face another extremely difficult winter amid fears of a sustained barrage of military attacks on its power grid during freezing temperatures.

Source: CNBC

4. Ferry capsizes off Cyprus, eight dead

At least eight people have died and 18 others are still missing after a passenger boat capsized off northern Cyprus, according to Unal Ustel, prime minister of the self-proclaimed Turkish republic there. The ferry was carrying 259 passengers and eight crew members when it began taking on water after departing the coastal town of Kyrenia on Sunday, and Ustel said the boat was hit by two waves before it capsized. The ship's captain and seven crew members have been detained as part of an investigation into "how this tragedy occurred". The boat has now sunk and authorities are attempting to recover its black box recorder, while a search and rescue operation with Turkish authorities remains under way.

Source: BBC

5. Bank of England warns AI threatens financial stability

Bank of England governor Andrew Bailey has written to G20 finance ministers and central bank governors warning that "frontier" AI models were "showing increasingly sophisticated autonomy and problem-solving abilities, as well as threat capabilities". In a two-page letter sent before their meeting in North Carolina this week, Bailey said frontier AI risked destabilising the "highly interconnected" global financial system via cyber-disruption that "can spread across jurisdictions". He also noted that "many jurisdictions do not have the protocols in place to manage the development, release, and deployment of advanced frontier AI models, heightening risks for the financial sector and beyond". The letter also raised concerns about increased use of leverage in bond and equity markets combining with high valuations in concentrated financial markets, particularly fuelled by investor optimism about AI.

Why it matters: Bailey warned that "a large shock or combination of shocks could concurrently trigger multiple vulnerabilities", calling on those tasked with safeguarding the world's financial systems to prioritise "appropriate steps to support safe and responsible model release and deployment on a global basis".

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Scottish Labour candidates back North Sea drilling

The two candidates in the Scottish Labour leadership race, MSPs Joe Fagan and Michael Marra, have both called for new drilling licences in the North Sea to be approved "as soon as possible". Both said the Rosebank oil and Jackdaw gas fields offered cleaner methods of extraction than could be relied upon abroad, and that approval was vital to support jobs. Fagan warned of offshore supply chains being "destroyed" if there was no movement, while Marra said drilling would be "vital" for the north-east of Scotland as well as boosting public finances. The winner of the leadership contest, which replaces Anas Sarwar who announced he was joining Andy Burnham's UK government via the House of Lords, is due to be announced on Saturday 19 September.

Source: BBC
