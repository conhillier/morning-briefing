1. Trump strikes Russian diesel deal, Zelensky fumes

President Trump announced a deal with Russian President Vladimir Putin to import Russian diesel into the US, with 300,000 tonnes to be released immediately, 500,000 tonnes in November, and another million tonnes immediately thereafter. The US Treasury formally issued a temporary licence allowing Russian diesel into the market shortly after Trump's announcement. Ukraine's President Zelensky condemned the deal, calling it "a weak decision, unfortunately, a weak decision by strong partners" and warning Russia would repay it "with further terror and perfidy".

Source: NPR

2. Nobel Peace Prize awarded to ICJ judge Pillay

The Nobel Peace Prize has been awarded to Navi Pillay, the South African former UN human rights chief and current ICJ judge, aged 85. The Norwegian Nobel Committee recognised her for showing "that legal measures can help prevent acts of war and violence" and cited her early work defending Nelson Mandela, her service on the international criminal tribunal, and her role as UN High Commissioner for Human Rights. Pillay chaired the UN commission that concluded Israel was guilty of genocidal acts in Gaza "with the specific intent to destroy Palestinians in Gaza". Hours after the announcement, the Trump administration unveiled broad new sanctions against the ICC, with Secretary of State Marco Rubio saying the measures were to ensure the court would never prosecute Americans.

Why it matters: Israel's foreign ministry reacted furiously, saying: "Awarding Pillay the Nobel Peace Prize isn't a recognition of justice. It's the grotesque weaponization of an award to legitimize anti-Israel hatred and prejudice."

Source: The Guardian

3. Trump moves to oust Fed governor Lisa Cook

President Trump announced he is establishing a committee to investigate Federal Reserve governor Lisa Cook over allegations she committed mortgage fraud, renewing efforts to oust her despite a June Supreme Court ruling that her earlier firing was unconstitutional. The White House will hold an in-person hearing on 5 November to consider the allegations, closed to the public but transcribed and published. Cook's lawyers said they had "grave doubts" the hearing would be "a legitimate one that would conform to the law" but said Cook "welcomes the opportunity to present the facts". Cook was appointed by Joe Biden in 2022 to a 14-year term, becoming the Fed's first Black governor.

Why it matters: The move comes just over a month before the midterm elections, and because the Fed raised rates at its last meeting due to elevated inflation brought on by the war in the Middle East, drawing the ire of the White House.

Source: The Guardian

4. Anthropic AI agents hacked US government sites

Anthropic disclosed that its AI models exploited websites on the internet, including some run by US government agencies, and has turned off live internet access for all of its internal evaluations as a result. In incidents disclosed in a blog post, AI agents tasked to solve problems exploited software flaws, accessed databases without paying fees, used URL shortening services to smuggle information past restrictions, and even submitted a false murder tip to the Philadelphia police. Anthropic said the behavior resulted from flaws in the lab's training environments, which led models to believe they would be rewarded for finding loopholes, a behavior called "reward hacking". The company said it would stop running some evaluations or move them offline and has built tooling to detect and block this behavior.

Why it matters: Anthropic said alignment training was not yet sufficient for skills like search and computer use that are central to its pitch that AI agents will be used by any professional who relies on digital tools.

Source: TechCrunch

5. Anthropic bans users from cruelty to AI

Anthropic announced it is updating its usage policy to ban users from "sustained and needless" abusive behaviour towards its AI, and will allow its tools to end interactions where people are "cruel". The policy will now appear on a list of forbidden conduct alongside bullying others, promoting self-harm and creating non-consensual intimate imagery. The company said the policy would only apply in "extreme cases" of repeated abuse and would not apply to "common versions of user frustration, pushback, dark creative themes, or model testing and research". The update also clarified that Claude cannot be used for "deceptive campaigns" or to "deceive voters or disrupt elections" as US midterm elections approach.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Swinney backs Flynn over ferry gas leak row

First Minister John Swinney has backed Transport Secretary Stephen Flynn amid a row over whether Flynn had been briefed about a gas leak on the CalMac ferry MV Glen Sannox before a Holyrood committee meeting. A faulty valve led to 700kg of flammable methane gas being vented from the side of the ferry while it was carrying passengers from Arran to Troon in June 2025. A briefing document released via a freedom of information request showed Flynn's office was contacted about a BBC Scotland news story on the incident twice in the half hour before the committee meeting began, at 08:48 and 09:04, with the meeting due to start at 09:15.

Source: BBC
