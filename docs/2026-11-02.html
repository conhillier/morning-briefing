1. Binance users pull $3.6bn after record hack

Binance customers withdrew $3.6bn (£2.7bn) of assets from the world's biggest cryptocurrency exchange as it reopened on Sunday following what is believed to be the largest hack in history. Between 06:00 and 15:30 GMT on 1 November, Binance processed $4.649bn in withdrawals and $1.01bn in deposits, resulting in a net outflow of approximately $3.64bn. Sunday's net withdrawals account for just over 2% of the $171bn of assets under its management. The attack has been attributed to TraderTraitor, a North Korean cybercrime group also known as the Lazarus Group, which stole a then-record $1.5bn from crypto exchange Bybit in 2025.

Source: BBC

2. Binance scammers target hack victims with fake compensation

Crypto scammers are now in "overdrive", Binance founder Changpeng Zhao has warned, as customers pulled billions in funds from the platform following the $6.3bn hack. Scammers appear to be targeting people affected by the breach by offering fake compensation rewards, with screenshots circulating online that promise $150 in free compensation for withdrawal suspensions. Binance has confirmed that it is false information and the screenshots are digitally altered. The hack also spooked broader crypto markets, with US spot Bitcoin ETFs recording $643 million in net outflows on Friday, their first trading session following the hack.

Why it matters: The odds of Bitcoin hitting the $100,000 level by the end of the year have fallen to just 3.5%, according to Nick Forster, chief executive of Derive.xyz, down from a 6% probability priced just before the hack.

Source: The Guardian

3. Ryanair warns of 20% fare rises in 2027

Ryanair CEO Michael O'Leary told CNBC on Monday that European airfares are set to rise "significantly," growing by as much as 20% next year as fuel hedges expire. The budget carrier's profit fell 15% to 2.15 billion euros ($2.3 billion) in the first fiscal half of the year, as the price of its 20% unhedged jet fuel doubled.

Why it matters: Ryanair's cost review will also include the use of AI to drive further efficiencies, as the airline tries to limit the financial damage from soaring fuel costs.

Source: CNBC

4. Qatar leads urgent Iran mediation talks

Qatar has been leading intense mediation efforts over the past 72 hours to prevent a new round of possible US attacks on Iran after Tuesday's midterm elections, with mediators visiting Tehran and an Iranian delegation travelling to Doha. Iran's foreign minister Abbas Araghchi said there is "still no definitive conclusion" to the mediation efforts, but noted talk of a two-day plan that would include Iran not attacking ships in the Strait of Hormuz to allow for direct talks to begin again between Washington and Tehran. Iran's supreme leader Mojtaba Khamenei told senior commanders this weekend that any threat to the country must be met with an immediate response "without geographical limitations." Brent crude oil is 60% higher today than it was before the war, which began eight months ago.

Why it matters: The lives of tens of millions of people in the Gulf hinge on President Trump's decision, according to NPR's reporting from the region, with no one - from senior European diplomats to Arab military generals - knowing what Trump will do.

Source: NPR

5. UN Western Sahara peacekeeping mission ends after 35 years

The UN's long-running peacekeeping mission for the disputed Western Sahara ceased operations on Sunday after 35 years, following a US-Russia standoff at UN headquarters. In the UN Security Council on Friday, Russia vetoed a draft US resolution that would have supported Morocco's demand for sovereignty over Western Sahara, then a rival Russian resolution to extend the mission was voted down, leading to an abrupt end of the force's mandate. The mission, known as MINURSO, was established in 1991 to monitor a referendum on self-determination that never materialised, and had a 2026-2027 budget of more than $62 million. The UN secretary-general "remains concerned about the security situation throughout the Territory and the risk of escalation of hostilities," his spokesperson said.

Why it matters: The end of the mission risks further straining ties between Morocco and Algeria and fueling an arms race, as the Polisario Front withdrew from the ceasefire in 2020 and the territory has since remained the scene of tensions and low-intensity hostilities.

Source: NPR

Closer to Home

Virgin Media O2 to cut thousands of jobs

Thousands of Virgin Media O2 staff are facing redundancy as the telecoms firm attempts to save money in the face of billions of pounds of debt and tougher competition. The broadband provider has not finalised the number of roles to be cut but it is likely to be a sizeable chunk of its 16,000-strong workforce, the BBC understands. As of 30 June, Virgin Media O2's total debt stands at £27.2bn while it holds assets worth £45.9bn. The BBC has also been told the firm was highly likely to announce it will stop all investment in fibre in its results on Tuesday, following reports it is close to agreeing a deal to use rival BT's fibre broadband network.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Vaping Products Duty at 2.20 pounds per 10ml from 1 October 2026

Vaping Products Duty came into force on 1 October 2026, charged at 2.20 pounds per 10ml of vaping liquid, applying to all vaping liquids manufactured in or imported into the UK. Tobacco duty also increased on the same date by a one-off pro-rata amount of 2.20 pounds per 100 cigarettes or per 50 grams of tobacco, in addition to the standard tobacco duty escalator. From 1 April 2027, all vaping products sold in the UK must carry a valid vaping duty stamp; wholesalers and retailers have a six-month grace period until 31 March 2027 to sell existing eligible unstamped stock.

Source: HMRC

Companies House pauses profit and loss filing requirement for small companies

From April 2028, the government will pause the requirement for small companies and micro-entities to file profit and loss accounts with Companies House, allowing abridged accounts and the option to fillet profit and loss information to continue after 1 April 2028. Mandatory software filing of accounts in iXBRL format using commercial software will proceed as planned from April 2028, with web and paper-based filing systems closing for accounts filings on that date.

Source: Companies House
