1. Joshua Wong jailed 10 more years in Hong Kong

Hong Kong pro-democracy activist Joshua Wong has been sentenced to an additional 10 years in jail after the city's High Court found him guilty of colluding with foreign entities in violation of the national security law. The sentence comes just two months before he was expected to be released in January 2027, having already been in prison since 2020. High Court Judge William Tam said he considered a 13-year prison sentence as a starting point but reduced it to 10 because of Wong's guilty plea and mitigation. Wong, now 30, was accused of conspiring with self-exiled activist Nathan Law and others to ask foreign countries or individuals outside China to impose sanctions or blockades against the government.

Why it matters: The United States consulate in Hong Kong said it 'strongly condemns' the additional sentence and called for Wong's 'immediate release', while Human Rights Watch described the ruling as 'cruel and unjust'.

Source: Al Jazeera

2. Bank of England set to raise rates today

The Bank of England is widely expected to raise interest rates for the first time in more than three years at noon GMT on Thursday, with most analysts predicting a rise from 3.75% to 4%. The Bank has left the rate unchanged at its last six meetings, having cut from its 2023-24 peak of 5.25%. Rising inflation, driven primarily by the cost of petrol and diesel, is pushing policymakers toward a hike, with the Consumer Prices Index standing at 3.5% in the year to September 2026 - well above the Bank's 2% target. A 0.25 percentage point rise would mean a £31 increase in monthly repayments for someone with a typical tracker home loan, according to UK Finance.

Why it matters: There would be an immediate impact on the 510,000 tracker mortgage-holders, while the 470,000 mortgage-holders on variable deals would need to wait for their lender's reaction to any Bank rate rise.

Source: BBC

3. British Ebola patient evacuated from DR Congo

A British healthcare worker who tested positive for Ebola is being evacuated from the Democratic Republic of the Congo to the UK for medical treatment. The patient, whose identity has not been disclosed, is in a stable condition and is expected to be transferred in a specialist ambulance to a 'high consequence' infectious disease unit on arrival. It is the first case involving a British national contracting Ebola in the current outbreak, which was declared on 15 May by DR Congo's health ministry.

Why it matters: The Africa CDC director general previously warned that the 'unprecedented' geographic spread of the outbreak meant it could reach the UK and other countries, and British health authorities say the risk to the wider public from imported Ebola cases remains low.

Source: The Guardian

4. Freezer hackers probed blood bank cold rooms in France

French authorities have confirmed that fridges used in medical facilities to store blood for donations were impacted by hackers, with temperatures set to rise - though staff intervened and the French Blood Establishment (EFS) said no blood products were lost. Russian hacktivist groups Noname057(16) and Z-Pentest Alliance posted claims they hacked Danfoss, sparking speculation they were behind the wider European supermarket freezer attacks, but analysts remain sceptical. Cyber-security firm Sekoia said evidence pointed to either Russian hacktivists or Iranian regime hackers, noting that 'Iranian-aligned actors have an established record of targeting internet-exposed industrial control systems'. Researchers from Strathclyde-based firm Lupovis found that honeypot traps in Europe were targeted but those in Africa and South America were left completely alone, suggesting the second wave of attacks was highly targeted.

Why it matters: No formal attribution has been made, and Sekoia also said the possibility remains that all three waves of attacks could have been carried out by different and distinct groups or copycats.

Source: BBC

5. South Korea expands naval patrols toward Hormuz

South Korea plans to extend the mandate of its Cheonghae naval anti-piracy unit and widen its area of operations to waters near the port of Duqm in Oman, approximately 650km (403 miles) from the Strait of Hormuz, following pressure from the United States to support its war on Iran. Under the new mandate, the unit would roughly double its operating radius to about 1,930km (1,200 miles), adding 75 personnel including a bomb disposal team and a P-8A maritime patrol aircraft. The motion will go to lawmakers after the cabinet endorses it next week, according to the Yonhap news agency. Officials told Yonhap the mission would be strictly noncombat and the unit would remain under South Korean command, operating independently of allied forces.

Why it matters: Opposition lawmakers warned that an expanded mission could expose South Korean forces to Iranian retaliation, with one MP saying 'If President Donald Trump boasts that South Korea was the first country in the world to dispatch troops, we would become the first country to join the Middle East war.'

Source: Al Jazeera

Closer to Home

Police embed in fire stations for Bonfire Night

Police officers are being embedded in fire stations and will travel with crews across Glasgow and surrounding areas tonight as part of Operation Amberlight, a new initiative to protect firefighters from attack during Bonfire Night. Up to 1,700 officers could be deployed as part of Scotland's biggest police operation since the visit of President Trump last summer, covering fireworks disorder, a Celtic vs Besiktas UEFA Europa League match kicking off at 20:00, and planned protest activity in Glasgow. Officers are working out of 10 fire stations across Glasgow, East Dunbartonshire and East Renfrewshire, and additional public order vehicles are being brought in from 18 English and Welsh police forces under mutual aid arrangements. The operation also contends with 18 Fireworks Control Zones this year, up from 12, following an upsurge of disorder in 2023 that left 62 officers injured.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Companies House accounts filing reforms updated for April 2028

The government has paused the requirement for small companies and micro-entities to file profit and loss accounts with Companies House from April 2028, and will continue to allow abridged accounts and the option to fillet profit and loss information after that date. From April 2028, all UK registered companies must file accounts in Inline eXtensible Business Reporting Language (iXBRL) format using commercial software, with web and paper-based filing systems closing for accounts filings on that date. Additional changes from April 2028 include a strengthened eligibility statement for companies claiming an audit exemption, a requirement to file component parts of accounts and reports together, and a limit on how many times a company can shorten its accounting reference period.

Source: Companies House
