1. Trump threatens to bomb US ally Oman

President Donald Trump threatened to bomb Oman - a US ally - if it "gets in the way" of his administration's talks with Iran, with Fox reporter Trey Yingst quoting Trump as saying: "If Oman gets in the way, we'll bomb the shit out of them." The threat came as the 60-day Memorandum of Understanding between the US and Iran expired on Monday, with Trump telling BBC News in the Oval Office that he was not seeking an extension. Iran's foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei told reporters that "an understanding has been reached regarding the map of the transit route" through the Strait of Hormuz, and said both sides would finalise details before issuing a joint statement. Trump also claimed during the same Fox interview that the US had a direct backchannel with Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), a claim an IRGC spokesman flatly denied.

Why it matters: The Strait of Hormuz, through which about a fifth of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas usually passes, has been largely closed for nearly six months, and the collapse of the MoU without a replacement deal leaves its reopening highly uncertain.

Source: BBC

2. Oil prices and Treasury yields surge on Iran fears

The 30-year Treasury yield advanced more than 4 basis points to 5.311%, reaching its highest level since June 2007, as oil prices rose amid expiring US-Iran ceasefire talks. West Texas Intermediate futures gained 2.6% to settle at $84.50 per barrel, while global benchmark Brent crude advanced 2.7% to close at $90.87 a barrel. The 10-year Treasury note yield was more than 2 basis points higher at 4.724%, and the 2-year Treasury note yield rose more than 1 basis point to 4.182%. Strategists at Barclays attributed the rise in rates less to inflation and more to the US budget deficit, high levels of AI-related issuance competing with Treasurys, and higher term premiums.

Why it matters: Elevated energy prices following the Middle East conflict have raised concerns about inflationary pressures, and investors are now awaiting July's Federal Open Market Committee meeting minutes, due Wednesday, for insights into the Federal Reserve's future rates trajectory.

Source: CNBC

3. Anthropic annualised revenue hits $65bn

Anthropic's annualised revenue run rate surpassed $65 billion at the end of July, up from $47 billion in May and just $9 billion at the end of last year, according to Bloomberg. The company's investors expect it to finish 2026 between $100 billion and $120 billion, according to the Financial Times. Anthropic is expected to hit the public markets ahead of rival OpenAI - possibly as soon as this fall - and will be seeking a public valuation of $2 trillion or more, which would make it the largest market debut on record. Anthropic was last valued at $965 billion in late May, when it raised a $65 billion round.

Source: TechCrunch

4. US-Canada tariff deadline looms in 48 hours

"Intense" trade talks between Canada and the US are going down to the wire as Canadian negotiators have been camped out in Washington for a week, trying to pin down an agreement before new 50% tariffs on some $20bn (C$28bn) worth of Canadian imports come into effect on Wednesday. Prime Minister Mark Carney said the negotiations were "very delicate and intense" and that it was "not the time to negotiate in public", while US-Canada Trade Minister Dominic LeBlanc said on Monday "our job is not yet done" as he left the US Trade Representative's office. The US has been asking Canada to remove its remaining retaliatory tariffs on American autos, adjust its dairy quotas, and lift the ban on US alcohol sales. A new poll from Abacus Data indicates that 74% of Canadians feel the trade dispute has had an impact on their household.

Why it matters: Trump has warned that "the time to get tougher" will be if the countries fail to reach a deal, and Carney has said he has "plans for any situation that may arise" should the talks collapse.

Source: BBC

5. Russia jails anti-war politician for 11 years

Russia's prominent anti-war politician Lev Shlosberg, aged 63, has been sentenced to 11 years and one month in a penal colony for "discrediting" the country's armed forces and "spreading false information" about them. The court in Pskov found Shlosberg guilty following comments he published on social media; the prosecution had demanded 12 years and one month in jail. Shlosberg condemned the verdict, saying its only task was to "punish a person for his political, public and civil position", and his party Yabloko said it would appeal. The ruling came just minutes before Russia's Supreme Court rejected Yabloko's appeal against a ban on it taking part in September's parliamentary elections - Yabloko was the only registered party to oppose Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine launched in 2022.

Why it matters: All 10 other parties taking part in the 18-20 September election support the war in Ukraine, meaning any remaining formal parliamentary opposition to the conflict has now been eliminated.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Scotland's first bluetongue cases spark vaccine shortage

Farmer Fiona Johnson's fledgling farm in Creetown recorded the first ever case of bluetongue in Scotland after she purchased a small number of animals locally; the infected lamb had been drooling, dropping food from its mouth and its legs were stiff. Experts believe that midges infected with the potentially deadly disease were blown over the border from England, where there are currently 439 cases. Scotland now has four cases across four farms in Creetown in just over a week, including the first cow to test positive, and all animals which have tested positive in Scotland have been euthanised. Vet John Hamilton of Ark Vet Centre in Lockerbie said they had 20,000 doses of the vaccine in stock at the start of the week but "that disappeared over 48 hours", warning that animal movement restrictions could cause significant financial disruption.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Business rates cut 20% for pubs, clubs, live music

From April 2027 (the 2027/28 financial year), pubs, social clubs and live music venues in England will receive a 20% cut to their business rates bills, saving the typical pub an estimated £1,100 in the next financial year. This 20% cut comes on top of a 15% relief already announced for 2026/27 bills. The package is worth around £100 million a year.

Source: HM Treasury

Companies House accounts filing reforms delayed to April 2028

Accounts reforms under the Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023 will now come into effect from April 2028, rather than April 2027, giving all companies one full accounting year plus 9 months (21 months) to prepare. From that date, small companies and micro-entities must file profit and loss accounts but may opt out of publication on the public register, abridged accounts will be removed, and all companies must file accounts via commercial software in iXBRL format.

Source: Companies House
