1. CIA chief makes unannounced Moscow visit

CIA Director John Ratcliffe reportedly travelled to Moscow on Tuesday, with US media partner CBS confirming the trip. Flight tracking data confirmed a large US military aircraft travelled to Russia from the US via Latvia, departing Riga airport at 05:50 GMT and appearing to land in the Russian capital at around 07:04 GMT. The plane returned to Riga shortly before 17:00 GMT. Neither Washington nor Moscow confirmed the nature of the visit or who Ratcliffe was due to meet.

Why it matters: John Foreman, a former British military attache to Moscow, said: "The Americans don't normally send such a high ranking official to Moscow unannounced at short notice unless something is up." The trip is thought to be Ratcliffe's first to Russia as CIA director.

Source: BBC

2. Canada fires back with up to 50% tariffs

Canada has announced counter-tariffs as high as 50% on a range of US goods, targeting nearly C$28bn ($20bn; £15bn) worth of American products ranging from steel to furniture, fresh tuna and cotton T-shirts. The levies are set to come into effect on 8 September. Finance Minister Francois-Philippe Champagne characterised Canada's retaliation as "proportionate" and "strategic", and said Canada would also offer businesses and workers an additional C$7.5bn for support programmes. Trump this week threatened to hike US tariffs on Canadian automobiles to 50% as of 1 January, prompting Prime Minister Mark Carney to accuse Trump of wanting to "destroy" Canadian industries.

Why it matters: Supply chains developed over decades are set to see increased trading costs, ultimately hitting businesses of all sizes and consumers through potentially higher prices, and the escalation puts into question the future of the North American free trade pact USMCA.

Source: BBC

3. China rejects US Iran sanctions as 'illegal'

China has vowed to protect its interests after the US announced plans to widen economic sanctions against Iran and its trading partners. Foreign ministry spokesman Lin Jian said China was firmly opposed to what it called "illegal unilateral sanctions" and would take "all necessary measures" to safeguard its rights. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent had warned that any nation financially partnering with Iran would be isolated, and said the Treasury had imposed sanctions on almost 60 entities, individuals and vessels. Analysts at Capital Economics cast doubt on the effectiveness of the measures, noting that roughly 90% of Iran's oil goes to China, which "has not recognised US sanctions in the past and is unlikely to be cowed this time either".

Why it matters: The announcement comes ahead of planned talks between Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping next month, and Washington is likely to be wary of potential retaliation from Beijing, which processes the majority of the world's rare earths and other critical minerals.

Source: BBC

4. UK shares missile tech to boost Ukraine

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham said the UK will help Ukraine make its own long-range missiles by sharing classified technical information for British components, with defence firm MBDA to release details on UK-made parts for the SCALP missile. The SCALP is the French-produced version of the UK's Storm Shadow, an air-launched missile with a range of more than 250 kilometres (155 miles). Experts cautioned the move "won't help Ukraine through this winter and likely won't start having an effect until next year, at the earliest". The Kremlin accused Burnham of "fanning the flame" of the war, a claim Burnham dismissed, asking reporters: "Who started the fire?"

Why it matters: Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said his country is receiving substantially fewer Patriot defence systems even as Russian ballistic attacks are increasing, meaning the SCALP technology could eventually equip Ukraine with deep-strike offensive capability to hit Russian missile launch sites.

Source: NPR

5. UK energy bills to hit three-year high

Energy bills for millions of households are expected to rise this winter to the highest level for three years, under a price cap to be announced by industry regulator Ofgem on Wednesday. Analysts expect Ofgem to say prices will go up by 4% compared with the current cap, affecting those on variable tariffs in England, Scotland and Wales. Cornwall Insight has forecast that a household using a typical amount of gas and electricity, paid through direct debit, will pay £1,729 a year from October, up from £1,663 between July and September. Energy UK estimates total debt has collectively risen to £6bn, with an expectation of it increasing to about £7bn by the end of the year.

Why it matters: The average price of gas has been 61% higher over the past three months compared to late 2025, according to Energy UK, and firms are calling for more government support for those struggling to pay as unpaid bills and charges continue to rise.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

GPS tracking push for domestic abuse suspects

Domestic abuse campaigners are calling for greater use of GPS location tracking to monitor high-risk suspects following the murder of Stacy Hunter, who was killed at her home near Houston in Renfrewshire by her estranged husband while he was on bail. An online petition is calling for a "Stacy's law", started by Lynne Mackenzie, herself a domestic abuse survivor. Scottish ministers have previously faced criticism over the slow rollout of GPS equipment, which is used far less in Scotland than in some other countries; the independent body Community Justice Scotland issued an urgent National Improvement Recommendation urging the Scottish government to accelerate more sophisticated monitoring systems. Scotland's victims minister Kirsten Oswald said the government would carefully consider the petition.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

20% business rates cut for pubs from April 2027

The government announced a 20% cut to business rates bills for pubs, social clubs and live music venues from April 2027, described as fully funded. This follows an earlier 15% cut on pubs and live music venues bills from April 2026, which the government said saved the average pub an additional £1,650 in 2026/27 with around 75% of pubs seeing their bills fall or stay flat. An independent review of pub and hotel business rates valuations, led by Jerry Schurder, was launched on 24 August 2026 and will report to HM Treasury by the end of March 2027.

Source: HM Treasury

Bank Rate held at 3.75% at July 2026 MPC meeting

At its meeting ending on 29 July 2026, the Monetary Policy Committee voted by a majority of 6-3 to maintain Bank Rate at 3.75%; three members voted to increase Bank Rate by 0.25 percentage points, to 4%. CPI inflation had fallen to 2.6% since the previous meeting, although it is expected to rise later in the year as higher energy prices continue to pass through. The next MPC decision is due on 17 September 2026.

Source: Bank of England
