1. Kenya records first-ever Ebola death

Kenya has reported its first-ever Ebola death after a Kenyan man who had been living in the DRC for seven years died after returning to Kenya. The man had been treated in the DRC after falling ill about a month ago, then travelled overland to Kampala before flying to Nairobi on 3 October. Health minister Aden Duale said officials had listed 28 contacts of the deceased, including family members and healthcare workers, with so far only one additional test turning positive. Duale said authorities were also looking for 23 passengers and four crew members who were on the same flight.

Why it matters: The current DRC outbreak has killed 4,082 people out of 8,463 confirmed cases since the Bundibugyo strain emerged, a strain for which there is still no vaccine nor specific treatment, raising the risk of further cross-border spread.

Source: The Guardian

2. Houthis strike Aden airport, Saudi intercepts missile

Yemen's internationally recognised government has accused the Houthis of firing at least two ballistic missiles and several drones at Aden International Airport, hitting the runway minutes before a flight from Cairo was due to land. The incoming Cairo flight was diverted to Jeddah to protect passengers, and the airport terminal was evacuated with two planes removed from the tarmac. The Yemen Ministry of Transport warned the incident "could easily have turned a civilian flight into a horrific humanitarian disaster, claiming the lives of dozens of innocent people in an instant." Separately, the Saudi-led military coalition said it intercepted and destroyed a ballistic missile launched by the Houthis north of Riyadh.

Why it matters: Pakistan and Turkiye on Monday agreed to activate "deterrence measures" under their mutual defence pact with Saudi Arabia and rapidly deploy forces to the Gulf kingdom to counter Houthi attacks, signalling a potential escalation of the regional conflict.

Source: Al Jazeera

3. Russia kills two in 'massive' Kyiv strike

At least two people have been killed in a Russian missile attack on Kyiv as Moscow said it launched a "massive strike" across Ukraine. Kyiv's mayor Vitali Klitschko said the two people were killed in the densely populated Darnytskyi neighbourhood in a fire caused by the overnight attack, with two others rescued while the fire was being extinguished. Russia's Ministry of Defence said its forces also hit port facilities in the Odesa region, as well as sea vessels used in the interests of Ukrainian armed forces.

Why it matters: Kyiv has come under relentless bombardment in recent weeks, with Russian air attacks on energy infrastructure, rail and road transport, including the bridges that connect the city over the Dnipro River.

Source: Al Jazeera

4. Former German spy chief arrested for espionage

August Hanning, who led Germany's Federal Intelligence Service (BND) from 1998 to 2005, was arrested on Tuesday on suspicion of treasonous espionage, spying out state secrets, attempted treason, and espionage. The 80-year-old was arrested in Bad Toelz-Wolfratshausen in southern Germany, while a former aide identified as Manfred D. was simultaneously arrested in Steinfurt in western Germany. Authorities believe the two agreed in 2010 to have Manfred D. continuously transmit intelligence from the BND to Hanning in exchange for payment, with Manfred D. allegedly providing Hanning with approximately 2,000 documents over the years. The current proceedings stem from evidence found during a search of Hanning's residence in connection with a separate child abduction case.

Why it matters: German opposition lawmaker Konstantin von Notz called it a "hard-hitting and deeply troubling story" and demanded a swift investigation, while also calling for banning former officials with high security clearances from engaging in private consulting work after leaving office.

Source: NPR

5. IMF chief warns of energy, debt and AI threats

IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva has warned that an energy shock, high public debt and the AI investment boom all threaten global growth. On AI, she cautioned that market disappointment could turn into a "far-reaching shock", noting that AI companies are under pressure to deliver productivity and profit gains to justify their sky-high valuations. However, she said that if done right, AI could add half a percentage point to global growth a year, according to IMF research. Her warnings came as Brent crude rose above $101 a barrel and French 10-year bond yields jumped as much as 12 basis points to 4.85%.

Why it matters: Georgieva called for "decisive action" in high-debt advanced economies including rate hikes, while also flagging that AI poses "substantial perils" including large-scale labour market fallout, serious cyber and stability risks and frontier models threatening to escape human control.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Police failing revenge porn victims, super-complaint filed

Lawyers have filed a super-complaint after research involving 100 people who reported intimate images shared online without consent found that police routinely fail to investigate such crimes. Data from 39 police forces in England and Wales shows that charges were lodged against perpetrators in an average of just 4.5% of cases last year, with some forces recording figures lower than 1%.

Source: The Guardian
