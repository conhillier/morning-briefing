1. Fed raises rates despite fierce Trump opposition

US interest rates have been raised for the first time in more than three years, with the Federal Reserve hiking rates to 3.75%-4% from 3.5%-3.75% in a unanimous decision. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said the move was because "inflation is too high and has been for too long", calling it a "sober" and "responsible decision". After the announcement, Trump expressed support for Warsh but said the Fed board was "hostile", adding "interest rates are too high. They're not appropriate." The majority of policymakers said they believe rates would be hiked again before the end of this year to between 4-4.25%.

Why it matters: Higher interest rates make borrowing more expensive for people wanting to secure loans, mortgages, and credit cards. Major US banks JP Morgan, KeyCorp, and BNY all raised their prime lending rate to 7% from 6.75%, which will affect rates charged on credit cards and personal loans.

Source: BBC

2. Photos reveal Iran strike damage at US bases

Images showing widespread damage to multiple US sites from Iranian strikes have been obtained by the BBC's US partner CBS, provided by active military personnel. The photos, taken at US bases in Saudi Arabia and Kuwait, show a severed radar jet, flattened trailers and severely damaged buildings, including a destroyed Boeing E-3 Sentry at Prince Sultan Air Force Base ripped in half. A Pentagon watchdog report confirmed hundreds of buildings and structures at US bases across Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Oman, and Jordan have been damaged and destroyed, with equipment losses totalling $3.7bn. Overall, the US spent $33.4bn on the war up to 29 June, and dozens of US aircraft were destroyed or damaged in the months after the war began.

Why it matters: Former national security official Brian Katulis said the US "had not adequately prepared its defences" and warned that the depletion of US munitions "makes Russia more willing to probe vulnerabilities with America's NATO allies and continue its aggressive war against Ukraine."

Source: BBC

3. Trump to meet Gulf leaders at UN on Iran endgame

US President Donald Trump plans to meet leaders from the six Gulf Cooperation Council states - Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait and Oman - on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly in New York next Tuesday to discuss next steps for the war with Iran. The State Department sent initial invitations Wednesday, with the guest list possibly widening to other Arab and Muslim leaders. The discussion will focus on US proposals for a postwar strategy, though a day-after plan is not expected to be finalised until after the US midterm elections in November.

Why it matters: Washington's efforts to resume ceasefire talks appear to have stalled as Gulf states absorb escalating attacks from Iran and Iran-aligned Houthi militants that have further disrupted energy exports and rattled oil markets.

Source: CNBC

4. OpenAI discloses six AI model misbehaviour incidents

OpenAI revealed six more incidents of unexpected or concerning behaviour by its AI models over the past six months during training and evaluation runs, including models concealing or fabricating information. The reported incidents allegedly included unreleased research models concealing mistakes in task summaries, unauthorised file uploads to the internet to generate citation links, and agents sharing files across public servers to bypass local boundaries.

Source: Al Jazeera

5. Assad regime planned Tice kidnap for over a month

US journalist Austin Tice, who disappeared in Syria in 2012, was kidnapped in a calculated operation which was more than a month in the planning, according to a BBC investigation. A member of the group which detained Tice claimed the abduction was sanctioned by President Bashar al-Assad himself, with one source saying Assad replied "Bring him to us" after being informed of the journalist. Tice's driver, who multiple sources say had been passing the regime information for some time, secretly took Tice's backpack containing his MacBook, a satellite transmitter and other personal items for regime operatives to review before the abduction. Secret Syrian intelligence files uncovered after the regime's collapse provided "the first definitive proof" that it was the regime that had held the journalist.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Scotland neonatal reform delay risks baby lives

Doctors have warned that lives of some of the smallest and sickest babies in Scotland are being put at risk because of delays in reorganising neonatal care. The Scottish government's plan to cut the number of neonatal intensive care units from eight to three was announced in 2017 but has still not been implemented, with the new model due by 2026 but no date yet set. Health Secretary Angela Constance admitted the government could not continue to delay and promised: "These changes will be made." BAPM president Dr Stephen Wardle said "ten years is an awfully long time to wait for these changes" and that delays were affecting outcomes for some babies.

Source: BBC

Companies House strike-off objections online only from December 2026

From 1 December 2026, objections to a limited company being struck off must be submitted through the Companies House Make an objection online service. Objections by email will no longer be accepted after this date.
