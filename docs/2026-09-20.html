1. Ukraine launches massive drone attack on Moscow

At least two people have been killed and six injured in a "massive" overnight Ukrainian drone attack on the Moscow region, Governor Andrei Vorobyev has said. Vorobyev said 249 drones had been shot down, and he reported fires in several residential houses, with a local warehouse also ablaze. Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin said 450 drones heading for Moscow had been shot down in what he called an "unprecedented attack", and a facility at an oil refinery was damaged. Sheremetyevo - Moscow's biggest airport - warned passengers about "temporary restrictions" in place, while at Vnukovo airport all arrivals and departures were halted, though restrictions at both airports were lifted by Sunday morning.

Why it matters: Russia and Ukraine have recently intensified their strikes, targeting energy-generating and transport facilities, as well as major logistics hubs.

Source: BBC

2. Houthis hit Riyadh with ballistic missiles and drones

Iran-backed Houthi militants in Yemen say they carried out drone and ballistic missile attacks on the Saudi capital Riyadh, as well as energy sites on the country's Red Sea coast. Saudi authorities said they intercepted and destroyed a ballistic missile fired at Riyadh on Saturday, without confirming any further strikes or casualties. A column of black smoke was seen rising from fuel tanks near the Saudi capital's international airport, where flights were disrupted temporarily on Saturday. The US State Department urged Americans in the Middle East to "exercise vigilance and be prepared for potential flight cancellations and other travel disruptions", warning that the "military conflict has the potential to escalate rapidly".

Why it matters: On Friday, investment banking giant JP Morgan said it was struggling to predict how oil prices would be impacted by the war, telling investors in a rare note that "we simply don't know how to model the endgame".

Source: BBC

3. Greenland security deal welcomed by NATO

The US and Denmark have announced a deal on Greenland's security, resolving a diplomatic dispute sparked by President Donald Trump's threats to seize it by force. Trump said Denmark would grant the US "permanent control over security, and all other needs" in the semi-autonomous Danish territory in the Arctic, and that the deal came with "no cost to the United States". An unnamed US State Department official outlined that the deal includes "permanent access, basing and overflights rights" and bans non-NATO countries from building bases in Greenland. Denmark's Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen said the deal was expected to be signed at the UN General Assembly next week.

Why it matters: NATO said the deal would "help advance security, stability and cooperation in this vital region", while French President Emmanuel Macron said it helped "to reaffirm Danish sovereignty" over the island.

Source: BBC

4. Russia votes in annexed Ukrainian territories

Russia's parliamentary election for the first time includes residents of regions that Moscow illegally annexed from Ukraine after the start of its full-scale invasion more than four and a half years ago. Russian authorities say the Donetsk, Luhansk, Kherson and Zaporizhzhia territories have over 3.5 million voters; they were annexed by Russia in September 2022, despite not fully being under Moscow's control, and the move has not been recognized internationally. A human rights activist described residents as being coerced to participate, with Moscow-appointed authorities threatening public sector workers with dismissal if they failed to vote and making pensions and social payments conditional on participating. The European Commission said the voting represented "another blatant violation of international law" and that the EU "does not, and will never recognize" the elections or their results.

Why it matters: Ukraine urged foreign governments and international organisations not to recognise the election, and warned Ukrainians that taking part in organising the vote could carry criminal liability.

Source: NPR

5. Michael Marra elected Scottish Labour leader

Michael Marra has been announced as the next Scottish Labour leader, receiving 4,266 votes from Labour members compared with Joe Fagan's 2,485. Almost half of party members turned out to vote in the leadership contest. Marra takes over following Anas Sarwar's decision to stand down in order to take up a seat in the House of Lords. Marra inherits a party that had a historically bad result in May's Scottish Parliament election, leading a group of just 17 MSPs, making them the joint second-largest party at Holyrood, tied with Reform UK.

Why it matters: Pollster Mark Diffley warned that "the party has become a bit of an irrelevance" and spelled out three priorities for Marra: become relevant, develop a relationship with the prime minister, and spell out appealing policies.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Deadly synthetic drugs reshape Scotland's addiction crisis

Efforts to bring down Scotland's sky-high rate of drug deaths are being frustrated by the arrival of hundreds of powerful new synthetic substances. In the past three years, there has been an explosion in the supply of new synthetic cannabinoids and opioids, including nitazenes and orphines, some of which are hundreds of times more potent than heroin. In 2024, nitazenes were implicated in 76 drug-related deaths in Scotland, according to official figures - more than three times the number of the previous year. Police Scotland raided two suspected nitazene factories in the past 14 months, making five arrests in total, including at an alleged illicit drugs laboratory in Patna, Ayrshire, in July 2025, which Deputy Chief Constable Jane Connors described as the UK's first suspected nitazene laboratory.

Source: BBC
