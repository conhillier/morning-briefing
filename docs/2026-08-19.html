1. Trump pauses Canada tariffs, claims deal reached

US President Donald Trump announced a three-day pause on 50% tariffs against Canada, citing an imminent deal between the two countries. The announcement came less than two hours before a levy on nearly $20bn (C$28bn) of Canadian imports was set to go into effect. In his post, Trump also said a final trade deal could allow the revival of the Keystone XL pipeline, which would carry 830,000 barrels of oil a day connecting Alberta to the US. Negotiators were discussing reducing US tariffs on Canadian autos from 25% to 15%, according to a Reuters report citing anonymous sources.

Why it matters: The US Chamber of Commerce warned that higher tariffs would "damage both economies, drive up costs for US families, further disrupt critical supply chains, and risk the 13 million American jobs that depend on trade under the US-Mexico-Canada Trade Agreement".

Source: BBC

2. Unitree robot maker surges over 500% in Shanghai debut

Chinese humanoid robot maker Unitree Robotics made its stock market debut in Shanghai on Wednesday, with shares rising 542% in early trading. The Hangzhou-based company raised about 6.1 billion yuan ($905 million) in its IPO, with shares hitting as high as 1,100 yuan before settling at 968.1 yuan. The IPO drew backing from Chinese AI company DeepSeek, which invested about 140.8 million yuan, while existing investors include Tencent. Ahead of its listing, Unitree unveiled a new humanoid robot called "Superman", which it says can jump two meters from a standing position and run at speeds of up to 12.66 meters per second.

Why it matters: Morgan Stanley in June raised its forecast for China's humanoid robot shipments to 50,000 units this year, nearly double its previous projection of 28,000, and estimated China's humanoid robot market will grow from $2 billion this year to $15 billion by 2030.

Source: CNBC

3. OpenAI institutes new safeguards after Hugging Face breach

The company disclosed it had paused reinforcement learning (RL) for two weeks following the incident but had since restarted many less-risky models, while its "largest planned frontier RL run remains on hold". The new safeguards include stronger network isolation practices, more detailed monitoring of models during development, and a monitoring system that aims to issue alerts within 30 minutes of concerning activity. OpenAI estimates the compute burden of monitoring will be roughly 20% of whatever process is being monitored.

Why it matters: OpenAI representatives said the measures were also provoked in part by the cybersecurity capabilities of the forthcoming Astra model and the overall pace of progress in AI development, suggesting the controls will increase in strictness as models become more capable.

Source: TechCrunch

4. US refineries now taking half of Venezuela's oil

Venezuela is exporting about half of its oil output - more than 500,000 barrels per day - to the United States, according to Under Secretary of Energy Kyle Haustveit, speaking at an industry event in Houston on Tuesday. Venezuela's total national output stands at roughly 1.25 million barrels per day, up from about 1 million barrels per day at the end of 2025, when only about 135,000 barrels per day were going to the US. Haustveit said the US is also sending more than 100,000 barrels per day of naphtha to Venezuela, where it is blended with heavier crude, calling the arrangement "a beautiful energy partnership". PDVSA vice president Jovanny Martinez said Venezuela's crude output would reach 1.245 million bpd by end of August, with exports up 19.7% this year.

Why it matters: The Financial Times reported in July that the US has collected more than $13bn in revenue from Venezuelan oil sales this year, though the Council on Foreign Relations noted the administration has not publicly disclosed how much oil it has sold, how much revenue it has collected, or how the funds have been used.

Source: Al Jazeera

5. Meta faces sweeping child privacy trial from 29 states

A jury trial brought by 29 US states, including California and New York, began on Tuesday, with states seeking billions of dollars from Meta and demanding fundamental changes to Instagram and Facebook. States are calling for Meta to end "like" counts, infinite scroll, autoplay of video content, and disappearing posts, as well as implementing parental verification for teenage users and changing "dopamine-manipulating recommendation algorithms". The states involved represent nearly two-thirds of the country's population.

Why it matters: A recent separate ruling in New Mexico fined Meta $942m and ordered similar changes, while declaring Meta a "public nuisance" akin to a factory polluting the air - though Meta said it would appeal that ruling.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Taxpayers to foot bill for Edinburgh PFI hospital shortfall

Tens of millions of pounds of taxpayers' cash will be needed to bring the Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh (RIE) up to standard when it transfers to public ownership next year. NHS Lothian and private operator Consort Healthcare reached a deal in which Consort will set aside up to £86.3m for upgrades - of which £23.4m has already been spent - but an NHS Lothian assessment said this sum is "anticipated to fall well short" of what is needed. Fire safety works alone are likely to top £90m, with a report to NHS Lothian's finance committee predicting a deficit of £9.7m not including further fire preventative measures. The PFI deal, signed under Tony Blair's Labour government, is expected to have cost the taxpayer more than £1bn by the time it expires in December next year.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Companies House accounts filing rules change from April 2028

From April 2028, small companies and micro-entities must file profit and loss accounts with Companies House, but may opt out of publication on the public register. All UK registered companies will be required to file accounts in iXBRL format via commercial software only, with web and paper-based filing systems closed for accounts filings from that date. The implementation date was moved from April 2027 to April 2028, giving companies one full accounting year plus 9 months (21 months) to prepare.

Source: Companies House
