1. Serbian president resigns, snap elections set

Serbia's president Aleksandar Vučić has resigned after more than a year of student-led protests and growing public discontent over allegations of government corruption. He addressed supporters outside the presidential building in Belgrade, saying his administration had preserved peace and stability during his nine-year tenure. Under Serbia's constitution, presidential elections must be held no more than three months after his resignation, meaning Serbians will go to the polls twice before the end of the year. Parliamentary speaker Ana Brnabić, also a Progressive Party member, will assume the role of interim president until a new one is elected.

Why it matters: The upcoming parliamentary elections will pit Vucic's Progressive Party against a student-led, anti-corruption movement called Students Win and a coalition of pro-Western opposition parties known as European Serbia.

Source: BBC

2. South Africa mass shootings leave 28 dead

Twenty-eight people have been killed in two mass shootings hours apart in South Africa. Eighteen people were killed when eight gunmen stormed a bar close to Johannesburg on Saturday evening, with 14 more injured, with police believing the motive was robbery as personal belongings had been taken, though they could not rule out turf wars between illegal mining groups. In a separate shooting in the early hours of Sunday morning, 10 people were killed outside an entertainment venue in a township near Cape Town and a further 11 were injured. The two mass shootings came days after 11 people were killed in the city of Durban when gunmen stormed a house in the KwaMakhutha township.

Why it matters: A recent spate of mass shootings has raised fears that the problem is deepening, with violence often linked to turf wars between gangs engaged in drug trafficking and illegal mining.

Source: BBC

3. Brent crude tops $107 after Trump rejects Hormuz deal

Oil prices jumped Monday in Asia trading after U.S. President Donald Trump rejected an Iranian peace proposal aimed at ending the Middle East conflict and reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Brent crude gained 2.89% to $107.34 a barrel as of 2:23 a.m. ET, while U.S. crude futures for November delivery rose 1.87% to $94.14 a barrel. Trump turned down Iran's conditional offer and told aides he expects U.S. strikes on the country to resume after November's midterm elections, according to The Wall Street Journal, citing unnamed U.S. officials. Trump later confirmed to reporters that he had rejected Tehran's latest proposal: "They made a proposal but I rejected it."

Why it matters: Trump said earlier this month that he expected the conflict, which began with U.S. and Israeli airstrikes on Iran on Feb. 28, to conclude soon after the midterm elections, with oil prices subsequently declining.

Source: CNBC

4. Five arrested near RAF Fairford in terror probe

Five men have been arrested on suspicion of preparation of a terrorist act after three vehicles were spotted near RAF Fairford in Gloucestershire, a base the U.S. military has been using to launch attacks on Iran. Police say they received a call just before 1 a.m. local time about suspicious vehicles heading towards the base, and aerial footage shows a bomb disposal robot maneuvering around three white vans. Temporary chief constable of Gloucestershire police Maggie Blyth said a cordon remains in place and around 85 households are currently unable to return to their homes. British police say they are investigating whether the suspects have links to Iran, noting that Iran's IRGC issued a statement in July calling any base used against Iran a "legitimate target".

Why it matters: If confirmed as an Iranian attack on America's British allies, it would mark a big widening of the Middle East conflict, though police have not said that yet.

Source: NPR

5. US-China tariff list released after Trump-Xi summit

The United States and China have unveiled a list of goods recommended for reduced tariffs following last week's summit between Presidents Donald Trump and Xi Jinping, covering $30bn of each side's imports. The agreement identifies 77 Chinese goods and more than 1,600 US products to be considered for more favourable tariff treatment, with Chinese goods including microwave ovens and artificial flowers, and US exports including poultry, dairy products and eggs. U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said the agreement would improve market access for about 30 percent of US exports to China. However, analyst Deborah Elms of the Hinrich Foundation said the announcement did not point to a major shift, noting that "both sides have largely listed goods that do not move the needle on overall trade flows".

Why it matters: Two-way trade between the US and China totalled $495bn in 2025, down 25 percent from the previous year, according to the US Trade Representative.

Source: Al Jazeera

Closer to Home

Edinburgh University lecturers begin five-week strike

Lecturers at the University of Edinburgh have begun a five-week strike as part of a long-running dispute over budget cuts and compulsory redundancies, with members of the University and Colleges Union (UCU) stopping work until 30 October. The union said the cuts could lead to 1,800 job losses, and hundreds of staff had already lost work when contracts were not renewed, while the university announced the closure of 90 courses earlier this month. The dispute stems from PS140m of cuts announced by the university last year, which the UCU described as "unprecedented" in Scottish higher education. Principal Prof Sir Peter Mathieson accused the union of "weaponising" students and insisted the university had to take difficult decisions to secure its future.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Vaping Products Duty of £2.20 per 10ml starts 1 October 2026

A new excise duty of £2.20 per 10ml on all vaping liquids manufactured in, or imported into, the UK comes into force on 1 October 2026, whether products contain nicotine or not. Retailers and wholesalers may continue to sell eligible unstamped stock already held until 31 March 2027, but from 1 April 2027 all vaping products outside duty suspension must carry a valid vaping duty stamp. Treasury analysis indicates Vaping Products Duty is expected to raise more than £550 million a year by 2030-31.

Source: HMRC
