1. Milei threatens sanctions over Falklands oil

Argentine President Javier Milei announced sweeping actions aimed at stopping the development of the deep-sea Sea Lion oil project, about 220 kilometres north of the Falkland Islands, where British company Rockhopper Exploration and Israel's Navitas Petroleum plan to begin drilling in the coming months and start pumping oil in 2028. Milei threatened tougher sanctions against companies involved in the offshore drilling and announced new funding for a naval base in Argentina's southernmost province of Tierra del Fuego. "Argentina will not stand idly by," Milei said. "Any further advance on the Malvinas Islands will be considered a violation of our national security." 2. VW board approves 50,000 more job cuts

The board of German car giant Volkswagen has approved a plan to cut another 50,000 jobs as part of a sweeping turnaround programme, bringing the total number of roles the company plans to shed by 2030 to 100,000. The firm also said that by 2035 it would cut the number of models it produces by 50% and reduce the complexity of its offering by 75%. VW said a "fundamental adjustment of the global workforce capability is necessary" to safeguard competitiveness, with the restructuring described as the biggest in the company's almost nine-decade history. As of 2025, VW employed more than 660,000 people worldwide.

Why it matters: VW is also considering the future of its Emden, Zwickau, Hanover and Neckarsulm plants, where it has said production capacity exceeds demand, with alternative uses for these plants being assessed.

Source: BBC

4. Bond yields signal persistent inflation fears

A sharp sell-off in global government bonds is exposing investor anxiety that the macroeconomic backdrop may be shifting toward persistently higher inflation. Brent crude gained more than 1% on Thursday to reach $96.64, a one-month high, while US West Texas Intermediate prices rose 1.6% to trade at $92.52 a barrel. Japan's 10-year government bond yield moved above 3% for the first time since 1996, while German 10-year bund yields rose to levels not seen since 2011.

Why it matters: Investors say deglobalization and greater geopolitical fragmentation will make inflation structurally more persistent than it was during much of the 2010s, with odds for a rate hike at the next Federal Open Market Committee rising to more than 66% following Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole speech.

Source: CNBC

5. Nvidia buys AI platform Hugging Face for $12.9bn

Nvidia has agreed to buy artificial intelligence platform Hugging Face in a deal valued at about $12.9bn (PS9.5bn), one of the AI chipmaker's biggest acquisitions as it expands into software. Under the agreement, Nvidia will pay about $11.9bn to Hugging Face investors and offer up to $1bn in stock-based incentives to employees who join the company. Hugging Face is used by more than 18 million developers and hosts more than three million AI models, with more than 200,000 companies using the platform. Nvidia said Hugging Face would remain open to developers and that users would not be required to use its chips or services.

Why it matters: The deal would give Nvidia access to one of the world's largest AI developer communities as competition in the sector intensifies, and could also help Nvidia expand its presence in AI software as some of its biggest customers, including Microsoft, Meta and OpenAI, develop their own chips.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

UK army halts exercises amid budget squeeze

The British army has been ordered to halt non-essential training exercises in an effort to save money, with collective training exercises for UK-based units suspended for most of this year. Cancelled exercises reportedly include those involving tanks and Apache helicopters, according to reports in the Times and BBC. The army did not deny the cuts, saying it was "prioritising activity that contributes most directly to readiness, deployability and operational effectiveness," while also noting it is investing PS2bn to transform training over the next 15 years. Prime Minister Andy Burnham is under pressure to increase defence spending to 3% of national income by 2030, rising to 3.5% by 2035, though plans set out in the defence investment plan include spending rising to only 2.7% of GDP by 2027/28.

Source: The Guardian
