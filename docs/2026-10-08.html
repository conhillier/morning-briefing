1. UK Jerusalem consulate stays as 'mission'

Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband said the building "will not be closing" and will be known as the UK Mission, Jerusalem from Thursday. Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Saar said 20 diplomats and the consul general would leave Israel while a task force of seven could remain "with a very limited scope of activity". Miliband said the UK reserved the right to take "reciprocal action" but would not do so while "discussions are under way and credible".

Why it matters: The compromise means the heat has been taken out of a dispute that could have further worsened UK-Israel relations, though FCDO sources said maintaining a UK presence would help build back the mission in the future.

Source: BBC

2. US shelters wanted Syrian general in Lebanon

A BBC and NPR investigation has found that former Syrian general Bassam al-Hassan, wanted for crimes against humanity, is being harboured in Lebanon under an arrangement involving the FBI and other US and Lebanese authorities. Hassan oversaw Syria's chemical weapons programme and was a senior adviser to former President Bashar al-Assad. In return for information primarily about the 2012 abduction of US journalist Austin Tice, Hassan has been offered "residency", "protection" and help securing his "living needs", according to a detailed record of a meeting reviewed by the BBC. Sources with knowledge of the arrangement said Hassan receives money from US authorities through an intermediary, making it harder to trace, despite being sanctioned by the US government.

Why it matters: Sources with knowledge of the investigation have expressed concerns that Hassan's arrangement with the US could continue indefinitely, at significant expense but with little gain, while Syrian officials say they want him returned to face trial.

Source: BBC

3. Samsung profits hit record $80bn on AI demand

Samsung Electronics says it expects a nine-fold surge in its quarterly profits compared with a year earlier, driven by surging demand for memory chips used in AI data centres. The tech giant estimates its operating profit for the three months to the end of September will jump to 107.4tn won (£61bn; $80bn), its fourth quarter in a row of record earnings. Samsung's stock market valuation crossed $1tn (£757bn) earlier this year, and in June South Korea unveiled plans for at least $880bn in projects led by Samsung and SK Hynix to build out the country's chip manufacturing capabilities. US tech giants including Google, Amazon and Meta have pledged to pour more than $650bn into AI projects this year.

Why it matters: A chip shortage driven by surging demand has led firms including Samsung to raise prices, making products such as smartphones and computers more expensive.

Source: BBC

4. Trump rules out Iran deal, strikes loom

President Trump said Wednesday he no longer wants a deal with Iran, following reports that the US military is preparing for possible strikes against the country. NBC News reported that Trump and his national security team have discussed possibly resuming large-scale US military operations in the coming weeks, while Axios reported the possible resumption could include "massive bombing" of Iranian energy, infrastructure and nuclear targets. Trump has seen his approval ratings fall to a record low amid concerns over high living costs spurred by soaring gasoline and diesel prices.

Why it matters: Axios reported that military aggression could influence the outcome of the upcoming midterm elections, adding urgency to the prospect of renewed conflict.

Source: CNBC

5. Weston family buys Boots for $8.9bn

Canada's Weston family, through their holding company Wittington Investments, has bought UK pharmacy and beauty retailer Boots for $8.9bn (£6.74bn). The deal covers Boots' 1,800 stores across the UK and Ireland, employing 50,000 people, as well as the No7 beauty brand and a franchise arm in Thailand, and is also backed by investment firm Fairfax. The transaction sees the exit of long-term backer Stefano Pessina and his latest backer Sycamore Partners, and is subject to regulatory approval, expected to close in the first quarter of 2027. The acquisition marks the return of the Weston family's Canadian branch to the UK high street, after the family sold off the Selfridges department store chain for £4bn in 2022.

Why it matters: Veteran retail analyst Richard Hyman said the Weston family were "the most encouraging ownership of Boots for many, many years", adding that Boots "needs and deserves owners that will show it a bit of love".

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Royal Mail cuts 2,500 jobs in restructure

Royal Mail is to cut up to 2,500 jobs as the company seeks to restructure amid intense competition and the continuing decline in letter deliveries. The postal service, which has been fined a total of £37m since 2023 for missing delivery targets set by regulator Ofcom, said the cuts would focus on head office and "support function roles", to be completed by the end of next year. Royal Mail said the cuts would be achieved through "natural attrition" and a voluntary exit programme with no compulsory redundancies, and that there would be no reduction in operational frontline roles. The cuts are the first since the completion of the £3.6bn takeover of Royal Mail's parent company by Czech billionaire Daniel Kretinsky's EP Group last year.

Source: The Guardian
