1. US pulls all bombers from RAF Fairford

The US has withdrawn all of its bombers from RAF Fairford, the UK military base at the centre of an incident being investigated by counter-terror police. A Pentagon official confirmed on Sunday it had removed its bombers - thought to be about a dozen B-1s - from the base back to their home stations in America. The New York Times reported US commanders had received new threats about an Iran-backed plot against RAF Fairford, with the new information described as part of the same threat warnings from three weeks ago but with specific details emerging in the past 24 hours. Whitehall sources have not disputed that the decision from Washington was in response to the incident at the airbase in Gloucestershire.

Why it matters: The removal of the bombers from RAF Fairford will remove a foothold for the US outside of continental Europe, though the Pentagon has stressed its bombing aircraft remain able to conduct missions "anywhere, anytime", including through "operations that launch and recover from the continental United States".

Source: BBC

2. Spain's Sanchez calls snap election for November 29

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez has announced a snap general election after Parliament rejected housing relief measures proposed by his minority government. Anger had been building since late last month when an 87-year-old woman, Marai del Carmen Abascal, was evicted from her home of 70 years in Madrid after an international investment fund bought her building and increased the rent.

Why it matters: Opinion polls suggest that a coalition led by the conservative People's Party, with support from the far-right Vox party, is the most likely outcome - which would be the first time since the death of dictator Francisco Franco that a far-right party has entered central government.

Source: Al Jazeera

4. Trump names Jay Clayton as White House AI czar

Donald Trump on Sunday named Jay Clayton, the director of national intelligence, to also serve as the new White House AI czar. Clayton previously served as the top federal prosecutor in Manhattan and led the Securities and Exchange Commission during Trump's first term. During his Senate confirmation hearing, Clayton said AI was a "gamechanger" but it also posed "a threat".

Why it matters: Senator Mark Warner cautiously welcomed the appointment while noting that "the administration has resisted meaningful safeguards without offering a serious alternative for addressing the national security risks posed by increasingly powerful AI systems".

Source: The Guardian

5. OpenAI safety leader quits over broken culture

David Robinson, who led the writing of safety reports accompanying ChatGPT developer OpenAI's product releases, has quit the company, warning that its culture was broken and that AI firms were not "being nearly careful enough" about developing the technology. Robinson wrote in the Atlantic magazine that as "the company sprints from one launch to the next, it is failing to achieve the level of care that I believe is needed", and called for AI firms to run "like nuclear power plants or busy airports, with layers of redundancy and careful, time-consuming planning". OpenAI this week also announced it was scrapping the release of a next-generation AI model after researchers raised safety concerns during internal testing.

Why it matters: Robinson warned that OpenAI's internal culture of "unimpeded optimism" means safety failures will only grow as systems become more capable, and called for AI firms to rely on safety expertise from fields such as nuclear and aviation.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Scotland on course to miss child poverty targets

Scotland is on course to miss legally-binding targets to cut child poverty "by a wide margin", according to a report from the Joseph Rowntree Foundation (JRF). The charity found that one in five children was in poverty, with the figure averaging 21% from 2022 to 2025, against a legal target to push it below 10% by 2030. Overall, 940,000 people in Scotland were in relative poverty, representing 17% of the population, with poverty rates highest at 47% for children in ethnic minority families. The JRF called for a "much bolder and properly-funded plan to reduce poverty" by rewiring local government, public services and the tax system, warning that councils' funding was "stretched to breaking point".

Source: BBC
