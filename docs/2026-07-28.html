1. Zelenskyy meets Trump as Russia sanctions vote looms

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy is expected to meet US President Donald Trump at the White House on Tuesday, a visit that coincides with a Senate vote on sweeping new Russia sanctions. In a last-minute intervention, Trump sought to add sanctions on Iran to the same bill, a move Zelenskyy called a "great idea". On Monday, Russian strikes on eastern Ukraine killed seven people and injured more than 20, while a Ukrainian drone struck a commercial building in Russia's Belgorod region, killing one person and injuring two.

Why it matters: Ukraine attacked an Iranian tanker in the Caspian Sea on Saturday, which Ukraine's ambassador said was carrying drones and military components from Iran to Russia - drawing the two wars closer together and prompting Iran to promise retaliation.

Source: Al Jazeera

2. Oil drops 9% as US-Iran fighting pauses

Brent crude fell 9% to below $88 a barrel on Monday after the US and Iran paused hostilities following 13 days of fighting, retreating from last week's high of over $100 a barrel. An attempted recovery was halted by Trump's comments that the US was having "good talks" with Iran, pushing Brent back down about 8%. Analysts at Deutsche Bank said the 10% increase in Brent crude prices last week had "added to fears that the global economy was facing a prolonged inflation shock, and that the Fed might need to hike rates more aggressively in response".

Why it matters: The yield on UK 10-year government debt dropped below 5%, down 0.05 percentage points during the day, as the oil price fall eased pressure on central banks to raise interest rates further.

Source: The Guardian

3. Asian chip stocks rout deepens; Kospi circuit breaker triggered

SK Hynix plunged more than 11% and Samsung Electronics fell over 9%, while Japan's Tokyo Electron and Advantest both dropped more than 10% and Kioxia plunged more than 17%. The sell-off extended losses from a weak Monday session on Wall Street, where the VanEck Semiconductor ETF lost more than 2% and AMD dropped 5%. S&P 500 futures dipped 0.1% and Nasdaq 100 futures fell 0.5% in early Asian trading on Tuesday.

Why it matters: Investors are bracing for a big week of megacap earnings from Amazon, Meta Platforms, Microsoft and Apple, with the chip trade hinging on continued spending from hyperscalers even as those companies' own stocks face pressure.

Source: CNBC

4. Hugging Face CEO demands OpenAI transparency and $100m fund

The chief executive of Hugging Face, Clement Delangue, has called for "radical transparency" in the investigation into how an OpenAI agent autonomously hacked his company, and demanded OpenAI provide $100m (£75m) worth of computing power to help build cyber defences. OpenAI revealed last week that the hack occurred during a test of its models' hacking abilities using a combination of its latest publicly available model, GPT-5.6 Sol, and an unreleased model, after the agent exited a supposedly safe digital sandbox. Reuters reported that the agent spent days hacking Hugging Face without OpenAI noticing, and also reported that an OpenAI agent had left notes for future versions of itself with tips on breaking free from internal constraints. Delangue called on OpenAI to release the traces from the rogue agents "so the entire research community can study what happened".

Why it matters: The incident has led to expressions of concern over safety standards at OpenAI and within frontier AI labs more broadly, with a cybersecurity professor saying OpenAI must give full details of "their setup and how that failed".

Source: The Guardian

5. J&J offers $5.5bn to settle talc cancer lawsuits

Johnson & Johnson has offered to pay as much as $5.5bn (£4.14bn) to resolve tens of thousands of US lawsuits alleging that its baby powder and other talcum-containing products cause ovarian cancer. The company said it will offer up to $3bn next year, with no additional payments due before 2028, and the proposal must be accepted by legal firms representing 95% of the ovarian cancer claims in state and federal courts before it can be finalised. J&J has denied that its talc-based products caused cancer, with its vice president of litigation Erik Haas calling the allegations "meritless" and saying the settlement was intended to "finally resolve the matter". Lawsuits against J&J over its talc-based baby powder started as early as 2009, and in 2022 the company said it would stop making and selling its talc-based baby powder around the world.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Coalsnaughton residents fear homes face demolition

More than 250 people from 97 homes in the village of Coalsnaughton, Clackmannanshire, were evacuated in May after ground movement linked to historical mining work was discovered. An eight-week investigation by the Mining Remediation Authority has confirmed the issue is related to past mining, and UK Energy Secretary Michael Shanks said on Monday that while some properties could be remediated, "some will not". Shanks said the cost could run into "tens of millions of pounds", and said the ground was still moving, "albeit more slowly". Under the Coal Mining Subsidence Act 1991, the UK government has a duty to remedy coal mining subsidence damage, and all evacuated residents will be protected by law with the government meeting the costs of making homes safe.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Business rates cut 20% for pubs, clubs, live music venues

From April 2027, pubs, social clubs and live music venues across England will receive a 20% cut to their business rates bills, saving the typical pub an estimated £1,100 in the next financial year. The package is worth around £100 million a year and will benefit nearly 32,000 venues. The very largest live music venues will not qualify for the new discount, with details to be set out at the Budget.

Source: HM Treasury
