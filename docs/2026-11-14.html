1. Trump, Putin, Xi plan historic Shenzhen summit

Donald Trump, Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping are planning to meet on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (Apec) summit in the southern Chinese city of Shenzhen next week, the White House has said. No agreement was expected to follow the talks, an unnamed senior American official said, according to news agencies AP and Reuters.

Source: BBC

2. Europe agrees UK-led team for Ukraine peace talks

EU leaders have agreed that Britain, France, Germany, Italy and Poland, along with EU institutions, will form the team representing Europe in Ukraine peace talks. The agreement came after months of wavering over who would speak for the continent, following a summit dominated by European efforts to avoid being sidelined in US-led negotiations.

Why it matters: Representatives from Britain, France, Germany, Italy and the EU attended Russia-Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi this week, where both Moscow and Kyiv cited some progress, though there was no sign of a breakthrough on key issues such as territory.

Source: Sky News

3. UN: 25 seafarers killed near Hormuz since war began

At least 25 seafarers have been killed in and around the Strait of Hormuz since the US-Israeli war on Iran broke out in February, according to the United Nations. Khaled Khiari, assistant secretary-general for the Middle East, Asia and the Pacific, gave the figures during a UN Security Council briefing on Friday, covering the period up to November 11. The UN's International Maritime Organization tracked 136 security incidents involving vessels in Hormuz and the wider Middle East between March 1 and November 11, with 35 of those incidents coming in October alone. Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz is approximately 65 percent below pre-war levels, according to the latest report from the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO).

Why it matters: Khiari warned that disruptions to maritime navigation "continue to have significant global economic consequences, driving up energy, transport and insurance costs, fuelling inflation and threatening food security amid severely constrained fertiliser shipments."

Source: Al Jazeera

4. Nvidia scandal puts White House under pressure

The departure of Jensen Huang as Nvidia CEO has opened up intense scrutiny of Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, whose department was responsible for controlling exports of sensitive American technology and was being pressed to act on concerns about Megaspeed, an Nvidia customer suspected of helping divert advanced chips to China. A highly classified memo was shared with Lutnick about concerns surrounding Alice Huang and Megaspeed, but he resisted taking action against the company.

Why it matters: Members of Congress from both parties want answers, with Democrats launching an investigation into how the Commerce Department vetted Chinese companies approved to buy AI chips, and Republicans also questioning the administration's approach.

Source: BBC

5. Microsoft restores services after global outage

Microsoft said early on Saturday morning that its services had been fully restored after a worldwide outage affected Outlook, Teams and Copilot, disrupting more than 430 million users across more than 70 countries. The outage, which began on Thursday, hit institutions ranging from the Japanese government and the Philippines Supreme Court to the Australian spy agency, as well as trains, hospitals and courts in North America. Microsoft said the issue was introduced during a "network capacity upgrade" and was not caused by a cyber-attack or an artificial intelligence agent. UK cyber minister Liz Lloyd said the incident had "limited" impact on critical frontline services such as 999 calls and that there had been no disruption to the transport network.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Dept Q crew member dies at Edinburgh studio

A member of the crew for the Netflix series Dept Q has died after being seriously injured at FirstStage studio in Leith, Edinburgh. Emergency crews were called to the studio at about 15:00 on Thursday and the man was pronounced dead a short time later. Left Bank Pictures, which is involved in making Dept Q, said it was "deeply shocked and saddened by the tragic death" and that it was offering support for those affected. Police Scotland said the death was not being treated as suspicious but that inquiries into the circumstances were ongoing, while the Health and Safety Executive said it was "aware" of the incident.

Source: BBC
