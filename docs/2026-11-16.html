1. China confirms historic Xi-Trump-Putin summit

China confirmed on Monday that President Xi Jinping will meet with Vladimir Putin and Donald Trump in Shenzhen on Thursday, on the sidelines of the APEC meeting - the first-ever gathering of the three leaders. China's Foreign Ministry confirmation follows announcements from Moscow and the White House, with the Wall Street Journal having reported last month that Putin proposed the three-way sit-down to negotiate an end to the four-year war in Ukraine. The White House signalled the US intention to prioritise talks over nuclear non-proliferation and the Middle East war, while downplaying Ukraine - a topic high on Moscow's agenda. Analysts say the summit will likely yield little concrete result but presents an "almost 100% photo op" that lifts China's status, while Nvidia and chip-smuggling issues are expected to stay off the agenda.

Why it matters: China and Russia could turn more aggressive with their military ambitions over the next two years if they perceive Trump as politically weakened, according to Brookings Institution senior fellow Robert Kagan.

Source: CNBC

2. Megaspeed says Alice Huang left in 2025

Megaspeed, described as the biggest buyer of Nvidia chips in South East Asia, says that Alice Huang - a suspected China spy linked to former Nvidia boss Jensen Huang - left her executive role there in August last year. Megaspeed is suspected of giving backdoor access to Nvidia chips to Chinese companies via its data centres in Malaysia, Indonesia and Thailand.

Why it matters: US Congressman John Moolenaar said Jensen Huang has agreed to testify before the House Select Committee on China, as well as the Foreign Affairs and Intelligence committees, to understand "to what degree privileged information was accessed" and whether the alleged spy "influenced Nvidia's philosophy" on AI and export controls.

Source: BBC

3. Anthropic IPO set to debut on Nasdaq Thursday

Anthropic is expected to debut on the Nasdaq on Thursday with an IPO price currently expected at $70 to $78 a share, in what is being called the most important tech IPO in years. AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC are collectively investing $26 billion at the IPO price, with Amazon's existing stake potentially worth hundreds of billions of dollars. Anthropic has outstanding spending commitments of over $900 billion according to its latest prospectus, including at least $110 billion with Amazon over a decade, and recorded a net loss of $104 billion through the first nine months of 2026. The IPO could yield over $90 billion in proceeds, on top of Anthropic's $132 billion in existing cash including short-term investments.

Why it matters: The Nasdaq is down to start November after dropping 5.3% in October, its worst month since March 2025, making the IPO a defining event for the broader AI market at a moment when tech investors are desperate for good news.

Source: CNBC

4. Intel investors sue over 29% stock plunge

Shareholders have sued US chip giant Intel, accusing it of hiding the severity of a slump in personal computer (PC) sales that led to a nearly 29% one-day collapse in its stock on 30 October. The lawsuit, filed by legal firm Robbins Geller Rudman & Dowd on behalf of owners of Intel common shares between 23 July and 29 October, alleges Intel made "materially false and/or misleading" statements that failed to disclose the PC sales slowdown, artificially inflating its stock price. Intel announced in October that computer hardware sales had dropped sharply in the three months ending in September, with quarterly earnings falling 9%, or about $800m, to $8.1bn. Intel shares have fallen sharply in the past month by 37.5%, though the stock is still up 51% overall this year.

Source: BBC

5. Israel's Knesset gets Arab deputy speaker

Ahmed Tibi, chairman of Israel's Joint List party and leader of the Ta'al Party, has been elected deputy speaker of the Knesset on Monday. Avigdor Lieberman, leader of the right-wing Yisrael Beiteinu party, will be the Israeli parliament's chairman, according to the Knesset's Foreign Affairs and Defense Committee. The parties representing Palestinian citizens of Israel - the Joint List and the United Arab List (Ra'am) - made historic electoral gains in last month's election, securing 19 seats in parliament.

Source: Al Jazeera

Closer to Home

Jackdaw gas field begins flowing to Britain

Gas has begun flowing from the Jackdaw gas field, located 150 miles east of Aberdeen, after the UK government approved production last month. The field is operated by Adura under a joint venture between Shell and Norwegian state energy firm Equinor, and is expected to provide about 6% of UK gas production, enough to power 1.4 million homes. Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh said the gas would "help to heat millions of British homes and businesses this winter and beyond" and minimise the UK's use of imported higher emission gas. Over its expected 11-year lifespan, Jackdaw is forecast to support gas supply, protect hundreds of jobs and add 2.9bn pounds to the economy.

Source: BBC
