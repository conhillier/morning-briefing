1. Colombia quake kills at least 132

A magnitude 7.4 earthquake struck western Colombia on Monday, killing at least 132 people and triggering a national state of emergency. The quake hit at 07:34 local time at a depth of 103km in Choco province, with the epicentre near the village of San Jose del Palmar. At least 40 people died in the city of Pereira alone, where 65 buildings collapsed and people are believed trapped in 15 of them. More than 480 people were injured, and over 1,500 homes were damaged across the country.

Source: BBC

2. Trump demands 50 years' Iran compensation

US President Donald Trump has demanded Iran pay compensation for 50 years of alleged damages, framing the move as a direct response to Tehran's demand that Washington pay war reparations before reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Trump told reporters at the White House on Monday that the payments would cover deaths among US forces in the region as well as civilian protesters in Iran, claiming 52,000 people have been killed in Iran in the past four months. Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said talks with Oman were 'progressing smoothly and constructively', with agreement reached on a shipping route map, though some technical issues remain unresolved. US Central Command said it has redirected 55 commercial vessels since reinstating the blockade in July and 'disabled' at least two.

Why it matters: Former Obama adviser Charles Kupchan described Trump's remarks as 'banter', warning that 'neither the Iranians nor the Trump administration is being serious,' even as Iran cannot survive without oil exports and Trump faces pressure heading into US midterms with high gas prices.

Source: Al Jazeera

3. Ukraine drone strike kills 13 in Tatarstan

At least 13 people have been killed in a Ukrainian drone strike on Nizhnekamsk in the Russian republic of Tatarstan, more than 1,100 kilometres from the Ukrainian border. One child was among those killed and 75 more people were injured; Ukraine's Armed Forces said the city's Tanevo oil refinery had been hit. The Uzbek consulate in Tatarstan said seven of those killed were citizens of Uzbekistan, and Russian news agency RIA reported most victims had been staying in a hostel at the time of the attack. Russia's Investigative Committee said 'civilian individuals who were not involved in any military operations were present' where the drones struck.

Why it matters: The BBC described it as 'one of the deadliest single attacks on Russia since the start of Moscow's full-scale war in Ukraine in 2022,' as Kyiv steps up long-range strikes on facilities it says contribute revenue and logistics to Russia's war effort.

Source: BBC

4. Nvidia raises $500bn AI infrastructure fund

Nvidia has teamed up with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to raise $500bn (£370bn) in capital for artificial intelligence infrastructure. The investors are for the first time treating AI hardware and infrastructure - referred to as 'compute' - as an asset class. The financing will go towards Nvidia's own projects and those being built by its partners, likely including new data centres and factories to manufacture AI chips. CEO Jensen Huang said: 'We are bringing the world's leading long-term capital providers together to independently underwrite AI infrastructure.'

Source: BBC

5. OpenAI expands Daybreak cyber defence service

OpenAI has announced an expansion of Daybreak, its cyber defence service, adding a new cyber-focused model GPT-5.6-Cyber available only to approved customers. Daybreak will now consist of two tiers - Blue and Red - with Blue covering incident response, malware analysis and patch validation, while Red offers 'purpose-trained cybersecurity models' for security testing and vulnerability research. GPT-5.6-Cyber is built off of GPT-5.6 Sol and is currently only being made available for 'trusted customer partners', including reportedly Accenture, IBM, Crowdstrike and Cloudflare. OpenAI said 'the cybersecurity world is rapidly changing - threat actors will increasingly use AI to conduct cyberattacks at unprecedented speed and scale, including in fully autonomous ways.'

Why it matters: Critics have pointed out that such announcements also function as marketing opportunities for the AI labs, even as enterprises remain interested in buying protection from the companies that know the security risks first-hand.

Source: TechCrunch

Closer to Home

Rosebank and Jackdaw consultations close

Public consultations on updated plans for the Rosebank oil field off Shetland and the Jackdaw gas field east of Aberdeen are closing, edging a decision on whether to approve both sites a step closer. The Jackdaw consultation closed this week, while the Rosebank consultation closes next Monday; operator Adura - a joint venture between Equinor and Shell - published updated environmental impact assessments ordered by the Court of Session in Edinburgh, which ruled the fields had been unlawfully approved because the climate impact of burning extracted oil and gas had not been taken into account. Rosebank is expected to fill up to 500 million barrels of oil over its 25-year lifetime and could produce almost 250 million tonnes of planet-warming emissions, while Jackdaw is estimated to produce 35.8 million tonnes of carbon over its 11-year lifetime. Prime Minister Andy Burnham has left open the possibility of granting approval, saying he will honour existing licences while not issuing new ones.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Business rates cut 20% for pubs, clubs, live music venues

From April 2027, pubs, social clubs and live music venues across England will receive a 20% cut to their business rates bills, saving the typical pub an estimated £1,100 in the next financial year. The package is worth around £100 million a year and will benefit nearly 32,000 pubs, clubs and live music venues. The largest live music venues will not be eligible for the new discount, with details to be set out at the Budget.

Source: HM Treasury

Bank Rate held at 3.75% following July MPC vote

At its meeting ending 29 July 2026, the Monetary Policy Committee voted 6-3 to maintain Bank Rate at 3.75%, with three members preferring an increase of 0.25 percentage points to 4%. CPI inflation has fallen to 2.6% but is expected to rise later this year as the effects of higher energy prices continue to pass through. The next MPC decision is due on 17 September 2026.

Source: Bank of England
