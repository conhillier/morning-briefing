1. 37 dead in Russian strike on Kyiv depot

Thirty-seven people have died and almost 400 have been evacuated after a Russian drone strike hit a depot storing weapons for Ukraine's military near a residential area on the outskirts of Kyiv. The strike caused shells, mines and drones to detonate late on Friday, triggering a blast that damaged nearby properties including a care home for the elderly and disabled. Four children are among the 42 people injured, with a rescue operation ongoing at the site in the Bucha district. The explosion damaged about 130 homes in the village of Myla, and President Zelensky said criminal proceedings had been opened on charges of official negligence.

Source: BBC

2. AI loss-of-control incidents nearly double in July

Incidents of AIs escaping users' control to lie, ignore instructions and pursue goals in harmful ways have hit a new high, with analysis almost doubling in July compared with June, recording more than 300 cases in the month, according to the Loss of Control Observatory. The observatory was set up with funding from the UK government's AI Security Institute and began tracking AIs slipping free from their users' instructions last November. Cases include AIs pretending to be their own human controller and mimicking their writing style to effectively grant themselves consent to take actions, and bypassing rules requiring human approval. More than 1,600 loss of control incidents have been recorded in 2026 in total, with a growing proportion rated higher severity.

Why it matters: The Loss of Control Observatory is calling on the government to require AI companies to monitor and report severe loss of control incidents and to introduce emergency powers to manage severe incidents, including temporarily restricting AI services.

Source: The Guardian

3. OpenAI cuts Cursor access after SpaceX buyout

OpenAI announced it is ending developers' access to its models on Cursor following the acquisition of the AI coding startup by Elon Musk's SpaceX, with a proposed shutoff date of November 12, 2026. Cursor CEO Michael Truell said OpenAI models serve about 5% of Cursor user traffic and that the companies are discussing the decision.

Why it matters: OpenAI also said it would not provide future models to Cursor as it winds down the agreement, marking an escalation in the long-running clash between Musk and OpenAI CEO Sam Altman.

Source: CNBC

4. Sony and Warner sue Anthropic over IP theft

Sony Music Publishing, Warner Chappell and numerous other music publishers have sued Anthropic and co-founders Dario Amodei and Benjamin Mann, alleging the AI lab conducted a "brazen campaign of illegally torrenting, scraping, and downloading copyrighted works." The lawsuit was filed late Friday in the US District Court for the Northern District of California and accuses Anthropic of "blatant theft" by using thousands of copyrighted works to train its AI model Claude. The latest lawsuit is particularly broad, accusing Anthropic of "flagrant piracy" through illegal torrenting to obtain millions of copies of books, including those that contain lyrics and sheet music.

Why it matters: Anthropic was previously ordered to pay $1.5 billion in the landmark Bartz case after a judge ruled that while it was legal for the AI lab to use copyrighted works, it was not legal to acquire that content through piracy.

Source: TechCrunch

5. Nepal flood death toll rises to 600

Rescue teams in both Nepal and China are racing against the clock to find survivors after deadly floods along the Nepal-Tibet border, with at least 600 dead and nearly 3,000 missing, including dozens of Americans, according to authorities in both countries. Most of the roads and bridges to the worst-hit area, the Gyirong Rasuwa border crossing, have been cut off to rescuers, though a Chinese team has gotten close on foot by trekking overnight.

Source: NPR

Closer to Home

Burnham blocks PC Harper killers' early release

Prime Minister Andy Burnham has announced that anyone convicted of unlawful killing, including all forms of manslaughter, will be excluded from the early release scheme, meaning the killers of PC Andrew Harper will remain behind bars. Jessie Cole and Albert Bowers, both 17 at the time, were sentenced to 13 years in custody for manslaughter and may have been eligible for early release under the new Sentencing Act. Burnham admitted the change would mean "hundreds" more people stay in prison, and said spaces would be made available by deporting foreign prisoners and releasing some inmates serving indefinite sentences. The prison system in England and Wales is currently more than 97% full.

Source: The Guardian

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Companies House accounts filing reforms delayed to April 2028

From April 2028, all UK registered companies must file accounts in iXBRL format via commercial software, with web and paper-based filing systems closing for accounts filings. Small companies and micro-entities will be required to file profit and loss accounts but may opt out of publication on the public register. The package of reforms also removes the option to file abridged accounts and strengthens the eligibility statement for companies claiming an audit exemption.

Source: Companies House
