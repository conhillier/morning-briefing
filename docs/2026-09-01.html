1. Nepal flood death toll reaches 987

The death toll from devastating floods on the Nepal-Tibet border has reached 987, according to Nepal's disaster agency. Some 3,916 people remain missing, of whom more than 600 are workers believed to be trapped in a vast network of mud-filled tunnels at hydropower sites along the Trishuli River. Engineers have been pumping air into the tunnels, and while a team did manage to gain entry to one of them, it withdrew after finding no one there. Nepalese Prime Minister Balendra Shah said on Monday that 279 people had been rescued from the sites, describing the floods as "not an ordinary" event.

Why it matters: At least 11 hydropower projects either completed or under construction were damaged in the floods, with each plant employing hundreds of workers including local and migrant labourers and high-skilled engineers.

Source: BBC

2. How the Nepal border flood unfolded

A CCTV camera at Gyirong on the Chinese side of the Nepal-Tibet border captured a dark wall of water, mud, boulders and ice surging down the valley at 10:59:53 local time on 26 August. The immigration system at Nepal's Rasuwagadhi crossing recorded its final transaction at 8:35:03am local time, less than 10 minutes before the torrent appeared on the footage. Seismic instruments detected movement in the mountains at 8:37am, and researchers later attributed the signal to the collapse of part of a glacier that triggered the flood. China said the mudslide reached the border crossing from its origin in around seven minutes.

Why it matters: At least 275 Indians remain missing in Nepal, according to India's foreign ministry, and electronic records show 89 Nepali and foreign nationals had cleared immigration to leave Nepal for Tibet in the final hours before the disaster.

Source: BBC

3. Oil tops $91 as US-Iran strikes resume

Brent crude rose more than 1 percent on Tuesday to top $91 a barrel, extending a more than 2.5 percent gain from the previous day, after the US and Iran exchanged fire for the first time in more than a month. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations reported late on Monday that a tanker was struck by three unknown projectiles while crossing the Strait of Hormuz; no casualties were reported and no government or group has claimed responsibility. Oil prices have been volatile since a 60-day ceasefire between the US and Iran expired in mid-August, denting hopes of a return to pre-war levels of maritime traffic in the strait, a conduit for about one-fifth of global oil supplies in peacetime. After peaking at nearly $94.40 a barrel on August 21, Brent has been trading in the range of $86-$91 since the truce lapsed.

Source: Al Jazeera

4. Shein shares tumble in Hong Kong debut

Shares in fast-fashion giant Shein fell by as much as 10% in early trading on their Hong Kong stock market debut on Tuesday, before recovering somewhat to close around 3.5% lower at just under HK$47 each. Shein had priced its shares at HK$48.56, raising 13.6 billion Hong Kong dollars ($1.7bn) and giving the company a stock market valuation of $26.3bn - roughly a quarter of its once-estimated worth of nearly $100bn. The listing follows failed attempts to go public in the US and UK, where concerns were raised over labour practices and environmental impact. In July, Shein reported a $99m quarterly loss as its sales slowed after the US struck down an import duty exemption on small packages.

Why it matters: The disappointing debut suggests the market is not convinced that Shein's growth can make a "comeback", said Charu Chanana, chief investment strategist at Saxo, who added that the slump in shares signals Shein's cheap prices are "becoming harder to sustain", which may lead to higher prices for customers.

Source: BBC

5. Global bond sell-off deepens, yields surge

Global government bonds are selling off again, pushing yields to new highs, as oil prices have risen above $91 a barrel, stoking inflation fears. Japan's 10-year benchmark government bond hit 3% for the first time in a generation. The US 10-year Treasury yield rose 2.6 basis points to 4.78%, described as the highest since early 2025, while the equivalent UK gilt yield was up slightly at 5.14%. The European Central Bank is widely expected to raise its key interest rates by 0.25 percentage points at its next meeting on 10 September, and traders see a better-than-even chance of rate hikes in the US and Japan this month.

Why it matters: Stock futures are pointing to a lower open when European markets open, with Wall Street also expected to fall later after modest declines on Monday.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Glasgow mum dies in childbirth, son survives

Chelsea McGillivray, 31, from Rutherglen in South Lanarkshire, suffered an unknown cardiac event during labour at the Queen Elizabeth University Hospital in Glasgow last Monday and could not be resuscitated despite CPR. Her son Tommy was taken to the neonatal intensive care unit after the traumatic birth but got home healthy two days later. All of Chelsea's prenatal tests had been normal and the family said she had no underlying health conditions. Her family has set up a fundraiser so that her partner Jack, who is self-employed, does not have to worry about money while taking time off work, which has raised more than 85,000 pounds.

Source: BBC
