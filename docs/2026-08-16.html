1. Indonesia quake kills 47, toll set to rise

A magnitude 7.7 earthquake struck off the coast of Indonesia's Flores island early Saturday, killing at least 47 people and collapsing buildings across the region. The quake hit at 5:58 a.m. local time at a depth of 10 kilometres, with its epicentre 68 km north-northwest of the city of Ende in East Nusa Tenggara province. The quake was followed by at least 235 aftershocks, with the largest recorded at magnitude 6.2, and triggered landslides that buried or cut off many remote villages across six regencies on Flores Island. The disaster flattened at least 157 houses and damaged nearly 200 more, forcing about 2,000 villagers to abandon their homes and move to temporary shelters, while more than 100 public facilities including schools, medical centres and churches were destroyed.

Why it matters: The death toll is expected to rise because landslides triggered by the earthquake buried or cut off many remote villages, and damaged infrastructure and a lack of heavy equipment are hindering rescue efforts.

Source: NPR

2. Israel kills 11 in Lebanon in deadliest day since June truce

Eleven people have been killed in Israeli strikes in southern Lebanon on Saturday, in one of the deadliest days of attacks since an agreement aimed at reducing hostilities was announced in June. Lebanon's prime minister said seven people were killed in a strike on a house on the outskirts of Ansar, 70km south of Beirut, with state media reporting three children and two women among the dead. Israel's Defence Forces said they had killed Ali Samir al-Haj Hassan, whom they described as a Hezbollah battalion commander in the Radwan Force unit, after a Hezbollah attack that seriously injured three Israeli soldiers. A later round of strikes hit the town of Deir al-Zahrani, killing four and wounding five others, according to the Lebanese Ministry of Health.

Why it matters: Lebanon's President Joseph Aoun condemned the strikes as "repeated violations" of the June ceasefire framework, warning they sent a "clear message" to the US-led negotiations, with Lebanese and Israeli negotiators not set to meet again until a planned eighth round in September.

Source: BBC

3. Lake Kariba death toll rises to 68

The death toll from the Lake Kariba ferry disaster in Zimbabwe has risen to 68 after 22 more bodies were recovered on Saturday, police said. The ferry capsized on Tuesday on Lake Kariba, on the border with Zambia, after an investigation suggested it was overloaded and turned over during heavy winds. Eighteen children were among those killed, in what is believed to be the country's deadliest such disaster. Officials said the vessel was authorised to carry 90 people but was packed with 114 adults, five crew members and an unspecified number of children.

Source: The Guardian

5. Anthropic revenue surges 14-fold to $11.5bn

Anthropic's revenue surged more than 14-fold in the second quarter from a year earlier, according to Bloomberg News, with the AI company reporting preliminary revenue of more than $11.5 billion for its latest completed quarter, up from $787 million in the same period last year and $4.73 billion in the first quarter of 2026. Anthropic also posted positive adjusted operating income in the second quarter, though the figures are preliminary and could still change. The company said in May that its run-rate revenue crossed $47 billion, up from roughly $10 billion in revenue in all of 2025. Anthropic's early meetings with prospective investors ahead of a potential IPO have been high-level and have not included discussions about specific financials or a valuation, with CFO Krishna Rao leading the meetings.

Why it matters: An IPO as soon as this fall could make Anthropic one of the first major private AI companies to tap public markets, unlocking access to billions of dollars in additional capital to fund escalating compute infrastructure costs.

Source: CNBC

Closer to Home

Scotland tourism booms as Europe swelters

Scotland is enjoying a surge in visitors, with a 15% rise in flight bookings to Scottish airports in June and July compared with a drop in bookings to London and the rest of England, according to VisitBritain figures. Festival sales in Edinburgh are up 30%, according to Marc Crothall, chief executive of the Scottish Tourism Alliance, who said: "Edinburgh is jumping, which is great. And that's a massive draw for international visitors." Long-term plans to increase direct flights from North America have come to fruition in the last two years, while a rise in visitors from continental Europe - possibly looking to escape the heat - has also added to the mix. An average overseas visitor to Scotland spent just over 900 pounds in a week in 2024, compared with just over 300 pounds in three nights for a British holidaymaker.

Source: The Guardian
