1. EU demands border action after Ceuta death toll hits 72

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen has called for 'united action' on border security after tens of thousands of migrants crossed from Morocco into the Spanish exclave of Ceuta. Spain now estimates that about 69,500 migrants have returned to Morocco, exceeding initial estimates of 50,000, while the official death toll on the Spanish side stands at 72, with 11 deaths on the Moroccan side. In a letter to Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez, von der Leyen praised his 'swift handling' of the crisis but warned that 'the protection of all our external borders is a shared European responsibility'. EU interior ministers will hold emergency talks by video conference on Tuesday to draw lessons from the episode.

Why it matters: Von der Leyen urged the EU to 'double down' in five areas including strengthening external borders and dismantling smuggling networks, signalling the crisis will reshape EU migration policy. Italy temporarily suspended the Schengen arrangement with Spain, and Czech Prime Minister Andrej Babis urged a temporary suspension of Spain's Schengen membership, straining relations among EU member states.

Source: BBC

2. Iran denies US talks as Trump insists deal near

Iran says it is negotiating with Oman to reach 'an understanding' over a temporary route through the Strait of Hormuz to allow commercial shipping to resume, but flatly denies it is talking to the US. US President Donald Trump said talks on ending the war in Iran were 'going on right now' and called it 'the last chance for them to sign a good document', but Tehran's denial appeared to irk him, prompting him to call Iran's leadership 'unbelievably duplicitous' on Truth Social. Vessel-tracking data from Kpler shows commercial shipping through the strait has slowed, with 51 vessel crossings between 30 July and 2 August, a drop of around 28% compared to the previous four days when 71 crossings were recorded. Around 20% of the world's crude oil and liquefied natural gas usually flows through the strait.

Why it matters: The conflicting accounts leave the status of any peace process deeply uncertain, and Trump warned he was 'ready to go anytime we want' if talks fail, keeping the threat of renewed strikes alive.

Source: BBC

3. S&P 500 nears record; oil rebounds in Asia

The S&P 500 climbed 1.5% on Monday, leaving the benchmark index just shy of its closing record of 7,620.90 set on 2 June, as markets reacted to conflicting signals about Iran negotiations. The tech-focused Nasdaq Composite jumped 2.1%, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 1.3% to hit a record high of 53,178.41. Among the 'Magnificent Seven', Meta Platforms rose 6% and Microsoft, Amazon and Alphabet climbed between 4.4% and 4.9%. Brent crude fell about 5% on Monday before climbing back to around $85 per barrel as of 04:00 GMT on Tuesday, up more than 1.3% from Monday's close, as Asian stock markets slipped.

Why it matters: Analyst Melissa Brown of SimCorp told Al Jazeera she was 'extremely surprised by the magnitude of the rally', noting it came 'against the backdrop of relatively negative sentiment, and without a real fundamental reason to support it', suggesting fragility beneath the surface.

Source: Al Jazeera

4. Palantir surges 12% on blowout Q2 earnings

Palantir topped second-quarter earnings estimates on Monday, with revenue surging 93% year-over-year to $1.94 billion against an expected $1.80 billion, sending shares up 12% after the announcement. US commercial revenue soared 149% from a year ago to $764 million and has jumped 380% since 2024, with the company now forecasting US commercial revenue 'in excess of' $3.42 billion in 2026. CEO Alex Karp told CNBC: 'Forget consensus. To my knowledge, no businesses at our scale has even grown half this much.' The company lifted full-year revenue guidance to between $8.15 billion and $8.16 billion, up from prior guidance of $7.65 billion to $7.66 billion.

Why it matters: Palantir's US government revenue also grew 90% to $809 million, and Karp said the strong growth 'looks like this is going to go on for at least another 18 months', suggesting the AI software trade still has significant runway despite the stock having lost 29% this year.

Source: CNBC

5. Xbox Series X price jumps £170 amid chip costs

Microsoft has hiked the price of its premium Xbox Series X console with a disc drive to £670, up from £500, citing rising memory and storage chip costs. The baseline Xbox Series S now costs £430, a roughly 43% increase on its previous £300 price tag. In the US, basic consoles rose by $100 to $499, and consoles with more memory increased by $150 to $749, while in the EU a Series S with more storage will cost roughly €200 more than before. Rival Sony hiked PlayStation 5 prices by £90 in March, and Microsoft said in June it had 'hoped another price increase would not be necessary'.

Why it matters: Random-access memory has shot up in price partly due to demand from AI firms, and graphics card prices have also risen because of their use in generating AI content, meaning consumers are effectively subsidising the AI boom through higher device costs.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Scotland exam results day: grades out from 08:00

More than 148,000 people across Scotland are set to receive their exam results today, with Nationals, Highers, Advanced Highers and Skills for Work Awards issued from 08:00. The results are being delivered by the new exams body Qualifications Scotland, which took over from the SQA after a number of controversies in previous years. Text messages and emails are due from 08:00 to the 67,000 candidates who have an activated My Qualifications Scotland account, with every learner also receiving results by post. Support lines including Qualifications Scotland on 0345 279 1000 and Skills Development Scotland on 0808 100 8000 will be open from 08:00.

Source: BBC
