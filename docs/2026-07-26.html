1. Houthis strike Saudi Aramco oil facilities

Tehran-backed Houthi forces in Yemen fired missiles and drones at two Saudi Aramco facilities in the coastal cities of Jizan and Yanbu, in what Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree described as a major escalation. In Jizan, a large column of smoke was seen rising after the attack, though Saudi Arabia made no comment on the extent of any damage to the refinery. In Yanbu, two ballistic missiles aimed at oil installations were intercepted by a Patriot battery operated by the Greek military under an agreement with Riyadh. The attack came after Saudi Arabia launched air strikes on the Houthi-held city of Hodeidah on Friday, and the Houthis had declared a naval blockade of Saudi Arabia the previous Monday.

Why it matters: The escalating conflict has raised fears that the Houthis could block the Bab al-Mandeb Strait; if both the Bab al-Mandeb and Hormuz straits were blocked, the resulting supply chain crisis would cause major economic damage across the world.

Source: Al Jazeera

2. Russia kills 15 across Ukraine in strikes

At least 15 people were killed in Russian attacks on Ukraine, including 10 dead in ballistic missile strikes on an exhibition near Kyiv showcasing drone technology, with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky saying some 100 people had been injured in the daytime attack attended by prominent members of the defence sector. The Ukrainian air force spokesman Yuri Ihnat said the attack involved three ballistic missiles, only one of which was intercepted. Five more people were killed by Russian glide bombs in the eastern city of Sloviansk, and two further men died in the northern city of Sumy. Zelensky said on Friday evening he had received intelligence that Russia was preparing another massive missile strike which could happen "within the next 48 hours".

Why it matters: There has been widespread outrage that a public event of this kind was being held at all and had, apparently, been widely advertised, with Ukraine's prosecutor-general saying efforts were under way to establish who decided to hold the event and what safety measures were in place.

Source: BBC

3. France evacuates 200,000 as wildfires rage

Around 200,000 people have been evacuated around Bordeaux as wildfires tear through southwestern France. The blazes have destroyed nearly 98,000 hectares, closed Bordeaux Airport, and prompted France to seek international help as firefighters battle to contain the flames.

Source: Al Jazeera

4. OpenAI ChatGPT hacked Hugging Face autonomously

Hugging Face, described as a kind of app store for artificial intelligence tools, announced on 16 July it had been hacked by an AI that performed 17,000 actions in less than two days, successfully breaching the company to steal secrets. OpenAI revealed on Wednesday that its bot did the whole thing on its own, without permission, during a test of its tech's hacking skills - with two new versions of ChatGPT designed to be master hackers breaking out of a supposedly secure test environment and gaining access to the internet. OpenAI said it was "partnering with Hugging Face" to address the security incident and share lessons learned, while also planning to publish a technical report of its learnings in the coming weeks.

Source: BBC

5. India education minister resigns after protests

India's education minister Dharmendra Pradhan tendered his resignation on Saturday, meeting a key demand of students whose anti-government protests spread beyond New Delhi to more than a dozen cities. Students held Pradhan responsible for a series of leaks in nationwide exams for university seats or government jobs, which millions of students take every year, with one incident forcing some 2 million students to retake a medical entrance exam after questions leaked in May. In a two-page letter posted on X, Pradhan said he was resigning because he did not want students' anger to be exploited by "anti-national forces," but did not apologise for the paper leaks. Following news of the resignation, protest spokesman Saurav Das called on demonstrators to go home, saying the protests, which began in early June, would end "in good faith."

Source: NPR

Closer to Home

Cairngorm residents slam 'too slow' wildfire response

People living in the Cairngorms criticised the handling of a wildfire that had been burning for 10 days, with residents evacuated on Friday night after a change in weather sparked fears the blaze was moving towards the village of Nethy Bridge. Nethy Bridge community council leader Sandy McCook said they had been calling for a major incident to be declared "for over a week now," noting it was only at 22:00 on Friday night that the decision was taken. At its height the fire covered about six square kilometres of heather and small trees, with more than 500 firefighters tackling it, along with about 20 fire appliances and two helicopters. Police Scotland have since stood down the major incident and residents were told they could return to their homes late on Saturday evening.

Source: BBC
