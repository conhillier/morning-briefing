1. Iranian attack kills two Filipino sailors on Saudi tanker

Saudi Arabia condemned what it said was an Iranian attack on its supertanker Sidr on Monday night in the Strait of Hormuz, confirming the loss of two Filipino seafarers. The Saudi national shipping company Bahri said the vessel was "involved in a security incident while transiting the Strait of Hormuz" at around 23:40 local time on Monday. A South Korean-owned supertanker, Senegal Prosperity, was reportedly struck east of Musandam around the same time, with maritime security firm Marisks reporting it was hit by three unknown projectiles. Iran said two tankers had caught fire after hitting mines while using an "unauthorised route" through the strait, and the US military said the latest strikes on Iran followed "recent attempted attacks by the IRGC against commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz".

Why it matters: The Strait of Hormuz, through which a fifth of global oil and gas shipments usually pass, has effectively been closed since the start of the US and Israel's war with Iran in February, and the attack on two tankers each loaded with two million barrels of Saudi crude oil signals continued danger to global energy supplies.

Source: BBC

2. Iran fires on Gulf neighbours after US bombardment

Iran fired on US allies across the Gulf early Wednesday following a night of American bombardment, with Kuwait, Jordan and Bahrain all reporting attacks. The violence came after a monthlong lull in fighting, with Iran's currency hitting another record low of 2.20 million rials per US dollar, a 10% hike from last week's record. One US strike hit a home hosting a wedding in Kuhestak, killing four people - two women and two children aged 4 and 16 - and wounding at least 68, according to Iranian state media. Since fighting resumed, the price of Brent crude has climbed to around $95, up more than 30% from the start of the war.

Why it matters: A senior analyst at Crisis Group warned that "at some point, one of the sides may see a need to escalate in order to break this cycle," cautioning this "could bring the two sides to a point that they have been trying to avoid until now, which is a full-scale war."

Source: NPR

3. Chevron expands in Venezuela after US oil deal

Chevron announced it is expanding its existing operations in Venezuela's Orinoco Belt, days after the Trump administration struck a deal to develop 17 Venezuelan oil fields with an estimated 65 billion barrels of oil. As part of a joint venture, Chevron plans to invest over $7 billion over the next five years, expanding its production to approximately 600,000 barrels of oil per day. The announcement was made at a press conference at the Miraflores Palace in Caracas alongside US Energy Secretary Chris Wright, Chevron CEO Mike Wirth, and Venezuelan President Delcy Rodriguez. Chevron is the largest foreign oil operator in Venezuela and first established a presence in the country in the 1920s.

Source: NPR

4. UK PM Burnham tries to calm bond market fears

Prime Minister Andy Burnham moved to calm volatile bond markets on Wednesday, promising decisions would be "grounded in fiscal responsibility" at his first prime minister's questions. At one point on Wednesday, the cost of 10-year borrowing for the UK government hit 5.29%, its highest level since 2008, before easing slightly. Analysts believe higher interest rates on public debt could wipe out up to half of the PS24bn headroom Rachel Reeves built up against Labour's fiscal rules. Chancellor John Healey is due to deliver his budget on 28 October, with the Office for Budget Responsibility expected to take a snapshot of market interest rates around now for its forecasts.

Source: The Guardian

5. Lords push for AI 'kill switch' power in UK

A group of peers led by Liberal Democrat Lord Tim Clement-Jones is calling for the British government to be able to deactivate powerful AI systems and switch off the country's data centres if they pose a threat to national security. The measure has been proposed as an amendment to the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill currently making its way through Parliament, and is one of 65 amendments to that bill debated this week. Separately, on 8 September, Labour MP Alex Sobel plans to introduce an AI Security Bill with support from campaign group ControlAI, which if successful would make the UK the first G7 country to bring in legislation effectively halting the development of superintelligent AI. The push follows a report from the UK's Centre for Long Term Resilience identifying hundreds of incidents of AI tools ignoring instructions, evading safeguards and deceiving humans.

Why it matters: The Centre for Long Term Resilience said "loss of control" incidents had increased since its previous report in March and called for the government to introduce emergency powers to manage such incidents.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Glasgow Council faces critical PS110m budget gap

Scotland's public spending watchdog, the Accounts Commission, has warned Glasgow City Council is at a "critical" point in its finances, with a combined PS109.7m budget gap for 2026-27 and 2027-28. The council also had a PS45.7m overspend from the 2024-25 financial year, and the commission found there were no "established longer term financial planning arrangements" in place. Two key pressures are driving the crisis: an ongoing equal pay dispute - the council previously paid out around PS770m after female workers were found to have been underpaid - and homelessness costs, which are expected to cost the council PS56m this year. The Accounts Commission said decisions over the next few months would have a "significant bearing" on the council's future financial sustainability.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Business rates cut 20% for pubs from April 2027

The government announced a 20% cut to business rates bills for pubs, social clubs and live music venues from April 2027. This follows an earlier 15% cut on pubs and live music venues' business rates bills brought in from April 2026, which saved the average pub an additional £1,650 in 2026/27 with around 75% of pubs seeing their bills fall or stay flat. An independent review of pub and hotel business rates valuations, led by Jerry Schurder, was launched on 24 August 2026 and will report to the Treasury by the end of March 2027.

Source: HM Treasury
