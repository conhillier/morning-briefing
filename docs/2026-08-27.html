1. Nepal-Tibet floods: 1,500 missing, 270 dead

More than 1,500 people have been confirmed missing after deadly flash floods hit the Nepal-Tibet border early Wednesday, sweeping away roads, bridges and entire villages. At least 270 people have died in Nepal, while Chinese authorities say three people have died and two have been rescued on the Tibetan side. The US Geological Survey says a glacial collapse triggered the floods, after initially reporting the event as a 4.4 magnitude earthquake. According to the Nepal Tourism Board, 644 overseas nationals have not yet been located, including 65 US citizens, 178 people from India, 33 from the United Kingdom, 35 from Australia and 25 from Canada.

Why it matters: Rescue efforts are being hampered by the destruction of entire roads, with the World Food Programme's country director describing access as "impossible" under current circumstances, and aid workers expecting more than 30,000 households to be impacted by the disaster.

Source: NPR

2. Collapsed glacier confirmed as flood trigger

Preliminary investigations have pointed to a collapsed glacier near the Langtang Lirung peak as the cause of the flash flood on the Nepal-Tibet border, scientists told the BBC. An analysis by the International Centre for Integrated Mountain Development found that water levels in rivers downstream rose between seven to nine metres within 30 minutes, washing away several monitoring stations. Oxford University professor Mike Searle said the event likely happened because of "two different processes - tectonics with the Himalayas uplifting, and then climate change with increasingly high temperatures and melting of glaciers."

Why it matters: Scientists say the whole glacier appears to have collapsed "in one go", which Searle described as "very unusual", adding that "these floods happen all the time, but not at this scale."

Source: BBC

3. Nvidia revenue doubles; Amazon triples chip order

Nvidia reported $96bn (£71bn) of revenue during the second quarter, more than double the amount for the same period a year earlier, with the company expecting revenue of $108bn in the next quarter. The company's data centre division alone generated $89bn in the period, up by 117% from a year earlier. Nvidia shares rose 4.7% in after-hours trading after the figures beat Wall Street's expectations.

Why it matters: Nvidia's financial success has helped transform it into the world's most valuable firm, with a market capitalisation of more than $5tn, and demand for its computing hardware is now central to the global AI boom.

Source: BBC

4. Nvidia closes in on $12.9bn Hugging Face deal

Nvidia has agreed to buy Hugging Face for $12.9 billion, according to The Information, citing a source familiar with the matter, though Business Insider reported the talks had not yet produced a signed agreement and could still fall apart. Hugging Face, founded in 2016, is one of the most popular hubs where developers share and download open-source AI models, and the company was recently generating about $150 million a year in revenue. The price marks a huge jump from Hugging Face's last known value - the company raised $235 million in 2023 in a funding round that valued it at $4.5 billion. Hugging Face had previously turned down a $500 million investment offer from Nvidia in late last year that would have valued it at $7 billion.

Why it matters: Buying Hugging Face would give Nvidia a strong foothold in the world of open-source AI, helping protect its dominance in AI chips by keeping more of the market dependent on its hardware rather than closed AI labs building their own processors.

Source: TechCrunch

5. Meta pays $18bn to settle child harm claims

Social media giant Meta has agreed to an $18bn (£13.3bn) settlement with US states and territories to resolve claims that Facebook and Instagram harmed children, with a California judge approving the deal. Meta must pay 48 states, plus the District of Columbia and three US territories, marking the company's largest payment over child safety litigation to date. Meta said the payment "will be distributed in annual instalments over a 10-year period" and the company denied any wrongdoing. As part of the settlement, Meta will implement new default protections including a two-hour daily time limit across Instagram and Facebook for teens, a night mode blocking notifications between midnight and 6am, and a school mode muting notifications between 08:00 and 15:00 during school days.

Why it matters: California Attorney General Rob Bonta said the settlement "is a good blueprint" for other social media platforms including TikTok, Snap and YouTube to follow, and said his office would be "more focused" on bringing changes to the entire social media industry if they do not.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Edinburgh's Princes Street reopens after seven weeks

Edinburgh's Princes Street is to fully reopen on Saturday, seven weeks after a major fire at the B-listed former Debenhams building in the early hours of 9 July. City of Edinburgh Council said "several tonnes" of debris had been removed from the site, and tram links would resume subject to "testing" on Friday, with bus services returning along Princes Street rather than the current diversion along George Street. The Scottish government had previously committed £5m to the recovery effort to allow some businesses to offset lost business, with Edinburgh Bus Users Group estimating that around 100,000 bus passenger journeys were impacted every day during the closure.

Source: BBC
