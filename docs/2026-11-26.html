1. UK net migration hits lowest level since 1997

UK net migration fell to 77,000 in the year ending June 2026, according to the Office for National Statistics. The ONS said this figure has more than halved compared with the year ending June 2025, which was updated to 204,000. Excluding the Covid-19 pandemic, when international travel restrictions were in place, this is the lowest recorded net migration estimate since 1997. More British nationals are estimated to have left the UK in the year to June 2026 (245,000) than moved to the country (109,000).

Why it matters: The figure finally meets the target set by David Cameron before the 2010 general election of getting net migration below 100,000, a goal that successive governments failed to achieve for sixteen years.

Source: The Guardian

2. UK-EU treaty texts published with 10-year exit clause

The BBC understands the deal covering food exports has a 10-year termination clause that would mean if one party ended the agreement, they would have to pay to rebuild border checks infrastructure. An internal analysis forecasts a 16% boost to agricultural exports under the deal. Plans to use secondary legislation, including granting direct so-called 'Henry VIII powers' to ministers to align UK and EU laws, will also be detailed in the legislation.

Why it matters: Opposition leader Kemi Badenoch has said she would scrap the deal and Reform UK's Nigel Farage has suggested he would refuse to pay the termination costs, raising questions about the deal's durability under a future government.

Source: BBC

3. US tariffs target 14 partners, China faces 40% threat

The Trump administration has unveiled proposed tariffs on more than a dozen trading partners, with rates starting at 15% for the EU, South Korea, Switzerland and Taiwan and rising to as much as 35% for Vietnam. China faces a capped rate of 20% on top of an existing 25% tariff, but that rate could rise to 40% if the trade truce reached in South Korea last October is not extended beyond January. The US Trade Representative's Office said it intends to cap the new duties at lower levels for countries that have completed trade deals or are still negotiating with Washington. US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said the new policy likely will not take effect for months, with a senior US official saying the duties could take effect as early as January.

Why it matters: India and Vietnam face proposed tariffs of 25% and 35% respectively, but those rates could fall sharply to 17% and 20% if ongoing talks with the US produce reciprocal trade agreements, giving both countries a strong incentive to conclude deals.

Source: CNBC

4. Poundland collapses, 11,000 jobs at risk

Poundland has collapsed into administration, putting 11,000 jobs at risk after its US owners failed to find a buyer to keep it in business. Poundland boss Barry Williams said the US owner Gordon Brothers had removed 'all borrowing facilities and access to our bank accounts' on 27 October, and adequate funds had not been released since then to keep the business solvent. Gordon Brothers said it had loaned the firm £85m over a year to help save it from insolvency but denied accusations it had run the company into the ground to profit from selling off its 600 shops piecemeal. Labour MP Liam Byrne said the House of Commons' Business, Innovation, Science and Trade Committee will recall Gordon Brothers before Parliament.

Why it matters: Prime Minister Andy Burnham said the news of Poundland going into administration just weeks before Christmas would be 'deeply concerning for workers and their families', while administrator Alix Partners said it would look at all options including sales of the business either in whole or part.

Source: BBC

5. Iran women on death row after January protests

Six women have been sentenced to death this year in connection with January's anti-government protests in Iran, according to the UN's human rights rapporteur for Iran, Mai Sato. Among them is Taraneh Rahimi, 21, who her cousin says has been unable to walk properly after her legs were beaten, and who signed a confession after interrogators threatened her sister. January's protests began over economic grievances and grew to become Iran's largest against the clerical establishment since the 1979 Islamic Revolution, with the US-based Human Rights Activists News Agency (Hrana) saying it has identified more than 7,000 people killed in the crackdown that followed. Amnesty International's Iran researcher Raha Bahreini describes it as a 'horrific rise' in politically motivated executions, with women protesters sentenced to death in numbers not seen for decades.

Why it matters: The Iranian embassy in London told the BBC it categorically rejected allegations of torture and that death sentences were issued only in cases involving 'intentional murder' or the taking up of weapons, not for peaceful protest.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

GMB accuses CMAL of sabotaging Ferguson shipyard

GMB Scotland has accused CMAL boss Kevin Hobbs of 'intentionally undermining' the Ferguson shipyard's reputation in a 'pre-planned' attack after he told MSPs the Port Glasgow yard should not be given more CalMac ferry orders. Hobbs, who has been chief executive of government-owned Caledonian Maritime Assets Ltd since 2016, said he was '100%' against plans to directly award contracts for two small CalMac ferries to Ferguson's, arguing it would be quicker and cheaper to tender bids from shipyards including those in Turkey, Romania and Poland. GMB Scotland general secretary Rhea Wolfson said in a letter to Transport Secretary Stephen Flynn that CMAL chair Morag McNeill once told board members that Ferguson's would only build another CalMac ferry 'over her dead body'. Ferguson's is currently completing MV Glen Rosa, due for handover in February, and the only other work it has in prospect is a plan to build some tugs funded by the government.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

HMRC late payment interest rate rises from 16 November 2026

The Bank of England base rate increased to 4.00% from 3.75% on 5 November 2026, triggering a rise in HMRC interest rates. Late payment interest is set at base rate plus 4%; repayment interest is set at base rate minus 1%, with a minimum floor of 0.5%. Changes take effect on 16 November 2026 for quarterly instalment payments and 24 November 2026 for non-quarterly instalment payments.

Source: HMRC

Companies House pauses P&L filing; mandates iXBRL software from April 2028

From April 2028, all UK registered companies must file accounts in Inline eXtensible Business Reporting Language (iXBRL) format using commercial software; web and paper-based filing systems will close for accounts filings from that date. The government has paused the requirement for small companies and micro-entities to file profit and loss accounts with Companies House from April 2028, and will continue to allow abridged accounts and the option to fillet profit and loss information. Additional changes from April 2028 include a strengthened eligibility statement for audit exemption claims, a requirement to file component parts of accounts together, and a limit on how many times a company can shorten its accounting reference period.

Source: Companies House
