1. Canada fires tariff salvo back at US

Prime Minister Mark Carney described the measures as "dollar-for-dollar" retaliation, though Canada later removed dozens of seafood items after pushback from its fishing industry. Both US and Canadian officials have said they want a deal, but no talks are under way after negotiations collapsed in late August.

Why it matters: Canada's GDP grew 3.3% in the second quarter and it gained 181,000 jobs from April to July, but around 41,000 jobs were lost in August alone, coinciding with the new US tariffs and the collapse of trade talks.

Source: BBC

2. AfD demands power after Saxony-Anhalt breakthrough

Germany's far-right Alternative for Deutschland won 43.8% of the vote in Sunday's Saxony-Anhalt state election, falling just three seats short of an outright majority in the 83-seat state parliament. The party's leader in the state, Ulrich Siegmund, declared a "clear mandate to govern" and said discussions with other parties began on Sunday night. Chancellor Friedrich Merz described the result as "seismic", saying his CDU's vote share was almost halved to 17.2%, calling it the worst result the party had suffered "in years, if not decades". No far-right party has controlled a German state since World War Two, and governing at state level would mean powers over policing and local education.

Why it matters: Merz acknowledged that results in upcoming regional elections in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania and Berlin could have "increasing significance", while the AfD's deputy parliamentary leader warned that without cooperation from other parties "we might end up in severe chaos and maybe we have a new election".

Source: BBC

3. JLR confirms 4,000 job cuts over two years

Jaguar Land Rover confirmed on Monday it will reduce its global workforce by around 4,000 roles over two years as part of an effort to save £1.7bn, with cuts falling mainly on the 26,000 UK salaried and management employees at plants in Solihull, Warwickshire, Merseyside and its Coventry headquarters. The company, owned by Indian conglomerate Tata, has been hit by strong competition, Trump's US tariffs, and last year's cyber-attack, which eventually cost around £200m and caused pre-tax profit to collapse to just £14m from £2.5bn a year earlier. CEO PB Balaji said the cuts were aimed at "reducing organisational complexity" and would let the company break even on sales of about 300,000 vehicles a year. Business minister Jonathan Reynolds has ruled out using taxpayers' money to deter the redundancies, while Unite's general secretary Sharon Graham said she would push for retraining and redeployment over compulsory losses.

Source: The Guardian

4. Mistral raises €3bn, valued at €21bn

French AI startup Mistral raised 3 billion euros ($3.5 billion) in a funding round led by memory chip giant Samsung Electronics, giving it a post-money valuation of more than 21 billion euros - up from 11.7 billion euros a year ago. The Scaleup Europe Fund, a European Union-backed investment fund managed by EQT, and existing investor PSG Equity also participated. CEO Arthur Mensch told CNBC the funding would go toward building more infrastructure including its own data centres, and that the amount of compute the company owns is set to grow "around 100% in the next five years". Mensch said he expects Mistral's annual recurring revenue to exceed $1 billion this year and that the new funding is "accelerating and enabling further growth down the line in 2027".

Why it matters: Mistral is betting on open-weight AI models and a "sovereign AI" pitch to position itself as a non-US, non-Chinese alternative for enterprise customers, but faces intense competition from highly capable Chinese players.

Source: CNBC

5. Miami crash investigators recover flight recorders

Investigators have recovered the flight data recorder and cockpit voice recorder from the Amazon Air Boeing 767-300 that crashed at Miami International Airport on Sunday, and are sending them to NTSB headquarters for evaluation. Five people were killed and five others seriously injured when the plane, operated by 21 Air and arriving from San Juan, Puerto Rico, overshot the runway shortly before 14:00 local time; it struck navigational aids, hit a white Ford van owned by a contract cleaning company, then collided with a Toyota Corolla, coming to rest about 1,300ft (394m) beyond the runway. NTSB chairwoman Jennifer Homendy said it was too early to draw conclusions, describing the crash scene as "devastating" and adding that recovering victims' bodies remained the current priority. Investigators will examine flight history, crew experience, radar data, aircraft performance, and whether the runway should have had an engineered materials arresting system (EMAS), which Homendy said would be a "key" part of the investigation.

Why it matters: Two of the four runways at Miami International Airport remained closed following the crash, with passengers warned to expect further disruption after Sunday's cancellations affected the arrival of aircraft and flight crews.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Fatal hit-and-run near Edinburgh Airport; hunt under way

Police are hunting two women and a man who fled on foot after a fatal multi-vehicle crash on the A8 near Edinburgh Airport on Monday morning. Officers were called at around 09:25 to a collision involving a grey Mercedes A250, a black Renault Captur, a grey Skoda Karoq and a grey Hyundai i30 between the Newbridge and Gogar roundabouts; the 58-year-old driver of the Hyundai i30 was pronounced dead at the scene. A police helicopter and dog unit have been deployed in the search, while four other casualties were taken to hospital and later released. Inspector Thomas Mallinson said: "It is vital we trace the three people who left the area before police arrived."

Source: BBC
