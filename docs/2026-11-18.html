1. Ukraine strikes Crimean Bridge in 'unprecedented' attack

Ukraine severely damaged the Crimean Bridge overnight using cruise missiles and drones, with a large arched section apparently destroyed and the road section declared "out of action" by Ukraine's military. President Zelensky called it an "unprecedented operation" resulting from a seven-stage effort that gradually destroyed Russian air defence systems, radars and electronic warfare equipment over a month. Russia recorded the biggest Ukrainian drone attack since the war began, with 2,256 drones downed, for the second consecutive day.

Why it matters: The bridge is a crucial logistical and symbolic link for Russian forces heading toward the front line, and the timing comes just ahead of Putin's meeting with Trump and Xi Jinping in China, which Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov did not rule out as a connection.

Source: BBC

2. Iran fires drones at Bahrain in third attack

Iran fired drones on Tuesday at a US radar installation in Bahrain in its third attack on American military targets in the Persian Gulf island kingdom in five days. Bahrain said its air defence systems successfully intercepted the attacks, and the US military said the attack inflicted no damage to US equipment or injury to American personnel.

Source: NPR

3. Nvidia revenue doubles; board rules out Huang return

Nvidia reported quarterly revenue of $115.84bn, doubling year-on-year and beating Wall Street's expectation of $109.15bn, in its first earnings report since the ouster of CEO Jensen Huang. Board chairman Harvey Jones said Nvidia "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO," adding that the board accepted his voluntary resignation and nodding to interim CEO Colette Kress's 13-year tenure at the company. Nvidia's stock had tumbled more than 10% following Huang's ouster, dropping its valuation to $3.7tn from an all-time high of nearly $6tn in October, though shares ticked up 5% in after-hours trading on Tuesday. Huang issued a public statement saying he would be willing to return if the board decided it was in the best interest of shareholders.

Why it matters: Nvidia controls about 90% of the AI chips market and the world's top AI companies rely on it for their technology, meaning the company's leadership turmoil can have an outsized effect on the US economy.

Source: The Guardian

4. Trump lands in Shenzhen for APEC summit

US President Donald Trump landed in Shenzhen on Wednesday for his third meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping since the two countries agreed a trade truce. Xi personally greeted Trump at Shenzhen Bao'an International Airport - an unusual gesture, as Chinese presidents rarely welcome visiting guests on arrival.

Why it matters: The meeting comes amid heightened tensions over the Nvidia espionage scandal and Chinese rare earth export restrictions that remain about 75% below normal levels, according to the Washington-based Center for Strategic and International Studies.

Source: Al Jazeera

5. UK inflation rises to 3.6% on fuel costs

UK inflation rose to 3.6% in October, up from 3.5% in September, driven primarily by higher motor fuel prices, according to figures from the Office for National Statistics. Transport prices rose 7.5% year on year, up from 6.6% in September, with petrol rising by 5.2 pence per litre to an average of 174.7p, while diesel rose 7p per litre to 199.5p - the highest since records began in 1996. In better news for households, food prices rose at their slowest rate since September 2021, at 0.9%, with chocolate prices falling 2.9% and fish prices also driving inflation lower. Meanwhile, factory gate prices rose by an annual rate of 5.1% in October, up from a revised 4.7% in September.

Why it matters: While fuel drove inflation higher, the fall in food price inflation to its lowest rate since September 2021 offers some relief to household budgets.

Source: The Guardian

Scottish e-bike seizures nearly double in a year

The figures were submitted to Holyrood's justice committee as part of an inquiry into crime and antisocial behaviour involving electric bikes and scooters, and show recorded incidents involving the vehicles rose to 3,208 in the same period, up 1,689 from last year, while crimes and offences involving them rose from 1,730 to 3,367.
