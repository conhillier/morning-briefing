1. Russia strikes second Kyiv bridge Saturday

A Russian strike hit the Northern Bridge in Kyiv on Saturday morning, Mayor Vitali Klitschko said, damaging the road surface and trolleybus overhead wires. Traffic on the bridge from the left to the right bank was blocked, Klitschko wrote on Telegram. The strike follows repeated Russian drone attacks on the city's Southern Bridge since Thursday, which prompted Kyiv's defence council to hold a crisis meeting on Friday. Following that meeting, Klitschko said the city was in a "very dramatic situation" as Moscow was battering its "critical infrastructure, logistics, housing" and other key facilities.

Why it matters: The two bridges link the eastern and western parts of the capital and had not been hit before Thursday, signalling a new stage in Moscow's attacks.

Source: BBC

2. G7 releases 100 million barrels to ease crisis

The G7 announced it will release 100 million barrels of oil and diesel from reserves, beginning immediately and lasting four months, following US President Donald Trump's threat to ban diesel exports. The plan includes a "substantial release" of diesel within 20 days, with discussions around additional diesel releases as necessary, G7 leaders said in a joint statement.

Why it matters: Avoiding a ban on US diesel exports will offer significant relief to countries reliant on imports of the fuel, including the UK, where prices at the pump topped £2 a litre for the first time on Friday.

Source: BBC

3. UAE: Flydubai co-pilot staged terrorist attack

The prosecutor general of the United Arab Emirates has said a Flydubai co-pilot "attempted to carry out a terrorist act" on flight FZ1073, using a crash axe against the pilot inside the cockpit. State news agency WAM quoted Hamad Saif al-Shamsi as saying the attacker tried to take control of the aircraft's flight controls. The plane, carrying 174 passengers, was flying from Dubai to Tel Aviv on Wednesday when the co-pilot wounded Indian pilot Smit Machchhar, who had opened the cockpit to seek help. The aircraft briefly plunged about 17,000 feet before new pilots brought it under control and diverted it to an emergency landing at Prince Sultan bin Abdulaziz International Airport in Tabuk, Saudi Arabia.

Why it matters: Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said on Friday that Israel was looking into whether the co-pilot had acted under anyone's direction, warning that anyone involved would pay a "heavy price".

Source: Al Jazeera

4. Spain's Sanchez loses key housing vote

Spain's Congress rejected two government decree-laws aimed at tackling the country's housing crisis, in a defeat for Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez that has heightened speculation he might call a snap election. MPs rejected the first decree by 178 votes to 172 and the second by 184 to 166. The decrees, drawn up in response to nationwide protests following the eviction of 87-year-old Maricarmen Abascal from her Madrid flat, included measures such as the suspension of evictions of vulnerable tenants until 2030 and tax increases for tourist apartments. The government's defeat underlines that Sanchez, a Socialist, does not command a congressional majority, and sources told Spanish media the government would spend the weekend deciding whether to dissolve parliament.

Why it matters: The defeat has heightened speculation that Sanchez might call an early general election, which was not due until next summer.

Source: BBC

5. US adds just 29,000 jobs in September

US employers added just 29,000 jobs in September, a sharp drop from last month, with the unemployment rate rising slightly to 4.2%, according to the US Bureau of Labor Statistics. The numbers were under half of economists' expectations of just under 70,000 new jobs, with most gains concentrated in healthcare, which added 17,000 new jobs, while information, financial and professional industries saw losses. Earlier figures were also revised down: initial reports from July and August altogether dropped by 60,000, and the labour market contracted by 10,000 jobs in July after revisions. Growth in average hourly earnings slumped to 3%, described as the lowest rate in over five years.

Why it matters: The September release cools expectations for the US Federal Reserve to increase rates again in its final meeting before the 3 November midterms, though most central bank officials had pencilled in at least one more rate hike before year's end.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Syngenta took £1m public cash before Grangemouth closure

Agricultural chemical firm Syngenta collected around £1 million of Scottish Enterprise funding to create new jobs just months before announcing plans to close its Grangemouth site, putting 377 workers at risk. The cash was part of a £2.2 million award made in May last year to expand production at the plant. Syngenta said it intends to repay the funding in full to Scottish Enterprise if it ceases operations at Grangemouth after consultation. Local MP Brian Leishman, who described a meeting with the company as going "poorly", said Syngenta had "offered one final solution and that is the closure of the plant" and called the situation "very final".

Source: BBC
