1. Israel opens criminal probes into Gaza killings

Israel's military announced its first criminal investigations into troop conduct in Gaza, focusing on the killings of 5-year-old Hind Rajab and her family, and of 15 Palestinian paramedics. The military admitted in Wednesday's statement that troops had fired at the car carrying the 5-year-old, four of her cousins and her aunt and uncle as they fled an Israeli invasion of Gaza City. The military also admitted that troops had fired toward the ambulance dispatched to rescue Hind, killing the two medics aboard. The military said it would not probe three other attacks that killed aid workers from World Central Kitchen and Doctors Without Borders, having completed reviews of 150 incidents of troop conduct in Gaza.

Why it matters: Rights groups and the Palestinian Red Crescent have rejected the military investigations as insufficient, with both calling for an international investigation, and Dyab Abou Jahjah of the Belgium-based Hind Rajab foundation calling the probes a "charade meant to whitewash the culprits instead of convicting them."

Source: NPR

2. Five powers condemn Israel E1 settlement tenders

The UK, France, Germany, Italy and Canada issued a joint statement calling Israel's plans to open tenders for settlement construction in the E1 area east of Jerusalem "unacceptable" and urging Israel to "retract the plans immediately". The Israeli housing ministry issued a tender for 1,234 of the 3,401 housing units approved by the Israeli government last August, covering about 12 sq km between East Jerusalem and the settlement of Maale Adumim. The deadline for bids is 19 October, just days before Israel's general election on 27 October, making it much harder for any future government to overturn any tenders issued. The UN secretary general also said the E1 plan posed an "existential threat" to a contiguous Palestinian state.

Why it matters: Opponents of the E1 project have warned that it would effectively block the establishment of a Palestinian state because it would cut off the north of the West Bank from the south, and prevent the development of a contiguous Palestinian urban area connecting Ramallah, East Jerusalem and Bethlehem.

Source: BBC

3. US debt surpasses $40 trillion record

The US federal government's accumulated debt has reached a record $40 trillion, according to a daily update from the Treasury Department. Government spending is expected to exceed tax revenues by more than $2 trillion this year, and the government will spend more than $1 trillion this year just to pay interest on the debt, which is about 15% more than it spent on interest last year. NPR's Scott Horsley noted that what is remarkable is that debt is hitting a new high during relative prosperity, leaving the US with less fiscal flexibility when the next major challenge arises.

Why it matters: The rapid debt growth is occurring during a period of relative economic prosperity, meaning the US has reduced room to manoeuvre when the next major economic or fiscal challenge arises.

Source: NPR

4. North Korea fires missile after US drill cut

North Korea launched a suspected ballistic missile toward the sea on Thursday, according to Japan's prime minister's office, which detected the suspected launch but gave no further details. The launch came a day after North Korea dismissed a US decision to scale back its 11-day Ulchi Freedom Shield exercises with South Korea. Kim Yo Jong, the influential sister of North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, said the scaling back was "not even worthy of comment" because "The provocative, aggressive nature of the drills won't change even though their duration and size were reduced."

Why it matters: Kim Yo Jong warned that if the US calculates its gesture as one of "so-called good faith, they will not get the desired answer", signalling that North Korea does not view the drill reduction as a meaningful diplomatic overture.

Source: NPR

5. US deports 20 to Liberia under 1,200-person deal

Twenty deportees from the United States arrived in Liberia on Thursday at the Roberts international airport outside the capital, Monrovia, as the first tranche of a new agreement that will see the west African country receive as many as 1,200 people under the Trump administration's immigration crackdown. Liberia's information minister Jerolinmek Piah said the 1,200 deportees will include African nationals as well as nationals from North America, South America and the Caribbean. The US has agreed to extend visitor visas for Liberians from 12 to 36 months and pledged $124m in assistance as part of the arrangement.

Why it matters: A new report by Refugees International and Human Rights First found the Trump administration has reached deals with at least 35 countries and has sent roughly 23,000 people to 26 of them as of early August, making the Liberia agreement the largest single arrangement so far.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Scottish judge overrules parents on child treatment

A judge at the Court of Session in Edinburgh gave doctors permission to provide "potentially life-saving" medical treatment to three children whose parents had refused consent. Two boys from Jehovah's Witness families opposed blood transfusions on religious grounds - one was being treated for aggressive brain cancer called medulloblastoma and the other had a blocked kidney complicated by sickle cell disease. The parents of a third child, a 10-year-old girl bitten by the family dog, refused a tetanus jag because they "disputed the medical science" and preferred natural approaches. Judge Lord Braid ruled that each child came from "loving parents" but that their welfare outweighed any family objections.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Companies House accounts reforms delayed to April 2028

From April 2028, small companies and micro-entities must file profit and loss accounts with Companies House, but may opt out of publication on the public register. All UK registered companies will be required to file accounts in iXBRL format via commercial software, with web and paper-based filing systems closing for accounts filings from that date. The implementation date has been moved from April 2027 to April 2028, giving companies one full accounting year plus 9 months (21 months) to prepare.

Source: Companies House

Bank Rate held at 3.75% by MPC, July 2026

At its meeting ending on 29 July 2026, the Monetary Policy Committee voted by a majority of 6-3 to maintain Bank Rate at 3.75%. Three members voted to increase Bank Rate by 0.25 percentage points, to 4%. CPI inflation has fallen to 2.6% since the previous meeting, although it is expected to rise later this year as the effects of higher energy prices continue to pass through.

Source: Bank of England
