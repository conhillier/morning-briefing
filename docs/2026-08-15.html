1. Venezuela frees 131 political prisoners

Venezuela says it has freed 131 people considered political prisoners, in a sign of progress for national reconciliation following the United States' removal of former President Nicolas Maduro. Those released had been granted "alternative measures from incarceration" under the Program for Peace and Democratic Coexistence, the country's interim government said.

Why it matters: Foro Penal said that 391 political prisoners remained behind bars as of August 10, and the joint statement from US-brokered talks between the government and opposition did not address the issue of political prisoners.

Source: Al Jazeera

2. OpenAI C-suite chaos ahead of IPO

OpenAI's chief revenue officer Denise Dresser announced her sudden departure on Thursday, two days after operating chief Brad Lightcap said he was ending an eight-year stint at the company - leaving OpenAI's C-suite in turmoil as the company tries to justify its $852 billion valuation and prepare for a historic IPO. Dresser, Lightcap, and Fidji Simo are now all gone, adding to earlier departures in April that included marketing chief Kate Rouch. CEO Sam Altman's president and co-founder Greg Brockman told employees that run rate revenue increased more than 20% month over month in July, including 32% growth for business customers. OpenAI named Dali Rajic, former COO of cybersecurity company Wiz, as its new chief revenue officer.

Source: CNBC

3. Berkshire boosts Alphabet to third-largest holding

Berkshire Hathaway, now operating under CEO Greg Abel, owned about 106 million Alphabet shares worth $37.9 billion at the end of June, after its stake jumped 83% in the second quarter, making Alphabet its third-largest US-listed equity holding behind only Apple and American Express. The increase largely reflects a $10 billion private stock purchase announced in early June, when Alphabet sought fresh capital to finance its AI infrastructure buildout. Berkshire also boosted its holding in Delta Air Lines by 44% during the quarter to 57.3 million shares valued at about $5.4 billion. The conglomerate ended a run of 14 straight quarters as a net seller of equities, emerging as a buyer with nearly $20 billion in net purchases.

Why it matters: Berkshire's cash level fell to $365.5 billion at the end of June from a record $397.4 billion three months earlier, as the company began putting more capital to work through investments and share repurchases.

Source: CNBC

4. Europe heatwave hits 300 million people

An intense heatwave - Europe's fifth this summer - is affecting some 300 million people, more than half of Europe's population, with officials saying it is lasting longer than the deadly 2003 heat wave that killed tens of thousands. In France, Italy, and Spain, some 92 million people are sweltering in temperatures exceeding 95 degrees Fahrenheit in cities largely without air-conditioning. In the UK, temperatures hit 100 degrees on Thursday in parts of southern England, and drought has been declared in three-quarters of England and all of Wales. Romania was forced to begin shutting down its second nuclear reactor at Cernavoda nuclear plant due to low water levels in the Danube.

Why it matters: July was the hottest month on record in the United Kingdom, and the country's weather agency says 2026 is on track to be the hottest year on record. Some UK water companies have warned they could run out of water within six weeks.

Source: NPR

5. Japan floods kill eight near Tokyo

Record-breaking rains outside the capital Tokyo on Friday led to widespread flooding and at least eight deaths, with several victims found on flooded roads trapped in submerged cars. Some parts of Chiba prefecture outside Tokyo saw three times more rain in half a day than they usually see for the whole month of August, according to Japan's meteorological agency. Evacuation orders were issued for more than 100,000 residents, about 40 percent of prefectures, cities and towns were flooded, and more than 20,000 households lost power. Thousands of travelers were stranded at Narita International Airport, and trains and highways were shut down during the peak of the Obon holiday travel season.

Source: NPR

Closer to Home

Burnham pledges £65m for drought-hit farmers

Prime Minister Andy Burnham has announced £65m of funding to help farmers manage the devastating drought, saying "farmers are at the sharp end of a changing climate and they should not be carrying that risk alone." Most of the funding - £50m - will go to the sustainable farming incentive, bringing the total SFI budget to £290m, while £15m is earmarked to support the building of on-farm reservoirs. Burnham chaired a Cobra meeting on extreme heat, wildfires and drought this week, with Downing Street seeking a cross-government response. Campaigners have warned the £15m for reservoirs is reallocated money from existing funds and will only fund a "fraction" of reservoirs needed.

Source: The Guardian

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

20% business rates cut for pubs, clubs, live music venues

From April 2027 (2027/28 financial year), pubs, social clubs and live music venues across England will receive a 20% cut to their business rates bills, saving the typical pub an estimated £1,100 in the next financial year. The package is worth around £100 million a year and will benefit nearly 32,000 pubs, clubs and live music venues. The largest live music venues will not qualify for the new 20% discount.

Source: HM Treasury

Bank Rate held at 3.75% by 6-3 MPC vote

At its meeting ending 29 July 2026, the MPC voted 6-3 to maintain Bank Rate at 3.75%; three members voted to increase Bank Rate by 0.25 percentage points to 4%. CPI inflation has fallen to 2.6% since the previous meeting, although it is expected to rise later this year as the effects of higher energy prices continue to pass through. The next rate decision is due 17 September 2026.

Source: Bank of England
