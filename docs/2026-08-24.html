1. Canada-US trade war erupts after talks collapse

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney confirmed Canada will match US tariffs "dollar-for-dollar" from 8 September, including levies on steel, dairy, appliances and electronics, after trade negotiations broke down late on Friday. The new 50% US tariffs cover about $20bn (£15bn; C$28bn) of Canadian imports - roughly 5% in total - including wine, dairy, cement, clothing and hockey equipment. Carney said the US presented "unacceptable" terms, including curbing Canada's ability to strike new trade deals with other countries, while US negotiators accused Canada of making "new demands and walk backs" on previously agreed terms. On Sunday, US trade representative Jamieson Greer told Fox News there were no plans to resume talks.

Why it matters: The collapse upended a deeply integrated trading relationship underpinned by the USMCA pact, which covers $1.6tn (£1.2tn) in annual trilateral trade across Canada, the US and Mexico, with no clear path to resolution.

Source: BBC

2. US to launch 'economic D-Day' against Iran Monday

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced the US will unveil "the single greatest financial offensive ever" against Iran on Monday, describing it as the "endgame" for Washington's campaign against Tehran. The new measures will add to an existing sanctions regime already targeting Iran's banking, energy, aviation and cryptocurrency sectors. The escalation comes after both sides missed a 60-day ceasefire window, closing off the formal truce mechanism aimed at ending the war now in its sixth month. Iran's state-controlled Persian Gulf Strait Authority warned that vessels violating its transit rules in the Hormuz could face "fines, seizure, or confiscation" during future passages.

Why it matters: Bessent warned that "any nation that serves as a financial artery of a withering regime should expect to share in its isolation," signalling the threat of secondary sanctions on countries still transacting with Tehran.

Source: CNBC

3. Burnham flies to Kyiv with Storm Shadow pledge

UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham will travel to Kyiv on Monday - his first foreign visit as prime minister - to mark Ukraine's 35th anniversary of independence and announce that the UK will allow European defence company MBDA to declassify information about UK-made components of the Anglo-French Storm Shadow/Scalp missiles to help Ukraine build its own version. Burnham will also announce that the UK will help France set up assembly lines in Ukraine for long-range missiles, a promise French President Emmanuel Macron made last month. The prime minister will co-chair a meeting of the coalition of the willing, comprising 34 countries that have promised to help secure Ukraine in the event of a peace deal with Russia. This weekend it emerged that Russia had withdrawn its ambassador in London without naming a replacement, prompting speculation it was downgrading its diplomatic presence in the UK.

Why it matters: While the announcement will probably take years to have an impact on the war, it is likely to escalate tensions with Moscow at an already fraught moment in UK-Russian relations.

Source: The Guardian

4. DRC Ebola outbreak kills 2,642, Pope urges action

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo has reached 5,515 confirmed cases and killed 2,642 people, with 51 new cases detected in Ituri and North Kivu provinces, according to the latest government figures. The case fatality rate has climbed from about 20 percent in early June to nearly 48 percent, meaning almost one in two people infected are dying. The outbreak, declared in mid-May, is the DRC's 17th Ebola epidemic and has been designated a Public Health Emergency of International Concern by the WHO - its highest alert level - also covering neighbouring Uganda.

Why it matters: The outbreak is now on track to surpass the deadliest Ebola epidemic on record - the 2014-2016 outbreak in West Africa that killed more than 11,000 people - and WHO officials say the response needs to be scaled up two to threefold.

Source: Al Jazeera

5. DR Congo and M23 agree peace roadmap in Switzerland

The Democratic Republic of Congo government and the Rwanda-backed M23 rebel group have agreed a roadmap for peace talks after five days of talks in Switzerland. In a joint statement, they pledged to "pursue negotiations towards comprehensive peace and conflict resolution" and set up a mechanism to monitor the ceasefire, with the first verification mission due to take place on Monday in Minembwe, an area of South Kivu affected by fighting. The roadmap sets out a timeline for talks under a US-brokered peace framework signed in 2025, though disagreements remain over issues including sanctions against M23, the release of detainees and the timing of political reforms. Talks were attended by M23, the DR Congo government, Qatar, the US, Togo, the African Union Commission and host Switzerland.

Why it matters: Fighting has continued in North and South Kivu despite repeated ceasefire commitments, and thousands of people have been killed with hundreds of thousands of civilians forced from their homes during the decades of conflict.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Glasgow gangland fears after Kirkintilloch garage fire

Police are investigating a deliberate car fire at Kelvinside Motor Company in Kirkintilloch at 22:55 on Saturday, days after officers attended a "disturbance" at the same property. The garage was previously targeted in a fire attack in April last year, which police investigated as part of Operation Portaledge - the gangland probe covering a feud that produced 84 separate incidents including fire bombings and attempted murders. The latest incident came just 24 hours after police confirmed they were also investigating a car crash into a house in Eaglesham, south of Glasgow, on Friday afternoon. Police Scotland said there were "no reported injuries" and officers were keeping an open mind over links to Operation Portaledge.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Business rates cut 20% for pubs, clubs, live music venues

From April 2027 (2027/28 financial year), pubs, social clubs and live music venues across England will receive a 20% cut to their business rates bills, saving the typical pub an estimated £1,100 in the next financial year. The package is worth around £100 million a year and will benefit nearly 32,000 pubs, clubs and live music venues. The very largest live music venues will not be eligible for the new 20% discount, with details to be set out at the Budget.

Source: HM Treasury

Bank Rate held at 3.75% after MPC July 2026 vote

At its meeting ending on 29 July 2026, the Monetary Policy Committee voted by a majority of 6-3 to maintain Bank Rate at 3.75%, with three members voting to increase Bank Rate by 0.25 percentage points to 4%. CPI inflation has fallen to 2.6% since the previous meeting, although it is expected to rise later this year as the effects of higher energy prices continue to pass through. The next MPC decision is due on 17 September 2026.

Source: Bank of England
