1. Colombia quake death toll tops 180

Rescue workers in Colombia are working against the clock to free survivors trapped in buildings and under rubble following Monday's magnitude 7.4 earthquake. More than 180 people have been confirmed dead - most in the cities of Cali and Pereira - while the official death toll stands at 181, with an aggregated figure from local officials putting it potentially above 240. President Abelardo de la Espriella declared a state of emergency and reported that almost 2,600 had been injured and almost 200 people remain missing. More than 1,100 homes have been destroyed and more than 8,000 damaged, with more than 70 aftershocks recorded since Monday's earthquake.

Why it matters: In Cali alone, Cali Mayor Alejandro Eder said 239 people remained trapped and 45 buildings had completely or partially collapsed, including floors of a hospital dedicated to paediatric care, underscoring the scale of the ongoing emergency.

Source: BBC

3. Oil near $90 as Hormuz talks stall

Brent crude rose more than 2 percent overnight into Wednesday, taking the international benchmark to close to $90 a barrel, as attacks on shipping in the Middle East cast doubt on prospects for a Hormuz deal. Brent futures for October delivery stood at $89.61 as of 03:00 GMT, up about 24 percent compared with before the start of the US-Israel war on Iran in late February. The US Energy Information Administration said in its latest market outlook that it did not expect oil production in the Middle East to return to near pre-conflict levels until early 2027, and for the price of Brent to average $87 a barrel in 2026.

Source: Al Jazeera

4. Nvidia raises $500bn AI infrastructure fund

Nvidia has struck a deal with six large Wall Street firms - Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR - to raise more than $500bn (£370bn) to fund datacentres, chip factories and power stations needed to fuel the AI boom. CEO Jensen Huang said on X that it marked "a major milestone for Nvidia and the AI industry" and that the funding "will make AI factories more accessible to the companies, industries and nations building the future". Nvidia, which has a market value of $5.3tn, confirmed it had signed memorandums of understanding with the six firms to offer funding for hardware such as datacentres and chips used to run, train and build AI. The company did not disclose the financial terms, investment commitments by individual firms, or a timetable for deploying the planned $500bn.

Source: The Guardian

5. Spotify to label and limit AI artists

Spotify is creating a label for AI-generated artist identities called "AI personas" that will appear on artist profiles and track listings on playlists, and from September such artists will be blocked from personalised recommendations by default. The move aims to differentiate deepfake artists from real performers who might lose out on streams to artificial rivals. Spotify said it would use human review and AI investigative tools to determine if artists were fake, and would not rely on self-disclosure alone. Last year Spotify revealed it had removed 75 million spam tracks from its platform over a 12-month period as AI tools created a flood of fake music, with its legitimate catalogue standing at 100 million tracks.

Why it matters: Spotify said: "True artist-fan connection can only be built on a foundation of trust and authenticity" and that "listeners need to be able to trust that the artist behind the music is who they say they are," reflecting growing concern about AI's impact on the music industry.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Loganair quits Dundee Airport entirely

Loganair has confirmed it is withdrawing all of its operations from Dundee Airport, ending its service from Dundee to London Heathrow on 23 October. The airline said the public service obligation (PSO) contract was "financially unsustainable" and that it would support its Dundee-based employees to explore alternative roles. The Dundee to London Heathrow service currently runs twice a day during the week and once on Sundays, and a PSO has operated since 2014 to maintain the route. The closure of its operating base in Dundee will also impact air travel to Orkney and Shetland.

Source: BBC
