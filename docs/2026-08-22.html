1. Russia kills 16 in Kryvyi Rih mall strike

At least 16 people have been killed in a Russian double-tap drone strike on a shopping centre in the central Ukrainian city of Kryvyi Rih, local officials said. President Volodymyr Zelensky described the daytime attack in his home town as 'absolutely cynical and despicable', carried out in 'two waves', with the second strike targeting emergency workers at the scene. There are still nine people - including two children - reportedly missing, and rescue teams from four other Ukrainian regions have been urgently deployed.

Why it matters: The attack comes a day after 17 people were killed and more than 40 injured in a major Russian missile and drone attack on Ukraine's capital Kyiv, suggesting an intensification of strikes on civilian infrastructure.

Source: BBC

2. Canada-US trade talks go to the wire

Canada-US trade minister Dominic LeBlanc said negotiators 'have more work to do' after spending some eight hours in meetings with US trade representative Jamieson Greer ahead of a Friday night deadline to finalise a deal. Leaked details suggest the US will agree to reduce tariffs on Canadian steel and aluminium from 50% to 25%, and tariffs on Canadian automobiles from 25% to 15%. Canada could lose 90,000 jobs if the threatened additional tariffs were implemented, according to estimates from Calgary-based economist Trevor Tombe. A Leger poll found 56% of Canadians wanted Canada to take a hardline approach to the talks.

Why it matters: Conservative opposition leader Pierre Poilievre warned that any agreement including 'one-sided' tariffs on Canadian industry is 'a bad deal', and several provincial leaders have publicly criticised the deal terms, signalling a tough battle for Prime Minister Carney to secure buy-in.

Source: BBC

3. Iran president calls for end to US war

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian called for an end to the months-long war with the United States, stating that Tehran holds 'a position of power and dignity'. Pezeshkian defended the June memorandum of understanding agreed with Washington against hardline domestic critics in parliament, saying 'they cannot find even a single clause in this agreement that indicates capitulation.' However, Iran's military leadership separately warned the country remains ready to strike back, with Major-General Ali Abdollahi saying Iran's armed forces would respond to 'new threats with crushing, punishing and devastating responses.' Omani and Iranian foreign ministers also held a phone call discussing ways to resume dialogue and developments affecting navigation in the Strait of Hormuz.

Why it matters: US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is expected to outline planned sanctions against Iran at a news conference on Monday, marking a shift by the Trump administration towards economic rather than military pressure as the war nears the six-month mark.

Source: Al Jazeera

4. US borrowing costs rebound despite Treasury intervention

Long-term US borrowing costs rose again after the Treasury Department's debt buyback intervention proved short-lived, with the interest rate on 30-year bonds rising to around 5.27% on Friday. Yields had briefly fallen to 5.18% from an almost two-decade high of 5.34% following the Treasury's announcement. John Canavan, lead analyst at Oxford Economics, said the response was 'unsurprisingly short-lived', with traders focused on 'daunting' amounts of global borrowing. Economists at Capital Economics said 'much of the initial fall in 30-year yields has now been reversed', adding that the Treasury move was 'mainly a signalling mechanism'.

Why it matters: Movements in bond yields can affect mortgage rates and car loans, and gold climbed to a more than three-month high on Friday amid the ongoing uncertainty in the global economy.

Source: BBC

5. TikTok pays $400m in child privacy settlement

TikTok has agreed to pay $400m (£293m) to the US to end a lawsuit alleging its platform violated children's privacy, in one of the largest ever settlements over the issue. Under the terms, TikTok and ByteDance will immediately pay the DOJ $300m, with another $100m due when the government vacates a 2019 consent decree with the Federal Trade Commission. ByteDance was most recently valued by investors at $550bn.

Why it matters: The same law - COPPA - is now at the centre of a jury trial brought by attorneys general of 29 US states against Meta, which faces penalties that could exceed hundreds of billions of dollars.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Shoreham: fourth family member dies

Saja El-Khawas, aged six, died on Friday in hospital, three days after being pulled from the sea off Shoreham in West Sussex in an incident that also killed her mother, father and sister. Her sister Sara El-Khawas, 14, and her parents Hassan El-Khawas, 55, and Zeina Alayn, 37, had died on Tuesday. Sussex police said the family had travelled from their home in London and entered the water from the beach at Shoreham Fort around 2pm on Tuesday, before getting into trouble. Police said their current hypothesis is that 'this was a tragic accident, with no third-party involvement'.

Source: The Guardian
