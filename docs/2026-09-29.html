1. RAF Fairford suspects bailed, Rubio blames foreign actor

Five British nationals from London were detained on suspicion of explosives offences and terrorism after three suspicious vans were reported near RAF Fairford in the village of Whelford, Gloucestershire. On Tuesday, the head of counter-terrorism policing announced all five men had been released on bail, with sources saying detectives believe it is unlikely they were on the brink of a mass casualty attack. After the suspects' release it emerged that no explosive material had been found in the vans.

Why it matters: UK counter-terrorism police said they are keeping "an open mind" and are considering whether the activity was "committed by proxies, or individuals either knowingly or unknowingly working on behalf of a foreign state."

Source: The Guardian

2. UK diesel price hits all-time high

The price of diesel on UK forecourts has hit an all-time high average of 199.18p a litre, surpassing the previous peak of 199.09p set in June 2022 after Russia's invasion of Ukraine. The RAC estimates that filling an average family car with diesel now costs almost £110, which is £31 more than at the start of the Iran war - a rise of 40% since 28 February. The price of petrol also hit 174.13p on average, some 41p more than at the start of the war. Trump has said he is "very seriously" considering a US diesel export ban to try to curb soaring prices.

Source: The Guardian

3. Australia's RBA hikes rate to 15-year high

The Reserve Bank of Australia has lifted its key interest rate to 4.6%, its highest level since 2011, in a decision that was unanimous and widely expected. The increase - the fourth rate hike this year - came from a prior level of 4.35% and will add to repayment costs for millions of mortgage holders. The RBA highlighted a broadening Middle East conflict and strong AI-related demand as factors pushing up prices globally, and warned it was ready to lift interest rates again if needed. Treasurer Jim Chalmers said the US war on Iran - not government spending - was to blame for inflation.

Why it matters: The board said it was possible the ongoing US war on Iran could push inflation higher and drag global economic activity further down, and markets are now pricing in a further hike by February and more than a 50% chance of another by mid-2027.

Source: The Guardian

4. Argentina threatens legal action over Falklands oil

Argentine President Javier Milei has set a two-week deadline for the Sea Lion oilfield project to be scrapped, threatening international arbitration against the UK if it is not. The Sea Lion oilfield, around 130 miles (209 km) from the Falkland Islands, contains an estimated 1.7 billion barrels of oil and is a joint venture between Britain's Rockhopper Exploration and Israel's Navitas Petroleum.

Why it matters: The move heightens tensions between the UK and Argentina over sovereignty of the archipelago, known in Argentina as Las Malvinas, more than 40 years after a British military task force ejected Argentine forces which had invaded in 1982.

Source: BBC

5. US bans Canadian alcohol and dairy imports

A US ban on several Canadian imports, including alcohol, dairy and motorcycles, has come into effect as the trade war between the two countries drags on. The ban applies to nearly C$1bn ($710m; £530m) worth of Canadian liquor exported to the US, as well as whey products used in protein powder.

Why it matters: Economists and businesses have warned the bans will add uncertainty to the future of Canada's trade relationship with the US, its largest trade partner, on top of existing 50% tariffs on Canadian goods including dairy, alcohol, steel and aluminium.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Oban stroke patient, 80, faces 100-mile care move

John Russell, 80, from Oban, has been stuck in Lorn & Islands Hospital for 16 months after suffering a stroke in April last year, and his family have been told he could be placed in a care home as far as Edinburgh - about 93 miles by train or 122 miles by road. His wife Teresa, 79, who uses a wheelchair and a walking frame, said she had been prepared to care for him at home with some help, but his discharge was delayed and his health has deteriorated.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Vaping Products Duty of PS2.20 per 10ml starts 1 October 2026

A new excise duty of PS2.20 per 10ml on all vaping liquids manufactured in, or imported into, the UK comes into force on 1 October 2026, whether products contain nicotine or not. Retailers and wholesalers may continue to sell eligible unstamped stock already held until 31 March 2027, but from 1 April 2027 all vaping products outside duty suspension in the UK must carry a valid vaping duty stamp. Businesses that do not comply with the new rules may face civil or criminal sanctions.

Source: HMRC
