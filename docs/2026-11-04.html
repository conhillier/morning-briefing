1. Russia kills 29 at Ukraine aid site

The death toll from a Russian attack on a humanitarian aid distribution site in Komyushuvakha, a village in Ukraine's southern Zaporizhzhia region, rose to 29 on Tuesday, with 48 still missing. Russian bunker-busting bombs filled with nearly 1.5 tons of explosives were used in Monday's attack, and three days of mourning were declared in Zaporizhzhia. The dead included four police officers and two rescue workers. Russia also launched 289 attack drones, 17 ballistic missiles and 59 cruise missiles across nine regions in an overnight barrage, triggering emergency power cuts.

Why it matters: Acting regional head Mykhailo Semikin called it "one of the worst tragedies in terms of the number of civilian victims in our region since the start of the full-scale war."

Source: NPR

2. Binance hack: fake interviews exposed in detail

Binance has revealed that hackers who stole a record $6.3bn (£4.7bn) used fake job interviews and a compromised internal AI tool to infiltrate its systems. One engineer was contacted through a legitimate job-hunting platform and invited to bogus interviews, then instructed to download malware disguised as an "assignment" during a second interview. Hackers then used that engineer's compromised computer to gain control over two other engineers' laptops. The company said it had set aside more than 100% of the funds needed to cover the loss, with 98% of token assets replenished as of Saturday.

Why it matters: The FBI has formally stated that North Korea's Democratic People's Republic of Korea is responsible for the incident, with the TraderTraitor unit - also known as the Lazarus Group - previously blamed for a then-record $1.5bn hack of crypto exchange Bybit in 2025.

Source: BBC

3. Supermarket freezer cyber-attack hits UK meat firms

Businesses in the UK's food supply chain have been impacted by the cyber-attack that hit supermarket fridges and freezers across Europe on Monday, with the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) saying it is aware of "a couple of instances where vulnerable technology had been exploited in the UK." The British Association of Independent Meat Suppliers (AIMS) said two of its members saw their fridges begin to defrost, while another saw cold units rise to 100C (212F) in temperature. Around 40 Delhaize supermarkets in Belgium and Luxembourg were affected, with smaller numbers hit in Sweden, Ireland, the Netherlands, Estonia and Finland. The NCSC's chief executive Dr Richard Horne said the incidents pointed to an urgent need to invest in renewing technology, warning that vulnerabilities could be found and exploited more quickly via "AI in the hands of our adversaries".

Why it matters: Horne warned that "that technology base we rely on as a society has not been invested on to the extent it needs to be," noting many firms had extended systems beyond their end of life and not kept them disconnected from the internet.

Source: BBC

5. Palantir commercial revenue tops US government for first time

Palantir reported $984 million in US commercial revenue in its third quarter, up 148% from the prior year, surpassing US government revenue of $953 million for the first time. Total revenue grew 96% year-on-year and 20% from the previous quarter to $2.31 billion, beating analyst expectations of $2.18 billion. However, total contract value from US enterprises came in at $1.46 billion, down from a record $2.13 billion in the previous quarter, suggesting some slowing in sales.

Why it matters: The decline in total contract value quarter-on-quarter is a potential warning sign even as enterprises move faster than ever to adopt Palantir's AI software, with the next quarter historically being the biggest for tech purchasing.

Source: TechCrunch
