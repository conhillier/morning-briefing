1. US and Iran pause strikes for second day

The United States paused its attacks on Iran for a second straight day on Sunday, and Tehran said it had done the same. A regional official involved in mediation efforts called the pause a "positive signal that helps their efforts to de-escalate." The official said both countries want to return to the interim ceasefire deal, with a compromise centring on having Iran run vessel transit through the Strait of Hormuz with fewer restrictions on ships. The 60-day period that began when the interim deal was signed in mid-June is now well into its second half, with major issues including Iran's nuclear program still set aside.

Why it matters: Global energy supplies and the economy remain in the balance as gasoline prices have risen again, and concerns continue about shipping on the Strait of Hormuz and the Bab al-Mandeb strait.

Source: NPR

2. Wildfires force 300,000 from homes in France and Spain

Devastating wildfires in Spain and France have forced more than 300,000 people to evacuate their homes. In France, around 50,000 people were evacuated overnight into Sunday, bringing the total to over 250,000, according to interior minister Laurent Nunez. More than 242,000 acres have been burned in France this year, which Nunez described as a historic record. In Spain, about 60,000 people have been evacuated near Madrid, and interior minister Fernando Grande-Marlaska declared a national state of emergency because of the wildfires.

Source: NPR

3. Israeli settlers burn mosques across occupied West Bank

Israeli settlers set fire to two mosques, cars and agricultural land in the occupied West Bank, Palestinian officials say. The attacks came two days after a clash near the village of Tal that left four Palestinians and two Israelis dead. The mayor of Qusra said settlers set fire to a mosque under construction and spray-painted graffiti including the word "revenge" on its walls. The Israel Defense Forces said it had arrested more than 130 individuals over the weekend in what it called a "counter-terrorism" operation.

Why it matters: Israel's prime minister said he would speed up the establishment and legalisation of farm outposts in the West Bank in response to the killings, and new Israeli outposts have already appeared in the area since the Friday clash.

Source: BBC

4. Two killed at Seattle food festival shooting

At least two people were killed and five others, including a 2-year-old child, were wounded in a shooting at a downtown Seattle food festival on Sunday. Police said multiple people were shot in the city's downtown area on Sunday evening and urged people to avoid Seattle Center, where the Bite of Seattle food festival was being held. Witnesses reported hearing several shots around 6 p.m. local time. The Seattle Times and local TV station KOMO reported the casualty figures, citing the Seattle Fire Department.

Source: CNBC

5. Shein posts $99m loss as tariffs bite

Fast-fashion giant Shein swung to a $99m (£74.1m) quarterly loss in the first three months of 2026, compared with a net income of $395m a year earlier, after US President Donald Trump removed an import duty exemption on small packages. The filing was part of Shein's preparations for a stock market debut in Hong Kong, after the China Securities Regulatory Commission gave approval for a Hong Kong share sale on 10 July.

Why it matters: The removal of the US de minimis exemption, which had allowed goods valued at $800 or less to enter the US without tariffs, has had what Shein called "an adverse impact" on its US sales and overall revenue growth.

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Cairngorms fire sees 'welcome progress' after 12 days

First Minister John Swinney confirmed "welcome progress" had been made tackling the Cairngorms wildfire, speaking after a meeting of the Scottish government's resilience committee on Sunday. The blaze has now burned for 12 days with more than 500 firefighters tackling it, and Police Scotland said a significant emergency services response - including three helicopters - was still ongoing. Residents of Nethy Bridge, who were evacuated on Friday night, were allowed to return home on Saturday, and the fire was no longer considered a major incident. The Abernethy Highland Games, scheduled for 8 August, have been cancelled because the local community centre is still being used by emergency services.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

20% business rates cut for pubs, clubs, live music venues

From April 2027 (2027/28 financial year), pubs, social clubs and live music venues across England will receive a 20% cut to their business rates bills, saving the typical pub an estimated £1,100 in the next financial year. The package is worth around £100 million a year and will benefit nearly 32,000 pubs, clubs and live music venues. The very largest live music venues will not be eligible for the new 20% discount, with details to be set out at the Budget.

Source: HM Treasury
