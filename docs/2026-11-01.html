1. German cost of living protests turn violent

Rioters looted shops in central Frankfurt late on Saturday as clashes erupted in German cities over the cost of living, with incidents also reported in Berlin, Cologne and Hamburg. Police used water cannon to disperse protesters targeting emergency workers with fireworks, stones and bottles, and in Hamburg 120 people were arrested. The demonstrations were organised by campaign group Gen Z regelt to protest against underfunding in schools, the high cost of living, public spending cuts and military service, with authorities reporting as many as 18,000 protesters in Frankfurt, Munich and Berlin. Roman Poseck, interior minister of Hesse state, said that "around 100 clearly identified left-wing extremists" had infiltrated the protest in Frankfurt.

Why it matters: The unrest follows similar youth-led mass protests in France, where more than 320,000 people also demonstrated on Saturday, pointing to a broader pattern of European youth unrest over cost-of-living pressures.

Source: BBC

2. G20 fractures as Rubio struggles for unity

There was no joint communique or family photo at the G20 foreign ministers' meeting in Atlanta, chaired by Secretary of State Marco Rubio, after several foreign ministers refused to be photographed with Russia's Sergey Lavrov. Rubio said the US is open to diplomacy with Iran but would not rule out a new round of attacks, even as the US military works to keep the Strait of Hormuz open, stating that US Central Command has had to "defend these ships from drones and missiles almost every single night." Germany's foreign minister Johann Wadephul sought to highlight common ground, saying all parties share the goal of getting Russia to the negotiating table, while Poland's foreign minister Radek Sikorski used the meeting to push for a ceasefire in the Black Sea.

Why it matters: The failure to produce a joint communique or family photo underscores deep divisions among G20 nations over Russia's war in Ukraine and the ongoing Middle East conflict.

Source: NPR

3. AI neocloud firms face financial wobble

A once-obscure corner of the AI ecosystem known as neocloud firms - data centre companies that lease computing power to AI developers - is facing growing investor concern over their financial stability. CoreWeave, the industry leader, has accumulated $35.1bn (£26.3bn) in debt, and its stock has lost a quarter of its value over the past month, with shares in Nebius, IREN and Applied Digital experiencing similar falls.

Source: BBC

4. Companies report tangible AI earnings gains

A number of major companies are beginning to put measurable numbers on the financial benefits of AI investments in their third-quarter earnings. Corporate AI investment reached $581.7 billion in 2025, up 130% from the previous year, according to the Stanford HAI 2026 AI Index report.

Why it matters: With hyperscalers like Microsoft, Amazon, Google and Meta expected to spend $700 billion combined this year on AI infrastructure, investors are now demanding measurable gains rather than promises of future returns.

Source: CNBC

5. Iran signals openness to US diplomacy

Iran's security chief has described a letter from US Vice-President JD Vance as signalling a "positive" shift, in what is reported to be an effort to revive talks and avert further strikes. Tehran says it is awaiting Washington's response while insisting the Strait of Hormuz will remain closed until its conditions are met.

Source: Al Jazeera

Closer to Home

Scottish councils demand rethink on local government overhaul

Council leaders have called on the Scottish government to "rethink" its proposals to replace Scotland's 32 councils with six to ten large regional authorities and between 120 and 160 smaller community bodies. The Scottish government's own medium-term financial strategy projects a £4.8bn shortfall between spending plans and expected income by 2029-30, adding further pressure to the negotiations.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Vaping Products Duty of PS2.20 per 10ml takes effect

Vaping Products Duty came into force on 1 October 2026, charged at PS2.20 per 10ml of vaping liquid, applying to all vaping liquids manufactured in or imported into the UK from that date. A Vaping Duty Stamps Scheme has also launched; from 1 April 2027 all vaping products sold in the UK must carry a valid vaping duty stamp, with a six-month grace period allowing wholesalers and retailers to sell existing unstamped stock until 31 March 2027. Tobacco duty rates also increased on 1 October 2026 by a one-off pro-rata amount of PS2.20 per 100 cigarettes or per 50 grams of tobacco, in addition to the standard tobacco duty escalator.

Source: HMRC
