1. Saudi Arabia shuts Hormuz-bypass pipeline after Iraq drone attack

Saudi Arabia has closed its 1,200km East-West pipeline after it was struck by drones launched from Iraq, with Riyadh saying it shut the line as a precaution after satellite images showed scorched ground and smoke near the site. Iraq fired a military commander and launched an investigation after admitting the attack originated in the Maysan governorate, a province bordering Iran. The pipeline has been moving 4% to 5% of global oil supply, according to Reuters, citing ship tracking companies and analysts. Saudi Arabia said it would support Iraqi government efforts to prevent further attacks but reserved the right to take "all necessary measures to safeguard its sovereignty and security".

Why it matters: The closure squeezes both sides of the Arabian Peninsula and has pushed crude oil prices to over $100 a barrel for the first time since July, the source says.

Source: BBC

2. Israel destroys major Hezbollah tunnel complex in Lebanon

Israel's military used more than 1,000 tonnes of explosives to destroy two tunnels spanning more than 2km beneath the Ali Taher Ridge in southern Lebanon, triggering seismic activity equivalent to a magnitude 4.1 earthquake according to the US Geological Survey. The IDF said the underground infrastructure included a command centre used by Hezbollah's Badr unit containing dozens of rockets, missiles and unmanned aerial vehicles, as well as anti-tank weapons, mines and explosives. "This was strategic infrastructure that was constructed over two decades, funded and planned by the Iranian terror regime," the IDF said. The destruction completed a mission to destroy eight tunnels and establish a "security zone" in the area.

Why it matters: At least 4,383 people - including 395 women and 259 children - have been killed in Lebanon by Israeli attacks since 2 March, according to the Lebanese health ministry, with at least 29 killed this week.

Source: BBC

3. US inflation holds at 3.4% as fuel costs hit records

US consumer prices rose 3.4% in the year to August, unchanged from July, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics, with gasoline prices up 3.9% in August alone and accounting for more than a third of overall monthly inflation. Real average hourly earnings fell 0.3% over the past year, meaning wages are failing to keep up with rising costs. According to CME Group data, 85% of traders are now betting on a quarter-point rate hike at the Federal Reserve's meeting next week.

Why it matters: Interest rates have been held for five meetings in a row at between 3.5% and 3.75%, but the persistently hot inflation reading - well above the Fed's 2% target - is making a rate hike next week widely expected.

Source: BBC

4. Xi arrives in India for BRICS summit amid war tensions

The two-day summit on 12 and 13 September brings together leaders including Russia's Vladimir Putin and Iran's President Masoud Pezeshkian, with the Middle East war, the Ukraine conflict, global trade turbulence and energy security expected to dominate discussions. Russia will also hold discussions on conducting trade settlements in digital currencies at the summit, the Kremlin said.

Why it matters: The bloc now represents 11 member countries accounting for roughly 49% of the world's population and about 40% of global GDP, and the summit's discussions on de-dollarised payments reflect growing tensions with Western-led financial institutions.

Source: Al Jazeera

5. Anthropic blocks AI bioweapons use, names state hackers

Anthropic has published a threat intelligence report revealing it blocked scientists who used its Claude AI in ways that could support biological weapons development, highlighting five case studies of such misuse between December 2025 and August 2026. The report also named hacking group ShinyHunters and China-based labs as among those misusing the technology, and said a group consistent with Russia-based Midnight Blizzard allegedly used AI to build a system that automatically rewrote malware code to evade security defences. Claude was also used by actors linked to a Russia-based cyber espionage campaign and by an Iranian propaganda institution, the report said. The report additionally noted six cases where Claude was used to develop software for conventional weapons including firearms, missiles and armed drones.

Why it matters: Anthropic said biological misuse is "one of the most serious risks of frontier AI model" and that without correct safeguards such capabilities "could have catastrophic consequences".

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Military jet blamed for UK air traffic meltdown

The air traffic control outage that grounded thousands of UK flights on Tuesday was caused by a UK military aircraft submitting "spurious" flight data into the Nats system, causing a four-hour shutdown that cancelled more than 2,000 flights and affected hundreds of thousands of passengers, according to a Financial Times report citing four people briefed on the incident. Nats operations officer Kathryn Leahy told airport and airline managers the flight processing system had no dedicated backup because Nats was in the midst of a "complete overhaul of its systems" and duplicating data would cost "vast amounts". Transport secretary Heidi Alexander, who said she did "not believe the issue was unavoidable", summoned Nats chief executive Martin Rolfe and gave him a week to report on causes, while commissioning an independent Civil Aviation Authority review. Ryanair chief executive Michael O'Leary said Rolfe's position was "untenable" and accused him of "telling lies to save his own skin".

Source: The Guardian
