1. Houthis seize Red Sea port of Mokha

Yemen's Houthi rebels took over the port city of Mokha on Thursday, confirmed by Ahmed Baash, a commander with the National Resistance Forces, and Hazam al-Assad, a member of the Houthis' political bureau. Mokha is about 80 kilometres (50 miles) from the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, a waterway through which about 12% of the world's goods are typically shipped. Residents told AP that Houthi forces could be seen throughout the city, markets were shuttered, and three doctors said they fled a Mokha hospital after it was seized. The U.N.

Why it matters: Analysts said the takeover gives Iran another potential point of leverage in its war with the U.S., and Baraa Shiban of the Royal United Services Institute warned the Houthis could make shipping in the Red Sea more hazardous by laying mines in the water.

Source: NPR

2. Oil at $105 as ECB hikes rates to 2.5%

The European Central Bank raised its main interest rate from 2.25% to 2.5% - the highest level since March last year - and warned that inflationary pressures would be "longer lasting than we had anticipated", according to ECB president Christine Lagarde. Brent crude passed $105 a barrel before slipping back to about $104.5, a 3.3% rise on the day, while British gas prices rose above 203p per therm, the highest since December 2022. The interest rate on benchmark 10-year UK gilts hit 5.36%, the highest since August 2007, while Germany's 30-year government bond yield rose to 5.08%, the highest since December 2003. EU gas stores are only 67% full, well below the five-year average of 84%, raising fears of a scramble for supplies before winter.

Why it matters: Lagarde explicitly acknowledged that gas prices could rise further owing to supply disruptions or an unusually cold winter combined with low storage levels, and said headline inflation is not expected to return to around target until the end of 2027.

Source: The Guardian

3. Mokha seizure pushes oil and gas higher

The price of oil jumped to $105 a barrel amid signs the Middle East conflict will not be resolved quickly, with Brent crude going back above $100 on Wednesday before continuing to climb. In the UK, natural gas rose above 200p a therm for the first time since the end of 2022, while storage levels in Europe are much lower than normal for the time of year. UK 10-year bond yields hit their highest since 2007, while 20- and 30-year bond yields reached levels not seen since 1998. Chris Beauchamp, chief market analyst at IG, said "it feels like investors worldwide are now waking up to the crisis in oil markets" and warned the surge in energy prices could weigh heavily on the global economy if it continues.

Why it matters: The UK's Ofgem price cap is already due to increase by 3.6% at the start of October, and if energy prices remain high for an extended period, households still face steeper bills, with higher bond yields also feeding through to fixed-rate mortgage costs.

Source: BBC

4. Canada pledges air defence and drones to Ukraine

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney hosted Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Calgary on Thursday, where the two leaders signed an agreement to work toward a "hundred-year partnership" covering defence, critical minerals and energy. Canada announced C$350m (US$253m) in support through the US Jumpstart programme, and offered C$450m in loan guarantees to enhance Ukraine's energy supply for the coming winter. Carney also announced a deal that will see 30% of new Canadian drone production head directly to Ukraine. Zelenskyy said he planned to meet Donald Trump in New York City in late September, probably on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly, with Washington bidding to revive long-stalled talks to end the war.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

UK economy grows 0.4% in July

The UK economy expanded by 0.4% in July, the Office for National Statistics said, far exceeding economists' predictions of no growth. It follows growth of 0.3% in June and zero growth in May. The ONS said expansion in the services, production and construction sectors all contributed to July's figure.
