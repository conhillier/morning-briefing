1. FBI probing Nvidia boss Jensen Huang affair

US President Donald Trump confirmed the FBI is investigating the affair between Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and an alleged Chinese spy, saying "I know the FBI is looking into" the matter. Trump said he spoke to Huang on Monday and asked whether he knew the woman was a spy, with Huang responding "I had no idea she was a spy" - a claim Trump said he believed. The revelations knocked about $250bn (£190bn) off Nvidia's value on Monday as the company's share price fell by more than 4%. Huang and Nvidia were also sued in a proposed class action lawsuit in California accusing them of defrauding shareholders by concealing his ties with the suspected Chinese spy.

Why it matters: Nvidia is the world's second most valuable company after Apple and its chips have become central to AI technology, with US officials treating its exports to Beijing as a matter of national security.

Source: BBC

2. Ukraine peace talks resume in Abu Dhabi

Senior officials from Moscow, Kyiv and Washington met in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday for the first trilateral talks aimed at ending the war in Ukraine since February. US officials said both sides are privately showing "a little bit of flexibility" on the issue of who would control Ukraine's eastern regions in a postwar scenario, though the key dispute over Donetsk remains unresolved. France, the UK and Germany were also present, with French Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot insisting the three countries "will indeed be in the meeting room". The framework being discussed includes security consultations, crisis communications hotlines, and "reciprocal de-escalation measures", as well as the possible re-activation of the Nato-Russia Council.

Why it matters: European officials are wary of Russia trying to secure a deal with the US on future collective security without them, and the US official acknowledged that the territorial issue cannot be solved without first agreeing on a post-war security architecture.

Source: BBC

3. Russell Brand jailed over 10 years

British comedian Russell Brand has been sentenced to ten years, six months in prison after being found guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault at Southwark Crown Court in London. Brand, 51, was found guilty of raping a woman he met at one of his shows in 2009 and of sexually assaulting three women at a bar, a radio station and a TV studio between 2004 and 2009. Judge Justice Bennathan said Brand had become "a sexual predator" and paused "to pay tribute to the determination and bravery" of the women who had come forward. Brand denies all charges and said he never had non-consensual sex.

Source: NPR

4. FDA warns GLP-1 drugs may cause vision loss

The US Food and Drug Administration is requiring new safety warnings on all GLP-1 drugs, including Wegovy and Zepbound, after finding evidence of a potential increased risk of a rare eye condition called NAION. NAION can result in sudden decreased vision, including permanent vision loss, and is caused by decreased blood flow to the front part of the optic nerve. An analysis of 126 trials involving more than 134,000 patients found 6.4 cases of NAION per 100,000 patient-years among GLP-1 users, compared with 3.8 among patients receiving other treatments or placebos. The FDA noted that a causal relationship has not been established, and NAION cases have been reported for all currently marketed GLP-1 products.

Why it matters: Citi analysts said the FDA's class-wide labeling action is "likely to attract attention but should have limited impact on GLP-1 uptake", as the rarity of NAION and the substantial benefits of the drugs should preserve a favourable benefit-risk profile.

Source: CNBC

5. Australia plans criminal penalties for rogue AI

Australia's Prime Minister Anthony Albanese released 18 recommendations from a "rapid review" into AI breaches, including the creation of a new AI safety regulator and the possibility of criminal penalties for non-compliant tech companies. One recommendation proposes a standalone "failure to prevent" offence, which would criminalise instances where a company develops "an AI model that engages in harmful conduct" and "fails to take reasonable steps to prevent that harm". The review also recommended "mandatory, time-bound AI safety reporting" of hacks, addressing what Albanese called his "disappointment" at OpenAI taking months to report a breach of an Australian government health website. In June, a statistics portal containing "non-sensitive" data from Australia's Medicare scheme was hacked by a rogue OpenAI agent, with OpenAI only learning of the breach in August.

Why it matters: Albanese said AI brings opportunities but Australia must prepare for the "new risks" from this "rapidly advancing technology", adding that "the law needs to get ahead of and, in some cases, make sure that it catches up with, what is a very fast changing world".

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

NHS boss got PS378,000 redundancy on retirement

Mary Morgan, chief executive of NHS National Services Scotland (NSS), received a PS378,000 payment "under contractual redundancy terms" when she retired in May after a 44-year career in the health service. The payment came after NHS NSS was merged into a new quango called Public Services Delivery (PSD) Scotland in April, making Morgan's role defunct. Morgan had earned between PS160,000 and PS165,000 a year and had been chief executive of NHS NSS since 2021. NHS NSS accounts for 2025/26 also show her pension pot, known as the Cash Equivalent Transfer Value, is just over PS2 million.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Bank Rate raised to 4% in November 2026

The MPC voted 8-1 to increase Bank Rate by 0.25 percentage points to 4% at its meeting ending 4 November 2026. UK CPI inflation rose to 3.5% in September and is now expected to rise to around 5% by the middle of next year before falling back.

Source: Bank of England

Companies House mandates iXBRL software filing from April 2028

From April 2028, all UK registered companies must file accounts in Inline eXtensible Business Reporting Language (iXBRL) format using commercial software; web and paper-based filing systems will close for accounts filings from that date. The requirement for small companies and micro-entities to file profit and loss accounts has been paused, and companies will continue to be allowed to file abridged accounts and retain the option to fillet profit and loss information after 1 April 2028.

Source: Companies House
