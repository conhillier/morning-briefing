2. US pledges $1bn to Colombia's new right-wing president

The US has pledged $1bn (£740m) to Colombia hours after new right-wing populist president Abelardo de la Espriella was sworn in. The US state department said the "security package" would help achieve "shared goals" including fighting organised crime and illegal immigration. In his first official speech at a military base, de la Espriella pledged to crush armed insurgents and said "there will not be a single place in our territory where criminals can feel safe." In one of his first acts as president, de la Espriella said Colombia would join the "Shield of the Americas" security partnership founded by Trump.

Source: BBC

3. Hormuz talks drag as Iran issues six conditions

Traffic in the Strait of Hormuz remains severely restricted even as Iran and Oman report closing in on a deal to manage shipping in the critical waterway. Iran's National Security Council laid out six conditions for Hormuz to reopen, including the US ending its naval blockade, withdrawing air and naval forces, compensating Iran for war damage, and lifting sanctions and unfreezing Iranian assets. Oman described negotiations as "positive and constructive" while condemning continuing attacks on vessels in the strait.

Why it matters: A senior NATO Defense College fellow told Al Jazeera the talks are "convoluted" because of the indirect process, describing the pattern as the US and Iran "talking past each other," with positive signals from intermediaries repeatedly contradicted by subsequent statements from Tehran.

Source: Al Jazeera

4. OpenAI pauses Astra over cyberattack capability fears

OpenAI has suspended work on some aspects of its upcoming model Astra after an internal review found it had reached a "critical cybersecurity threshold," meaning it could independently identify and carry out cyberattacks against traditionally well-protected real-world systems.

Why it matters: The disclosure comes as OpenAI and AI labs such as Anthropic have disclosed other incidents in which AI models breached their sandboxes and posed threats during cybersecurity tests, raising concerns about humans' ability to control advanced AI models.

Source: TechCrunch

Nicola Sturgeon told the Edinburgh Festival Fringe she has not spoken to Peter Murrell since he was jailed for embezzling more than £400,000 from the SNP, and said she would not visit him in prison. For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Business rates cut 20% for pubs, clubs, live music venues

From April 2027, pubs, social clubs and live music venues across England will receive a 20% cut to their business rates bills, saving the typical pub an estimated £1,100 in the next financial year. The package is worth around £100 million a year and will benefit nearly 32,000 pubs, clubs and live music venues. The Chancellor confirmed that wider business rates reform, including Small Business Rates Relief, will be addressed at the Budget.

Source: HM Treasury
