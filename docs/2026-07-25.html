1. Russia kills 10 at Kyiv drone exhibition

At least 10 people were killed and some 100 injured in Russian ballistic missile strikes on an exhibition near Kyiv showcasing drone technology, attended by prominent members of the defence sector. Ukrainian air force spokesman Yuri Ihnat said the attack involved three ballistic missiles, only one of which was intercepted. Five more people were killed by Russian glide bombs in the eastern city of Sloviansk, and two men died in Sumy early on Saturday. President Zelensky said the incident was being investigated, and Ukraine's prosecutor-general said efforts were under way to establish who decided to hold the event and what safety measures were in place.

Why it matters: There has been widespread outrage that a public event of this kind was being held at all and had, apparently, been widely advertised. Zelensky also warned on Friday evening that Russia was preparing another massive missile strike that could happen within the next 48 hours.

Source: BBC

2. 220,000 flee wildfires in France and Spain

French authorities have ordered the evacuation of almost 60,000 people near Bordeaux, taking the total number of people told to flee wildfires in France and Spain to more than 220,000. In Spain, a fire near Madrid is described as "beyond the capacity" of firefighters to contain after three fires west of the capital merged into a single blaze, with Spain's government declaring a national emergency. Madrid's regional president Isabel Diaz Ayuso described it as the "worst fire in the history of the region", with at least 6,000 hectares burned in the Madrid region and almost 9,000 hectares consumed in the Avila blaze. In France, the Gironde blaze has burned through more than 19,000 hectares of forest and destroyed about 80 homes, with fire and rescue chief Marc Vermeulen saying conditions are more severe than during the devastating fires that hit the region in 2022.

Why it matters: Spain's Interior Minister Fernando Grande-Marlaska said authorities were doing everything possible to prevent the Madrid and Avila fires from merging, while Francisco Martin warned that numbers of evacuees could rise as "embers are flying for miles and the fire is jumping great distances".

Source: BBC

3. US fires on tanker as Saudi-Houthi exchange escalates

The US military said on Friday it had fired on and disabled a tanker ship in the Gulf of Oman that was attempting to evade the American blockade on Iranian ports, with Navy Captain Tim Hawkins saying the crew "attempted to run the blockade at least four times prior" before US forces fired into its engine room. Saudi Arabia launched airstrikes on Houthi targets in the Yemeni port city of Hodeida and a nearby island, after the Houthis claimed responsibility for a missile attack causing fires in the Saudi city of Jizan early on Saturday. The Houthis warned that Saudi targeting of "civilian facilities in Hodeida and Kamaran island constitutes a dangerous escalation" and that "these recent crimes will not go unanswered." The US and UK are set to hold a conference in London next week on the Strait of Hormuz, with US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth and Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff Dan Caine attending.

Why it matters: Oil prices, which had breached $100 a barrel this week on growing concerns over Red Sea shipping threats, dropped back to a little over $96 on Friday after two Chinese-chartered supertankers successfully passed through the Bab el-Mandeb Strait.

Source: BBC

4. Trump threatens 'massive' Iran escalation

The US military conducted its 13th consecutive night of strikes against Iran, aimed at degrading Iran's ability to threaten maritime traffic, while Iran launched retaliatory drone strikes on US military facilities and bases in Bahrain and Jordan. The national average price for regular gasoline in the US climbed to $4.10 per gallon, up more than 17 cents from a month ago, according to AAA.

Why it matters: The conflict on Thursday pushed global benchmark oil prices to over $100 per barrel, their highest level since May, though the price fell below $92 a barrel on Friday.

Source: NPR

5. SpaceX Starship completes 13th test flight

SpaceX launched its massive Starship rocket on Friday evening from Starbase, Texas, in a 13th test flight and the first since the company's record IPO last month. The Starship spacecraft made a "soft splashdown" in the Indian Ocean, SpaceX said, "coming to rest intact in the Indian Ocean and providing critical views of an intact heatshield for the first time." About 18 minutes into the flight, SpaceX successfully deployed 20 of its new Starlink V3 satellites into orbit for the first time, with the satellites designed to be larger and more powerful than earlier versions, equipped with solar arrays generating twice as much power as prior generations. The Super Heavy booster made a "hard splashdown" after only a subset of its engines successfully ignited during the landing burn.

Source: CNBC

Closer to Home

Cairngorms fire declared major incident, Nethy Bridge evacuated

Police Scotland declared a major incident at the Cairngorms National Park wildfire - now burning for 10 days - after "a significant change in weather conditions" increased the risk of fire spreading towards the village of Nethy Bridge, with all residents evacuated as a precaution. More than 100 firefighters, 11 fire engines, wildlife units and a command support unit remain at the scene, with helicopter water-bombing operations ongoing.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Business rates cut 20% for pubs, clubs, live music venues

From April 2027, pubs, social clubs and live music venues across England will receive a 20% cut to their business rates bills, saving the typical pub an estimated £1,100 in the next financial year. The package, worth around £100 million a year, will benefit nearly 32,000 venues and will be fully funded partly through reviewing reliefs for businesses such as vape shops. The largest live music venues will not be eligible, with details to be set out at the Budget.

Source: HM Treasury
