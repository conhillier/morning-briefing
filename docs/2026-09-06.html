1. Witkoff and Kushner head to Kyiv after Putin talks

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are set to land in Kyiv on Sunday after holding three-hour closed-door talks with Russian President Vladimir Putin at his official residence in Moscow on Saturday. The White House said the envoys had "discussed substantive plans for next steps" with Putin, with an announcement expected in the coming weeks. Putin adviser Yuri Ushakov described the talks as "constructive, extremely frank" and said economic issues and "mutually beneficial Russian-American projects" were discussed in considerable detail. It will be the first trip by Trump's top negotiators to Ukraine's capital, and Putin and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky have agreed to halt strikes on each other's capitals for three days.

Why it matters: An announcement on next steps in efforts to end the war in Ukraine is expected in the coming weeks, according to the White House.

Source: BBC

2. US and Iran trade ship strikes in Gulf

The US and Iran traded retaliatory attacks on ships on Saturday, in the latest round of strikes since fighting flared up a week ago following a month of relative calm. The US military said it had "permanently disabled" two Iran-linked oil tankers, including one near Kharg Island, and "completely destroyed" a third in the Gulf of Oman; Iran's state media confirmed the strikes and said Tehran had attacked three US-affiliated vessels in response, as well as three oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz along an "unauthorised" route. US Central Command said it "successfully evaded" earlier IRGC attacks targeting a US aircraft carrier and guided-missile destroyer, with no American troops harmed. On Friday, the average price for one gallon of diesel in the US hit $5.85, compared to an average of $3.71 a year ago.

Why it matters: The squeeze on the Strait of Hormuz has raised energy prices globally, and ahead of the US midterm elections in November, Trump is facing pressure over how much the war is costing American consumers.

Source: BBC

3. UK budget to be shaped by Middle East war

Chancellor John Healey has indicated the UK will face a tough budget on 28 October, saying the conflict in Iran would probably have a strong influence on his first budget. Healey said he and Prime Minister Andy Burnham were "in lockstep in our determination to meet the fiscal rules", while economists have predicted he will need to raise taxes or implement significant cost-cutting measures to protect the £24bn fiscal headroom that his predecessor Rachel Reeves had after her March statement.

Why it matters: Healey said he wanted to ensure the country had a robust "buffer against uncertainty" amid increasing global instability, and committed to cutting the welfare bill to help stabilise the public finances.

Source: The Guardian

4. AfD eyes historic majority in Saxony-Anhalt vote

Voting is under way in the eastern German state of Saxony-Anhalt in an election that could see the Alternative for Germany (AfD) become the first far-right party to take power at a state level since World War II. Recent surveys place AfD at 40 percent, well ahead of the centre-right CDU at 23 percent, and the party has a chance at winning an absolute majority if several smaller parties fail to clear the 5 percent threshold to enter parliament. Thousands of demonstrators gathered for a pro-democracy festival the night before the vote, with rally organisers estimating between 15,000 and 20,000 people attending.

Why it matters: For the AfD, the Saxony-Anhalt elections are described as a stepping stone towards its larger ambition of securing national power in the 2029 federal elections.

Source: Al Jazeera

5. JLR to cut up to 4,000 jobs after profit collapse

Jaguar Land Rover (JLR) has informed workers and their union that it will open a voluntary redundancy programme for salaried and management team members, with as many as 4,000 jobs to be cut over two years, as the company needs to save about £1.7bn over that period. The carmaker, owned by Indian conglomerate Tata Motors, saw profits collapse to just £14m before tax - down from £2.5bn the year before - following a cyber-attack that caused a 27% drop in overall production and cost about £200m, combined with the impact of US tariffs and falling sales. Retail and wholesale sales volumes fell by about 70,000 and 90,000 respectively, partly due to Trump raising tariffs on vehicles imported into the US to 25% before agreeing a deal for 10% for the UK. Unite's general secretary Sharon Graham said she and business secretary Jonathan Reynolds would meet JLR chief executive PB Balaji next week after a weekend of intensive talks.

Why it matters: The job cuts will be unwelcome news for Prime Minister Andy Burnham, who has pledged to "safeguard sovereign manufacturing" and reindustrialise Britain; about 30,000 of JLR's 44,000 employees are based in the UK.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Paddington fire halts Heathrow rail services

A grass fire near Scrubs Lane, North Kensington caused a major electricity supply failure on Saturday afternoon, blocking all rail lines between Paddington and Heathrow airport and forcing the evacuation of 380 passengers stranded on an Elizabeth line train. London Fire Brigade was called just after 4pm with six fire engines and 40 crew tackling the blaze, while the Elizabeth line was suspended between Paddington and Heathrow Terminals 4 and 5, and between Paddington and Reading. Great Western Railway services to Reading were also cancelled, and disruptions were expected until the end of Saturday. Passengers travelling to the airport were advised to use the Piccadilly line, although that line also experienced severe delays between Heathrow and Acton Town.

Source: The Guardian

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Business rates cut 20% for pubs from April 2027

The government announced a 20% cut to business rates bills for pubs, social clubs and live music venues from April 2027. This follows a 15% cut on pubs and live music venues' business rates bills brought in from April 2026, which saved the average pub an additional £1,650 in 2026/27. A separate independent review of pub and hotel valuation methodology, led by Jerry Schurder, will report to the Treasury by the end of March 2027.

Source: HM Treasury
