1. IDF confirms firing on car that killed Hind Rajab

The Israeli Defense Forces (IDF) has confirmed for the first time that it opened fire on a car carrying five-year-old Palestinian girl Hind Rajab, who was killed along with six relatives in Gaza in 2024. The IDF said its examination indicated "IDF troops fired at a vehicle" travelling contrary to advance notice published by the IDF Spokesperson. The military has announced a criminal investigation by the Military Police Criminal Investigation Division into Hind's killing, the deaths of two paramedics whose ambulance was shelled, and the killing of 15 Palestinians - including medics and a UN worker - in March 2025. The story was made into an Oscar-nominated film called The Voice of Hind Rajab.

Why it matters: The Hind Rajab Foundation said it had "no confidence" in the investigation, stating that Israel's internal investigations were "fundamentally flawed and cannot be regarded as credible mechanisms of accountability".

Source: BBC

2. Israel opens E1 West Bank settlement tenders

Israel has opened tenders for the construction of 1,234 settlement homes in the E1 area east of Jerusalem in the occupied West Bank, with a deadline for bids set for 19 October - just days before Israel's general election on 27 October. The development would in effect divide the West Bank in two and isolate East Jerusalem, according to opponents. UK Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband condemned the tender as "an unacceptable and destructive act" and said the Israeli Chargé d'Affaires had been summoned to the Foreign Office, demanding "an immediate reversal". Miliband said the British government would "in the coming weeks" set out "a comprehensive set of measures" including targeted sanctions at those who participate in illegal settlement expansion.

Why it matters: Israeli anti-settlement watchdog Peace Now warned that even partial implementation would "fundamentally" alter "the geographic and strategic character of the area by severing the northern West Bank from the south and isolating East Jerusalem", ruining the possibility of a two-state solution.

Source: BBC

3. Trump threatens economic war on Iran allies

President Donald Trump announced on Truth Social that the US would inflict "TREMENDOUS Economic Consequences" on any country that helps or does business with Iran, describing it as "economic D-Day" and "the most crushing economic operation ever taken against any country". He warned that oil smuggling, swap lines, cash transfers, exchange houses, ship registries and front companies all needed to stop, saying "You know who you are". The move comes after a 60-day ceasefire with Iran expired on Monday with no diplomatic or military resolution to the conflict the US and Israel began at the end of February. While Trump did not name China, the BBC noted that Chinese businesses are the biggest buyers of Iranian oil, a key source of Tehran's funding.

Why it matters: The conflict has significantly disrupted traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, through which around 20% of the world's crude oil and liquified natural gas normally passes, pushing up global energy prices.

Source: BBC

4. US national debt tops $40 trillion milestone

US national debt has surpassed $40 trillion (£29.4 trillion) - more than double what it was a decade ago - according to Treasury figures, with the total standing at $40.05 trillion as of 18 August. The Congressional Budget Office had projected overall borrowing would reach only $39.6 trillion by the end of fiscal year 2026, meaning the milestone has been hit earlier than expected. The interest rate on 30-year bonds hit 5.34% on Tuesday - described as the highest level in almost 20 years - before easing to 5.18% after the Treasury announced it would increase bond buyback operations from $2 billion to $4 billion, effective from 9 September to 4 November. Federal Reserve minutes released Wednesday showed "several participants" were in favour of increased rates last month, with many saying rate hikes would "likely be necessary if inflation did not decline".

Why it matters: The CBO says the US is nearing its $41.1 trillion debt ceiling, with debt projected to climb to about $64 trillion by 2036, while the US debt-to-GDP ratio stands at 125.8% according to the IMF - one of the highest among the world's largest economies.

Source: BBC

5. Over 100 dead in CAR goldmine collapse

More than 100 people have died after an artisanal goldmine caved in due to a landslide at the village of Zamboye, about 50km from the town of Baboua in western Central African Republic, close to the border of Cameroon. The landslide struck on Tuesday afternoon, with footage on social media appearing to show an avalanche of sand descend on a group of miners and cover them. Red Cross volunteer Gervais Ganagoroh told the Associated Press that more than 100 bodies had been recovered, and a senior official at a local mining association confirmed the number to Reuters. Authorities in Cameroon's Eastern region confirmed at least three of its citizens had been identified as victims, and the death toll is expected to rise as rescue efforts continue.

Why it matters: At least 40 people have died in several mining incidents in the Central African Republic this year alone, highlighting the persistent dangers of artisanal mines that operate with weak safety standards.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Edinburgh hospital heart clinics closed by water leak

A water leak at the Royal Infirmary of Edinburgh has forced the temporary closure of two specialist vascular procedure centres that offer heart-related diagnostic and treatment procedures. The leak resulted in mould being found in some non-clinical areas, and NHS Lothian said the temporary reduction of clinical areas may result in delays or appointments being rescheduled in some specialist services. Medical director Dr Tracey Gillies said there was "no infection risk associated with this leak to staff or patients" and that remedial work was underway. The closure comes as BBC Scotland News has previously revealed that tens of millions of pounds of taxpayers' cash is expected to be needed to bring the hospital up to scratch when it transfers to public ownership next year, with new lighting, ventilation and electrical systems among the measures needed as the hospital's Private Finance Initiative contract ends in December 2027.

Source: BBC
