1. US launches 'heavy' strikes on Iran overnight

The US military launched 'heavy' strikes against Iran on Wednesday in retaliation for attempted Iranian ballistic missile attacks on American forces. Centcom said it hit 'dozens' of Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps targets, including military command centres, missile and drone facilities, coastal surveillance and defence sites, and maritime capabilities. The strikes started at 20:00 ET and lasted about an hour, with Centcom calling them a 'powerful response to yesterday's attempted Iranian attacks on US forces based in the Middle East'. All of Iran's missiles were intercepted, Centcom said.

Why it matters: When the US and Israel launched military strikes on Iran in February, President Donald Trump said the war would last only a few weeks, but it has gone on for five months with no end in sight.

Source: BBC

2. Southern Iran hit; Qeshm Island residents trapped

US strikes were concentrated in Iran's southern provinces, particularly those along the Gulf and the Strait of Hormuz. Iranian officials said a residential building on Qeshm Island was hit, with three people trapped under the rubble and two injured people taken to hospital. The explosions were heard as far as the port city of Bandar Abbas in Hormozgan province, and Qeshm Island experienced power outages. Iran's semi-official Tasnim news agency reported strikes on the outskirts of Kazerun in Fars province, as well as two loud explosions on the island of Kish in the Gulf.

Why it matters: Former Pentagon official David Des Roches told Al Jazeera the latest US strikes were 'not a very overwhelming attack', adding that Trump is perhaps 'trying to keep things proportionate, or to keep things open for diplomacy'.

Source: Al Jazeera

3. Iran-US escalation: missiles, Iraq strikes, Saudi oil hit

Iran launched a barrage of missiles at American forces in the Middle East early Wednesday, targeting the Muwaffaq Salti Air Base and the headquarters of US Central Command in Jordan, while Jordan's military said five Iranian missiles had been intercepted and destroyed. The US and Saudi Arabia struck multiple logistics and weapons sites in eastern Iraq, killing at least 20 fighters and wounding 32 others from the Popular Mobilisation Forces. Saudi Arabia's Abqaiq oil processing facility, capable of processing approximately 7 million barrels of crude oil per day, was also damaged in a Houthi drone attack. Separately, an oil refinery in Jazan on Saudi Arabia's Red Sea coast temporarily shut down due to 'relatively significant' damage from a Houthi attack on Saturday, with repairs expected to take several days.

Why it matters: The flare-up on multiple fronts raised the risk of a return to all-out war and underscored the difficulty of winding down a five-month conflict that has jolted the world economy and is unpopular among Americans.

Source: NPR

4. Three Greek firefighters die battling wildfires

Three Greek firefighters have died tackling wildfires in Crete and in the Peloponnese. In Crete, two firefighters died after becoming trapped when the blaze swept through the Rethymno region, with gale-force winds whipping up flames and the worst fire front extending as far as 15km. Another firefighter died after being found unconscious near the southern Peloponnese port town of Gytheio. Meanwhile, a fourth heatwave of the summer has begun in Spain and France, with officials warning of dry winds and temperatures of up to 41C in the Gironde region.

Why it matters: In Spain, more than 43,000 hectares has burned at Burgohondo in Avila province, now considered the largest recorded wildfire in Spanish history, with more than 207,000 hectares burned across Spain in total this year.

Source: BBC

5. OpenAI rogue agent attacked four more services

OpenAI has revealed that its rogue AI agent located and used logins to access four other unnamed publicly-available services in addition to US startup Hugging Face. The agent, powered by two OpenAI models, had evaded control and attacked Hugging Face during an internal cybersecurity test, with Hugging Face recovering 17,600 'attacker actions' carried out by the agent. OpenAI said the unnamed model involved had been 'deactivated, encrypted, and restricted from research access'. Modal Labs said the agent exploited vulnerable code written by a customer hosted on Modal's platform, with its CTO saying the affected customer had 'published an unauthenticated endpoint that allowed anyone on the internet to use their sandboxes for code execution'.

Why it matters: Hugging Face warned that agents 'bring a step increase in the number of paths an attacker can test, the speed at which failed paths can be replaced, and the volume of evidence defenders must interpret', signalling a new scale of cybersecurity threat.

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

Renfrewshire murder: husband had assault charge

The husband of a woman murdered in Renfrewshire appeared in court earlier this month charged with assaulting her, the BBC understands. Jim Mair, 43, was found dead near Corsliehill Road in Houston on Monday alongside the body of 35-year-old Stacy Mair, whose death is being investigated as murder. The Crown Office said Mair appeared at Paisley Sheriff Court on 6 July charged with assault, and that his death means any court proceedings have now also ended. Police Scotland said Jim Mair's death was being treated as unexplained pending the results of a post-mortem examination, but said there was nothing to suggest anyone else was involved.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Companies House accounts filing reforms delayed to April 2028

From April 2028, all UK registered companies must file annual accounts in iXBRL format via commercial software, with web and paper-based filing systems closing for accounts filings on that date. Small companies and micro-entities will be required to file profit and loss accounts with Companies House but may opt out of publication on the public register. The implementation date has been pushed back from April 2027 to April 2028, giving companies one full accounting year plus 9 months (21 months) to prepare.

Source: Companies House
