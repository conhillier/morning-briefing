1. US to shut down all Iranian airlines Wednesday

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC that all Iranian airlines will be shut down from September 23, saying the US was pressuring Iran "like never before". The shutdown will be enforced by restricting airlines from obtaining fuel, landing services, or ticket sales, with non-compliant entities threatened with being "knocked out of the dollar system". Last week, the Treasury also announced new sanctions on Russia's state-controlled VTB Bank as part of its effort to economically isolate Iran by targeting its financial "enablers".

Why it matters: The move is part of a broader US campaign to cut off Iran and its enablers globally, with Bessent saying Washington is putting a stop to Iran's enablers around the world.

Source: CNBC

2. G7 demands Iran stop arming Houthis

The foreign ministers of the UK, Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan and the United States issued a joint statement calling on Iran to "end its arming of and support" for the Houthis in Yemen, condemning Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia "in the strongest terms". The G7 said the recent escalation represented a threat to regional and global energy security, with more than 120,000 Yemenis displaced by the surge in fighting along Yemen's Red Sea coast. The statement was issued on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly in New York, where senior Iranian officials are due to speak. Meanwhile, at least six people were killed in Saudi strikes on the strategic Yemeni city of Mokha, including two children and a woman.

Why it matters: The G7 warned that Houthi actions "constitute a dangerous pattern of escalation that risks further exacerbating the conflict, undermining international trade and creating global economic instability".

Source: BBC

3. British Columbia sues OpenAI over school shooting

The Canadian province of British Columbia has filed a lawsuit in San Francisco federal court against OpenAI and CEO Sam Altman, alleging a mass shooting at a school in Tumbler Ridge could have been prevented if the company had warned police that the shooter had used ChatGPT to plan the attack. Nine people, primarily children, were killed in the February attack; the shooter, Jesse Van Rootselaar, had been flagged by OpenAI's safety team after conversations about gun violence, but the company did not alert police. The lawsuit says Altman published a letter saying he was "deeply sorry" OpenAI had not contacted law enforcement and promised reforms, but never followed through. The province is seeking damages to fund recovery efforts and an order directing changes to how the company handles ChatGPT conversations that could lead to violence.

Why it matters: The lawsuit alleges that OpenAI leadership, including Altman, overruled safety team members who recommended contacting police, and that after the shooter's account was deactivated she was able to get a new account and continue using the platform to plan her attack.

Source: The Guardian

4. Lithuania PM: we will fight back against Russia

Lithuanian Prime Minister Mindaugas Sinkevicus told the BBC's Newsnight that his country has made evacuation plans for a "variety of scenarios" but would "fight back" and "engage" if attacked by Russia. He said Lithuanians feel "constantly threatened" by Russia, in part because of drones entering the nation's airspace, and called for a "shift in the mindset" in the UK's approach to defence. The comments come just a week after NATO fighter pilots shot down a drone that entered Lithuanian airspace; Lithuanian Defence Minister Robertas Kaunas said the drone came from the direction of Belarus.

Why it matters: Lithuania, Estonia and Latvia all joined NATO in 2004 under Article 5, which states that an armed attack against one NATO member shall be considered an attack against all members.

Source: BBC

5. UK borrowing hits PS18bn, Budget pressure mounts

Government borrowing in August reached PS18.3bn, almost a fifth higher than the year before, as persistently higher inflation pushed up overall spending, according to the Office for National Statistics. Interest payments on government debt rose to PS8.8bn, the highest August level since records began in 1997. The figures pile pressure on Chancellor John Healey ahead of his first Budget on 28 October, with the Institute for Fiscal Studies warning that debt interest spending is "a worryingly large share of overall government spending".

Why it matters: Capital Economics deputy chief UK economist Ruth Gregory called it a "dismal backdrop for the autumn Budget" and warned it raises the likelihood of Prime Minister Andy Burnham's policy ambitions being "reined in or delayed to avoid big tax hikes and/or a backlash in the markets".

Source: BBC

Closer to Home

Glasgow homelessness crisis worsens ahead of new data

New figures on Glasgow's homelessness crisis are due to be published today amid fears the situation continues to worsen. Last September saw Glasgow record its highest ever total of households in temporary homeless accommodation at 4,332, including a record 3,225 children, while the number of open cases waiting to be housed hit 6,734. Social care worker Samantha Lyle said services were "absolutely inundated" with demand, and new council leader Ricky Bell said the city needed to "start acting as though this is an emergency". Between April and September 2025, almost half of Glasgow's homeless applications came from those with refugee or leave to remain status.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Bank Rate held at 3.75%, QT pace set at 46bn annually

The MPC voted 6-3 to maintain Bank Rate at 3.75% at its meeting ending 16 September 2026, with three members preferring an increase to 4%. The MPC also voted unanimously to reduce the stock of UK government bond purchases to zero via annual sales of 20 billion pounds alongside maturing gilts, at an average pace of 46 billion pounds per year by end of 2034.

Source: Bank of England
