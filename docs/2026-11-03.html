1. US strikes Iran ahead of midterm elections

The US has launched strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, US officials told CBS, the BBC's US partner. Iranian state news agency IRNA reported several explosions on the island on Monday, with US officials describing the strikes as limited and saying they were in response to Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps firing upon a US aircraft. Earlier on Monday, Trump told reporters he was in "no rush" to ramp up strikes, saying "We crippled them financially.

Why it matters: Iran's foreign minister Abbas Araghchi told IRNA that "very intensive" talks to try and end the war are under way with the US via mediators, and that Tehran was prepared for both diplomatic and military scenarios "more than ever before".

Source: BBC

2. Russia kills 29 at Ukraine aid distribution centre

At least 29 people were killed after Russian bombs hit an aid distribution centre in the village of Komyshuvakha in the southern Zaporizhzhia region, with another 10 injured and 48 reported missing. Regional head Mykhailo Semikin said at least nine 3,000kg aerial bombs hit the site, and that rescue teams had been forced to halt operations multiple times because of Russian drone attacks in the area. President Zelensky said after the first strike on the local school "used for humanitarian needs", there was a second attack on the place where people were sheltering, calling it "utterly horrific". A day of mourning has been declared for Tuesday across the entire Zaporizhzhia region.

Why it matters: Zelensky said the attack showed "Russia is sending the exact opposite signal" to peace, adding "Russia is counting on further killings", even after a recent joint Russian-Ukrainian civilian evacuation operation brokered by US First Lady Melania Trump.

Source: BBC

3. UK and EU agree draft Brexit food deal

The UK and EU have agreed a draft deal on removing post-Brexit checks from food and agriculture exports, ahead of the UK-EU summit later this month. The BBC understands the 200-page draft text for the so-called SPS agreement has been circulated to EU nations in Brussels for consideration at a meeting on Wednesday. The deal should remove the need for £200 Export Health Certificates, £25 Phytosanitary certificates, inspector fees, organic certificates, some port fees and other checks, and will also allow UK exports of fresh sausages, burgers, shellfish and seed potatoes to the EU to resume. The government claims the UK economy will eventually see £5.1bn worth of annual benefits as a result of the agreement.

Why it matters: A food deal will have particular significance for Northern Ireland, radically reducing the impact of the post-Brexit Irish Sea border, as food products from elsewhere in the UK currently require paperwork and some physical checks when arriving at Northern Ireland's ports.

Source: BBC

4. North Korea denies record Binance hack

North Korea has denied claims that it was behind the record $6.3bn (£4.7bn) hack of cryptocurrency exchange Binance, calling the allegations "absurd rhetoric". Kim Yo Jong, a top official and sister of leader Kim Jong Un, said the accusations are another "sinister attempt" by the US to damage Pyongyang's public image. The US FBI, the Secret Service and cyber analysts have said North Korea orchestrated the hack, which has been described as the largest in history, and attributed it to state-backed hacking group TraderTraitor, also known as the Lazarus Group. Binance said on Tuesday that a net $5.27bn has been withdrawn from the exchange since transactions resumed on Sunday, but that transaction levels have now stabilised.

Why it matters: Binance has said it has so far covered around 95% of the assets stolen in the breach and has repeatedly committed to covering all losses linked to the attack.

Source: BBC

5. Palantir US commercial revenue tops government for first time

Palantir beat Wall Street's third-quarter estimates, with U.S. commercial revenue jumping 148% to $984 million, surpassing US government revenue of $953 million for the first time. Overall revenue nearly doubled from a year ago and surpassed $2 billion for the first time ever, while net income more than doubled to $1.13 billion. Palantir lifted its guidance, now expecting US commercial revenue "in excess of" $3.56 billion for the full year.

Why it matters: Palantir's U.S. commercial revenue has more than quintupled over the last two years, driven by heightened demand for artificial intelligence tools from businesses.

Source: CNBC

Closer to Home

Shetland defence calls grow amid Russian activity

Calls are being made for the defence of the Shetland Isles to be strengthened after increased sightings of Russian trawlers off the coast of Scotland. Recent analysis by the Daily Telegraph revealed more than 500 incidents where Russian boats have been stationed above undersea cables linking Britain with Europe and the US. In July, Royal Navy warships and helicopters spent 21 days monitoring Russian activity in UK waters and the North Atlantic, a 25% increase on previous time spent monitoring. Liberal Democrat MP Alistair Carmichael said there had been just one local full-time soldier running a reserved unit in Shetland over the last 20 years, and called for restoration of a full-time presence and recruitment of more reserves.

Source: BBC

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Companies House mandatory iXBRL software filing from April 2028

From April 2028, all UK registered companies must file accounts in Inline eXtensible Business Reporting Language (iXBRL) format using commercial software; web and paper-based filing systems will close for accounts filings from that date. Separately, the government is pausing the requirement for small companies and micro-entities to file profit and loss accounts with Companies House from April 2028, continuing to allow abridged accounts and the option to fillet profit and loss information. Three additional changes take effect from April 2028: a strengthened eligibility statement for companies claiming an audit exemption, a requirement to file component parts of accounts and reports together, and a limit on how many times a company can shorten its accounting reference period.

Source: Companies House
