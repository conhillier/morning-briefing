2. Fed chair warns of possible US rate hike

Federal Reserve chair Kevin Warsh said at the annual Jackson Hole Economic Policy Symposium that policymakers will "have work to do" if they are not confident cost-of-living pressures are easing for Americans. Latest figures show prices rose 3.4% in the year to July, above the Fed's 2% target, while another inflation measure closely watched by the Fed is running at 3.7%. Warsh said given prices were rising by more than 2% on an annual basis, "the Fed's predominant focus right now should be on prices". The central bank's next interest rate decision will be made on 15-16 September.

Why it matters: Following Warsh's remarks, the rates market showed growing expectations of an interest rate rise in September, according to CME data, and analysts at Capital Economics said the speech left "the door open to a hike" earlier than previously expected.

Source: BBC

3. Warsh sharpens hawkish Jackson Hole message

Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh used his Jackson Hole speech to deliver a clearer warning that stubborn inflation could push the Fed toward a rate hike, answering critics of his earlier July news conference. Warsh recommitted to maintaining the 2% PCE goal, calling it a "firm, fixed target", and noted that 54% of components of PCE had been above 3% annualised inflation in the past 12 months, while 49% were above 3% in the past six months. He also confirmed that "short-term interest rates are the predominant tool to achieve the dual mandate", directly addressing concerns he would not act quickly on inflation. PCE inflation was 3.7% for July, according to government data released this week.

Why it matters: The speech puts Warsh more clearly at odds with Trump's demand for lower rates and raises the stakes for the September Fed meeting.

Source: CNBC

The dispute arose after Anthropic refused to allow the Pentagon to use its Claude AI model for fully autonomous lethal weapons or domestic mass surveillance, leading Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth to accuse the company of "arrogance and betrayal". 5. Lambda raises $1bn in debt for Nvidia chips

Lambda, an AI cloud company that buys computing chips and rents them out to businesses, has raised $1 billion in private, short-dated debt arranged by JP Morgan Chase to buy Nvidia's AI chips that it will lease to Microsoft.
