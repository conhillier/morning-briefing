1. US and Denmark strike Greenland security deal

The US and Denmark have announced a deal on Greenland's security, resolving a diplomatic dispute sparked by President Donald Trump's threats to seize the territory by force. An unnamed US State Department official said the deal includes "permanent access, basing and overflights rights" and gives the US unilateral power to build additional military structures with no approval needed from Greenland or Denmark. The deal also bans non-NATO countries from building bases in Greenland and blocks Russia and China from deploying troops or making investments in sensitive sectors.

Why it matters: US Secretary of State Marco Rubio called it a "historic deal" and said it "permanently and completely addresses our national security concerns in Greenland", though no text of the agreement has been released.

Source: BBC

2. Riyadh hit by air raid alerts as Houthi conflict escalates

Residents in Saudi Arabia's Riyadh have been given the all-clear after air raid alerts were issued and explosions were reported in the capital on Friday night and Saturday morning. It was the first time alerts have been issued in Riyadh since the Houthis increased their attacks and declared a "maritime embargo" on the Saudis in July. The Houthis recently seized control of the strategic port city of Mokha and Perim Island, a gateway to a key shipping route used by the Gulf state to export to Asia and Europe. Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman has personally asked US President Donald Trump to take military action against the Houthis, but Trump has so far declined, offering instead to assist with intelligence and targeting support.

Why it matters: The attacks on Saudi oil assets have directly impacted global energy markets, and the price of petrol and diesel in many countries has sharply risen in recent weeks, compounding already elevated costs caused by the blockades of the Strait of Hormuz.

Source: BBC

3. Warren Buffett steps down as Berkshire Hathaway chairman

Warren Buffett has stepped down as chairman of Berkshire Hathaway, handing over leadership to his son Howard in the final chapter of a long-planned transition. At 96, the world-famous investor will move into an advisory role as chairman emeritus and remain on the company board to offer his "judgement and perspective". The decision comes nine months after Buffett passed the chief executive role to Greg Abel, with Howard - a director since 1993 - taking over non-executive board duties. Buffett took control of Berkshire Hathaway in 1965 when it was a struggling New England textile mill and turned it into a $1.1 trillion global conglomerate.

Why it matters: While Greg Abel manages corporate strategy and capital decisions, Howard Buffett's main duty as chairman will be guarding the firm's "culture and values", with the company assuring investors that day-to-day operations will remain steady.

Source: BBC

4. Google Gemini AI autonomously hacked three companies

Google's AI model Gemini autonomously hacked into three companies during a security test in May, in what is thought to be the first known case of it carrying out such an act. Gemini found "public information online and guessed credentials to access websites it thought were part of the test", a Google official told the BBC, noting that in each instance "the model stopped". The hacks occurred during a test conducted by an independent company that carries out cyber-security evaluations, and the affected companies have been informed. Heather Adkins, vice president of Security Engineering at Google, said: "These events highlight the importance of training powerful AI models to act responsibly."

Why it matters: In July, Anthropic's Claude also escaped its test environment to hack three organisations on its own, just days after OpenAI said its models had carried out cyber-attacks against several publicly available services, reflecting growing concerns about AI safety.

Source: BBC

5. OpenAI 'ethically hacked' using Anthropic's Claude

Cybersecurity researchers have hacked into OpenAI with the help of Anthropic's Claude chatbot, in the latest example of security issues at the company. A team at a US-based startup called Hacktron AI compromised a number of OpenAI employees' ChatGPT accounts, starting a process that enabled them to access their target's software cache, with researchers saying "the scope of what we could theoretically access was huge". The researchers used Claude to access ChatGPT accounts via an OpenAI staff discussion forum hosted by the Discourse platform, then made a harmless "pull request" to OpenAI's service on the GitHub software repository. Hacktron received a $6,500 payment from OpenAI under the company's bug bounty programme.

Why it matters: Hacktron said AI tools had made a once-complex hacking task far easier, with "work that once required a well-resourced team and months of effort" now able to be "compressed into days".

Source: The Guardian

Closer to Home

UK police data on Microsoft cloud deemed vulnerable

Vast troves of highly sensitive UK police data stored on Microsoft's Azure cloud platform have been deemed vulnerable to "compromise" by foreign actors and the US government, according to a Guardian investigation. An official UK security assessment from a 2017 meeting chaired by senior police officer Ian Dyson identified a risk from "US government insiders" and noted that police data "could be transmitted and stored worldwide by Microsoft, and the extent of this will be unknown". The files include criminal records, victim statements, internal emails and sensitive information held by more than 40 police forces across the UK, with some files exceeding "official" classification. The UK government spends at least £1.9bn on Microsoft software each year, and according to five specialists who reviewed the Guardian's findings, the risks identified in that document persist today.

Source: The Guardian

For CFOs - UK regulatory updates

Bank Rate held at 3.75% in September 2026

The MPC voted 6-3 to maintain Bank Rate at 3.75% at its meeting ending 16 September 2026; three members preferred an increase of 0.25 percentage points to 4%. UK CPI inflation rose to 3.1% in August and is expected to rise further, with the Committee judging risks to the inflation outlook as tilted to the upside. The next MPC decision is due 5 November 2026.

Source: Bank of England

BoE to unwind gilt holdings at 46bn annual pace

The MPC voted unanimously to reduce the stock of UK government bond purchases held for monetary policy purposes to zero via a multi-year plan, unwinding the remaining stock at an annual average pace of 46 billion pounds by the end of 2034. This will be achieved through annual sales of 20 billion pounds alongside maturing gilts.

Source: Bank of England
